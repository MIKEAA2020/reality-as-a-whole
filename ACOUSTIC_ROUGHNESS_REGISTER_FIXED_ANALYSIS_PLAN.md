# Fixed retrospective test of an acoustic roughness-only rule

This plan is fixed before running the calculations below, but **not** before the study, its authors' results, or inspection of the available predictor table: the published high-versus-middle result and the fact that modeled roughness decreases while sharpness increases were already known. This is an exploratory secondary analysis, not a prospective preregistration, an independent replication, or a new finding of a register effect.

## Data and prediction

Use Eerola and Lahdelma's public [register-and-consonance repository](https://github.com/tuomaseerola/consonance-register), pinned to commit `cdc4fc748e1535d9c0dd197f4c429bee80ff9514`: `data/data.csv` and `roughness_and_sharpness.csv`. Verify exact SHA-256 hashes in the code. The participant source contains recruitment identifiers and demographics; use only the check, repeat-consistency and 16 rating columns, do not print, copy or publish participant-level rows or identifiers, and save only aggregate results.

Use the authors' symbolic-input Hutchinson–Knopoff model `Roughness`, not the audio-derived `Sharpness` column, to define an external-stimulus feature before looking at the person-level ratings. For every chord check that roughness for each of R6 and R7 is lower than for each of R4 and R5. Fail rather than reinterpret the contrast if that is not true. R4 is `R0`, R5 `Ru1`, R6 `Ru2`, R7 `Ru3` in the raw rating column names.

Test a deliberately narrow hypothesis: **for each tested chord and listener, expected reported consonance is a single nonincreasing function of the externally computed roughness alone**, with no register, sharpness, timbre, perceptual context or other influences, and measurement errors have conditional mean zero. Different people may have different nonincreasing functions. Then each person's expected mean of R6/R7 minus R4/R5 is nonnegative for every chord and their mean is nonnegative. No brain mismatch, subject constitution or phenomenal identity is assumed by this operational rule. A more flexible function allowing additional variables or a different physical mismatch is not tested by this constraint.

## Frozen eligibility and statistics

Primary eligibility: headphone check ≥4; the three repeat-difference fields are numeric with mean absolute difference <4; all 16 target ratings for four chords and four registers are numeric and between 0 and 10. Count excluded rows separately in this order and stop on duplicate participant keys. This is a simple reproducible subset of the article's criteria, **not** a reproduction of its reported n=55, because we do not apply the response-dependent negative-correlation exclusion. Do not deduplicate using names or other external identifiers. This choice avoids outcome-dependent exclusion but is a different analysis population. Report the source's published n separately.

Compute each person's mean rating over all 8 high trials minus their mean over all 8 middle trials, and the four separate chord-specific two-versus-two differences. Report equal-person overall mean, high and middle means, and 95% percentile bootstrap interval for overall difference from 10,000 resamples of persons with seed 7163. The chords are four fixed selected stimuli, not a random sample of all music; the interval is conditional on the sampled stimuli, assumptions and inclusion, and does not capture stimulus-selection uncertainty or prove the identity of reported and experienced valence. No hypothesis selection or p-value threshold will be substituted after seeing results. Show the empirical proportion of participants with negative, zero and positive average differences as descriptive, not independent chord-level evidence.

For each chord compute its middle and high **participant-mean** ratings, and show the paired mean difference. For all chords combined let `d=mean_high−mean_middle`. Any roughness-only monotone expectation has predicted high mean ≥ middle mean. Therefore for these two observed cell means, if d<0, the unweighted RMSE of any pair of such predictions is at least `−d/2`. This is a deterministic sample-fit bound, not a population or cross-validation bound, and cannot establish phenomenology. Check both predictor-file ordering and the bound computationally.

Primary high−middle interval entirely below zero supports a violation of the stipulated monotone roughness-only group-mean restriction under sampling and report assumptions. An interval including zero is inconclusive, not proof of the restriction. This observational contrast changes register and multiple acoustic features together; it cannot establish sharpness as the cause, isolate a neural mechanism, or refute the user's broader internal relational mismatch proposal. Generalization to pain, other cultures, other timbres, other subject types, and fundamental strings is not warranted.
