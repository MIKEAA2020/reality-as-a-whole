# Quantum information, neural music, and the surviving brain

## What the supplied articles contribute

The two supplied essays are relevant to different parts of the framework. [Vlatko Vedral's **“Quantum Information as Everything”**](https://vlatkovedral.substack.com/p/quantum-information-as-everything) argues for quantum information as fundamental and discusses the older Ur program's relation between two-component spinors and relativistic geometry. [**“Missing Brains and the ‘Music Model’ of Consciousness”**](https://scienceandculture.com/2025/08/missing-brains-and-the-music-model-of-consciousness/) comments on Gazzaniga and Queenan's analogy between multiscale neural activity and music, and argues for an immaterial mind. The former is a physics-oriented conceptual essay; the latter is a secondary philosophical commentary. Neither is a new experiment proving the whole-account.

**Result:** The spinor correspondence is substantive but needs more than a qubit's number of components to recover physical spacetime. The clinical cases show that striking brain reshaping can coexist with substantial preserved function, but they do **not** establish consciousness without a brain. In the central quantitative case, appreciable brain tissue remained and important impairments were reported. The music analogy encourages studying dynamic relations across different anatomies, but neither rhythmic coordination nor information alone establishes experience, its specific quality, or the boundaries of plural subjects.

All seven criteria remain joint requirements, particularly **full structural and experiential intelligibility**. First-person experience remains an accepted datum. No universal subject, external creator or temporal process that actualizes possibilities is added here.

## 1. What the Ur proposal actually does

The cited paper by Weizsäcker, Scheibe and Süssmann, [*Komplementarität und Logik III. Mehrfache Quantelung*](https://doi.org/10.1515/zna-1958-0901), contains a definite construction, not just a numerological comparison. In its section 2a it uses an **unnormalized** two-component complex spinor `u`, the identity and Pauli matrices, and the quadratic map

```text
k⁰ = u†u,             kⁱ = u† σⁱ u,           (k⁰)² − |k|² = 0.
```

The image is a future null vector (including zero). Global phase is lost. This is a standard spinor–null-vector construction, not a new derivation by this project. The original paper explicitly calls the four-vector representation redundant, chooses an interpretation as massless four-momentum, and states that at this stage it has not yet interpreted spacetime translations. Its later step introduces a momentum-space function, a null-cone constraint and a Fourier representation, and the paper explicitly discusses how translations enter there. It also says that its quantization prescription is chosen in part to recover known field-theory formulae and that further choices and doubling of state spaces are needed. These are important qualifications, not reasons to dismiss the program.

A normalized qubit pure state is a **ray**: two complex amplitudes have four real coordinates, but normalization and unobservable global phase leave **two** independent real parameters. An unnormalized nonzero spinor modulo phase carries three, corresponding to a future null vector with a scale. Thus “four real amplitude components” alone is not four independent spacetime coordinates, nor is this map by itself a derivation of spacetime events, general relativity, interactions or the Standard Model. The initial construction describes the null cone in a chosen vector representation; additional rules are needed to give it physical meaning and recover coordinates, transformations, dynamics and observable measurements. The original authors recognize several of these steps; we do not assume they claimed the simple component count alone was the full derivation.

The massless Klein–Gordon equation is a **scalar** equation. Components of the electromagnetic potential in a suitable gauge can obey massless wave equations, but photons additionally require electromagnetic field structure, gauge constraints and physical polarization/helicity. Calling the scalar equation alone *the photon equation* is therefore misleading. The original paper's abstract separately discusses coupling alternatives to obtain Maxwell equations; we have not audited that later derivation. Vedral expressly leaves the general-relativity argument for another post, so no gravitational derivation is supplied in this essay.

### A precise transformation check

For nonzero `u=(1,1)` take `B=diag(2,1/2)`, with determinant 1. The spinor map gives

```text
u:      k = (2, 2, 0, 0),
Bu:     k = (17/4, 2, 0, 15/4).
```

Both vectors are null, and they are related by a Lorentz boost in the z direction. But `B` is **not unitary under the fixed standard qubit inner product**. If the same matrix is applied and each output normalized as an ordinary qubit state, the fidelity between `|0⟩` and `|+⟩` changes from `1/2` to `16/17`. Hence the full `SL(2,C)` action must not be silently identified with ordinary unitary dynamics of an isolated fixed-inner-product qubit. This is an elementary illustration of a standard distinction, not an objection to relativity or a new discovery.

There are rigorous relativistic spin descriptions that use momentum-dependent inner products and transformations between appropriately defined state spaces; see Palmer, Takahashi and Westman, [*WKB analysis of relativistic Stern-Gerlach measurements*](https://arxiv.org/html/1208.6434). The example does **not** assert that actual relativistic frame changes change physical distinguishability, or that the cited Ur authors made that claim. It identifies an extra interpretive step needed to connect covariant spinor structure, usual qubit information and physical operations.

## 2. Classical–quantum interaction: a necessary qualification

Vedral argues that classical information interacting with quantum information cannot comply with principles such as information conservation. The concern is meaningful for particular proposed **fundamental, two-way dynamical hybrids**, but the blanket wording is too broad if applied to all classical–quantum operations. Dammeier and Werner, [*Quantum-Classical Hybrid Systems and their Quasifree Transformations*](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.05020), develop a formal hybrid-channel framework and discuss why gaining quantum information in a classical variable requires irreversible evolution under their stated conditions. We have read only their introductory portions, not their full results.

A simple **one-way** example is a classical bit `C` that controls a quantum bit `Q` by a controlled-NOT `U=|0⟩⟨0|⊗I + |1⟩⟨1|⊗X`. It preserves the block-diagonal classical–quantum state space, and `U²=I`. Start with a uniformly random C and Q in `|0⟩`: the output is an equal mixture of `|00⟩` and `|11⟩`. Global von Neumann entropy remains one bit, while Q acquires one bit of correlation with C; applying the same gate reverses the change. This shows consistent, nontrivial **classical-to-quantum control**, not unrestricted two-way interaction, not a reversible measurement by C of an arbitrary quantum input, and not proof of an ultimately fundamental classical sector. A reduced subsystem's entropy or information can change while global distinguishability and entropy are preserved.

The [finite checker](verification/ur_spinor_classical_control_gate.py) verifies these examples and their [receipt](verification/ur_spinor_classical_control_gate.json) records the numerical values. This does not establish that information is ontologically fundamental or that correlations are experience.

## 3. The medical cases: altered brains, not absent brains

The original essay behind the commentary is Michael Gazzaniga and Bridget Queenan, [*Is your brain really necessary for consciousness?*](https://iai.tv/articles/is-your-brain-really-necessary-for-consciousness-auid-3280?_auid=2020). Its prose through the conclusion was accessible on the alternate URL, despite a subscription prompt. It compares neural organization at multiple timescales to music and resists making linguistic performance a prerequisite for consciousness. That is a useful inclusive correction: a person's difficulty speaking, a disability, or an animal's lack of human language is not evidence by itself that experience is absent.

The underlying clinical reports require more careful description than the headline:

- **[Alders et al., *Volumetric MRI Analysis of a Case of Severe Ventriculomegaly*](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2018.00495):** the patient had large ventricles and cortical thinning, but measured gray matter was `432,184 mm³` versus a four-control mean of `599,645 mm³`, and measured white matter `333,606 mm³` versus `452,639 mm³`. Patient segmentation was manual; controls were processed automatically, and the comparison group was very small, so the ratios are descriptive, not a validated estimate of loss or neuronal count. There was preserved bilingual communication and musical participation, but also major cognitive, memory and mood problems and dependence on family. The authors discuss adaptive reorganization as a plausible interpretation; the case does not experimentally demonstrate its specific mechanisms.
- **[Esenwa and Leaf, *Colpocephaly in adults*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3669935/):** the 60-year-old woman had enlarged posterior ventricles, thin parieto-occipital white matter and partial corpus-callosum agenesis. She had significant visual disability, gait difficulties and recent memory deficits, alongside preserved language and considerable prior independence. At follow-up she needed assistance. This is not evidence that the entire back of the brain was literally absent.
- **[Persad et al., *Asymptomatic hydrocephalus*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8049644/):** the 72-year-old woman lived independently and was alert and oriented, with markedly thinned cortex and enlarged ventricles. She presented after a seizure; the authors regarded a causal link to chronic hydrocephalus as uncertain. No direct neural-wave constitution of experience was measured.
- **[Ferris et al., *Life without a brain: Neuroradiological and behavioral evidence of neuroplasticity necessary to sustain brain function in the face of severe hydrocephalus*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-019-53042-3):** the rat had severely altered and thinned brain tissue, not literally no brain. The authors report preserved performance in certain behavioral tasks and brain imaging responses. This is one case and cannot establish the animal's specific phenomenal character or a general conservation law for subjects.

These observations challenge **simple brain-volume-only or rigid normal-anatomy necessity claims**. They do not establish that brain tissue is generally unnecessary for human experiences, that a particular cortex-independent model is correct, or that the particular remaining anatomy by itself constitutes a subject. Nor do impairments negate the dignity or reality of the patients' experience. A large fluid space is not a measurement of neural organization, neuronal number, signaling capacity or quality of life.

## 4. Distinguishing music, wakefulness and experience

The music proposal points toward an experimentally meaningful candidate: organization across scales might predict an anchored experiential contrast beyond gross anatomical volume. It remains to specify the candidate dynamics, its boundaries, time window, physical readout, rival predictors, uncertainty and a genuinely relevant experiential anchor. Frequency bands and coordinated oscillations may participate in many processes; not every oscillation is a subject, every harmony pleasant, or every physical mismatch unpleasant. The project's [earlier musical empirical audit](MUSIC_QUANTUM_EXPERIENCE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md) already distinguishes auditory grouping from felt pleasantness, and its [calibration transport gate](EXPERIENTIAL_MISMATCH_CALIBRATION_TRANSPORT_GATE.md) states why a source-domain relationship may not transfer to a different brain or condition.

Gazzaniga and Queenan also present a list of sleeping versus waking bodily polyrhythms and say a cortex is an absolute requirement for conscious wakefulness. These should be treated as proposals, not established universal tests. Dreams can be experienced during sleep; in the [Siclari et al. study](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.4545), reports followed both REM and non-REM awakenings, and reported experience correlated with regional EEG features. That does not imply every non-report means no experience. Conversely, behavioral observations in severe hydranencephaly have been argued to support subcortical experience, but cortical remnants and interpretations are contested; see [Merker, *Consciousness without a cerebral cortex: A challenge for neuroscience and medicine*](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X07000891). The ventricular-enlargement cases do not resolve this controversy because cortex remained.

The secondary commentary argues that the analogy leaves out the composer's immaterial mind. It is right that a physical performance description does not, without a further account, settle **why or how a real experience is present**. But neither the analogy's use of neural processes nor the existence of musical composition proves a separate immaterial soul. An experiential ground, a distinct-soul view, and experience-respecting physicalist or neutral-ground accounts can agree that experience is real while differing on constitution. It is also inaccurate to equate every claim that mind depends on brain activity with **eliminative** denial of experience. The project excludes the latter, not all scientifically informed alternatives.

## 5. Constructive framework contract

These articles support a more demanding common task, not a shortcut:

1. **Physical recovery:** specify the physical state spaces, allowed operations and relational/time structures, and derive or recover tested quantum and spacetime results. A spinor correspondence is one ingredient, not gravity or the full universe.
2. **Diverse realizations:** specify which dynamical and causal relations must be preserved across anatomically different systems, and which differences matter. This is a testable **candidate** for substrate independence, not the assumption that all systems with similar rhythms feel alike.
3. **Actual experience:** identify a contrast as experienced by a subject, and describe how first-person evidence and any communicated report bear on it. Do not identify a report or processing pattern with the experience by definition.
4. **Subject incidence and quality:** say why a given organization constitutes one subject rather than several, and why it has this particular quality; compare same-physics, experience-respecting alternatives. Neither a music metaphor nor an information measure supplies these rules.
5. **Interventions and generalization:** where ethically appropriate, compare a preregistered multiscale candidate with volume, preserved anatomy, task and context baselines, using held-out individuals and condition-valid experiential evidence. Case reports are not a randomized intervention, and rare clinical cases may be heavily selected. No study design here is a clinical recommendation.

These conditions do not prove that reality is self-sufficient, experiential or necessarily actual. They explain what an account accepting those hypotheses must still produce, while preserving genuinely plural subjects and no external universal agent or master clock.
