# Fixed calculation plan: physical structures and the combination argument

## Question and scope

Apply the newly read section 8 of [Chalmers's *The Combination Problem for Panpsychism*](sources/uploads/combination.pdf) to the existing E-L four-spin kernel at `t=1/4`. Chalmers distinguishes narrow macroscopic physical structure from broader physical structures, including informational ones, and explicitly recognizes that invoking the latter still owes an account of phenomenal constitution. The following is a bounded illustration of that distinction, not a disproof of his argument and not a mapping from physical structure to experience.

## Prespecified calculations

Use the existing rational heat-bath transition kernel, all 16 states and their exact positive-probability transitions. Inspect four fixed descriptions: (i) all four spin signs, (ii) all three neighbor mismatch bits, (iii) the two outer mismatch bits, and (iv) one binary any-mismatch bit. Construct each projected **unweighted simple support graph**, retaining an edge if some positive-probability transition joins distinct projected values, and omitting self-loops. Report vertex and edge counts and degree multisets. Check the claimed shapes `4-cube`, `K4,4`, `4-cycle`, and single edge using elementary adjacency/degree tests; show the full-edge and two-outer projections commute with left/right reversal. This is support only: it deliberately discards transition probabilities and cannot prove causal equivalence or an autonomous quotient.

Independently calculate exact maximum total variation distance between projected transition rows among fine states in the same projection fiber. Zero is the standard strong-lumpability block condition; positive means the corresponding projection is not first-order Markov for all initial distributions. Compare against the prior exact audit for the two outer bits and all three bits. Do not describe the projected support graph as a phenomenological space, as an empirical finding, or as a substitute for the full transition kernel. Absence of strong lumpability does not disqualify an open subject.

## Assessment

Identify two separate issues: a nonisomorphic *physical* broad description may be available, but which description, interventions and environmental variables are justified still requires argument; even an adequate physically motivated description does not establish why it is experienced. Treat an experiential-ground L candidate, an experiential-ground connected-component rival, and an experience-respecting neutral-ground rival fairly on that shared physical domain. Do not infer any rival is genuinely metaphysically possible from a formal model or from the user's thesis that all genuine possibilities are actual. No new experimental participant, invasive intervention, physical-theory or clock assumption is made.
