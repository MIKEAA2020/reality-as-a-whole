# Reformulating composition: incidence, temporal extent, and persistence

## Decision

The two recent obstructions do not select the true composition law. They do show why it is premature to require that Γ return a single partition of all event tokens into disjoint, finite, temporally binned episodes.

The next candidate should distinguish:

1. **within-episode incidence:** which qualitative contents belong together in an episode;
2. **temporal extent:** which physical occurrences constitute that episode across time;
3. **persistence or lineage:** how episodes or subjects are related through change, including possible division or combination.

This is a revision of the proposed theory's output structure, not an empirical finding that experience has a particular temporal form. The physical/phenomenal identification is still missing.

## 1. Why a global connectivity class is too strong a default

Consider a purely formal candidate history:

| Stage | Proposed incidence |
|---|---|
| t₀ | A and B are separate |
| t₁ | a proposed joint organization contains descendants of both |
| t₂ | the organization is again separated |

This table is a hypothetical incidence assignment, not an observation of subject fusion or fission. Its purpose is to expose what different representations assume.

If all continuation and joint-incidence links are put into one undirected graph, taking connected components groups the earlier A and B together because of their later connection. The result no longer represents their stipulated separation at t₀. The same problem arises if numerical identity is inferred from every overlap or continuation relation: transitivity can turn a history of combination or division into one undifferentiated entity.

It does not follow that subject fusion or fission is possible, or that identity is nontransitive. It follows that **a model of possible change must distinguish numerical identity from causal continuation, overlap, and lineage**. If a theory excludes fusion and fission, it must state and justify that constraint rather than conceal it in a graph construction.

## 2. A less presumptive output for Γ

For a realized process P, let a candidate output consist of:

\[
\Gamma(P)=(\mathcal E, I, L).
\]

- E is a collection of putative episode occurrences.
- I relates each episode to its constituting physical occurrences and qualitative contents, with temporal information.
- L records the specified continuation or lineage relations between episodes, if such relations are part of the theory.

No claim is made that E already has a known cardinality, that I is a disjoint partition, or that L is numerical identity. If the theory asserts disjointness, overlap, a unique stream, or an identity criterion, it must add the corresponding justified restriction.

The qualitative law remains χ(P,e). Introducing E does not establish that any proposed episode exists. Calling candidate graph nodes “episodes” would merely supply the target assignment unless there is a warranted constitutive identification.

A finite sequence of time-indexed partitions is one special case, useful for models that assume nonoverlapping membership at each stage. It is not the general theory. In a relativistic setting, arbitrary coordinate slices cannot become constitutive simultaneity by fiat; any physical or phenomenal temporal structure used must be declared and justified.

## 3. Three distinct kinds of equivalence

The law must preserve a distinction among:

### A. Re-presentation of the same occurrence

Different coordinates or mathematical frameworks describe the same actual process. They must not create extra episode tokens merely because the descriptions differ.

### B. Distinct but constitutively equivalent realizations

Two actual implementations may instantiate the same organization. If realized-process sufficiency is true, their phenomenal organizations are equivalent. This is not numerical identity of their episode occurrences.

### C. A process changing through time

Earlier and later organizations may be causally related, overlapping, or constitutively continuous. None of these relations automatically proves numerical identity of a subject or establishes one temporally extended episode.

Much apparent unification comes from treating A, B, and C as the same relation. A viable constitution theory must specify which relation it is using in each inference.

## 4. A positive candidate form, with its unfilled component exposed

The revised minimal hypothesis is:

> **An actual episode is constituted by a temporally organized realization of jointly differentiated qualitative content. Its content incidence and temporal extent are determined by a realization law; persistence through change is specified separately rather than inferred from mere connectivity.**

This improves the target structure, but “jointly differentiated” is not yet a physical criterion. If it means phenomenally together, it names the target. If it means causally interacting, it requires the missing argument from interaction to phenomenal togetherness. The revised hypothesis must not claim to cross that gap by changing vocabulary.

A prospective completion would therefore need a specified relation J on the realized process that is independently identified with within-episode togetherness, together with a temporal realization rule. The requirements on J include:

- it must concern actual realization, not a chosen encoding;
- it must distinguish common content determination from mere copied monitoring where the candidate says that distinction matters;
- its episode predictions must survive the weak-link and temporal-origin audits;
- it must recover warranted phenomenal distinctions in its claimed domain;
- it must state whether it licenses disjoint, overlapping, or other incidence structures.

These requirements constrain a candidate; they do not define a known J. There is no justified J in the current repository that satisfies the full explanatory job.

## 5. What can be anchored without assuming the answer?

The inquiry can begin with warranted phenomenal occurrence and contrasts rather than trying to derive their existence from a toy graph. That is legitimate evidence, not a license to make every report infallible.

For example, a narrowly specified discrimination can anchor a target contrast in ordinary conditions. Then interventions can test proposed physical determinations of that contrast. But the evidence model must say why the same target identification remains warranted under the intervention, especially when monitoring or reporting is disrupted.

This yields a staged task:

1. Declare the phenomenal target and the conditions in which it is warranted.
2. Specify rival physical determinations and their separating interventions.
3. Model the reliability and failure modes of the evidence channels in those interventions.
4. Identify which candidates survive, preserving an unresolved set where necessary.
5. Only then ask whether the identified physical determination warrants a constitutive identity.
6. Test episode incidence separately; content identification does not automatically supply a subject boundary or persistence criterion.

The source/monitor construction already specifies the abstract separating contrasts. The missing contribution is not a larger synthetic table. It is a warranted phenomenal calibration and a substantive identity/composition argument. The repository contains no newly collected human data that could supply it.

## 6. Explicit stopping rule against simulated discovery

At this point another graph algorithm, integration measure, or list of toy consciousness labels would not select J, Γ, or χ. It could check an expressly proposed rule for consistency, but must not be reported as discovering the rule's phenomenal truth.

The next substantive advance requires at least one of:

- a **specified constitutive identity argument** with premises strong enough to determine a qualitative or incidence constraint and a defense of those premises;
- a **warranted phenomenal contrast** that excludes a serious proposed determination;
- an **independently specified physical composition law plus evidence or argument for its phenomenal significance**.

A new primitive can be proposed, but it must be counted as an additional hypothesis and its own foundation remains part of the zero-residue objective. In particular, making co-consciousness primitive would provide a target relation, not yet explain why a physical process instantiates it.

## 7. Current result

The unified candidate is now less vulnerable to three conflations: physical recurrence versus return to an event token, timescale versus temporal boundary, and lineage versus numerical identity. Its composition output can represent these distinctions without manufacturing a single global subject from connected history.

But the output remains unselected. We have not identified the physical relation that is qualitative togetherness, a true temporal realization law, or the intrinsic reason why a specified process is an experience. This reformulation is preparatory progress, not a completed constitutive principle.

No new tests or empirical findings are claimed. The existing 1,048 tests remain checks of the earlier mathematical constructions and conditional obstructions.
