# Music appraisal: full-paper reading and centrality consistency audit

## Main findings

The supplied paper is Bakas, Adamos and Laskaris, [*On the estimate of music appraisal from surface EEG: a dynamic-network approach based on cross-sensor PAC measurements*](https://doi.org/10.1088/1741-2552/abffe6). Reading it corrects and materially deepens our earlier [four-person feasibility report](MUSIC_APPRAISAL_EEG_FEASIBILITY_AND_REPORT_GAP.md):

1. **There was post-session confirmation.** The three song-preference categories were assigned during playlist preparation, but the paper also says participants were asked to confirm their ratings **after completion of the recording**. I previously reported the advance assignment without mentioning this confirmation because only the dataset README and paper abstract were available. The paper does not describe an independent immediate post-clip felt-valence rating, the questions used for confirmation, changes to ratings, or how disagreement was handled. The precise meaning and timing of the labels thus remain weaker than a separately measured, time-specific report of felt pleasantness.
2. The article reports **personalized** dynamic-network predictions with mean MAE `0.948` and R² `0.63` across 20 people, plus **leave-one-person-out** generic MAE `1.315` and R² `0.21`. A separate DEAP application reports personalized MAE `1.106` and R² `0.68`; its scale and task differ. These are **authors' results**, not our reproductions. Our previous four-person alpha phase-dispersion screen is neither their model nor an independent refutation.
3. The printed dynamic-centrality equation and the procedure of a cited toolbox are not the same. The equation as printed admits negative receive centrality on a valid directed ten-edge example, despite the paper's statement that both centralities are positive. The toolbox uses inverse factors and its available routine treats contacts as undirected. It is unknown which implementation or adaptation generated the published results. This is an identified **formula/implementation ambiguity**, not a finding that the authors' scores are wrong.
4. The supplementary ANOVA degrees of freedom exactly match the number of trials multiplied by the number of PAC features, less three. That makes dependence among features and repeated observations a major inferential concern. We cannot verify the statistical implementation without its analysis code and data.

## Task, data and decoder

The original 20 participants compiled their own playlists with ten songs each in categories 1, 3 and 5; the categories are judgments about liking the **song**, not measurements of suffering. An interview before the experiment could fine-tune playlist choices. After the entire session participants were asked to confirm ratings. The study used 14-channel, 128 Hz Emotiv scalp EEG, 80-second excerpts and randomized presentation. The paper describes left/right mastoid acquisition reference, 1–45 Hz filtering, trial-specific wavelet-enhanced ICA and common-average rereferencing for the study pipeline. The precise processing/reference stage of the public MAT arrays was not independently established; it remains misleading to label the public files' native reference simply as mastoid.

For each pair of sensors, PAC measures the association between lower-frequency phase and the phase of the higher-frequency amplitude envelope. Using seven EEG bands and 10-second overlapping windows, the paper constructs time-varying functional graphs. It describes keeping the ten strongest connections in each frame, calculating dynamic graph broadcast/receive measures, and retaining the two gamma receive centralities for each two-sensor subnetwork. Sequential forward selection, with an SVR trained on a training set and selected on a validation set, yields at most ten subnetworks; an SVR refitted on train plus validation is evaluated on the held-out test trials. The paper also compares static within-sensor PAC, static cross-sensor PAC and PSD features.

For each participant, 30 trials are randomly split into equally sized training, validation and test sets; the paper says this is repeated 50 times. Therefore its personalized result is not a test of a model trained on other people, and the 50 scores are overlapping reuses of the same small song collection, not 50 independent replications. The generic leave-one-subject-out procedure trains and validates on the remaining participants and tests on a held-out participant. In DEAP, the paper reports both personalized models and a generic model (generic MAE `1.768`, R² `0.12`); it is an additional dataset application, not a prospective trial of the exact original personalized models on new people. Figures 2–4 focus on subject 4, chosen for best personalized performance; this is illustrative selection, not representative evidence.

The paper states that the **choice of gamma receive-centrality features** was motivated by initial across-subject contrasts performed on its own data, before describing its test splits. Since label-dependent global feature-family choice can use information from later test subjects/trials, the printed split description alone does not establish a fully nested test of the whole analysis-selection procedure. This concern does not imply that the individual forward selection inside each split was performed on its test set; the described forward selection uses train/validation. No quantitative bias, score correction or actual leakage in an implementation has been demonstrated. An appropriate replication would prespecify the gamma choice externally, or repeat all label-dependent choices inside each outer training fold, and use a genuinely untouched independent test.

The paper calls `1.5` a random-assignment MAE comparator for its own data. Our balanced-category constant-3 full-sample reference is `4/3`; it concerns a different rule and the full balanced set, not necessarily the distribution or average error of each random split. Neither is by itself a reconstructed apples-to-apples test-set baseline. DEAP uses ratings from a different scale and experimental design, so its MAE is not directly comparable to the music dataset's.

## A testable equation discrepancy

Paper equation (6), visually inspected on page 6, prints

`P = ∏τ (I − α A[τ])`, with `α = 0.1` and normalized `Q = P / ||P||₂`.

The text interprets `Q` as a nonnegative weighted count of temporally feasible walks and states that both node centralities are positive. Those interpretations do not follow from the printed expression. For a check compatible with the paper's two-sensor, seven-band, directed 14-node construction, let a gamma node receive edges from ten lower-rhythm nodes, and use that same ten-edge binary adjacency `A` at two successive frames. There are no outgoing edges from the gamma node, so `A² = 0`. The literal expression gives `P = I − 0.2 A`; the target's column sum is `1 − 0.2 × 10 = −1`. Normalization by the positive norm preserves that negative sign. This is a mathematical counterexample to unconditional positivity, not a reconstruction of the article's actual graphs or their values.

The separately described resolvent product `∏τ (I − α A[τ])⁻¹` counts weighted walks under its usual convergence assumptions. For this nilpotent example, the same target column sum is `1 + 0.2 × 10 = 3` before normalization. This comparison uses an established construction, not a claimed new derivation; see [Grindrod et al., *Communicability across evolving networks*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.83.046120) and [the cited Sizemore–Bassett toolbox](https://github.com/asizemore/Dynamic-Graph-Metrics/tree/ab5a9ef630e3095724e0222cd0cbe595241cd07e). We inspected the toolbox's [`broadcastRecieveCentrality.m`](https://github.com/asizemore/Dynamic-Graph-Metrics/blob/ab5a9ef630e3095724e0222cd0cbe595241cd07e/broadcastRecieveCentrality.m): it multiplies inverse factors, not the printed factors. That specific routine sets `directed = 0`, and its thresholding depends on the input/contact conversion; it is **not** identical to the directed resolvent comparison in our witness. The study may have used a modified toolbox or the printed equation may contain a typographical omission; the available evidence cannot decide. Neither our witness nor the toolbox inspection computes their EEG results.

## Supplementary statistics: unit of analysis and reported effect sizes

For static PAC contrasts, the supplement reports `F(2, 176397) = 3.38`, `p = 0.03`, `f = 0.11` within sensors, and `F(2, 2469597) = 10.04`, `p < 0.01`, `f = 0.21` for the extended set. The reported methods provide 20 people × 30 trials, with 294 within-sensor and 4,116 extended PAC descriptors per trial. The equalities

- `20 × 30 × 294 − 3 = 176397`, and
- `20 × 30 × 4116 − 3 = 2469597`

exactly match the printed residual degrees of freedom of an ordinary three-group ANOVA treating each feature-trial measurement as an observation. This arithmetic is a strong **diagnostic**, not proof of the actual code or a substitute for inspecting the model. Such measurements share participant, trial, electrodes and frequencies and are not 176,400 or 2,469,600 independent people. If the analysis pooled them as independent without modeling that nesting, its ordinary p-values would not justify participant-level generalization. A participant-clustered or hierarchical analysis, with the tested contrast and multiplicity prespecified, is needed; we have not calculated one.

Under the conventional one-way fixed-effects identity `f = sqrt(F × df1 / df2)` for the same F statistic and residual degrees of freedom, the corresponding values are approximately `0.00619` and `0.00285`, not the reported `0.11` and `0.21`. The supplement also gives a paired `t = 6.19`, `N = 2,646,000`, and `d = 0.22`; **if** that N counts pairs and `d` means paired standardized mean difference `d_z`, then `d_z = t / sqrt(N) ≈ 0.003805`. Other effect-size definitions, denominators or reporting conventions may have been used; these numerical discrepancies alone do not identify which value is wrong. Notably `2,646,000 = 600 × (294 + 4116)`, again matching descriptor counts rather than participant counts. We have not reproduced the tests.

The supplement reports no significant overall association of the audio-envelope/PAC distance correlation with rating in its stated ANOVA. This is not proof that all audio or context effects are absent. Given adaptive song selection, no independent acoustic-control analysis here, scalp mixing and correlated measures, neither PAC nor a graph statistic by itself identifies physical interregional coupling or preference frustration. Cross-frequency coupling is not automatically immune to common sources, signal artifacts or nonlinear waveform effects.

## Impact on the framework

The paper is a serious prior dynamic-connectivity comparator and must be cited rather than reinvented. Its labels and network measures are relevant to music preference, but they do not establish the proposed misalignment-to-discomfort identity, the physical interaction preferences needed to calculate frustration, subject boundaries or the ontological experiential bridge. C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7, with experience accepted as a datum. B4–B5 and B10 remain open.
