# A relational clock through turning points

## 1. Direct time target and result

[Clock-choice equivalence](CLOCK_CHOICE_EQUIVALENCE.md) required ideal evaluations on a common physical space. We now construct a continuum clock whose scalar reading actually turns, and replace a single global reduction by an orientation-resolved atlas.

In one stationary constrained model:

- a periodic phase gives ideal unitary evaluations;
- its cosine reading reverses direction and is not a global clock;
- cosine/sine charts patch regularly across each other's turning points;
- chart Hamiltonians have the correct chain-rule relation and finite integrated transport;
- correctly transformed sequential record probabilities agree across a turning point;
- discarding the unresolved orientation implements a nonunitary channel and can erase physical information.

The chart singularity is not a singularity of the physical state. Conversely, a chart atlas does not manufacture an unbounded clock: a winding counter would be additional structure.

This is an exact constrained quantum example, using established parametrized-system/clock-chart mathematics. It is not a solution of gravitational refoliation or of clocks with arbitrary clock-matter interactions.

## 2. One fixed periodic constraint

Take H_kin=L2(S1,dtheta) tensor C2 and system Hamiltonian h=diag(0,1), in units where the clock period is 2*pi. On periodic first-Sobolev functions define the self-adjoint constraint

    C=-i*d/dtheta+h.

The bounded h commutes with the clock derivative. Fourier decomposition proves self-adjointness and gives the full kernel:

    (J phi)(theta)=exp(-i*h*theta) phi/sqrt(2*pi),
    H_phys=C2,  J^*J=I,  range(J)=ker(C).

Thus C J=0. C^2 is a positive constraint with the same physical kernel if a positive presentation is wanted. No auxiliary flow generated by C is being interpreted as observed physical evolution.

For physical density rho, J rho J^* is a normalized stationary history. The phase evaluation is a density-valued map

    E_theta=U(theta)/sqrt(2*pi), U(theta)=diag(1,exp(-i theta)).

Its effects integrate to identity. Point evaluation is not a bounded map on arbitrary L2 functions; it is well defined on this finite-dimensional smooth physical solution space. Conditioning is understood through probability densities or finite bins, not a nonzero probability for an exact continuous outcome.

The occupied clock Fourier sector has only two modes. Unitary conditional evaluation frames therefore do not imply arbitrarily sharp autonomous clock resolution: a continuous outcome label is not a proof of unlimited timekeeping accuracy. Detector triggering and a clock accuracy/resource analysis are not supplied by this kernel calculation.

The uniform phase density 1/(2*pi) is normalizable because the clock is compact. It does not contradict the earlier obstruction to a uniform normal clock on the integers or a noncompact translation orbit.

Periodicity requires exp(-i*2*pi*h)=I on the allowed physical sector. For a general h, the periodic constraint retains only compatible Fourier energies. We have supplied a commensurate spectrum, not derived universal energy quantization or a universal recurrence of reality.

## 3. A scalar clock that turns

Let q=cos(theta). Its velocity relative to the phase parameter is -sin(theta), vanishing at theta=0,pi. Every -1<q<1 has two preimages. Put

    alpha=arccos(q), s=sign(sin(theta)),
    theta_s(q)=s*alpha modulo 2*pi,
    v(q)=sqrt(1-q^2).

The orientation s distinguishes the two halves of the orbit; actual q-velocity has sign -s. The normalized frames are

    psi_(q,s)=U(theta_s(q)) phi,
    U_(q,s)=diag(1,q-i*s*v(q)).

Each is unitary. With the induced phase measure the evaluation density is

    E_(q,s)=U_(q,s)/sqrt(2*pi*v(q)),
    p(q,s|rho)=1/(2*pi*v(q)),
    p(q|rho)=1/(pi*sqrt(1-q^2)).

The weights are a Jacobian, not an arbitrary clock prior. The density diverges at the turning readings but is integrable; there are no atoms at q=+1 or q=-1. For example P(q in [1-epsilon,1])=arccos(1-epsilon)/pi.

For a wavefunction representation with ordinary dq measure, retain half densities

    eta_(q,s)=sqrt(|dtheta_s/dq|)*psi_(q,s)/sqrt(2*pi).

Then sum_s integral ||eta_(q,s)||^2 dq=||phi||^2. Normalized frame vectors and density-weighted physical amplitudes must not be conflated.

## 4. Chart generators and a regular atlas

On an open orientation branch,

    i*d psi_(q,s)/dq = h_(q,s) psi_(q,s),
    h_(q,s)=-(s/v(q))*h.

The generator diverges at q turning points. Nevertheless

    U(q_2,s) U(q_1,s)^*
        = exp[-i*h*(theta_s(q_2)-theta_s(q_1))]

has finite limits there: the integral of 1/sqrt(1-q^2) is finite. A divergent coordinate generator alone is not a dynamical singularity.

For half densities the corresponding equation is

    i*[d/dq-(1/2)*d log|dtheta_s/dq|/dq] eta = h_(q,s) eta.

The extra density connection is required; it is not an additional physical interaction.

To avoid using a singular chart, add r=sin(theta), with c=sign(cos(theta)). Its normalized frames and generator are

    U_(r,c)=diag(1,c*sqrt(1-r^2)-i*r),
    h_(r,c)=(c/sqrt(1-r^2))*h.

Use q charts where |sin(theta)|>1/2 and r charts where |cos(theta)|>1/2. They cover the whole circle, and each selected chart has generator norm below 2*||h||. In particular r is regular at every q turning point, and q is regular at every r turning point. On overlaps q^2+r^2=1 with the correct signs, so the two frames agree. Generators obey

    h_(r,c)=h_(q,s)*(dq/dr), dq/dr=-r/q.

Half densities transform by eta_r=sqrt(|dq/dr|)*eta_q. Branchwise transition and transport operators compose exactly. Choosing different local lifts of theta changes it by 2*pi; the supplied physical-sector holonomy is identity. These statements patch the reductions of the **same C**, rather than selecting a new Hamiltonian independently in each chart.

## 5. What forgetting orientation does

Specify the instrument that reads phase and then retains only q (or traces out the unresolved orientation in its conditional output). At fixed q, its reduced system state is

    rho_q=(1/2)[U_(q,+) rho U_(q,+)^*+U_(q,-) rho U_(q,-)^*]
         =[[rho_00,q*rho_01],[q*rho_10,rho_11]].

It is a dephasing channel, not a unitary clock-frame change. At q=0 the two orthogonal physical inputs |+> and |-> give identical reduced outputs I/2. Their complete coherent histories remain orthogonal. At q=+1 and q=-1 the two branch frames agree in the limiting density state, even though the q-coordinate derivative is singular. Information loss and a turning-coordinate singularity are therefore different issues.

Keeping the oriented reading recovers the unitary frames. Keeping a coherent orientation register can retain still more instrument-level information. The stated loss belongs to the specified reduced/coarse-grained output; it is not a claim that every coherent apparatus implementation with a q label destroys the same information.

The orientation s is a function of the supplied phase, not an independently derived clock momentum sign. A practical clock must provide a way to resolve it. The atlas does not obtain branch information from q alone.

## 6. Recorded events crossing a turning point

Choose ordered lifted phases

    theta_1=arccos(3/5),
    theta_2=pi,
    theta_3=2*pi-arccos(3/5).

Their (q,r) pairs are (3/5,4/5), (-1,0), (3/5,-4/5). The cosine turns at the middle event and repeats its earlier numerical reading at the third. The r chart handles the middle event regularly. The oriented q frames distinguish the first and third events.

Start from the physical state |+>. At the first two events perform a system X measurement; at the third perform Y. Write their outcome projectors as P_a, P_b, P_c respectively. The final Y readout detects the orientation-sensitive phase, rather than hiding it behind an even cosine response. Let U_j=U(theta_j). For branch (a,b,c), the chronological reduced product is

    B_S=P_c U_3 U_2^* P_b U_2 U_1^* P_a U_1.

The physical/root event operators are L_(j,a)=U_j^* P_a U_j. Hence

    B_root=L_(3,c) L_(2,b) L_(1,a),
    B_S=U_3 B_root.

All eight branch probabilities agree exactly. With 0 denoting the plus outcome, their lexicographic list is

    (2/125,18/125,72/125,8/125,2/125,18/125,9/250,1/250).

These are the ordered physical/Dirac instruments represented on ker(C) by J L J^*, not an assertion that bare C alone supplies autonomous detectors or their trigger sequence. The code computes them from both expressions, checks normalization and every prefix marginal, and reconstructs the U_j using the appropriate regular charts. This uses the same anchored instruments and supplied lifted event order throughout.

The code also verifies that substituting the first frame at the third event changes recorded probabilities. The repeated value q=3/5 is not enough to replace the event by a unique frame: U(theta_1) and U(theta_3) differ. Nor may one order the events by increasing q. That would change the ordered experiment rather than re-express it in another clock.

## 7. Full-cycle gluing and the remaining global problem

The code also follows nine rational points (q,r) around a cycle, including all four coordinate turning points. The successive transports telescope to identity, independent of the regular chart used. No discontinuity or physical-state ambiguity is produced by switching charts.

But theta itself is only a circle-valued clock. The initial and final phase readings, frames and system state coincide after a full unperturbed cycle. The net construction supplies no winding number. Distinguishing later cycles requires an additional persistent counter/record or another nonrecurrent degree of freedom. Those resources, their dynamics and their back-action cannot be conjured by a coordinate change.

The lifted event ordering in section 6 is supplied operational data. This model does not derive an arrow, autonomous event scheduling or the physical origin of a reliable counter. It demonstrates consistent chartwise time through genuine turning readings while exposing the exact information needed beyond a non-global scalar clock.

## 8. What this resolves, and the next direct time obstruction

We have gone beyond globally valid finite ideal frames: there is now a continuum constrained solution, an explicitly turning clock, a regular clock atlas, Jacobian-correct physical measures, invariant recorded experiments across a turn and a global recurrence limitation.

What remains includes clocks whose velocity depends on the system, genuinely different deparametrizations of interacting constraints, and gravitational constraints with multiple local lapses/refoliations. The present atlas uses a supplied good periodic phase and a bounded noninteracting h; it is not a proof that every quantum clock admits this construction.

This remains direct problem-of-time work within M0/W1-W6. It neither derives a preferred actual time nor settles phenomenal passage or necessary actuality.
