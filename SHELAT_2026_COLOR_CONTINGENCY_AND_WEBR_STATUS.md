# Shelat 2026: physical color contingency and R-model status

**Bottom line.** In the archived SART displays, the three training-related colors are not just labels: actual red, green or blue items occur exclusively on rare-orientation trials in these files. This makes color-based response preparation a concrete alternative worth testing, but it does **not** establish that participants learned or used the contingency, and a common warning signal alone does not explain any *difference* between the rewarded and selected-unrewarded colors. This is a retrospective descriptive audit, not a preregistered test of warning, awareness, or causation.

## What was checked

The independent [aggregate audit](verification/shelat_2026_warning_cue_audit.py) reads the two hash-checked public experiment ZIPs locally. It parses each six-item `actual_shape_colors` and `actual_bar_orientations` array, joins each data row to its subject/block/trial SART structure and checks the rare orientation against the participant's horizontal/vertical counterbalance. Training structures supply the subject-specific rewarded and selected-unrewarded color mapping; the remaining one of red/green/blue is novel. It verifies that recorded triggered-color labels agree with the actual arrays; it rejects malformed arrays, duplicate/missing joins, multiple RGB items, and mismatched orientation or labels. It reports only aggregate counts, not participant data.

All observed SART data rows are counted, **including fixed triggers, omissions, short RTs and otherwise excluded trials**. These denominators therefore differ from the prior accuracy/RT model datasets, which retained fast/slow trials after exclusions. Experiment 1 has 119,400 recorded rows; experiment 2 has 115,800 recorded rows matched to 115,800 of 118,200 scheduled structure rows. The additional 2,400 structure rows do not constitute observed displays in this audit. No attempt was made to impute those rows.

Here *rare* pools fixed, fast and slow triggers; each was checked against the rare orientation after counterbalancing. *RGB* means at least one exact red, green or blue entry in the logged actual array, not an inference that color was consciously seen or that the three colors were equally salient. Among recorded RGB displays, **6,355/6,355** and **6,049/6,049** were rare, respectively. Among displays without those colors, rare trials were **2,111/113,045 (1.87%)** and **2,149/109,751 (1.96%)**. Conversely, only **75.06%** and **73.79%** of rare trials contained an RGB item: RGB is not necessary for rarity. The percentages describe these complete recorded rows, not population estimates; participant clustering, stimulus programming and exclusions matter, and they should not be treated as independent Bernoulli samples or generalized using naive trial-level confidence intervals.

Fast and slow color totals alone are respectively 2,997 + 2,451 in experiment 1 and 2,907 + 2,302 in experiment 2; fixed rare trials contribute the remainder. Full color-by-trigger counts and denominators are in the [saved JSON](verification/shelat_2026_warning_cue_audit.json). No recorded nontrigger array contained any of these three colors; for triggered rows the explicit `triggered_colors` value matched the RGB category, including neutral. Each row had exactly one logged horizontal or vertical bar, matching the counterbalanced frequent/rare assignment. These are checks of recorded arrays and joins, not an independent observation of the screen or attention.

## What this changes—and what it does not

The colored item and target orientation are properties of the same logged display. This is a **concurrent predictive feature**, not evidence of an advance warning before the display. A participant could potentially learn to use RGB presence to anticipate that the required response is the rare one, or its appearance could affect alerting, search, response bias or speed–accuracy policy without such explicit learning. The archive contingency establishes opportunity, not learning, timing, subjective notice, or which mechanism actually operated. No increasing accuracy across blocks is not a direct test of an early or stable learned contingency.

All three RGB categories share this perfect recorded contingency. Thus an RGB-versus-neutral comparison cannot cleanly isolate reward history from presence of a predictive colored item. Conversely, this generic presence/absence explanation **alone** cannot distinguish reward from selected-unrewarded RGB; color-specific learned associations, perceptual salience, training history, and uncertainty remain. Our prior direct fast reward-minus-selection estimates were 0.051 log odds (95% CI −0.091 to 0.193) and 4.69 ms (95% CI −6.73 to 16.10), unadjusted; they do not establish equivalence or disprove reward effects. Those are fitted contrasts with sampling uncertainty; the contingency counts above are descriptive finite-file proportions and do not supply a causal adjustment.

A discriminating follow-up would experimentally vary the contingency of RGB occurrence with rare orientation, ideally counterbalancing physical colors and reward/selection history, while measuring accuracy, RT and response bias together. Separate manipulation or timing of a predictive cue could assess advance warning versus simultaneous search effects; eye tracking and explicitly measured contingency knowledge could help but cannot alone prove a mechanism. None of this dataset measures neural recoupling or momentary phenomenal subject unity. The experiment-2 registration remains inaccessible and its title, DOI and contents have not been verified.

## R/lme4 feasibility: not executed

Native R was absent and the earlier Debian package-index attempt could not reach the mirrors. The present [attempt plan](SHELAT_2026_WEBR_LME4_ATTEMPT_PLAN.md) investigated local webR as a possible alternative. Official [webR 0.6.0](https://github.com/r-wasm/webr/releases/tag/v0.6.0), released 2026-05-19, uses R 4.6.0 and Emscripten 4.0.8; its release notes warn that packages built for earlier webR versions are not binary compatible. A third-party [package repository pinned at commit `ce418e77dc74fe959db89e40afec101214f9017d`](https://github.com/Prolytiq/r-wasm-pkgs/tree/ce418e77dc74fe959db89e40afec101214f9017d/bin/emscripten/contrib/4.6) contains R 4.6 WebAssembly lme4 2.0-6 and several dependencies, but its binary listing lacks required `nloptr`; a source tarball alone does not supply the missing compatible wasm binary or a verified build chain. Other dependencies and the package build's exact Emscripten provenance were not established. The local environment did not expose an Emscripten compiler. A separate inspected GitHub repo had a 4.4 package listing, not an established compatible 4.6 `nloptr` binary. An older official 4.4 binary mentioned elsewhere is not sufficient evidence of compatibility. Third-party binaries were not independently security-audited; pinning identifies a revision but does not verify its safety or reproduce its build.

The npm registry also exposed 0.6.1 with a **2026-11-10** timestamp, later than the trusted current date 2026-10-31; it is not used as evidence of an available current runtime. No R runtime was installed or executed, no candidate binaries were loaded, and neither synthetic nor real-data R models nor the authors' scripts were run. No participant records were uploaded. The [previous independent Python reproduction](SHELAT_2026_REWARD_SELECTION_RT_AND_R_WORKFLOW_AUDIT.md) remains the available model comparison, not an lme4/lmerTest validation. A future comparison needs a compatible, trustworthy complete dependency chain, synthetic-first checks and explicit versions/convergence before any local archive model.

## Verification

The archive-integration test recomputed the saved aggregate JSON from both hash-checked ZIPs. Synthetic tests check correct categorization and rejection of mismatched tags, multiple RGB colors and wrong counterbalance. An initial manual expected-count test inadvertently used the number of all triggers instead of RGB triggers and the number of experiment-2 scheduled structures rather than recorded rows; it failed, was corrected against the independently recomputed table, and then passed. All 13 focused tests (including archive integration) and 45 selected project regression checks passed. The separate project battery and inventories are engineering checks, not evidence of psychological or philosophical claims.
