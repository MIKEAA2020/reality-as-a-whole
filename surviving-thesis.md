# Reality as a whole: the surviving thesis

## Core statement

A completely intelligible account of reality must explain reality as a whole, not merely describe how its parts behave given an unexplained framework. Neither contingent facts nor necessary facts receive an exemption merely by being called fundamental. If the account terminates in something genuinely self-explanatory, that is not a brute stopping point: its nature must actually account for its actuality and explanatory sufficiency.

The strongest experiential extension proposes that this ultimate intelligibility is intrinsic self-disclosure: reality’s ultimate basis is not merely available for an external mind to understand but is present to itself. On that proposal, reality as a whole cannot be wholly devoid of an experiential or intellective aspect. This does not require every physical object to be conscious.

**The first paragraph establishes the explanatory standard and what would satisfy it. The second identifies a substantive candidate for satisfying it, not a deduction already secured by the first.** The decisive remaining question is whether self-explanation requires self-presence—and whether self-presence supplies explanation rather than merely accompanying it.

## 1. Physical existence must make a physical difference

The defensible traceability principle is:

> For a claim of physical existence to have physical content, the alleged entity must make some physical difference, at least in principle. A posit whose inclusion or exclusion changes nothing physically whatsoever adds no physical content.

“Trace” here means a physical difference broadly construed: a contribution to states, relations, dynamics, correlations, or physically grounded counterfactuals. It does not mean a durable mark that a human observer can recover later.

This matters in three ways:

- An unobserved or inaccessible difference is not the absence of a difference.
- Destruction of a readable local record does not establish destruction of all information globally.
- Conversely, information preservation under specified reversible dynamics does not establish that every event must leave an independently recoverable, permanent record of its history.

The claim is therefore not “every existing thing leaves lasting evidence behind.” It concerns the physical content of an existence claim, not universal archival persistence. Nor is empirical indistinguishability alone a proof of metaphysical identity: the narrower conclusion is that a stipulated posit with no physical difference whatsoever contributes nothing to a physical account.

This principle motivates resistance to empty additions. It does **not**, by itself, establish consciousness as a condition of physical existence. Causal registration is not the same thing as conscious registration.

## 2. The relevant subject is the whole, not just its internal workings

A physical model can explain one event through another, derive consequences from laws, and specify how a detector would respond. These are genuine explanations. But they do not automatically explain why that model’s laws, states, modal structure, or concrete actuality obtain.

The central demand is not for a better inventory of internal relations alone. It is for an account that does not leave the explanatory framework itself outside scrutiny.

> Explaining everything within a framework is not yet explaining the framework and everything within it.

This does not assume that every philosophical account of structure must posit non-structural relata or experiential intrinsic properties. Those are disputed proposals. The more durable point is that whichever ontology is adopted—structural, dispositional, categorical, experiential, or otherwise—its claim to completeness must include its own basis rather than exempt it.

## 3. Complete means no unexplained residue

“Complete intelligibility” means that no genuine aspect of reality remains unexplained. It does not mean only that all contingent facts are explained while necessary facts may remain brute. An unexplained necessary fact remains unexplained.

There is a real asymmetry:

- An account that knowingly leaves a residue is incomplete.
- An account that genuinely leaves no residue cannot also be incomplete on account of such a residue.

This fixes the target without weakening it. But it does not certify any proposed account as having reached the target. Naming a theory “maximally explanatory” cannot establish its success.

Three questions must remain distinct:

1. **Standard:** What would complete explanation require? No unexplained residue.
2. **Realization:** Is such explanation possible, and does reality possess it?
3. **Identification:** What, if anything, realizes it?

The definition answers the first. A maximal principle of sufficient reason answers the second affirmatively. The proposal of an experiential ultimate addresses the third.

For the purposes of developing this thesis, maximal intelligibility can be taken as the governing requirement without repeatedly reopening a weaker meaning of “complete.” In assessing an unconditional proof about reality, however, the truth of that requirement remains a premise to defend. A demand for an account does not alone establish that reality satisfies it.

## 4. Necessity is not an exemption, but neither is it automatically brute

“Necessary” and “explained” answer different questions:

- Necessity concerns whether something could have been otherwise.
- Explanation concerns what accounts for its being so.

Merely saying “it could not be otherwise” need not explain why it could not be otherwise. But it would be equally mistaken to rule out explanation through necessity in advance. An account of a thing’s nature might genuinely explain why it exists necessarily.

The appropriate distinction is therefore:

> **Barely asserted necessity** does not close the account; **demonstrated explanatory necessity** might.

Mathematics supports a modest but important point: not every explanation is an appeal to an earlier event or an external efficient cause. Proofs and explanations through definitions, structures, and essences provide other patterns of understanding. Mathematics does not, merely by exhibiting necessary truths, prove metaphysical self-grounding or explain the concrete actuality of the universe.

Nor does mathematical necessity demonstrate the experiential thesis. A proof can make a truth intelligible without the truth itself understanding that proof. To exclude an analogous non-experiential ultimate requires an additional argument.

## 5. Ultimate explanation must be internal to total reality

If reality means everything that exists, nothing existing can stand outside it as an additional explainer. Any real explanatory ground is included in reality as a whole.

It follows that a complete explanation cannot depend on an unexplained external addition. It does **not** immediately follow that the whole is a single entity, a person, or a simple substance. The explanatory basis might be articulated as an entity, a nature, a principle, or a holistic structure; these alternatives require further adjudication.

The strongest formulation avoids making an ultimate ground cause itself into existence or merely assert itself:

> Reality must possess an internal explanatory basis whose nature accounts for its actuality and sufficiency, rather than borrowing sufficiency from something left unexplained.

This is the useful sense of “self-explanatory.” It need not assert a reflexive instance of the technical grounding relation. If grounding is defined as irreflexive, then “a fact grounds that very same fact” is excluded under that definition. That does not by itself settle whether explanation through an ultimate nature is possible.

An endless series or a circular network is insufficient **if it merely transfers the unanswered question**. But neither infinity nor circularity alone proves explanatory failure. The audit must ask whether a proposed structure actually explains the whole, rather than dismissing an entire class of structures by slogan.

A further distinction is essential: our uncertainty about whether a self-explanatory reality exists is not evidence that such a reality would itself be brute. Epistemic justification for a theory and ontological explanation within reality are different matters.

## 6. The strongest experiential extension

The experiential proposal can be reconstructed charitably as follows:

1. Complete intelligibility cannot leave an opaque ultimate fact.
2. Reality’s ultimate explanatory basis cannot obtain its intelligibility from something outside total reality.
3. **Bridge premise:** ultimate intelligibility must therefore be intrinsically disclosed, not merely externally describable or potentially understandable.
4. Intrinsic disclosure in the relevant sense is self-presence: there is something it is like for actuality to be present to itself, or an intrinsically intellective apprehension of itself.
5. Therefore reality’s ultimate basis has an experiential or intellective aspect.

The strongest intuition behind the bridge is this: experience is not known only through an external description of causal relations. There is also the occurrence’s being present in its happening. This offers a candidate for actuality that is not wholly opaque from within. The experiential proposal seeks to unite being and intelligibility rather than leave them separated into an indifferent world and a contingent interpreter.

That is a substantive metaphysical motivation. It is stronger than saying “nothing matters without observers,” and it does not require human witnesses or an external spectator.

Nevertheless, two steps remain unestablished:

### A. Self-explanation → self-presence

An explanatory basis being internal to reality is not yet reality understanding that basis. “Contains its own explanation” can mean that its nature accounts for it, without meaning that it consciously apprehends itself.

The strongest remaining opponent need not defend **brute** necessity. The opponent may propose a genuinely explanatory, non-experiential necessary nature. The experiential thesis must show why that proposal necessarily leaves something unexplained—not merely observe that it lacks awareness.

### B. Self-presence → complete explanation

Experience makes occurrence manifest, but ordinary awareness does not explain its own existence, nature, or every feature of its content. Knowing that something occurs is not the same as explaining why it occurs.

Consequently, “self-luminous” cannot do all the explanatory work by itself. The proposed ultimate must explain why its self-presence is also adequate self-understanding, and how its nature accounts for the actual world in detail. Otherwise the proposal exchanges an unexplained physical basis for an unexplained experiential basis.

In plain words:

> Nothing getting a free pass establishes that the ultimate cannot simply be left unexplained. It does not yet establish that explaining itself means being aware of itself—or that awareness alone finishes the explanation.

## 7. What the conclusion would and would not establish

If the bridge is successfully defended, the conclusion concerns **total reality, including its ultimate basis**. It does not imply that:

- every rock, electron, or physical subsystem is conscious;
- physical traces require observation by finite minds;
- a derivative physical domain cannot lack local experiencers;
- reality is a human-like person;
- the ultimate must lack all internal differentiation;
- a finite human account can exhaust reality’s own intelligibility.

A domain without experiencers is compatible with an experiential ground. A reality wholly without any experiential aspect is the stronger possibility this argument challenges.

Likewise, “absurd” must be used precisely. A brute ultimate fails the stipulated demand for complete intelligibility. That is not automatically a contradiction in formal logic. Calling it **globally explanatorily deficient** preserves the substantive objection without equating failure of explanation with logical inconsistency.

## 8. Coherence, explanatory achievement, and theory choice

No decisive impossibility proof is established in the exchange. But lack of refutation is not a constructive demonstration that the proposed ultimate succeeds.

Several objections require careful handling:

- **Irreflexive grounding:** a challenge to literal self-grounding, not an automatic refutation of every form of internal or essential explanation.
- **Subject–object duality:** self-presence need not be a relation between two independent substances. Conversely, describing it as non-dual does not yet establish explanatory adequacy. Absoluteness alone also does not establish the absence of all internal distinctions.
- **Modal collapse:** complete explanation entails necessitarianism only with additional assumptions—for example, that sufficient explanations necessitate every outcome and that the ultimate basis is necessary. The result is not obtained from completeness alone.
- **External verification:** total reality cannot have an external verifier. That absence does not refute its intelligibility, although our claims about it still require justification.
- **Human limitations:** inability of finite minds to comprehend a complete account does not prove that reality lacks one. Nor does it establish that reality has one.

Parsimony cannot rescue an account from explanatory failure merely because it posits less. If one account genuinely explains what another leaves brute, the accounts differ in explanatory achievement. However, a promise of total explanation is not yet greater demonstrated explanatory power. Comparative assessment must consider achieved explanation, coherence, adequacy, and theoretical cost—not just the number of posits or the ambition of the label.

## Final consolidated thesis

> Physical existence claims have physical content only insofar as their posits make some physical difference in principle. But physical description, however successful within its domain, is not automatically a complete explanation of reality as a whole. Complete explanation exempts neither contingent nor necessary fundamentals: the ultimate basis must actually account for its actuality and explanatory sufficiency, not merely be declared fundamental or necessary. Any such explanation must be internal to total reality. Intrinsic experiential or intellective self-presence is a serious candidate for how ultimate intelligibility might be realized, without requiring consciousness in every physical part. The further claim that only such self-presence can realize complete intelligibility remains the central thesis to establish—not something already proved by traceability, necessity, or the word “complete.”

**Audit verdict:** The demand for whole-inclusive, residue-free explanation survives strongly. The distinction between genuinely explanatory necessity and brute necessity survives. The distinction between an ontologically self-explanatory basis and our justification for positing it survives. The experiential conclusion survives as a focused, motivated metaphysical thesis, but not as an established entailment. A successful completion must explain both why non-experiential self-explanation is insufficient and why experiential self-presence is sufficient.
