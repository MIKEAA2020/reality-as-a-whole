# Relational plenitude: actual possibilities, histories and measure

## Aim and result

The working candidate is one self-sufficient, intrinsically experiential reality, articulated relationally into genuinely plural subjects and perspectives. The user adds the strong thesis: **whatever is genuinely possible is actual within the whole; what is not actual was not genuinely possible.** There is no earlier state of mere possibility and no process, simulator or creator that brings possibilities into being.

We can make one substantive distinction precise:

> Realizing every *finite local pattern* does not by itself realize every infinite history, fix their measure, or establish that those patterns constitute subjects or experiences.

An explicit finite comparison below realizes every outcome in two stipulated quantum-record sectors, while their relative frequencies differ. A separate infinite-history argument then shows a sharper conditional tension: if an exactly fair binary law holds for **every finite prefix**, and its countably additive measure is concentrated on actual, complete, single-valued histories, the set of actual histories cannot be countable. All of these are conditional mathematical results, not evidence that the real whole has infinitely many histories or that the premises of strong plenitude have been proved.

The basic probability mathematics is established: the [MIT probability notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-436j-fundamentals-of-probability-fall-2018/03cb805c752a7e89e52878e8e6270e34_MIT6_436JF18_lec02.pdf) derive the infinite fair-sequence measure from finite cylinders; [Sterkenburg and Grünwald](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/philosophy-of-science/article/pride-and-probability/CB15DB2546E744E9DCDDB2F4D14633B4) discuss the countable dense set of eventually-zero sequences. The contribution here is their application to the project's **strong actuality, contextual records and subject-measure** requirements, not a new theorem about infinite sequences.

## 1. What “all possibilities are actual” must distinguish

Let `R(d)` mean that description `d` is realized in its declared local scope within the one whole, and `P(d)` mean genuine possibility in the user's strongest sense. The adopted thesis is

    P(d) ↔ R(d).

This is a working metaphysical thesis, not derived merely by naming `P` and `R`. It does not say every string, every logically consistent description, every positive-probability quantum outcome, or every infinite mathematical sequence is genuinely possible. A successful account needs a **non-circular criterion** for what qualifies as a genuine possibility and for which local scope it is realized.

Scope matters. `result=0 in occurrence α` and `result=1 in a different occurrence β` can both be actual. In contrast, claiming both values for the **same occurrence, in the same respect** is inconsistent under the ordinary binary semantics. Indexing events by genuine circumstances is not a cosmic lottery, creation of a world, or an assurance that every imagined combination is compatible. If a description rigidly specifies a particular occurrence, any incompatible contrary description of that same occurrence is not made actual merely by finding a different occurrence elsewhere.

For a global proposition `q`, the separate global modal formalization `◇q → q` has consequences discussed in [the truthmaker relevance gate](PLENITUDE_TRUTHMAKER_RELEVANCE_GATE.md). One must not substitute an existential local realization reading for that global schema without checking the intended meaning. Neither reading alone supplies a criterion for experiential constitution.

## 2. A finite relational record structure

The existing [one-friend quantum circuit](SUBJECT_IDENTITY_LOCAL_FRIENDLINESS_TEST.md) has four measurement settings `(x,y)` and two possible output signs `a,b`. Its ideal correlations at full visibility are

    E₁₁=E₁₂=3/5,   E₂₁=4/5,   E₂₂=−4/5.

Use two **specified physical preparation conditions** `v=1` and `v=1/2`. The latter is an idealized Bell state mixed with white noise. In each setting and condition, the model uses

    P_v(a,b | x,y) = (1 + ab v E_xy)/4.

Every one of the four outcome pairs has strictly positive weight in every setting under both conditions. In a **stipulated finite mathematical whole**, place 40 record-occurrence slots in each `(v,x,y)` sector and assign them exactly the corresponding rational counts. There are 160 records for each preparation condition, 320 in total; the whole is one fixed relational structure, not an experiment that selects or creates a world. For a given condition, the occurrence-count ratios are:

| Condition | Counts per context | CHSH expression |
|---|---|---:|
| `v=1` | For `E=3/5`: 16, 4, 4, 16; for `E=4/5`: 18, 2, 2, 18; for `E=−4/5`: 2, 18, 18, 2 | `14/5` |
| `v=1/2` | For `E=3/10`: 13, 7, 7, 13; for `E=2/5`: 14, 6, 6, 14; for `E=−2/5`: 6, 14, 14, 6 | `7/5` |

Within each row, counts are ordered `(++,+−,−+,−−)`. All sign pairs appear in every corresponding context in both sectors. Yet their frequencies and correlations differ. Here *all finite alternatives in the stated support are present*, but that support alone does not determine the measure or correlation law. The visibility is part of the declared preparation condition: the two sectors are not the same quantum state yielding two different Born rules.

The table uses record relations and occurrence slots; naming or permuting a record does not turn its label into an independently fundamental object. Uniformly counting those finite slots produces the displayed ratios, but **the appropriateness of that counting measure is stipulated**, not derived from strong actuality. Nor does the construction prove the slots are physically realized, that the whole is self-existent, or that any record constitutes an experience. It contains no literal simulation or simulated people. The two conditions can be regarded as substructures of the same mathematical whole; no claim is made that two alternative total actualities are genuinely possible.

These contexts are different runs. The model does not assign all incompatible outcomes to the **same** run, or infer that an unperformed measurement has a pre-existing outcome. Matching the four quantum tables does not alone supply a local causal model, a physical preparation protocol, or a solution of the [Local Friendliness constraints](ACTUALITY_CAUSAL_INFERENCE_AND_BELL.md). What counts as a genuinely realizable quantum history remains an independent physical and interpretive question.

## 3. Finite plenitude does not settle infinite plenitude

Let `D` be the set of infinite binary sequences that are eventually all zero. This is a countable set: each sequence can be specified by a finite prefix followed by zeros. Stipulate one actual record-history for each member of `D`.

Every finite binary word `w` occurs as a prefix of some actual member of `D`: use `w000…`. Nevertheless, many infinite sequences, such as `101010…`, are not in `D`. Thus:

- Every finite local prefix can be represented as actual in the construction.
- It does **not** follow that every infinite sequence is actual.
- If a proposed criterion separately declares an omitted infinite sequence genuinely possible, the `D` construction **fails the user's strong thesis** and must be enlarged. We cannot call the omitted sequence impossible just because it is absent without justifying the criterion.

Conversely, finite-prefix admissibility does not itself prove that an unbounded complete infinite history is physically or metaphysically genuine. A local experiential subject is not assumed to live forever. The binary sequences are **record histories**, not established subjects, brains, worlds, or copies of a person.

The issue is not solved by calling the global set timeless: its internal relations may be represented without an external clock, but whether they give rise to local temporal order remains open.

## 4. The countable-additivity constraint

Suppose, **only for this conditional argument**, that:

1. Each relevant actual record center has one definite complete binary history, and the set `H` of realized histories is countable.
2. There is a normalized, **countably additive** measure over those actual histories; a center is mapped to its one history. Its pushforward measure is therefore supported on `H`.
3. For every `n` and every binary word `w` of length `n`, the first-`n` record distribution is exactly the fair law `μ([w])=2^(−n)`.

For any particular complete sequence `h`, its singleton is contained in the set of histories with its first `n` bits, hence

    μ({h}) ≤ 2^(−n) for every n, so μ({h})=0.

If `H` is countable, countable additivity gives `μ(H)=0`, contradicting assumption 2 that the measure is normalized on `H`. Therefore, under these assumptions, **the realized set of histories is uncountable**. This is a direct application of standard measure theory, not a claim that physical reality contains uncountably many subjects.

The assumptions matter. The argument does not hold without further work if records have only finite lengths, infinite exact fair statistics are not physically admissible, one center does not have a single complete classical history, the probability is not a countably additive measure over actual histories, or the declared subject/record measure is not normalized. Each is a substantive alternative; this result does not pick one. It also does not equate *probability zero* with impossible: under the ideal fair law each individual infinite sequence has measure zero, though finite prefixes all have positive measure. Whether any such complete sequence is genuinely possible in the user's strongest sense is a separate metaphysical and physical question.

If a further independent principle says **every** infinite binary history is genuinely possible and each must be actually realized as one distinct definite history, strong actuality itself requires at least continuum-many actual histories. That conclusion follows from the added principle and Cantor's cardinality result, not from `P↔R` alone. A history is not automatically a separate experiencer; the number of genuinely distinct subjects remains a constitution question.

## 5. Finite tests and measure underdetermination

The countability result does not allow us to infer uncountably many histories from a finite data sample. For any fixed `N≥1`, define a probability distribution on the **same countable `D`** as follows:

- Pick a formal prefix length `L=N+K`, with `P(K=k)=2^(−k−1)` for `k=0,1,…`.
- Give the first `L` bits independent fair weights; all later bits are zero.

This is a mathematical construction of a measure, **not a physical random creation process**. Every member of `D` has positive weight, and every finite word has positive prefix weight. The first `N` bits have exactly the fair distribution. Yet at `N+1` the all-ones prefix has weight `2^(−N−2)`, half the fair value `2^(−N−1)`. Thus every fixed finite battery can be matched while this particular infinite extension differs. This does not show that real experiments cannot test departures at longer accessible lengths; it shows that finite agreement alone is not proof of an exact infinite law.

Even on the same fixed `D`, one can choose positive measures with different weights. For example, let the first bit have probability `1/4` under one measure and `3/4` under another, while ensuring every finite prefix and every eventually-zero history receives positive weight. Both measures have the same stipulated actual histories and full finite support. Thus `P↔R` does not by itself select a subject-location measure or Born weights. A measure law may ultimately follow from the whole's constitution, but that bridge has not been derived.

## 6. What this advances—and what remains open

The working picture stays **one experiential whole, plural finite subjects, no universal agent assumed**, and all genuinely possible descriptions actual at their proper scope. This work adds a decision point for the plenitude thesis:

1. Specify independently the domain of genuine local possibilities, including which complete or infinite histories are admissible. Do not redefine “possible” as “already listed” and call that an explanation.
2. State whether actuality is a global modal identity, local realization within one whole, or both, and handle incompatible outcomes at the same occurrence.
3. Derive or justify a measure on actual subject-locations or histories; mere inclusion and support do not produce Born weights.
4. Derive subject incidence and qualitative experience from the relational constitution rather than calling each record or history a subject. A finite graph representation has formal elements; it does not by itself prove that purely relational ontology is fundamental or that an experience exists.
5. Recover physically justified correlations and local time from the proposed timeless experiential whole, and address non-optimality without assuming that suffering or wasted time is justified.

Self-subsistence, if established, already means the whole does not require an external cause; the open question is whether and why that self-subsistent experiential constitution is true, not what caused an already self-sufficient whole. None of the above proves necessity, possible=actual, the qualitative bridge or a complete framework.
