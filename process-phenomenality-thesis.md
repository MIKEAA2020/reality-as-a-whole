# Process, phenomenality, and self-location

## The strongest surviving thesis

> Treat phenomenal experience as real and investigate whether a conscious episode and a concrete process are one occurrence under different modes of presentation. Do not require phenomenal concepts to mean the same thing as physical concepts. Instead, specify the process independently, characterize phenomenal relations without defining them as behavioral performance, and test whether the process uniquely determines the relevant phenomenal structure. Keep physical probability, locally available evidence, and selection among observer-centers distinct. Where determination or selection is not justified, acknowledge underdetermination rather than invent a bridge law or observer lottery.

This is a disciplined identity research programme, not an accomplished derivation of consciousness. Its central gain is to replace a verbal identity claim with explicit tests of determination, correspondence, and explanatory sufficiency. Its central limitation is that **even a unique correspondence does not, without a further identity principle, establish numerical identity or complete explanation**.

## 1. One occurrence need not have one vocabulary

An experience may be picked out through first-person acquaintance, while a physical process is picked out through dynamics, causal history, and intervention. Different meanings and methods of identification do not establish two entities.

Accordingly, conceptual irreducibility does not by itself refute token identity. An identity discovered empirically need not be an analytic truth or a definition. If two rigid designators genuinely name the same entity, the identity is necessary, subject to the usual qualifications about existence across possibilities.

But this modal point is conditional:

> **If identity is established, necessity of identity may follow. Necessity of identity does not establish the initial identification.**

Stable dependence, interventions, and structural matching can support identity abductively. They do not automatically exclude necessary dependence between distinct properties, or a dual-aspect interpretation of one event.

There are also two different modal claims:

1. This very event is identical to itself under two names.
2. Every admissible realization of the specified physical nature has the same phenomenal profile.

The second concerns essential properties and psychophysical determination. It is not obtained merely from the first. Numerical token identity alone does not show that all phenomenal properties are fixed by an independently specified physical description.

## 2. “Complete process” must not conceal the conclusion

Let \(P\) specify a concrete process and \(E\) its phenomenal organization. The programme seeks a justified claim that fixing the relevant process nature fixes the phenomenal profile:

\[
\Box\bigl(P\text{ has the specified nature}\Rightarrow E\text{ has the specified profile}\bigr).
\]

The content of “specified nature” is crucial.

- If it already includes the phenomenal profile, determination is secured by inclusion and explains nothing about how a physical account fixes experience.
- If it includes only a report or input–output profile, it may omit physical details relevant to experience.
- If it is a sufficiently rich, independently specified causal-dynamical account, determination becomes a substantive hypothesis.

The strongest version therefore fixes a process vocabulary in advance and makes any later enrichment explicit. A failure of one model may show that its process description is incomplete; it does not immediately prove that phenomenality must be fundamental.

“No independent phenomenal freedom” is a useful constraint on a strong identity proposal. It is not sufficient for identity: necessarily linked but distinct properties can also lack independent variation. Nor does merely asserting necessity discharge the demand for intelligibility. The account must explain why the proposed determination holds.

## 3. The formal target: phenomenal expansions

Use two vocabularies:

- \(L_P\): process states, dynamics, interventions, and relevant physical relations;
- \(L_E\): phenomenal similarity, valence, co-presence, ownership, temporal organization, and other independently motivated experiential relations.

Let \(T\) be a proposed theory connecting them. For a fixed admissible physical model \(\mathcal P\), consider its phenomenal expansions satisfying \(T\).

Three outcomes matter:

1. **No expansion:** the physical description, phenomenal constraints, or connecting theory is inadequate or inconsistent.
2. **Multiple inequivalent expansions:** the stated theory does not determine the phenomenal structure. Additional evidence, physical detail, or justified principles are needed.
3. **One expansion, under the appropriate equivalence criterion:** the theory achieves a precise determination result for that domain.

This makes the research question sharper than “can a brain state be associated with an experience?” It asks which alternatives are excluded and why.

However, uniqueness must not be manufactured by stipulating exactly one bridge mapping. The connecting constraints require independent justification. A theorem establishes what follows from those constraints; it does not establish their truth.

### Relabeling versus a substantive alternative

Changing symbols without changing their interpretation is harmless. Changing phenomenal character is not automatically harmless merely because a relational description fails to distinguish the change.

The programme must therefore justify which transformations preserve all facts at issue. It cannot dismiss a proposed qualitative inversion as “just relabeling” by assuming that relational structure exhausts phenomenal character—the very claim under investigation.

Interventions can help anchor distinct dimensions, such as unpleasantness and sensory intensity. They establish identity only if the evidential bridge to the independently characterized phenomenal dimensions is sound. If some qualitative alternative survives every justified constraint, the result is underdetermination relative to the model, not permission to erase that alternative by terminology.

### What implicit-definition theorems can establish

In classical first-order settings, Beth-style definability connects implicit definability with explicit definability under specific conditions. The relevant implicit uniqueness concerns interpretations of predicates over the same fixed reduct, not merely abstract isomorphism between possible expansions.

Thus:

> “Unique up to isomorphism” is not automatically the hypothesis needed for an explicit-definability theorem.

A formal application must specify its logic, domains, reducts, predicates, and uniqueness notion. Even a successful definability result concerns the theory’s formal predicates. It does not independently show that those predicates capture actual phenomenality, or that two correlated entities are numerically one.

## 4. Derive access states without defining experience away

For a specified interface and intervention or policy class, histories may be grouped according to their predictions for future observations under the relevant actions. This can yield a canonical predictive-state representation under suitable assumptions.

Its scope must remain explicit:

- The equivalence depends on the interface, available interventions, and predictive horizon or criterion.
- Different interfaces may yield different state partitions.
- Such a construction need not identify a unique physical implementation, subsystem boundary, or subject.
- Predictive sufficiency does not establish phenomenal sufficiency.

The surviving distinction is essential:

> A minimal state for observation and control is a candidate access-state description, not automatically a phenomenal subject.

If experience is defined as whatever survives every report, action, and memory test, the resulting uniqueness is operational. It has not independently established that feeling is exhausted by those tests.

## 5. Phenomenology is evidence, not a concealed physical definition

The phenomenal side should include first-person evidence about:

- similarity and difference;
- pleasantness and unpleasantness;
- co-presence and unity;
- temporal order and continuity;
- bodily ownership and apparent location.

These constraints need not be infallible to be evidentially relevant. First-person acquaintance does not make every judgment about experience correct, and reports can be incomplete, distorted, or unavailable.

“Independent” here means **not defined into the physical model**. It does not mean causally independent of the brain or publicly accessible without mediation. Reports remain physical events, while what they purport to describe is not reduced to reporting behavior by definition.

A disciplined investigation combines first-person judgments with interventions and independent process measurements, retaining uncertainty about introspection, measurement, and interpretation. For non-reporting systems, extrapolation must be marked as extrapolation. Failure to obtain a report is neither proof of experience nor proof of its absence.

## 6. Correspondence is evidence for identity, not a theorem of identity

A complete, intervention-stable, one-to-one correspondence would be a substantial achievement. It would strengthen the case against contingent pairing and constrain rival accounts.

Nevertheless, numerical identity does not follow from a unique map alone. Distinct structures can be uniquely related under a theory. A structural identity criterion can authorize identification, but then that criterion is an additional premise requiring defense.

The strongest identity inference is therefore comparative:

> If one concrete occurrence accounts for the physical and phenomenal evidence, explains their necessary connection, and avoids the unexplained duplication or coupling required by rivals, identity gains explanatory support.

“No separate causal power” alone is insufficient: a necessary intrinsic aspect might add no independent force. Conversely, conceptual difference alone is insufficient to establish an ontological addition.

The investigation must distinguish three achievements:

1. **Determination:** the specified process fixes the phenomenal profile.
2. **Identity:** the phenomenal episode is that process, rather than something necessarily dependent on it.
3. **Explanation:** the account makes the determination or identity intelligible rather than merely asserting it.

A successful result at one level must not silently count as success at all three.

## 7. Probability and self-location need separate measures

Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu)\) represent a physical history model. A specified perspective \(S\) has accessible information \(\mathcal F_S\), allowing conditional probabilities such as

\[
\mathbb E_\mu[\mathbf 1_A\mid\mathcal F_S].
\]

This conditions a given measure on information. It does not derive \(\mu\), establish which systems are conscious, or justify every identification of physical chance with rational credence.

If a question genuinely concerns a center, use centered possibilities \((\omega,c)\). A normalized kernel \(\lambda_\omega\) over the centers available in history \(\omega\), when justified, gives a centered measure of the form

\[
\nu(d\omega,dc)=\mu(d\omega)\lambda_\omega(dc).
\]

Where counting and normalization are problematic, additional mathematical care is required. More importantly, the kernel needs substantive justification: a sampling procedure, a defensible symmetry principle, or an explicitly acknowledged prior. Symmetry alone need not select a unique normalized distribution, especially over infinite collections.

The surviving position is neither “all observers must be sampled uniformly” nor “self-location never needs a measure.” It is:

> Do not add a universal observer lottery merely to explain local perspective. Do specify and justify selection assumptions when the question actually concerns selection among centers.

This is the disciplined use of the simulation argument’s centered-probability structure. The excerpt does not supply enough assumptions to derive Bostrom-style simulation odds or a unique observer weighting. Its filename is not evidence for missing premises.

## 8. An empirical programme with explicit limits

A productive sequence is:

1. Specify a bounded domain, process model, phenomenal vocabulary, and proposed connecting constraints.
2. Calibrate human cases using interventions and first-person judgments.
3. Investigate dissociations: intensity versus unpleasantness, ownership versus sensation, appearance versus naming.
4. Test altered conditions and reduced-report situations without treating report absence as a direct phenomenal measurement.
5. Assess generalization across subjects and implementations, stating which invariants are supposed to matter.
6. Extrapolate cautiously to non-reporting systems and retain uncertainty.

Success should require more than fitting reports used to build the model. The model should face independent tests and specify what would count against it.

Generalization without arbitrary adjustments is a reasonable aim. A blanket ban on thresholds is not: a threshold can be legitimate if derived or empirically justified. Likewise, a full one-to-one mapping between every physical detail and every phenomenal distinction is stronger than needed; the relevant explanatory process variables and equivalence classes must be identified rather than assumed.

## 9. The fallback preserves the datum without declaring victory

If the process model leaves a phenomenal relation unmatched, possible responses include improving the model, revising a mistaken phenomenal judgment, reconsidering bridge constraints, or provisionally treating the relation as fundamental.

Phenomenal fundamentality does not mean phenomenal structure must vary independently of physical structure. A phenomenal-first theory could posit necessary determination too. Nor does fundamentality itself explain the relation: an unexplained necessary coupling remains an explanatory obligation.

The fallback is therefore methodological honesty—retain what needs explaining—rather than an automatic proof of a phenomenal-first ontology.

## 10. Place within the whole-reality project

The supplied excerpt starts mid-conversation and does not present a global cosmology or a derivation of the physical history measure. The surviving local identity proposal must not be mistaken for a complete account of reality as a whole.

For the larger zero-residue project, further obligations remain: why the physical framework and actuality obtain, why the relevant measure is justified, what individuates subjects, and why the psychophysical relation holds. A structural principle of sufficient reason can motivate resistance to arbitrary additions, but cannot establish a specific mapping simply by prohibiting unexplained alternatives.

This excerpt strengthens the earlier identity programme by supplying formal tests. It does not establish the earlier experiential-ground thesis or the alternative self-explanatory neutral-monist whole. Those remain separate global proposals.

## Final formulation

> The identity route should preserve phenomenal reality while testing whether an independently specified concrete process determines independently characterized experiential organization. Conceptual difference need not imply two events, but neither necessary dependence nor unique correspondence alone proves one event. Predictive-state uniqueness concerns access unless a substantive phenomenal identification is independently justified. Physical histories, locally available evidence, and observer-center selection require distinct probabilistic treatment. Where the model leaves alternatives open, the correct result is explicit underdetermination—not denial of experience or an invented selection rule. The aim is a justified identity and its explanation, embedded eventually in a non-brute account of the whole.

**Audit verdict:** The surviving advance is a precise, non-circular research target for process–phenomenal determination, coupled to a careful separation of physical and centered probability. No identity theorem, unique phenomenal mapping, or complete global explanation has yet been established.
