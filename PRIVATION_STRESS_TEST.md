# A targeted stress test of the privation conjecture

## Result in brief

The strongest current version is **aspect-relative privation of due fulfillment**, not the claim that bad events contain no actual positive processes, nor that every bad condition involves broken machinery.

This version handles the user's cruelty example more accurately and shows why protective pain is not automatically a counterexample. But it needs an independently justified account of due fulfillment. Without that, any theory can be relabeled as privation, and the conjecture loses discriminatory content.

This is a conceptual comparison, not an empirical test, a complete theory of pain, or a proof of axiarchism.

## 1. The candidate stated without switching meanings

**Constitutional clarification:** [The decombination/privation audit](DECOMBINATION_PRIVATION_CONSOLIDATION.md) distinguishes the dependent description of a lack from the positive constitution of its bearer. Privation does not supply a subject boundary or prove that actual deficiency is necessary for absolute self-knowledge. A locally missing due fulfillment neither automatically makes the whole deficient nor becomes fulfilled merely because the whole comprehends it.

Distinguish two predicates:

- **Positive actuality:** a being or occurrence has actual constitution and powers.
- **Fulfillment:** a particular being realizes a particular good in particular circumstances.

The proposal is:

> Every actual occurrence has positive actuality. Its badness, where present, consists in a shortfall of due fulfillment in some specified respect, not in an independent opposing substance.

If positive actuality is additionally called intrinsic Good, that is the goodness-of-being thesis. It must not silently become the claim that every action is morally justified or every experience pleasant.

A general schema is:

    bearer: b
    respect or capacity: r
    circumstances: c
    realized condition: F(b,r,c)
    justified due standard: T(b,r,c)

A privation exists when the realized condition falls short of the due standard in the relevant ordering. No common numerical scale or summation across subjects is assumed. One must specify the bearer, respect, circumstances, and ordering before concluding that a shortfall is bad in the intended sense.

The schema locates explanatory work; it does not supply the standard T or prove that all badness has this form.

## 2. Test one: ordinary limitation

### Case

A stone has no vision. A human is not maximally knowledgeable. Someone attends to one legitimate task rather than every other task simultaneously.

### Result

Absence alone is insufficient. The privation account must not classify every unrealized capacity, missing perfection, or forgone possibility as badness. The standard must be appropriate to the bearer and circumstances, including compossibility and justified demands.

This is not a minor exception: it shows why “everything is missing some possible good” cannot ground the theory. Finitude and evaluative deficiency are not interchangeable.

### Remaining burden

The standard of appropriateness cannot simply mean “whatever avoids verdicts we dislike.” A biological function can explain some standards without proving their final moral authority. Other standards may require relational or normative grounding.

## 3. Test two: acquisitive action without due regard

### Case

Someone pursues money or power, understands the effects on others, and disregards their legitimate needs or claims.

### Positive actuality

The agent has actual intelligence, effort, agency, and effective means. The pursuit of the end is not automatically the wrong-making feature.

### Proposed privation

The practical organization of the action lacks due integration of affected beings' standing. This can occur despite intact cognitive empathy, or even despite an empathic feeling that the agent overrides.

### Result

This is the most promising current application. It identifies a missing relation in the exercise of actual powers rather than pretending that the actual action is an absence. It also avoids a universal psychological claim that wrongdoers cannot understand others.

### Remaining burden

The theory must establish the legitimacy and priority of the affected claims. Not every frustrated desire, lost advantage, or unequal outcome establishes wrongness. The factual description that an agent omits another's interest is not enough unless consideration is due.

A bad outcome, a wrong act, culpability, and lack of empathic capacity must remain distinct. The schema alone does not settle responsibility or free will.

## 4. Test three: protective pain through functioning systems

### Case

A protective pain response occurs through systems performing their relevant warning function. The experience is aversive, but its warning role can contribute to avoiding a greater injury.

### The simplistic claim fails

If privation meant “every unpleasant event is a malfunction,” functioning protective pain would challenge it. That version should not be used.

### The refined claim survives this case conditionally

There can be a local shortfall of ease or undisturbed bodily fulfillment alongside successful warning and protection. Fulfillment in one respect does not entail fulfillment in every respect.

It is therefore consistent for an event to be:

- an actual positive process;
- functionally beneficial in a protective respect;
- aversive for the subject;
- not an all-things-considered wrong.

This is a compatibility result, not a completed reduction of aversiveness to deprivation. The positive condition whose privation explains the aversive evaluation must still be specified independently.

### What cannot be inferred

The usefulness of a warning mechanism does not establish that every occurrence, duration, or intensity of suffering is necessary. Nor does the fact that an event has a cost imply that allowing it is morally wrong. The relevant aspects and tradeoffs must be stated.

This refines the preceding audit: protective pain is not by itself a refutation of a sufficiently aspect-relative privation theory. Its main force is against a simplistic equation of badness with malfunction, and against an unargued reduction of actual felt character to an absence.

## 5. Test four: neutral absence versus aversive presence

### Conceptual comparison

A neutral experience and an intensely aversive experience can both lack a particular pleasure. They are nevertheless qualitatively different.

### Result

A bare absence of pleasure cannot by itself distinguish neutrality from suffering. A privation theory needs a richer standard or organization than “not pleasure.” The observation does not prove that no richer account works.

If the richer standard is simply “the absence of whatever we independently call suffering,” the theory has become a circular relabeling. If it specifies a positive mode of qualitative integration, stability, or fulfillment independently and shows how a shortfall constitutes suffering's evaluative feature, it offers a substantive identity hypothesis. No such full identity has yet been demonstrated here.

## 6. The anti-circularity requirement

For an arbitrary badness predicate B, one can define G as not-B and then write B as absence of G. This logical transformation alone explains nothing. It works for any classification and therefore cannot establish a distinctive ontology of value.

A nontrivial privation account must instead:

1. identify a positive fulfillment independently of the badness verdict it is meant to explain;
2. justify why it is due for this bearer in these circumstances;
3. show how the relevant absence or disordered integration yields the evaluative deficiency;
4. distinguish deficiency from mere difference, legitimate limitation, or a justified tradeoff;
5. retain the actuality of the event and its qualitative character.

“Independently” here means not merely defining the good as the negation of the verdict. It need not mean that every explanatory concept can be understood without any relation to its contrast, or that only a reductive physical definition is acceptable.

## 7. What the test supports

### Supported as a coherent architecture

Positive powers can be exercised in arrangements that lack due integration. The same event can succeed in one respect and be deficient in another. This resolves the apparent clash between positive actual activity and privative wrongness without denying harm.

### Better formulated than before

The user's money-and-power example identifies the right target: due practical integration of others' standing, not the mere presence of ambition and not a universal lack of empathic ability.

### Not established

The test does not demonstrate that every negative evaluation is privative, that the standards of due fulfillment are stance-independent, or that Good actualizes reality. It also does not establish an intrinsic negative substance or prove that universal privation is false.

## 8. Consequence for the leading whole-account

The strongest present extension is:

> One concrete actuality has positive powers and differentiated qualitative perspectives. Their fulfillment is aspect-relative and relational. Wrongness may consist in missing due integration of the claims grounded in those perspectives, rather than a separately existing negative principle.

Its core normative conjecture is that qualitative beings' fulfillment supplies reasons beyond arbitrary adopted goals. Its additional universal-privation conjecture is that every bad-making feature can be understood through the absence or disorder of justified positive fulfillment. These claims remain separate so their support can be assessed honestly.

The axiarchic claim is further still: these value relations must help explain the ultimate constitution or its concrete actuality. The stress test improves the privative account's formulation, but does not make that extra step automatic.

## 9. Next decisive target

The central target is a noncircular constitutive account of **due fulfillment**, not another declaration that all positive actuality is Good. It must explain why affected perspectives have standing, why their claims have the priorities they do, and why limitation is sometimes legitimate while deprivation is sometimes wrong.

A successful account could ground value and privative badness in one nature. Until then, the present advance is a specific, coherent, stress-tested candidate—not a proof of the ultimate axiarchic conclusion.
