# Bell experiments and quantum information: the Nobel research

## Scope and conclusion

The Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Alain Aspect, John Clauser and Anton Zeilinger “for experiments with entangled photons, establishing the violation of Bell inequalities and pioneering quantum information science.” The [Nobel Committee's scientific background](https://www.nobelprize.org/uploads/2026/05/advanced-physicsprize-2022-5.pdf) identifies a sequence, not one isolated discovery: experimentally testable Bell inequalities; optical tests and improved handling of measurement settings; and entanglement as an experimentally usable resource.

I read the extracted main text of the representative original papers linked below, plus all seven text chunks of the Nobel scientific background. This is a selective reading, not a review of every publication by the laureates or every Nobel reference. PDF/HTML text extraction can corrupt equations and figure data; I did not independently inspect the original figure images, calibrations, raw event records or reproduce statistical analyses. Specific supplementary and partial-reading limitations are listed at the end.

## 1. Clauser and collaborators: from theorem to experiment

**Theory.** Clauser, Horne, Shimony and Holt, [*Proposed Experiment to Test Local Hidden-Variable Theories*](https://www.johnclauser.com/_files/ugd/36ef59_05bf40eb0ab4457cac6bf6b0dc8d7077.pdf), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.23.880), generalized Bell's argument to practical photon experiments without requiring perfect correlations. Their original paper explicitly assumes that source-variable distributions do not depend on settings, as well as local response functions. Because the detectors available were inefficient, their proposed implementation also used a detection-related supplementary assumption. The authors recognized that assumption could be contested: calibrating a detector with different preparations does not automatically test its behavior for every hypothetical hidden-variable distribution.

In the standard two-setting, two-outcome CHSH expression, a Bell-local model with suitable setting independence obeys `|S| ≤ 2`; an ideal maximally entangled state with suitable settings predicts `2√2`. This is an established result, not a new derivation here. Stochastic locally factorized models are also constrained; Bell tests do not rule out hidden variables or determinism *as such*.

**Experiment.** Freedman and Clauser, [*Experimental Test of Local Hidden-Variable Theories*](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5qs9830k/qt5qs9830k.pdf), [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.28.938), used photons from a calcium atomic cascade and measured coincidences through polarizers at different orientations. They reported `δ = 0.050 ± 0.008`, exceeding their relevant bound `δ ≤ 0`, and agreement with quantum predictions. This `δ` is **not** the standard CHSH `S`; converting it without its normalization and assumptions would be misleading. As the authors state, interpreting their data as a test of the local-hidden-variable class required an assumption about detection probabilities when polarizers were inserted. Sparse detection and fixed settings left important loopholes.

## 2. Aspect and collaborators: stronger statistics and switching

Aspect, Grangier and Roger, [*Experimental Tests of Realistic Local Theories via Bell's Theorem*](https://journals.aps.org/prl/pdf/10.1103/PhysRevLett.47.460), built a brighter, laser-excited calcium source. They reported `δ = 0.0572 ± 0.0043`, and a separate generalized-inequality test not requiring rotational invariance, `S = 0.126 ± 0.014` against an upper bound of zero. Again this `S` is not standard CHSH `S`; optical detection assumptions still mattered.

Aspect, Grangier and Roger, [*Experimental Realization of Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen-Bohm Gedankenexperiment: A New Violation of Bell's Inequalities*](https://inters.org/files/aspectetal1982.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.49.91), used two-channel polarimeters and obtained the familiar CHSH-type `S = 2.697 ± 0.015`. The article expressly conditions its rejection of local models on the detected pairs being an unbiased sample: it did **not** close the detection loophole.

Aspect, Dalibard and Roger, [*Experimental Test of Bell's Inequalities Using Time-Varying Analyzers*](https://www2.unicamp.br/~chibeni/textosdidaticos/aspect-dalibard-roger-1982.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.49.1804), used rapidly switched optical paths so the effective analyzer configurations varied during photon flight. They found `S = 0.101 ± 0.020` against their inequality's `S ≤ 0`, about five reported standard deviations; this is a third, differently defined statistic, not CHSH `S`. They explicitly said their switching was **quasiperiodic, not truly random**, and called for random and complete switching for a more conclusive test. Losses also remained. Notably, a sentence in the Nobel background calls Aspect's settings random, whereas this original paper says otherwise; I follow the paper's explicit account rather than repeat the background's wording.

## 3. Zeilinger and collaborators: random settings, swapping and teleportation

**Space-like separated measurements.** Weihs, Jennewein, Simon, Weinfurter and Zeilinger, [*Violation of Bell's Inequality under Strict Einstein Locality Conditions*](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9810080), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.81.5039), separated observers by about 400 m, used fast physical random generators for settings, and stored local time tags separately for later comparison. A typical run gave `S = 2.73 ± 0.02` from 14,700 coincidences in 10 seconds. The authors explicitly acknowledged approximately 5% collection/detection efficiency and a fair-sampling assumption. Thus the locality improvement should not be mistaken for simultaneous closure of the detection loophole.

**Teleportation.** Bouwmeester, Pan, Mattle, Eibl, Weinfurter and Zeilinger, [*Experimental Quantum Teleportation*](https://arxiv.org/html/1901.11004), [Nature DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/37539), demonstrated conditional transfer of a photon's polarization state to another photon using an entangled pair and a Bell-state measurement. Their apparatus selected one Bell outcome, not a deterministic complete Bell measurement. Their threefold plots subtracted a measured spurious background; they also performed fourfold measurements for two nonorthogonal input states without background subtraction, reporting `70% ± 3%` visibility. The paper presents results for several input polarizations, not a separate measurement for every possible unknown state. The ideal full protocol requires classical communication and a corresponding correction; it does not transmit matter, clone an unknown state, or send usable information faster than light. The accessible arXiv presentation was uploaded later and identifies the Nature journal publication; it was read as that presentation, not as an independently checked typeset Nature version.

**Swapping.** Pan, Bouwmeester, Weinfurter and Zeilinger, [*Experimental Entanglement Swapping: Entangling Photons That Never Interacted*](https://web.physics.ucsb.edu/~quopt/swap.pdf), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.80.3891), created two pairs and performed a joint Bell-state measurement on one photon from each pair. Conditional on that outcome, the two other photons displayed entanglement despite never directly interacting. The actual setup used the same pump pulse and crystal twice; this is not a demonstration of two wholly causally disconnected sources. Fourfold coincidence data yielded a reported visibility `0.65 ± 0.02`; the paper itself says a stable visibility above about 0.71 would be required for a subsequent significant Bell-inequality test. This experiment should not be described as having itself demonstrated that Bell violation. Heralding/conditioning and comparison of records are essential; no retroactive signalling follows.

**Multipartite correlations.** Bouwmeester, Pan, Daniell, Weinfurter and Zeilinger, [*Observation of Three-Photon Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger Entanglement*](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/9810035), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.82.1345), produced a conditional three-photon GHZ state from two pairs and used measurements in complementary bases to distinguish coherent superposition from a mere mixture. The detection of the trigger and the other photons jointly defined the selected events. The paper explicitly treats a GHZ test of local realism as future work: its state-preparation demonstration is not itself that later test. The Nobel background lists a subsequent experiment, Pan et al., [*Experimental Test of Quantum Nonlocality in Three-Photon Greenberger–Horne–Zeilinger Entanglement*](https://doi.org/10.1038/35002043); its original text was not read for this audit.

## 4. The later loophole benchmark

Giustina, Versteegh, Wengerowsky and collaborators, including Zeilinger, [*Significant-Loophole-Free Test of Bell's Theorem with Entangled Photons*](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.03190v2), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.115.250401), used fast setting generation, spatially separated measurements, high-efficiency superconducting detectors and a Clauser–Horne–Eberhard inequality incorporating no-detection outcomes. They reported `J = 7.27 × 10⁻⁶`, with local-model upper bound zero, and a `p ≤ 3.74 × 10⁻³¹` under their stated analysis, allowing device memory and accounting for bounded random-setting predictability. This is the authors' inference, not my raw-data reanalysis. They used locally defined time slots to address coincidence selection and reported system heralding efficiencies of about 78.6% and 76.2%.

This addresses the major locality and detection loopholes simultaneously under stated experimental assumptions; it is not a proof free of every conceivable loophole. In particular, the authors' spacetime argument limits setting correlations with variables originating at photon emission; it does not prove absolute independence from all conceivable common causes in the earlier past. Statistical significance does not by itself exclude systematic or modeling error.

## 5. What follows for a relational, experiential whole

1. **Joint structure is experimentally consequential.** Entanglement cannot generally be replaced by a separable mixture that reproduces all the relevant measurement correlations. Swapping shows that operational relations depend on preparations, joint measurements and conditioning, not simply whether the two outer photons directly collided. This motivates taking relations seriously, but does not by itself establish that relations are the sole fundamental ontology.
2. **Bell locality is stronger than no-signalling.** With measurement independence and the experimental assumptions in place, observed Bell violation excludes the specified Bell-local factorization. It does not establish faster-than-light communication, rule out all realist or deterministic interpretations, or select a unique ontology. The Nobel background's informal equation of locality with no instantaneous signalling is too weak as a statement of the assumption used in Bell's factorization.
3. **The measure remains a separate obligation.** In the user's proposal all genuine possibilities are actual within the whole; that premise alone gives no frequencies or conditional probabilities for actual records. A candidate must reproduce no-detection-inclusive Bell-test statistics and, separately, the conditional teleportation and swapping statistics, with their explicit source, instrument and outcome selection rules. A finite actual whole can have trial-level setting independence or dependence; neither follows just from global fixity. See [the existing actuality and Bell gate](ACTUALITY_CAUSAL_INFERENCE_AND_BELL.md) and [selection-model checks](QUANTUM_ERASER_BELL_AND_SELECTION_MEASURE_GATE.md).
4. **No phenomenal conclusion is licensed.** Photon entanglement, a shared state, GHZ correlations and quantum teleportation do not demonstrate consciousness, identify a subject, count perspectives or establish that experience is transported. They neither establish nor refute the user's experiential-ground hypothesis. The unresolved subject/experience bridge remains independent of this optical evidence; see [quantum access structures](QUANTUM_ACCESS_STRUCTURES_AND_SUBJECT_COUNT_GATE.md).
5. **No clock or creation mechanism follows.** The timing relations used by the actual experiments are operational spacetime descriptions. They do not explain emergent spacetime or require an external clock for a proposed timeless whole; neither do they establish self-existence or necessary plenitude.

The useful progress is therefore a concrete empirical constraint and clarification of the account's outstanding obligations—not a new derivation, a discovery of consciousness in entanglement, or a completed solution.
