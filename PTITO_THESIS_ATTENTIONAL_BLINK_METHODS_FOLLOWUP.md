## 2. What the accessible methods add

**Design and comparability.** In each trial four concurrent rapid serial visual presentation streams, one per visual quadrant, contained letter distractors and two digit targets. The targets could appear in any stream. Same-stream trials were possible and treated separately; the explicitly stated main comparison was **different-stream within one hemifield** versus **different-stream across hemifields**. Thus the design controls the specific same-stream versus different-stream confound in that comparison, not all possible differences between spatial positions or hemispheric processing. Across-hemifield trials necessarily involve separate streams; within-hemifield trials can involve either, so pooling all within-field trials would not be equivalent to this planned matched comparison.

Two lag conditions were used, lag 2 and lag 8. The partial thesis extraction showed a 150 ms stimulus-onset interval, but did not reach the patient's procedure. The now-inspected final paper specifies **150 ms for controls and 233 ms for M.L.**, with no inter-stimulus interval in the control procedure. Under the paper's lag convention, their nominal onset separations are respectively 300/1,200 ms and approximately 466/1,864 ms. Four experimental blocks of 96 trials followed practice; M.L. received ten practice blocks, controls one. The published paper and [follow-up audit](SPLIT_BRAIN_BLINK_2004_2009_COMPLETE_RESULTS_AUDIT.md) provide the results; cell sizes after exclusions and raw trials remain unavailable.

**Participants and response.** The manuscript describes 22 neurologically intact controls and one 28-year-old left-handed man, M.L., with a reported complete callosotomy for epilepsy. It does not establish that all potentially relevant interhemispheric routes were severed or that any other patient in this project's studies is the same person. Because of his limited manual dexterity and the authors' statement that verbal responses could only be given for right-field stimuli, the experiment used **number of digits seen**, not digit identities. M.L. lifted zero, one or two fingers of the hand on the same side as the field, with the experimenter entering the response. Controls used separate left- and right-hand keyboard keys to indicate the counts. Responses were unspeeded. This makes a shared output or recording account a question for investigation, not a demonstrated artifact: the groups had distinct response channels and the experimenter's entry is not itself evidence of error.

The two targets were always presented, but that fact was not explicitly disclosed to participants; the authors characterize subjective chance as about one in nine for the combined two-hand response. That is an **author assumption about subjective guessing**, not an empirical estimate of actual chance strategy, independent hand responses or likelihood of informed guessing. Repeated trials could affect beliefs; the paper does not establish how this possibility was evaluated.

**Gaze.** For M.L., a camera focused on one eye and the experimenter rejected trials with detected eye movements; the text reports fewer than 2% rejected on this basis. For controls, eye movements were recorded in a separate pilot sample of five, whose results the authors say did not differ from the reported results. The accessible excerpt does **not** establish trial-level eye tracking for the main 22 controls, a quantitative detection threshold, or whether small shifts and other forms of leakage were excluded. These are limitations of accessible documentation, not evidence that fixation actually failed.

## 3. What a count can and cannot tell us

A correct paired count may be informative about both sides' target detection, but the recorded output is a **pair of hemifield counts**, not a direct description of digit identities, momentary centrality, or an explicit report of both items being experienced together. If both targets appeared in the same field, a count of one alone does not say which was detected; when they appeared in different fields, the locations can associate a count with a target, subject to false positives and the coding rules. The final paper reports its principal within/across contrast using joint count correctness and blink magnitude as lag 8 minus lag 2. It separately uses `T2 correct | T1 correct` for a control inter-hemifield analysis. The effects of detection errors, guessing and memory remain unresolved; do not substitute conditional T2 identification for the main count procedure.

The complete paper's results now show lower overall accuracy for within- than across-hemifield targets, with lag contrasts that the authors characterize as comparable. The [follow-up](SPLIT_BRAIN_BLINK_2004_2009_COMPLETE_RESULTS_AUDIT.md) gives the reported means, correct-pair estimand and patient/control timing difference. Patient-specific uncertainty and condition-specific trial exclusions are not supplied. A nonsignificant difference does not prove equal effects or rule out a clinically or theoretically meaningful difference. One patient does not establish a general callosotomy effect, even with many trials.

Competing explanations of the lag contrast include a capacity-limited stage, task-set/control dynamics and distractor interference; the thesis introduction itself discusses competing attentional-blink accounts. Residual common pathways, alerting, response preparation, memory and field-specific performance are possible contributors requiring appropriate controls, not findings demonstrated in M.L. Four streams strengthen the comparison by addressing one known confound, but do not isolate a unique neural or cognitive bottleneck.

## 4. Consequences for the substantive project

The earlier [attention audit](ATTENTION_CAPACITY_VERSUS_LIVED_UNITY_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md) was right to treat the study as task-specific, not an equivalence proof or direct shared-field demonstration; its generalized description of second-target report should now be read in light of this **counting protocol**, not as if the participant identified each successive digit. Neither sequential count reports nor an inferred shared limitation by themselves establish a same-window cross-field conjunction. Importantly, Lopez's proposed experiential centrality premise is stronger than—and should not be collapsed into—a shared functional resource. This follow-up neither refutes that philosophical conditional nor demonstrates that its experiential antecedent is present in this experiment.

The refined protocol still classifies neither `Jlocal` nor `Jglobal` on the [existing L/C comparison](LAYERED_UNITY_AND_SUBJECT_QUALITY_COMPARISON.md). It gives no justified mapping from a human neural system to the toy's four spins, no exhaustive census of experiential fields, and no independent `q`-to-quality calibration. A response failure is not evidence that experience is absent, and even a well-supported global conjunction would not automatically settle detailed unity or ontology. First-person testimony remains meaningful evidence and should not be replaced by the count task.

Remaining useful inputs include ethically shareable anonymized trial-condition and response coding or aggregate condition tables, including false positives, condition-specific counts, uncertainty and exclusions, as well as any clarifications of fixation monitoring and manuscript-to-publication changes. No request has been sent. Do not reconstruct trial-level significance or individual outcomes from summary means.

A noninvasive, consent-based follow-up could separately vary lag and matched stream placement, collect gaze and separately recorded response channels, and prespecify a meaningful equivalence margin and within-person uncertainty. To address lived unity rather than only performance, add separately evaluated, time-anchored first-person questions about centrality and coarse/local conjunction, with controls for task comprehension, inference, memory and access failure. Such questions would need their own validation and transport argument; the digit-count task is not that validation. No new surgery or involuntary participation is proposed.
