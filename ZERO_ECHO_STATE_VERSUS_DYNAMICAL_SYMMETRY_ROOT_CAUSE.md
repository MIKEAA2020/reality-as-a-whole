# Does the type-swap conflict actually break the echo?

## The diagnostic question

The [previous investigation](ZERO_TYPE_REFLECTION_LOCALITY_ROOT_CAUSE.md) showed that the free relative-angle kinetic operator and the proposed type swap do not commute. But the very same model had a swap-invariant, uniform coherent echo. Does this incompatibility invalidate that echo, or was a symmetry of one state being mistaken for a symmetry of all dynamics?

**Result:** in the four-bin scalar model, the existing uniform angular state is fixed by the swap and annihilated by both the free and symmetrized kinetic operators. The operator conflict does **not** by itself destroy that particular state. However, among swap-even pure angular vectors, it is the only one whose entire free-kinetic orbit stays swap-even. A specific swap-even excitation develops an exactly calculable odd component, despite returning to the even sector later. Thus one-state agreement cannot establish symmetry of the operator, its excitations, or an incidence measure.

The earlier quantum checks connect these matrices to the original coherent record and constrained pair. A new test applies the free dressed operator to an even excitation of those actual encoded branch vectors and verifies the predicted odd coupling. The evolution parameter used below is a mathematical parameter of a *candidate* kinetic Hamiltonian, not evidence of an external clock or of realized physical dynamics.

## 1. Why the original state survives

At the anchor, after the previously specified record/pair dressing is removed, write

```text
Q = [−3  3  0  0]       R = [0 1 0 0]
    [ 3 −7  4  0]           [1 0 0 0]
    [ 0  4 −7  3]           [0 0 1 0]
    [ 0  0  3 −3]           [0 0 0 1]
H_free = −Q,        e = (1,1,1,1)/2.
```

Then `Re=e`, `Qe=0`, and `Q_sym e=0`, even though `[Q,R]≠0`. Therefore, if used as the only Hamiltonian on the angular factor, either candidate leaves e unchanged under `exp(−iHt)`. Tensoring e with an arbitrary reference record vector gives the analogous statement before the unitary dressing. This does **not** say the record, clock, subject, or whole-history sector is uniquely fixed: the kinetic kernel has a whole reference-fiber degeneracy.

The prior verification explicitly checked selected original old-record/pointer vectors, encoded constrained pair signs, and clock prefixes: the undressed branches have the common reference needed for this argument; the finite operator and chosen swap are dressed back to that same carrier. The clock-transported full-history action has its own pulse-parity dressing. One must not replace it indiscriminately with the bare anchor R. The present calculation does not assert that this kinetic evolution is the actual clock history or that the echo alone selects a physical Hamiltonian.

## 2. Exactly which even pure states stay even?

The necessary and sufficient condition for `R exp(iQt)v=exp(iQt)v` for every real t is that `(R−I)Q^k v=0` for every k≥0. Necessity follows by differentiating at t=0; sufficiency follows from the matrix exponential. For this Q the first three constraints already give

```text
ker [(R−I); (R−I)Q; (R−I)Q²] = span{(1,1,1,1)}.
```

Indeed `Rv=v` means `v=(a,a,b,c)`. The first derivative forces `b=a`, and the second then forces `c=a`. The constant vector is sufficient because Q annihilates it. This is valid for complex amplitudes, not only probabilities. Adding the same reference fiber to every bin gives the dressed constant sector described above.

For pure *rays*, swap invariance also permits R eigenvalue −1. Here the odd space is the single line generated by `(1,−1,0,0)/√2`, and Q does not preserve that line. Continuity prevents an R-invariant ray from changing its ± eigenvalue along the orbit. Thus, within this four-bin scalar model and this free evolution, the uniform ray is also the only pure ray invariant under R at every parameter value. This is not a statement about all possible larger-carrier states or actual experiences.

## 3. A concrete anomaly and an exact diagnostic

Take the normalized, swap-even vectors

```text
v = (1,1,0,0)/√2,     w = (1,−1,0,0)/√2.
```

The free Hamiltonian couples them: `|⟨w|H_free|v⟩|=2`. Its exact odd amplitude and probability are

```text
⟨w|exp(−iH_free t)|v⟩ = (e^(−2it) − e^(−12it))/5,
P_odd(t) = (4/25) sin²(5t) = 4t² + O(t⁴).
```

At `t=π/10`, the odd probability is `4/25`; at `t=π/5`, it is zero again. This is a useful troubleshooting control: symmetry at the beginning **and** at a later checkpoint does not establish symmetry at intermediate values. The state retains a nonzero even component throughout because its uniform-mode projection is conserved; the nonzero odd probability therefore genuinely breaks R invariance of this pure ray at the intermediate point.

An even subtler control is `v₂=(1,1,1,0)/√3`. Its first odd derivative vanishes, but its second does not: its odd probability starts as `6t⁴`. A test of one state or one derivative can consequently miss the conflict. By contrast, the symmetrized kinetic operator commutes with R and keeps every initially even angular state even, at the previously identified cost of a non-neighbor edge.

### Check on the original recorded pair

The [new test](verification/zero_echo_state_symmetry_dynamics_test.py) prepares the two-bin even excitation from the **actual** label-0, positive-pointer echo branches at the anchor, encodes their constrained pair, and applies the existing dressed free operator. It checks the original branch norms and the entire output against the displayed four-bin action. The odd part of `H_free v` has squared norm `4` relative to the normalized input, as predicted, and all output pair labels retain the opposite-pair constraint. The earlier type-swap test separately established the swap action on these physical branches. This is a test of the specified recorded subspace and chosen full-carrier extension, not proof of physical accessibility of that excitation or of an actual time evolution.

## 4. Stationarity and type invariance do not force a pure ground state

A density matrix invariant under both Q and R need not be the uniform pure state. In fact all normalized positive scalar-bin density matrices commuting with both are

```text
ρ_p = p |e⟩⟨e| + (1−p)(I−|e⟩⟨e|)/3,    0≤p≤1.
```

Q has four distinct eigenvalues, so a matrix commuting with it is diagonal in its eigenbasis. R connects every pair among the three excited eigenspaces and leaves the ground eigenspace fixed, forcing the three excited diagonal weights equal. For p=0 the state is stationary and swap-invariant but entirely supported on excited modes; its expectation of `H_free` is `20/3`. Thus the pure-history conclusion must not be silently extended to mixed states. Stationarity is also not the same as a unique underlying actual occurrence or subject.

## 5. Why physical symmetry does not settle incidence

Even grant a physical state `ρ` invariant under the physical R and a separately postulated two-type incidence extension. For `0≤α≤1`, the formal block state

```text
σ_α = α ρ ⊕ (1−α) ρ
```

has the same physical marginal ρ for every α when physical effects act identically on the blocks. But a combined swap of the physical factor and the two labels sends α to `1−α`; invariance of the *extension* requires α=1/2. The verifier checks both the marginal and the failure of invariance at α=1/3. This is a conditional countermodel for the inference: it does not establish that these labels are real subjects, that α is an actual incidence measure, or that unequal-weight complete worlds are genuinely possible.

The [earlier incidence analysis](ZERO_SOURCE_INTEGRATION_STATIONARY_SUBJECT_INCIDENCE.md) already made its equal-weight conclusion conditional on invariance of actual incidence under a combined physical-and-type transformation. The present diagnosis supports that qualification rather than overturning it. A fixed physical state, a dynamical symmetry and an incidence-preserving symmetry are three different claims.

## 6. Root-cause chain and next discriminating issue

1. **Why was there an apparent contradiction?** The same swap was tested both on a particular uniform state and against an entire operator; these are inequivalent tests.
2. **Why does the selected state hide the mismatch?** It lies in the common fixed/zero sector of both candidate operators. Infinitely many inequivalent operators can agree on one vector without agreeing elsewhere.
3. **Why do excitations reveal it?** The swap changes which bin meets the positive region, while the free path kinetic connects the original neighbors. It couples even and odd swap sectors. The previous report traces this to the discontinuous interval action and the incompatible choices of topology, symmetry and connectedness.
4. **Why elevate this swap from a state stabilizer to a law?** That requires an independent account of its action on all relevant observables, processes and admissible states, not merely a construction that fixes one prepared echo. Choosing the symmetrized generator, changing topology, or restricting attention to the uniform sector are different proposals with different costs.
5. **Why elevate a physical law to an incidence symmetry?** Even a genuine symmetry of the physical dynamics does not alone determine the number, identity or measure of lived occurrences. The type-extension control makes that missing bridge explicit; equally, it does not refute the user's proposed one-constitution account.

The next task is to investigate what, in the same candidate constitution, would make a proposed transformation an admissible symmetry of both dynamics and actual incidence—or to find a more fundamental relation that yields the required balance without that additional assumption. Zero, the free kinetic criterion, the relevant observables, and the physical–experiential identity remain to be derived together; a formal model or an asserted equivalence does not do that work.
