# Retinal MaxEnt: published antecedents and limits of marginals

## Source and reading scope

Granot-Atedgi, Tkačik, Segev and Schneidman, [*Stimulus-dependent Maximum Entropy Models of Neural Population Codes*](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1002922), was previously only partially read. The accessible PLOS HTML's abstract, introduction, results, discussion, methods and figure captions have now been read (chunks 0–15). The source embeds mathematical expressions and some numerical values as images; these were **not** all visually checked. Figures themselves were not audited, and original data, code, fitting and entropy calculations were not independently replayed. References were accessed but not individually checked. This is more than an abstract but not a full equation, figure or reproducibility audit.

Wainwright and Jordan, [*Graphical Models, Exponential Families, and Variational Inference*](https://doi.org/10.1561/2200000001), §§3–4 and 5.1–5.4 were read to the extent documented in [the progress report](EFT_AND_GRAPHICAL_MODELS_READING_PROGRESS.md). Its exact and approximate probability-theory results are not themselves biological or experiential claims.

## What the retinal paper already did

- For a 100-cell salamander retina recording under repeated full-field flicker, the authors distinguish static vocabulary models, time-dependent models (T1/T2), and stimulus-dependent models (S1/S2). The T2 model constrains single-cell firing rates at each phase and overall pairwise covariance; S2 uses stimulus-filter-dependent fields plus stimulus-independent pair terms. These are **published antecedents**, not new methods from this project.
- Their S2 model outperformed S1 in held-out repeats of the same stimulus and captured aspects of time-dependent noise correlations. That does not establish transfer to new movies: the paper says it tested generalization across repeats, and proposes tests on novel stimuli as future work.
- The S2 terms are symmetric and stimulus-independent by construction; the authors explicitly say they are **functional**, not physical/synaptic connections. The interpretation of any GLM history coupling as causal also requires assumptions about unmeasured common inputs and intervention, even if the model is temporally directional.
- The paper distinguishes signal from noise correlations using independently shuffled repeats; their shuffle description permutes within each time bin, while our earlier analysis used whole-trial label permutations preserving whole trains. Both have a known same-trial collision chance when independently permuting with replacement in the pairing sense. Our block-restricted shuffle and fitted-score identity remain project-specific computations, not a claim to invent the underlying controls.
- The paper acknowledges recording drift, discards the first 100 adapting repeats, and splits remaining repeats odd/even for training and testing. Its models can be fit without stimulus repeats but repeated presentations enable the reported evaluation. By contrast, our retinal check used first 100 versus last 20 repeats under an **unverified 120-repeat alignment assumption**, from a different recording. Drift can therefore affect our comparisons differently.
- The authors compute the entropy of static and conditional models independently and note that the models need not marginalize consistently. Upper bounds on total and conditional entropy do **not** by themselves give a strict bound on their difference; their information-transmission figure is an estimate. Information per bin or per second also requires care with temporal dependence and the stated bin-independence assumptions.
- Their methods explicitly choose T1 and empirical-frequency baseline regularizers using the test set, to favor those baselines. Such tuned values are not untouched-test estimates for those baselines. This does not by itself overturn their S2 versus S1 comparison; it limits comparisons involving the tuned baselines. Our smoothing sensitivity is likewise exploratory, not a preregistered model-selection result.

The S2 model and our phase-dependent field plus shared pairwise-tilt model are related but not identical: we use phase-specific independent estimates as a fixed reference, fitted offsets and a ridge penalty, not their stimulus filters or an exact reproduction of T2 moment constraints. In particular, adding couplings to our fixed reference can alter the resulting phase-specific model rates; they are not forced to remain exactly at the reference rates. Its fit should not be presented as the authors' S2 or T2 implementation.

## Exact nonidentification examples

The executable [pairwise-marginal gate](verification/pairwise_marginal_information_gate.py) writes [its receipt](verification/pairwise_marginal_information_gate.json). These are standard finite counterexamples, not new biological evidence.

1. Three fair binary variables can have every singleton and pair marginal identical to independent fair bits while the joint law is restricted to even parity. The true entropy is then 2 bits rather than 3, and the KL divergence to the independent/MaxEnt distribution is 1 bit. A strictly positive example mixes half even-parity law and half uniform law: the same lower-order marginals coexist with entropy about 2.81128 bits and KL about 0.18872 bits. Thus moment-matched MaxEnt selects a particular joint distribution; it does not prove that higher-order dependence is absent. Full pattern data can in principle distinguish these examples.
2. Reproducing Wainwright–Jordan §4.1.1, Example 4.1, each edge of a three-node triangle has valid marginals consistent with fair singletons, but their disagreement probabilities are `1/5`, `1/5`, `4/5`. Any joint distribution would have `P(X₀≠X₂) ≤ P(X₀≠X₁)+P(X₁≠X₂)`, violated here. Locally consistent pseudomarginals on a loopy graph need not describe any global distribution. This is the authors' example, not a novel theorem.

The two examples address different questions: many *globally valid* distributions can share observed marginals, while locally consistent edge tables can fail to define *any* global distribution. Neither demonstrates that either situation occurs in the retinal dataset. For our ten-cell fits we enumerate all 1,024 global states, avoiding this pseudomarginal relaxation, but finite sample, model selection, timing and biological replication uncertainties remain.

## Relevance to the relational framework

Statistical adequacy, physical causal coupling, phenomenal quality and the partition into subjects are separate claims. A successful local or pairwise model may be useful while leaving different global laws, mechanisms and experiential interpretations open. Conversely, this nonidentification does not disprove relational or experiential grounding. An experiential bridge and a test discriminating candidate subject boundaries remain outstanding; these articles do not establish self-existence, plenitude or universal consciousness.
