# Foundation: comparing the two existing necessity routes

## Question and result

The first step after the Roy analysis is to compare—not repackage—the existing **actuality-first** and **truth-grounding** routes to necessary experience. Both can yield a valid conditional conclusion, but neither's decisive premise has been independently established. A finite comparison also shows that, even when the stipulated global possibility–actuality principle holds, it does not by itself fix whether there is one subject or more than one.

These are checks of inference and premise placement, **not a proof that any formal countermodel is a genuinely possible reality**. The semantic tools are standard modal logic, not a claimed new modal derivation. The sources and prior arguments are in [necessary occurrence](NECESSARY_OCCURRENCE_CANDIDATE.md), [truth and experience](NOTHINGNESS_TRUTH_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_NECESSITY.md) and [plenitude foundation](PLENITUDE_FOUNDATION_GAPS_AND_SYMMETRY.md).

## Same notation, two routes

Let `E` mean “some concrete experience occurs,” `T` mean a proposed necessary truth, and `□` quantify over genuine alternatives **if** the model correctly represents them. No external time, creator or conversion of possibilities into actualities is introduced.

1. **Actuality-first.** Take actual `E` as the datum. If the independently justified nature of occurrence entails `E → □E`, then `□E` follows. The missing bridge is the second premise: the actual fact alone does not establish that its absence is impossible. Alternatively, the previously examined global schema `◇φ → φ`, applied to `¬E`, yields `E → □E` by classical contraposition. It moves the burden to justifying that global schema; it does not derive the schema from experience.
2. **Truth-grounding.** If `□T` and `□(T → E)`, then `□E`. The crucial additional premise is that the necessary truth has, in every relevant alternative, an experiential ground or equivalent necessity-entailing connection. Necessary abstract truth alone does not establish that experiential grounding; the stronger premise must be argued for, not concealed in the label `T`.

These conclusions say **some** experience is necessary, not that this specific subject, entire content, subject plurality, or physical laws are necessary. Even proving `□E` would not on its own establish why experience is concrete or self-explanatory, or prove the full `◇φ → φ` schema for arbitrary global propositions.

## Common adversarial controls

The [finite verifier](verification/foundation_route_comparison.py) checks these cases, using accessible worlds as formal evaluation points and a stipulated count of subjects for each point. Here `E` holds exactly when the count is positive. `T` is a Boolean placeholder, not a theory of truthmaking.

| Formal control | Actual `E` | `□T` | `□E` | Global `◇φ → φ` for all valuations | Subjects at actual point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Two mutually accessible points: counts 1 and 0 | yes | yes | no | no | 1 |
| One reflexive point: count 1 | yes | yes | yes | yes | 1 |
| One reflexive point: count 2 | yes | yes | yes | yes | 2 |

In the first control, the missing premises `E → □E` and `□(T → E)` are false: it demonstrates that neither follows merely from `E` and `□T` in this semantics. The second and third meet the global schema because each frame has only one reflexive point; they differ in stipulated plurality. They are **different formal models**, not two incompatible global worlds both admitted within a single plenitude. Thus even granting global collapse does not logically supply a particular subject count. Nor do the toy counts constitute experiential subjects or establish that a physically identical arrangement can support arbitrary phenomenal assignment.

The controls also do not refute either bridge: a proponent can deny the first frame is genuinely admissible. The required work is to give an independent, non-circular reason for that denial and to say what grounds the subject-count and qualitative claims. Global consistency and ungrounded exclusion are not enough by themselves.

## Updated gate verdict and next precise challenge

- **B1:** actual experience is the starting datum; necessary concrete occurrence and its self-explanation remain **open**.
- **B2:** the cited schemas have checked conditional consequences; the strongest possibility–actuality identity and its scope remain **open**.
- **B4:** global necessity does not settle subject plurality; an incidence rule remains **open**.

The next substantive challenge is to select an independently motivated account of **why a necessary truth must be grounded in experience**, and test whether its grounding relation requires experiential occurrence in its premises or can support it without restatement. Compare it fairly with the actuality-first route and defensible theories of truth that do not require such a ground. A formal implication or one finite example cannot settle that philosophical dispute; evidence for an intrinsic experiential constitution and compatibility with the physical and subject gates would also be required.
