# Fixed plan: Ptito thesis and the attentional-blink evidence

## Prior knowledge and source discovery

The prior [attention audit](ATTENTION_CAPACITY_VERSUS_LIVED_UNITY_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md) examined the publisher's Ptito et al. 2009 abstract, introduction and exposed snippets, not the full journal article. It identified one complete-callosotomy patient, 22 control participants, four streams and the absence of a reported significant within-versus-across difference; a null significance test is not equivalence. Those findings are known.

A bounded search has now located Alexia Ptito's University of Montreal [master's thesis record](https://umontreal.scholaris.ca/items/f0f7ced8-2b39-45a1-919f-408731119850), whose bilingual abstract describes the same kind of four-stream study. The PDF is listed by the institution. The abstract alone does not establish that its analyses match the published journal article word-for-word, and no full thesis text has yet been inspected for this follow-up. The university's rights notice requires respect for copyright: link to the institutional copy and do not commit the PDF or its text.

## Fixed procedure

1. Retrieve and read the accessible thesis text, including its table of contents and the original experiment's methods/results/discussion if they are reachable. The document retrieval tool may only parse the first 30 PDF pages; explicitly state the page/chunk coverage and any inaccessible later sections. If a later section is inaccessible, do not claim to have inspected it. Use indexed excerpts only as such and do not represent them as a full-source reading.
2. Identify the thesis's relation to the article using title, author, date, coauthors and study details. Treat it as an author-supplied academic manuscript, not a separately independent replication or automatically identical peer-reviewed version. Compare only features actually readable in both. Do not infer participant identity across studies from initials.
3. Extract, where accessible, the target stimuli, design, sample, data exclusion, response coding, statistical estimands and exact result statements; distinguish intra- versus inter-hemifield comparisons and target accuracy from blink depth. Highlight limitations, uncertainty, clinically relevant heterogeneity and alternative functional interpretations. Do not infer exact confidence intervals, unreported controls or absence of experience.
4. Re-evaluate only the pre-existing attention/phenomenology chain and four-spin conjunction target; explain exactly what the additional accessible source changes, if anything. Patient-specific details should be kept to published, task-relevant facts; no patient-level raw records, new participant outcomes, signals or private materials. Preserve the distinction between timing of sequential stimuli and same-window experience, and between physical/functional resource contention and subjective unity.
5. If the decisive methods and results cannot be reached, report that limitation, preserve the existing gate, and provide a precise author/library request. Do not turn partial access into a claimed complete read or invent a numerical analysis from unextracted plots.

## Output and validation

Record the location, reading scope and evidence limits in a small follow-up report, link it to the earlier audit and status, refresh inventories and check links. Run relevant existing regressions if the changed claims affect them. The goal is improved source provenance, not a new toy model, q measurement or metaphysical proof; C3 and B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open absent genuinely independent evidence.
