# Retrospective follow-up plan: reward, selection history and persistence

The supplied Anderson et al. 2011 paper left uncertainty about the separate contributions of reward and prior target selection. Search results, already seen before this plan, identify later studies that report direct comparisons and task-dependent findings; this is a targeted retrospective follow-up, not a systematic review or a blind test.

1. Read primary full-text methods, results, controls and limitations for Anderson and Halpern's value-dependence experiments, the later within-participant rewarded/unrewarded comparison, and Milner et al.'s five-experiment task-dependence study if available. Confirm authors, year, sample, task, directly tested contrasts, uncertainty and any apparent counterevidence; do not rely on search snippets alone.
2. Compare the original 2011 result and 2026 Shelat task, distinguishing high versus low reward, reward versus unrewarded former target, reward versus absent distractor, and current goals/test context. Explicitly separate significance from a direct interaction or equivalence test and avoid generalizing a null beyond its design.
3. For the already fitted 2026 accuracy model, compute model-implied reward-minus-selected-unrewarded and reward-minus-neutral contrasts at observed blocks 1–4 and both trigger levels, using the fixed-effect covariance and two-sided Wald intervals. Specify this after knowing the model and paper results but before inspecting these contrasts: all 16 per model are exploratory, unadjusted and conditional, not preregistered tests. Do not substitute them for a direct experimental manipulation of the proposed mental mechanism. Retain no participant-level output.
4. Preserve the difference between behavioral/oculomotor selection, mechanism and phenomenal subject/experience. Record sources, access, limitations and what is not reproduced; update links and inventories, run checks, and present the report.
