# Source follow-up: publication, supplementary figures and recordings

## Why the earlier work stopped short

The previous assessment read the supplied main texts but stopped short of searching systematically for Fraser's published chapter, recovering the supplementary plots, or locating the retinal recordings and original code. That was an incomplete follow-up, not an established finding that these materials were unavailable. The supplementary captions had been retrieved as text; the figures themselves had not been rendered. The following checks narrow, but do not eliminate, those limitations.

## Fraser: the publication exists; a full version comparison is still missing

James D. Fraser's **[“Towards a Realist View of Quantum Field Theory”](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198814979.003.0014)** is a published chapter of *Scientific Realism and the Quantum*, edited by Steven French and Juha Saatsi, pages 276–292. The supplied 18-page PDF identifies itself as a draft for that volume; the [PhilSci Archive entry](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/14716/) identifies the same-titled text as a preprint.

We tried the [publisher's full-text chapter page](https://academic.oup.com/book/36983/chapter/322308346): our page tool was redirected to the abstract page and did not return the chapter's full text. The [Oxford record](https://academic.oup.com/book/36983/chapter-abstract/322308346?redirectedFrom=fulltext) gives the chapter title, author and bibliographic details. Search-index snippets from [a third-party corrected-proof text](https://dokumen.pub/scientific-realism-and-the-quantum-0198814976-9780198814979.html) show the opening differs in wording from the submitted draft; attempting to fetch that page itself returned an internal server error. These snippets are not a trustworthy complete copy or a full comparison. No substantive agreement or difference across the whole chapter is thereby established. We therefore retain the assessment as an interpretation of the supplied **draft**, not a verified assessment of the published text.

## Schneidman: actual dataset located, but not replayed

Princeton's [dataset record](https://dataspace.princeton.edu/handle/88435/dsp015425kd84r), **“Spike Trains of Retinal Ganglion Cells Viewing a Repeated Natural Movie,” DOI [10.34770/V0V4-3H52](https://doi.org/10.34770/V0V4-3H52)**, explicitly associates these spike trains with Schneidman et al. as well as Puchalla et al. The linked [README](https://dataspace.princeton.edu/bitstream/88435/dsp015425kd84r/7) was retrieved and read. It describes 40 simultaneously recorded, non-consecutively numbered cells, spike times in seconds, 145 repeats of a 26.5-second movie, and a recording length of 3,842.5 seconds. The metadata alone does not establish which of every dataset or figure in the Nature paper can be reconstructed from this archive.

The record lists:

- [Data_Spike_Trains.txt](https://dataspace.princeton.edu/bitstream/88435/dsp015425kd84r/8), 2.85 MB;
- the README above, 3.05 kB;
- [Display_Spike_Trains.pdf](https://dataspace.princeton.edu/bitstream/88435/dsp015425kd84r/9), 3.51 MB, a visual raster display.

The text extraction tool returned an initial chunk of the **actual spike-time file**, beginning with cell `c4_bush2_spks`, and indicated 365 chunks in total. We did not retrieve, validate or process all of them, and have not performed a reproduction or estimated any statistics from the data. The separate display PDF was text-extracted only, not visually rendered. The DOI's newer Data Commons redirect presented a bot-verification page, but the older DataSpace record, README and spike text are accessible to our page tool: **do not call the dataset unavailable**. Our sandbox's direct outbound access does not include the Princeton host; the page tool supplies text in chunks and did not place the full source file in our workspace. A full reproducible replay requires a complete byte-level download (or an equivalent verified transfer), data parsing, explicit fit/holdout and estimator choices, and differentiation of the archived stimulus and recordings from the other experimental conditions. The archive has no code file listed; we have not located or inspected the original analysis code. The repository page says items are all rights reserved unless otherwise indicated, so we have not copied its data into our public repository.

The README specifies 145 repeats. A later analysis by Cocco and Monasson, **[“Fast Inference of Interactions in Assemblies of Stochastic Integrate-and-Fire Neurons from Spike Recordings”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1102.5171)**, describes a 40-cell natural-movie set from the Schneidman work with 120 repeats. We inspected the later paper's searchable abstract and section 3.2, not its code or complete source data. We cannot determine whether that is a subset, another processing choice, or a different recording; do not silently treat the totals as interchangeable.

## Supplementary figures: captions read, graphs still not visually inspected

The Nature article links [supplementary figure 1](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnature04701/MediaObjects/41586_2006_BFnature04701_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), [figure 2](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnature04701/MediaObjects/41586_2006_BFnature04701_MOESM2_ESM.pdf), and [figure 3](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnature04701/MediaObjects/41586_2006_BFnature04701_MOESM3_ESM.doc). The page tool returned the captions of all three but no visual plot rendering or source binaries. They respectively report comparisons across 5, 20 and 50 ms bin widths for ten-cell groups; across subgroup size (250 sampled groups per size); and average local versus interaction fields with size. We do not infer axis limits, exact plotted values, variability or unreported significance from captions. The figures are linked by the publisher; their uninspected status is a **tool/output limitation**, not a finding that they are inaccessible to the user.

## Relevant later evidence, clearly delimited

Tang et al., **[“A Maximum Entropy Model Applied to Spatial and Temporal Correlations from Cortical Networks In Vitro,” DOI 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3359-07.2008](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3359-07.2008)**, study different cortical preparations. We inspected the abstract and selected results/discussion returned by searchable text, not every method, figure, supplement, data or code. They report that an equal-time model captured much of the observed correlation but concatenating such states did not capture the observed temporal dependencies. This is relevant external evidence for the statistical-versus-dynamical distinction, not a direct test of our subject rule or the same retinal experiment.

Tkačik et al., **[“Searching for Collective Behavior in a Large Network of Sensory Neurons,” DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003408](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003408)**, report a larger salamander-retina study, using an extension with a global population-spike-count constraint for groups of 40 or more and analyzing up to 120 neurons. We inspected the abstract, selected methods/results and figure captions via the article page, not the whole article, raw recordings, code, figures in detail or supplement. This is more than the original 200-cell extrapolation, but it does not verify that extrapolation, validate a causal boundary, or establish subjects.

## Consequences

The earlier physical-versus-phenomenal caution remains: static fits and correlations cannot, by themselves, validate the assumed causal interventions, identify experiencers, or settle metaphysical substrate independence. The key correction in source status is positive: **an original-paper-associated retinal spike-train archive has been located**, and a full-data follow-up is possible in principle. The version comparison, visual supplementary audit and data/code replay are still open; none should be described as completed.
