# Retrospective reading plan: subject incidence and indirect communication

## Starting point

The [split-brain identity audit](SPLIT_BRAIN_PRIVATE_SUBJECT_IDENTITY_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md) left P versus C-layered underdetermined. Project work has already audited Miller's subject-relative unity, a subject-first versus field-first fork, the limitations of behavioral cross-access, the physical selector L and its non-IIT status. In initial discovery snippets, Schechter's 2015 *The Subject in Neuropsychology* reconstructs an indirect behavior-and-sensation communication argument for two minds, with an L.B. hand-writing example. This is a promising independently motivated candidate, but no full article has yet been inspected in this pass; it is not a new discovery. Review snippets of Schechter's 2018 book and Roelofs' 2016 paper have also been seen. We will not imply the complete book or cited clinical sources were read.

## Fixed objectives

1. Obtain Schechter 2015's primary philosophical text if accessible and inspect its full argument, notes and limitations; otherwise explicitly record exact scope. Identify the operational relation, its role, subject-versus-person distinction, and empirically sourced example without claiming independent verification of its cited studies. Check a primary experimental source underlying a key example if available. Keep patient identity, anatomy, timing and causal alternatives explicit.
2. Formulate a fair, **defeasible** candidate incidence criterion motivated by her argument, on a common physical and experiential domain. Separate the operational pathway inference, evidence for first-person content, the alleged incidence bridge, subject/agent distinctions and concurrent window from each other. Ensure the rival single-subject case is not a straw man and model nonresponse as unknown.
3. Apply the criterion specifically to P's nested private three-subject completion and C-layered, not just left/right-only disconnection. Analyze whether the criterion has the evidence it needs for local–global distinctness and give specific discriminating conditions and counterexamples (shared mediator and internal routing). Do not manufacture counts, calibrated measurements, one-to-one subject-to-hand assignments, IIT values, or new empirical outcomes. Clearly separate necessity/sufficiency assumptions from results and avoid universal impossibility claims.
4. Compare criterion strengths and explanatory costs fairly, including token privacy, ordinary sensory feedback, attention and time-window/redescription invariance. Outline a safe, prespecified prospective comparison and explicitly say when it is unimplementable. Preserve honest uncertainty, q-quality and PAINgen conversion-code limitations, and open C3/B4–B5/F2/B9/F3/B10.
5. Write a bounded audit and update navigation, run relevant existing checks and inventories. No new arbitrary finite regressions, clinical contact, or invasive procedure.
