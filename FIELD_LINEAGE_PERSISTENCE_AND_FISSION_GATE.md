# Field lineage, persistence, and fission: a conditional comparison

## Result

The previous [field-individuation audit](FIRST_PERSONAL_GIVENNESS_AND_FIELD_INDIVIDUATION_AUDIT.md) identified a tension in calling two distinct field tokens *the same thin field-subject*. This continuation asks a different question: can **distinct field-occurrences be stages associated with one persisting person**? Yes as a formal proposal, if the persisting person is not simply identical to each distinct field token. That is an ontological choice, not a proof that people are streams, sets, fields or substances.

Two explicit rules can turn a stipulated lineage graph into strict one-to-one stage links, but they disagree. A symmetric fork also shows a cost of demanding both ordinary single-successor persistence and a decision depending only on each individual edge's unchanged local description: if either equally supported successor gets the predecessor's identity, symmetry forces the other to get it too. Distinct simultaneous successors then violate transitivity of identity. A branch-sensitive rule can consistently deny persistence in the fork, but it gives up the specified edge-local independence. This is an **additional locality tradeoff**, not a new discovery of the familiar identity-versus-fission argument.

No source or calculation establishes actual fission, a field, or a subject. Nor does this investigation change the standing proposal of a self-sufficient whole into a temporal production story.

## 1. Source and existing work

[Olson's Stanford Encyclopedia entry on personal identity](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/identity-personal/) (substantive revision 2023), sections 3–5, distinguishes persistence from evidence of it, discusses psychological and physical accounts, and analyzes fission, non-branching and overlapping temporal parts. We read the introduction and sections 3–5 as extracted in chunks 0 and 3–4; we have not audited the entire entry or independently read the cited primary Lewis, Parfit or Perry texts for this note. Marya Schechtman's [Routledge Encyclopedia discussion of responses to fission](https://doi.org/10.4324/9780415249126-V024-2), version 2, §4, was also read as a secondary discussion. These accounts are about personal persistence and its philosophical rivals; their authors are not being attributed our field-first, graph or physical claims.

The existing [ties and persistence gate](SUBJECT_TIES_PERSISTENCE_REDESCRIPTION_GATE.md) already proves a closely related **symmetric fusion** obstruction for a hypothetical four-spin selection. The [incidence reformulation](EPISODE_INCIDENCE_REFORMULATION.md) separates incidence, temporal extent and lineage; [causal record lineage](CAUSAL_RECORD_LINEAGE.md) constructs operational transport for explicit quantum channels while stressing that transport is not personal identity. The present comparison adds a concrete **fission-direction locality condition**, an alternative to a strict local non-branching selector, and an explicit distinction between subject-field tokens and overlapping continuant persons.

## 2. Objects, relations and a restricted domain

Fix a finite sequence of three local stages `t=0,1,2`, with at most one candidate occurrence per slot at each stage. In the verifier there are exactly two labeled slots per stage, including isolated ones; labels are bookkeeping, not names of real subjects. The stages are an ordered modeling convention, not a fundamental or external universal time.

Let `v` denote a stipulated, distinct field-occurrence or field-stage token, and let `R_t(u,v)` be a stipulated directed relation from one stage to the next. For this note, interpret it only as **candidate continuation support**. It might be informed by causal transport, suitable psychological continuity, phenomenal temporal integration, or other facts, but those interpretations have different evidential and constitutive requirements. The graph does not establish any of them. If `u` and `v` are distinct field tokens and thin subjects are identical to their respective fields, then their thin subjects are also distinct.

We separately ask whether `u` and `v` are associated with the **same continuant person**. In a non-overlapping-person variant, use a relation `S` on the field-stages: `S(u,v)` means that the two stages belong to one and the same person, not that the two stages are identical. Assume at most one field-stage per person at each selected stage and at most one person per field-stage. Then `S` is an equivalence relation, with no equivalence class containing two different slots at the same time. This is a restricted model: overlapping persons, shared stage-tokens, multiple concurrent fields per person, temporal gaps, and other accounts need different structures.

Accordingly, `R` can branch or merge without being numerical identity. The inferred `S` cannot identify distinct same-stage occupants under the exclusivity premise. Identity applies to the **person associated with** each stage, not literally to the stage tokens. A person may persist during dreamless sleep or other gaps on an account with additional non-experiential stages; the present three-stage graph cannot settle that question.

## 3. The fission-direction locality obstruction

Compare two local graph specifications with the same distinct earlier stage `u` and later stages `a,b`:

| Model | Candidate continuation edges | Symmetry |
|---|---|---|
| Single continuation | `u→a`; `b` has no edge from `u` | No requirement to exchange `a` and `b` |
| Symmetric fork | `u→a` and `u→b` | A symmetry fixes `u` and exchanges `a,b`, including **all** inputs relevant to the rule |

Both `a` and `b` are stipulated distinct simultaneous stages. This is a formal comparison of input descriptions, not a claim that two global worlds or persons are metaphysically possible. In the single-edge specification, a candidate rule may accept `S(u,a)` as ordinary persistence. Now impose the following **strong locality** requirement: the decision for `u→a` is unchanged between the two inputs whenever the intrinsic edge-local data declared sufficient for that decision are unchanged. In the comparison they are stipulated unchanged; the extra `u→b` edge is deliberately outside that local input. This does not assert that an actual causal mechanism can be duplicated without affecting either branch.

If the rule returns a determinate strict-identity assignment, is covariant under the fork's full symmetry, and says `S(u,a)` in the single case, then locality gives `S(u,a)` in the fork. Symmetry gives `S(u,b)` too. Transitivity yields `S(a,b)`, contrary to the distinct-simultaneous-person restriction. Thus **all those premises cannot hold together**.

There are several legitimate exits:

- let the identity decision depend on the other continuation or other global context, as a non-branching criterion explicitly does;
- deny the single-edge sufficiency premise, or deny the adequacy of the edge-local data;
- deny the full symmetry because additional relevant facts distinguish the branches;
- leave identity unresolved rather than outputting a complete decision;
- adopt overlapping continuant persons and abandon the premise that each field-stage belongs to at most one person;
- reject the stipulated branch structure, the one-stage-per-person condition, or the underlying person ontology.

Requiring invariance only under a symmetry of **all relevant inputs** matters. A physically symmetric reduced graph can conceal an asymmetric realization or an extra primitive identity fact. Conversely, calling an unrepresented asymmetry real without evidence does not derive it. A symmetric probability distribution over rival assignments is also not itself a single definite identity fact.

This result does **not** show that a person is harmed or destroyed by a physical copy, or that numerical identity lacks moral importance. The stronger requirement that no other branch matters is precisely what a non-branching view rejects. The apparent cost of that rejection—the outcome for one branch can depend on what happens to another—is part of the philosophical debate, not a contradiction.

## 4. Two usable—but unvalidated—selection rules

For each bipartite `R_t`, consider two label-covariant rules. Both use the full relation at that boundary, and neither is given by first-personal givenness or by the physical action-unity gate.

### Rule M: mutually unique adjacent links

Select `(u,v)` exactly when `u` has one outgoing R-edge and `v` has one incoming R-edge. Declare the selected links, and their transitive closure through consecutive boundaries, to place stages in the same continuant-person class. The declaration that unlinked classes are different persons is an **additional closed-world stipulation**; otherwise the links are merely a set of supported identifications.

For arbitrary finite layered graphs with edges only between adjacent stages, M selects a partial one-to-one matching at each boundary. Each connected component of selected links is a chain: one cannot leave a node along two selected links in the same temporal direction, and a component cannot return to a layer after moving past it. It therefore contains at most one node in each layer. Its connectedness classes give a consistent restricted `S` relation. This is a general graph argument; the finite verification is a check, not its proof.

In the single-edge case M selects `u→a`; in the symmetric fork it selects neither edge. Under the **closed-world** interpretation this means the predecessor person ceases and two later persons begin, even though both later stages can retain R-relations to it. Under an epistemically modest interpretation it only says that M did not warrant either identity. These must not be conflated. The target includes all potentially relevant continuers and predecessors; missing an edge can change the answer. The rule is conservative and extremely sensitive to whether weak or spurious edges are included.

### Rule G: unique maximum matching

Enumerate the one-to-one matchings of a boundary's R-graph and choose the maximum-cardinality matching **only when it is unique**. When there are several maxima, assert no specific links and mark the result unresolved; an empty output in the script is not a proof that no identity obtains. On a graph with no edges the unique maximum is the empty matching. This is a global bookkeeping convention, not a general solution to persistence, and does not say that the largest number of continuers really survive.

The rules select the same links on one edge and the symmetric fork, although M's closed-world interpretation and G's unresolved-tie interpretation differ. They select different links here:

```text
A → X, A → Y, B → Y
```

M selects no edge because every edge touches an endpoint with degree two. G selects `A→X, B→Y`, the unique maximum matching of size two. It treats the other edge as lineage without identity. The joint structure breaks the relevant choice symmetry; the graph does not thereby prove the two identity links. If implemented over several boundaries, G can also produce chains wherever its unique matchings are asserted, but treating a tie as lack of identity would require an extra premise.

This difference is useful: exact equivariance and strict one-to-one consistency **do not select a unique identity criterion**. The candidates have different counterfactual and explanatory commitments even on the same input. Neither establishes a real count, and neither supplies the intended phenomenal incidence `J`.

## 5. An overlapping-person alternative

An alternative discussed in the encyclopedia literature represents two different temporally extended persons as sharing a pre-branch stage while having distinct later stages. Schematically:

```text
person P = (u, a)       person Q = (u, b)       P ≠ Q
```

Here the two persons are distinct throughout in the sense of distinct extended objects; `u` is a common stage, not two objects that become one or one object numerically identical to two. Strict identity remains transitive. Our notation is a small illustrative representation of a stage-sharing response, not an exposition or endorsement of all of Lewis's theory. It also does not establish that every directed path is a person or that the whole is a set of paths.

In this option one local **field-token** `u` can be shared as a stage by two continuant persons. If thin field-subject `=u`, there is still one thin field-subject at that occurrence under that identity; the distinct extended persons are not themselves numerically identical to `u`. Saying there are two continuant persons occupying the common stage and one thin field-token counts **different categories**, not an arithmetical contradiction. It does, however, reject the restricted unique-person-per-stage premise of §2 and faces a substantive question about whether a single field may be a stage of two persons. If person and thin subject are instead stipulated to be the very same entity, this response cannot simply invoke multiple occupancy without revising that stipulation.

The option has costs: it makes the number of extended persons associated with an earlier stage depend on the later structure of the whole history, may multiply persons before any divergence, and requires principles selecting which extended aggregates are persons. Such whole-history dependence need not be a backwards causal influence; under a timeless account it can be a global individuation rule. But it is still a substantive metaphysical and interpretive cost, not something proved by relativity or plenitude. It does not determine what an earlier subject should expect, how probability is assigned, or what ethically matters.

## 6. Interface to evidence and to the four-spin comparison

The same fixed current physical state and action-unity statistics can be accompanied by different **stipulated** identity rules. That is underdetermination by those statistics, not a claim that complete physical or phenomenal facts are identical across all proposed accounts. Their total identity facts can differ, and each rule may require different additional facts. Neither exact two-input control nor retention of records automatically supplies phenomenal ownership, a persisting person or a calibrated numerical-identity assay.

A responsible empirical comparison would first specify local first-person contrasts and independently measured candidate physical/psychological continuation, define the competing rules before analysis, model memory/attention/report failures, and identify a discriminating observation if one exists. Even an excellent report of continuity is evidence, not automatically a metaphysical criterion. Where rules predict the same distributions of all specified observations, no statistical test of those observations can choose between them. No human intervention or new clinical result is proposed here.

Four-spin L and C remain physical boundary candidates, not demonstrated fields. The presence or absence of a second L output, an `R`-edge, or a connected physical graph cannot be substituted for the missing physical-to-phenomenal bridge. Nor does a first-person field's local givenness by itself decide the person-level persistence rule. Qualitative law, occurrence individuation, realization, persistence and the self-sufficiency of the whole all remain open.

```sh
PYTHONPATH=verification python -m unittest field_lineage_persistence_gate_test
```

The [script](verification/field_lineage_persistence_gate.py) exhausts the 16 bipartite two-by-two graphs for both rule implementations and all independent endpoint relabelings; verifies the single-edge, symmetric fork and merge, and asymmetric examples; and checks all **256** three-stage two-slot histories under **2,048** stagewise relabelings for M-class consistency and covariance. The [receipt](verification/field_lineage_persistence_gate.json) records the outputs. Three focused tests include a transitive chain and the case on which M and G differ. Nothing in these checks supplies experiential stages, proves a metaphysical possibility, establishes a human identity or demonstrates a correct persistence rule.

C3 continues to mean **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7. B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open; this formal exercise is not an empirical or substantive framework pass.
