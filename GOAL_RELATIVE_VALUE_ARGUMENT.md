# Goal-relative evaluation: the rigorous result and its limits

## The proposed argument

1. Good/bad are meaningful only relative to goals and circumstances.
2. Objective badness would require badness for every possible goal.
3. Anything bad for one goal is good for its opposite.
4. Therefore nothing is universally bad, and badness is incoherent or hollow.

The argument contains a sound conditional obstruction to unrestricted goal-independent instrumental rankings. Its conclusion about the incoherence of badness is stronger than that obstruction warrants.

## A precise conditional theorem

Fix circumstances c, feasible outcomes X, and admissible goal-evaluation functions U. Take u(x;c) > u(y;c) to mean that x is better than y for goal u in circumstances c.

Assume U is nonempty and closed under sign reversal: if u is admissible, so is -u. Define x as strictly worse than y for every goal iff

    for every u in U: u(x;c) < u(y;c).

No pair x,y satisfies this condition. Select u in U. The condition requires u(x;c) < u(y;c), but applying it to -u requires -u(x;c) < -u(y;c), equivalently u(x;c) > u(y;c). Contradiction.

The same proof rules out being strictly better for every goal. The result is symmetric; unrestricted reversal prevents universally strict instrumental Good as well as universally strict instrumental Bad.

The proof uses goal evaluators rather than assuming all goals have numerical representations. An equivalent order-theoretic formulation assumes every admissible strict preference ordering has an admissible reversed ordering. Neither formulation establishes that every normative evaluation must have this structure or that every reversed goal is normatively authoritative.

For weak rankings, care is needed: universal weak disadvantage under sign reversal forces equality for every evaluator, rather than a strictly negative ranking. A goal that is indifferent to both outcomes also blocks universal strict disadvantage. Whether a goal promotes failure of another is exact only where the goal representation really reverses the relevant ordering; ordinary-language “opposites” need not supply such a reversal.

## What follows

There is no universally strict instrumental ranking across an unrestricted reversal-closed goal domain. Merely selecting one goal cannot establish its unconditional normative authority.

This is an important constraint on axiarchism: a foundation cannot simply stipulate flourishing, complexity, persistence, coherence, or satisfaction as its supreme goal and call the resulting ranking objective without explaining that goal's authority.

## What does not follow

### Relational evaluation is not incoherent

The predicate “x is bad for goal g in circumstances c relative to alternative y” is perfectly consistent. Its truth can be objective in the sense that it is determined by the relevant facts, not by a speaker's verdict. A poison can undermine survival while advancing an aim of killing. These are compatible relational facts, not contradictory assertions about the same fully specified predicate.

Lacking an unconditional unary predicate does not invalidate a conditional relational predicate. Nor does failure of universal strict ranking make the latter meaningless.

### Objectivity is not identical to usefulness for every possible goal

The proposed second premise is a particular definition, not a logical consequence of the word “objective.” At least three issues must be distinguished:

- objectively true instrumental relations once a goal is specified;
- endorsement or usefulness across every possible goal;
- stance-independent normative reasons, including possible reasons to reject a goal.

The theorem addresses the second. It does not refute the first. It does not settle whether the third exists: a normative realist disputes that every possible goal is an equally authoritative standard, rather than claiming that every person must find harm disadvantageous to their chosen project.

Rejecting that realist position may be reasonable, but doing so needs an argument beyond the availability of an opposing goal. Conversely, merely asserting privileged goals does not establish realism.

### The first premise also has scope conditions

Instrumental evaluations require an end or standard and relevant circumstances. Extending that analysis to *all* normative and phenomenal evaluations is substantive. For example, whether suffering's aversive character supplies a reason independently of an adopted project is precisely disputed. Neither side is established simply by giving survival and killing as opposed goals.

### The result does not establish privation theory

Goal-relativity alone does not show that badness is a deprivation of positive Good. It is compatible with many theories of value, including accounts on which neither Good nor Bad is a fundamental actualizer. If unrestricted goal-reversal defeats universally strict Bad, it defeats universally strict Good in the same way. An asymmetry favoring ultimate Good therefore needs another premise.

## Consequence for the whole-account

The privation-based axiarchic extension remains a conjecture, with a sharper statement of its debt. It must explain what makes a constitutive fulfillment normatively authoritative, rather than merely successful relative to one adopted goal. It must also explain why this value is relevant to actuality rather than solely to evaluation.

Possible research routes include arguing for constitutive standards of agency or experience, independently grounded reasons concerning affected perspectives, or a necessary identity between full intelligibility and value. These are routes to examine, not established escapes from the objection. Consistency alone does not select benevolence over coherently pursued destruction.

If the user's premises are adopted as definitions of all value and objectivity, the appropriate conclusion is that neither Good nor Bad can supply a universally strict goal-neutral instrumental ranking. The premises cannot simultaneously establish that Bad is hollow while leaving an unrestricted universally Good foundation untouched.

## Verdict

The rigorous result is **no universally strict instrumental ranking across all reversal-closed goals**. The stronger conclusion **badness is an incoherent concept** does not follow. The result is nevertheless a substantive challenge to any axiarchic theory that leaves its normative standard unexplained.

This is a conceptual proof, not an empirical finding; no executable research result or new test count is claimed.

## Clarification: unqualified badness and goodness of being

The user's intended second premise concerns **bad without qualification**, not merely the word objective: unqualified badness would have to hold across every possible goal. Accepting that stipulation, the reversal argument excludes universally strict instrumental badness. It equally excludes universally strict instrumental goodness. Thus “everything actual is Good” cannot mean that everything serves every possible goal.

A different, coherent candidate thesis is **goodness of being**: every actual being is good insofar as it has positive actuality or realizes capacities, while its badness concerns missing or disordered fulfillment. The relevant contrast is not two competing substances. But this is a further constitutive/metaphysical hypothesis, not a consequence of universal goal quantification. If Good simply names actuality, the identity is definitional and has not yet established normative value or explained existence. If actuality has intrinsic value, that stronger identity needs justification.

The thesis must distinguish actuality as such from moral goodness of each event. A harmful act can realize powers while destroying another being's capacities. Its actual occurrence does not vindicate it. Privative badness still needs a standard of what is missing or disordered, and the framework must account for actual aversive experience without treating suffering itself as an absence of experience.

The strongest charitable formulation is: positive actuality is fundamental; badness is a defect in how actual beings and their relations are fulfilled, not an independent opposing substance. This is an intelligible conjecture compatible with the whole-account, but it does not yet entail that normative Good actualizes reality.
