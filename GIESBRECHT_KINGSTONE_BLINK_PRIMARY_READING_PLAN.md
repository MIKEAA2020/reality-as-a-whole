# Fixed plan: earlier split-brain attentional-blink experiment

## Source discovery and known findings

After the [Ptito methods follow-up](PTITO_THESIS_ATTENTIONAL_BLINK_METHODS_FOLLOWUP.md), a targeted search for fuller primary studies found the UCSB Attention Lab's [publications page](https://attentionlab.psych.ucsb.edu/publications), which links a copy of Giesbrecht and Kingstone's 2004 paper, *Right hemisphere involvement in the attentional blink: Evidence from a split-brain patient*, *Brain and Cognition* 55, 303–306. Search excerpts describe one split-brain patient and an asymmetry with a more pronounced blink for right-hemisphere targets; Ptito's accessible introduction says the earlier two-stream design confounded same/different stream with within/across hemifield comparisons. Those findings are already known. The lab offers the paper as a scientific courtesy but says files may not be reposted without permission: cite its URL rather than store the paper in the repository.

## Procedure fixed before reading the paper

1. Read the laboratory-hosted four-page primary paper in full if the document-fetch service permits. Record access scope, text or figure limitations, and exact source locations. Do not claim raw-data access, full figure inspection or independently checked numbers when unavailable.
2. Extract task timing, target/distractor and response design, actual comparison groups, anatomy, controls, analytical definition and patient-specific results, including available uncertainty. Distinguish authors' anatomical/functional inference from actual observations and note untested alternatives.
3. Compare with Ptito's four-stream count-report protocol on matched contrasts and the particular stream confound. A later study with a different patient may be a targeted behavioral extension, not automatically an independent or exact replication of the same task, mechanism or experiential conclusion. Avoid patient identity inferences from initials alone.
4. Return to Lopez's proposed experienced centrality rather than replacing it with shared computational resources. Distinguish first-person experience, agent, reporter, subject and physical subsystem; temporal target report from same-window conjoint content; and subject incidence from qualitative character. Report what could change an inference and what remains unidentifiable.
5. If results or methods are inaccessible, say so and stop at available evidence. Do not create a new synthetic regression or assert that brain patients instantiate the toy. Do not infer inaccessible experience is absent. C3, B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open absent genuinely new evidence.
