# Participatory realism and intelligibility from within the whole

## Result and current target

Jenann Ismael's [*A Participatory Universe in the Realist Mode: On the Separation of Observational and Agentive Perspectives in Classical and Quantum Mechanics*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-025-00843-4) offers an important challenge to any picture in which a knower stands outside the reality it knows. It does **not** show that reality is unreal, that minds create the universe, or that the whole must itself be a universal agent or subject. The paper explicitly seeks an objective realist account of participation.

For our candidate the productive distinction is between:

1. **the fact that reality contains local participants and their physical records;**
2. **the restricted questions a local participant can answer while its answer is itself part of the physical situation;** and
3. **the constitutive intelligibility of the whole, including all genuine experience, relations and local limitations.**

A limit on (2) is not automatically a limit on (3). Conversely, declaring (3) does not give an embedded subject unrestricted knowledge or solve the physical and experiential bridges. The user's C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7. This is not permission to set aside the character of experience, subject constitution, or unanswered physics as 'unintelligible by definition'.

## 1. What Ismael argues—and what it does not entail

Ismael calls the dependence between a representation and an event it represents **'Interference'**, expressly distinguishing that term from *quantum interference* (p. 2, note 1). Her examples include a self-defeating yes/no display, a person's guesses influencing another person's purchase, public forecasts changing the outcomes forecast, and self-fulfilling predictions (pp. 2–5). These are not all the same mechanism. The display example concerns truth conditions and the physical act of answering; the guessing example depends on an actual causal response. In neither is ordinary semantic self-reference enough to prove the non-existence of the surrounding reality.

Her proposal is that classical conditions often permit robust records of the environment and past even though some predictions can affect the future, whereas quantum experiments bring a more pervasive dependence on the chosen interaction into view (pp. 4–11). She explicitly stresses that a relativistic picture need not involve objective temporal unfolding, and associates the familiar temporal asymmetry with thermodynamics (p. 10). That claim indicates a research direction, not a derivation of thermodynamic asymmetry or local time in our model.

She discusses Everett, relational quantum mechanics and retrocausal interpretations as examples, while setting aside certain other theories for her particular argument (pp. 7, 12–14). Their different ontologies must not be combined as if the paper proves they are equivalent. The Everett discussion treats observers as emergent in approximately classical histories; it does not demonstrate that decoherence by itself explains a particular felt quality, uniquely individuates subjects, or derives the Born measure. Ismael herself says an ontological account must show how subjects arise and experience is recovered (p. 12). Our stronger conclusion that this remains an unresolved experiential-constitution task is a project assessment, not a claim that she offers a complete consciousness theory.

**Do not universalize the classical contrast.** Many classical measurements disturb their surroundings and public predictions can alter present systems; local action's effects need not be confined to a verbally defined 'future' without a specified causal and temporal model. There can also be useful quantum measurements, including repeatable measurements of a prepared eigenstate. The distinction between observational and agentive attitudes is informative but is not a theorem that no quantum measurement can reveal any pre-existing property.

## 2. A fixed-history check of embedded self-prediction

Ismael's self-defeating example is an instance of a well-known diagonal limitation, not a new theorem. Compare Wolpert's [*Physical limits of inference*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2008.03.040). The following elementary application uses a closed set of local record relations, not a temporal actualization process and not an argument that a timeless whole requires a universal subject.

Let `r∈{0,1}` be a public local forecast of a later local binary record `x`. Suppose the actual response rule is `x=1−r`; the forecasting device is required to announce `r=x` on that same run. The two physical record pairs allowed by the response rule are `(r,x)=(0,1)` and `(1,0)`. **Both are definite and internally consistent records**, but neither satisfies the proposed accuracy requirement. Equivalently, the combined conditions `x=1−r` and `r=x` have no binary solution.

This has four distinct consequences:

- The particular requirement of a correct, publicly disclosed binary forecast in this counteracting protocol is impossible. The target event itself is definite in each permitted history; it is not an undefined liar sentence. A different device can observe the completed pair and truthfully report it.
- If the system instead uses `x=r`, both `(0,0)` and `(1,1)` are consistent: self-reference does not by itself forbid correct prediction. If the forecast is concealed from the responding mechanism, or the response rule is not counteracting, the first no-go does not follow.
- A forecaster allowed to abstain is not a total binary forecaster; a probabilistic forecast, inaccessible forecast, or an alternative instrument is a different task. A distribution over the two consistent record pairs is also not a correct announced binary forecast on each pair.
- A timeless description may represent the ordered records and their dependence all at once. An ordered record relation is not a fundamental external clock, a process creating a possible outcome, or by itself a proof that such an ordering emerges from the proposed whole. The physical response rule must still be derived or modeled.

Thus requiring **one local participant to deliver an infallible actionable binary forecast of every event including adversarial responses to its own forecasts** adds an inconsistent demand in this protocol. This conditional constraint does not prove that the whole is self-sufficient, that all truths are locally knowable, or that explanatory completeness is impossible. For knowability and the user's global possibility/actuality thesis, see the distinct [knowability gate](KNOWABILITY_AND_ACTUALITY_FOUNDATION_GATE.md): its modal conclusions hold only under its explicit scope and epistemic premises. The Knower-style fixed point in [the historical self-knowledge note](COMPLETE_SELF_KNOWLEDGE.md) is a different semantic argument; neither requires adopting that note's superseded universal-knower proposal.

## 3. Quantum participation: an already worked physical interface

The existing [timeless joint-event gate](TIMELESS_CLASS_OPERATORS_DECOHERENCE_AND_JOINT_EVENTS.md) provides a sharper application than simply saying 'observation creates reality'. In its explicitly specified constrained three-dimensional example, `P` and `Q` commute with the constraint but not with each other. Without a prior `P` measurement, the `Q=yes` probability is `1/5`. Insert an actual unread projective `P` measurement, and the same `Q` probability is `1/2`. The procedures have different physical instruments and yield different predictions; the second is not a passive revelation of the first experiment's statistics. Its symmetric joint-event candidate also has a negative linear expectation, so a constraint-invariant formula is not automatically a joint probability. This is an ideal finite control, not a claim that all measurement necessarily disturbs all properties, or that the model represents real experience.

The [contextual plenitude gate](CONTEXTUAL_PLENITUDE_AND_RECORD_IDENTITY_GATE.md) adds the distinct Peres–Mermin condition: the ideal six context correlations cannot be reproduced by the particular single context-independent value assignment. Separate context-indexed records avoid that logical conflict, but do not establish that these records are genuinely possible, physically actual, or experiential. Giving all genuine possibilities actuality **within** the whole does not license inventing one unperformed same-token joint record. Nor does it derive probabilities. The [quantum-eraser and Bell gate](QUANTUM_ERASER_BELL_AND_SELECTION_MEASURE_GATE.md) separately distinguishes conditioning on outcomes from changing an unconditional distant marginal.

These are known measurement, histories and contextuality results, cited in the linked reports. The project-specific gain is their **joint constraint on how a plural, timeless whole could be interrogated from within**, not a claim to have discovered those results or solved the measurement problem.

## 4. Accuracy about the no-go results

Ismael's discussion usefully warns against assuming that all quantum observables have context-independent, pre-existing values, but several statements require tighter scope:

- **Kochen–Specker:** the contradiction uses specified functional/compatibility and noncontextual value-assignment assumptions in suitable Hilbert spaces. It does not forbid every hidden-variable model, all pre-existing properties, or an outcome-independent observer in every context. The paper's p. 7 refers to the independence assumption as 'contextuality'; the independence assumption is normally called **noncontextuality**.
- **Bell:** standard CHSH excludes a combination of Bell-local factorization, a setting-independent source distribution and other protocol assumptions, not 'realism' alone. Contextual or nonlocal theories and approaches revising other assumptions must be assessed on their own terms. The earlier [Bell gate](ACTUALITY_CAUSAL_INFERENCE_AND_BELL.md) states the required assumptions and empirical limits.
- **Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph:** [*On the reality of the quantum state*](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys2309) is not a blanket exclusion of all preparation-independent hidden-variable theories. In the stated ontological-model setting, it constrains overlap of distributions for distinct pure quantum preparations, under preparation independence and the theorem's other assumptions, against quantum predictions. The publisher's abstract was read; the full theorem and figures were not inspected here. It does not prove experiential realism or a particular whole ontology.
- **Bohm and retrocausality:** Ismael's footnote places Bohmian mechanics both under supplemented ontology and altered dynamics. In the standard nonrelativistic formulation, Bohmian mechanics supplements the usual Schrödinger wave evolution with actual configurations and a guiding law; this differs from modifying the Schrödinger equation as in collapse theories. This does not endorse Bohmian mechanics or resolve its nonlocality/relativistic extension. [Price's *Does Time-Symmetry Imply Retrocausality? How the Quantum World Says “Maybe”?*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.shpsb.2011.12.003) explicitly makes the argument conditional on further ontological assumptions; time symmetry alone is not a general retrocausality theorem. Only abstracts and selected descriptions of these sources were consulted, not their complete arguments.

There are bibliographic errors in the supplied paper's reference list: its item 6 is Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph although page 7 also uses `[6]` for Bell; item 14 credits Everett with the title and citation of Pusey–Barrett–Rudolph. Its correct Everett reference is item 13. These errors do not decide the philosophical claims, but readers should not use the mistaken entries to identify sources.

## 5. Integration and unresolved obligations

- **B1–B2:** participation doesn't prove self-existence or the strongest modal thesis. The counterprediction case concerns a particular inconsistent operational demand, not the impossibility of actual experience or the incoherence of strongest nothingness. Why the self-sufficient experiential whole has its proposed constitution remains open.
- **B4–B5:** Ismael's nested, locally situated participant is compatible with genuinely plural experiential perspectives. Neither embeddedness, decoherence, causal interaction nor a useful physical record supplies the subject boundary, identity, phenomenal quality, or the experiential ground. No universal consciousness follows.
- **B6–B8:** candidate instruments must respect quantum contexts, reference matching and joint-event consistency. Relations between records do not derive local clocks, emergent spacetime, gravity or a continuum limit. Existing unitary, histories and Bell gates are constraints, not a common-sector recovery.
- **B9:** a record distribution is not yet a justified subject reference class or measure.
- **B10–B12:** subjective reports remain valid evidence of experience, with communication and protocol errors separately modeled. Comparison of experience-respecting alternatives requires more than a label such as 'participatory'; parsimony must count the missing mappings. The data and assumptions needed for a decisive discriminating experiment have not been supplied here.

The old files [Complete self-knowledge](COMPLETE_SELF_KNOWLEDGE.md) and [Complete intelligibility: grounded determination](COMPLETE_INTELLIGIBILITY_ARGUMENT_AUDIT.md) reflect an earlier perfect, universal-knower target. Their formal arguments can be assessed on their stated assumptions, but those framing claims are **superseded** by the user's current non-perfect, plural-subject target; they are not governing commitments of this report.
