# Exploratory mixed-report transition and EEG-window diagnostic plan

This plan follows already observed S01, S09 and S02 results, including the failed S02 transfer. None of these participants is untouched, and this is not a prospective preregistration or a new holdout. Its purpose is to investigate coverage and potential confounding, **not** to rescue or refit the previously frozen model, choose a favorable score, or establish experiential truth. Do not request a new participant unless an explicitly specified and credible candidate emerges.

Use only the previously analyzed rivalry scans: S01 1,3,5,7,10,12; S02 1,2,4,6,8,11; S09 1,2,3,5,7. Re-fetch previously supplied files if necessary; verify each against the previously committed Dropbox content hash/size or SHA-256 before use, retain only in ignored private cache, and write only aggregated results. Keep the prior 250 Hz, five assumed 42-second trial, code-state carry-forward, 0.2-second center grid and 2-second POz Hann-tapered feature. Marker timing and code-to-percept interpretation remain provisional.

## Diagnostics

1. **Window context and artifact coverage.** For every grid center labeled mixed, report separately by participant how often its 2-second window has an artifact, and among clean windows the mean and median fraction of samples carrying mixed report, unknown, and each pure code. Also calculate the fraction of squared Hann taper weight on samples carrying mixed report. The latter describes temporal weights, **not an additive attribution of spectral power**, since Fourier amplitudes interfere and the signal is mean-centered. Report analogous artifact fractions for pure centers. A centered label need not describe the rest of the window.
2. **Matched nonmixed transition controls.** Build contiguous reconstructed code-3 segments. For each segment choose the valid 0.2-second grid center closest to its midpoint (earlier center on ties); exclude segments with no grid center, inadequate two-second trial margins, or an artifact-marked feature window, and count exclusions. Candidate controls are nonmixed grid centers with at most 0.2 seconds distance from a *change* into or out of a mixed segment, with valid clean two-second windows. For each eligible mixed segment in chronological order within its trial, choose the unused control in that same trial nearest in time, provided separation is **at least 2 seconds** (no EEG-sample overlap within the pair) and **at most 6 seconds**; break equal-distance ties by earlier control center. Report unmatched counts, matching distance, control code, pair-level state composition and before/after imbalance. Each mixed segment and each control are used at most once, but windows can overlap between different pairs and matching is greedy, not optimal. The requirement deliberately selects some segments and not others. No match on transition direction, duration, elapsed time or stimulus/eye assignment is claimed; same trial and proximity do not eliminate confounding.
3. **Descriptive EEG contrast.** For each participant, summarize mixed-minus-control POz low/high-tag log10-power ratio at matched pairs (mean, median and proportion positive), plus low and high tag power relative to the mean of their ±1-Hz flanking powers in dB for each side of the pairs. Flank power is a rough local spectral contrast, not validated signal-to-noise, causal specificity, or proof of a reliable SSVEP. No p-values or center-level confidence intervals; event windows remain dependent and three people are not population data. The contrast is continuous, not a fitted classifier or evidence of prediction. Report individual participants rather than pool them as independent observations.
4. **Timing sensitivity as a diagnostic.** For each paired mixed and control window, compare the center report code against the report state 0.2 seconds earlier and 0.2 seconds later (using within-trial samples). Report proportions unchanged; this describes *label sensitivity to assumed timing* only. Do not shift the EEG, refit, choose a lag, or claim these offsets estimate actual response latency.

Synthetic tests must check grid and boundary cases, midpoint selection, unknown labels, artifact rejection, no-reuse and distance constraints, context weighting, spectral calculations and timing sensitivity. Compare independently counted coverage with the prior S02 receipt. All results should be presented even when matching or coverage is poor. Do not infer that an unchanged or changed tag ratio explains the failure of the three-state decoder, whether a mixed percept occurred, or that any observation is a new validation. If a new model is contemplated later, it needs a separate written design, explicit held-out participants and safeguards against tuning on these three people.
