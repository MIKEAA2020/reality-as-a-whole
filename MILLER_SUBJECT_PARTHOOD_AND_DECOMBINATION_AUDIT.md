# Miller on subject-parts: what relativized unity does and does not explain

## The result for this project

The [subsequent bonding and subject-composition audit](PHENOMENAL_BONDING_SUBJECT_COMPOSITION_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md) reads the Miller–Siddharth exchange and examines Mørch's draft objection to subject-indexing. It distinguishes an introspective conditional interpretation from independent evidence for shared tokens or subject-parts.

Gregory Miller's supplied paper identifies a serious challenge for a **cosmic subject containing other subjects as proper parts**: how can the whole have one unified experience while its parts are bounded subjects? He proposes indexing unity and boundedness to different subjects. In that formulation, a local subject can be bounded **for itself** even though a larger subject unifies some of the very same experiences **for the larger subject**.

That is an important prior proposal, not a solution discovered by this project. It offers a coherent *response to a particular incompatibility argument*, conditional on its assumptions. It does not establish that such overlapping subjects exist, explain which subjects or qualities are constituted, or select a boundary law. Conversely, the present E-L candidate proposes an experiential ground without asserting a single cosmic subject: Miller's specific cosmic-subject reductio is therefore not automatically a reductio of E-L. E-L still owes an intelligible account of experiential grounding and plural subject incidence. Avoiding one premise is not solving those debts.

## 1. The argument, with its premises exposed

Miller distinguishes **priority monism** (one basic whole, while parts can exist) from **existence monism** (only one object exists), and assumes a part–whole ordering and a distinct dependence ordering (pp. 3–4). His stated version of cosmopsychism makes the cosmos a conscious subject on which other subjects depend (pp. 4–5). In the explicit reductio, premise 1 says the cosmos is a subject-whole and macro-subjects are its **subject-proper parts** (p. 9). Mere dependence on a whole does not, without an additional account, by itself establish phenomenal subject-parthood or shared experience-token identity.

His initial definitions are synchronic (pp. 6–7): experiences are **unified** when there is something it is like to have them together, and **bound** when they are unified and not unified with any experience outside the set. He describes their consequence as the **unity/boundedness inconsistency thesis (UBIT)**: unity cannot extend beyond a bound field, nor can such a boundary occur within a unified field. His **subject essence thesis (SET)** says subjects are essentially phenomenally unified and bound. He treats the combination and de-combination problems as the same structural problem when both posit subject–subject proper parthood (pp. 5–9).

The tension follows when one imports the same *unindexed*, exclusionary boundary into both uses: a part's bound field cannot extend into the whole's conjoint field, while a whole's unified field must contain the part's contents and more. That is a serious objection **if** the putative subjects really stand in that parthood/token-inclusion relation and the unindexed boundaries are obligatory. The argument does not by itself decide whether all grounding relations have this form, whether the premises are true, or whether all versions of subject combination have only this problem. In particular, the paper's claim that a failure to answer combination implies a failure to answer de-combination is an argued philosophical generalization, not established for every account of combination or every form of cosmopsychism.

## 2. Miller's preferred response—not merely different words for unity

In §4 (pp. 10–14), Miller preserves SET but modifies UBIT by indexing unity and boundary to a subject. His **relative** unity/boundary concerns a subject-part; his **absolute** unity/boundary concerns a subject-whole. “Absolute” in this argument does not automatically mean the cosmos: footnote 28 allows other subject-wholes, including nested ones. For a chosen part–whole comparison, an experience outside the part's field need not be unified **for the part**, while it can be unified with the part's experience **for the whole**. Different subject indices matter: a flat, unindexed no-extension rule would reject the very arrangement the revision allows.

On p. 9, footnote 23, Miller explicitly ties parthood to overlap and **sharing of token experiences**. In footnote 31 (p. 14), he says the unity relation itself is numerically identical, rather than doubled, though the part does not experience all of it. It would misrepresent his proposal to substitute two qualitatively similar but numerically different tokens or two independent unity relations without marking that as a different view. His p. 12 example has the local subject's experiences as a proper subset of the whole subject's experiences. The indexing formulation removes the *stated* cross-subject clash; it does not independently show that the same token can be owned by two distinct subjects, or that those subjects exist.

Miller also argues that relative unity cannot obtain without the corresponding absolute unity (p. 13), and offers two principles against an ad hoc objection: unity is for a subject, and a subject does not experience a unity between contents it does not have (footnote 30). His no-relative-without-absolute reasoning uses the stipulated subject-part/subject-whole setting and its picture of the relevant fields. It should not be transferred to an arbitrary non-subject ground or to comparisons between unrelated subjects without establishing the requisite parent and content relations. Nor do subject-indexed definitions on their own supply a rule selecting actual nested subjects.

In §§5–6 (pp. 14–18), he considers modifying SET instead: a *scattered* subject can lack a unified total field, or an *unbound* subject's field can extend beyond the boundary in the original sense. He favors relativizing UBIT as applicable to both orientations and more respectful of ordinary unity intuitions. His appeal to the inability to sympathetically imagine a scattered subject is a substantive philosophical argument, not an empirical proof that all alternative subject ontologies are impossible; footnote 37 explicitly says this objection does not apply to allowing phenomenal unboundedness. His discussion of split-brain alternatives (footnote 32) does not supply a clinical observation of nested subjects.

## 3. The same four-spin interface: what the distinction changes

We can state the contrast without inventing a new physical model. Use the existing `t=1/4` four-spin kernel, original one-update L rule, and stipulated left/right unpleasantness contrast. L selects the two outer pairs in that query; neither pair is an independently identified actual subject. Let `A` and `B` be two hypothetical, numerically distinct phenomenal aspect tokens associated with the left pair, and `C` and `D` two for the right. Their relation to the two mismatch bits is **additional stipulation**, not a measurement or a derivation of the phenomenal aspects. If needed, let each pair's q-labeled aspect have the original proposed q-dependent quality, leaving the other aspect's character unspecified. Do not assume that these four symbols exhaust actual experience.

Consider two *research descriptions*, not a claim that either is genuinely possible under the user's strong global modal thesis:

| Package | Stipulated subject fields on this common physical model | What is additionally asserted |
|---|---|---|
| **Local-only L** | `L = {A,B}`, `R = {C,D}` | L and R are bounded subjects with their own conjunctions; no further encompassing subject is asserted in this explicitly restricted comparison. |
| **Nested N** | `L = {A,B}`, `R = {C,D}`, `W = {A,B,C,D}` | L and R remain bounded relative to themselves; W is a distinct subject with an encompassing conjoint experience, and L/R are its subject-parts. The **same tokens**, not copies, appear in the nested fields. |

In N, the left field is not bounded **absolutely in the old unindexed sense**, because `A,B` also participate in W's larger unity. It is bounded **for L**, because L is not stipulated to have C or D. There is no contradiction between those differently indexed claims under Miller's revised terms. The table represents W's subject-parthood and unity as **extra assumptions**: set inclusion alone does not make a set an experiencer, establish a proper-parthood relation between subjects, or produce phenomenal togetherness. The model-level W is a four-spin aggregate, **not** an assertion of a cosmic mind. Nor is N simply the original exclusive L incidence rule; it is a deliberately expanded competing package.

Both packages may be assigned the same physical kernel, the same q outputs and the same L/R report predictions by construction, if the observable interface is restricted to those local reports. They make different stipulated claims about whether W is a further subject. This is a **conditional matched-interface comparison**, not a proof that all observable consequences must coincide: a justified report or action law for W, or a genuinely identity-sensitive assay, might distinguish a more complete pair of theories. Merely demanding that W never report is an added restriction, not evidence it cannot. The earlier [shared-token gate](SHARED_EXPERIENCE_VERSUS_DUPLICATED_TOKENS_GATE.md) separately explains why identical local physical/report histories do not by themselves settle shared versus coordinated duplicate tokens. Our N shares tokens as Miller does; its local-only comparison does not establish duplication.

## 4. Three distinct targets and remaining debts

1. **Nested cosmic subject.** Miller's argument and preferred revision apply most directly if the cosmos is a subject and local subjects really are subject-parts with shared token experiences. Even with his revision, one must justify the whole's conjoint experience, the parthood and token sharing, and the content of each subject's point of view. Relativizing a boundary is not a derivation of any of these.
2. **Experiential ground, no assumed universal subject.** E-L proposes an intrinsically experiential whole but does not identify it with a single subject-field or postulate that local subjects are subject-parts of a cosmic subject. The project must still explain how “experiential ground” is intelligible without silently positing a subject of an unowned experience, and how real local experiences and their subjects are grounded in it. Possible interpretations include that the intrinsic nature grounds subjects relationally rather than consisting of a freestanding subjectless episode; this is a proposal, not a supplied constitution. Actual local experience is not denied or downgraded to a mere report.
3. **Plural basic subjects or another experience-respecting ground.** A theory can begin with plural perspectives or with a neutral ground that genuinely constitutes experience. It then owes its own selection, constitution, qualitative and physical bridges. These are serious competitors, not assumed false because they lack a cosmic mind. A commitment that every genuinely possible reality is actual within the timeless whole does not establish which competing account is genuinely possible or settle its explanatory direction.

Miller's footnote 25 argues that submergence is incoherent on his priority-monist picture because fixing the whole's complete intrinsic nature fixes its parts and their relations. That is his **substantive whole-fixation assumption**, not an independently proved account of the parts' natures. Even if a sufficiently rich complete whole-description fixes all local facts, mere necessitation does not tell us why a given field is subjectively unified, determine the direction of metaphysical grounding, or yield a physically testable mapping. The same warning applies to E-L: “the ground contains the answer” is not the missing answer.

At the operational level, a proposed test has to distinguish *subject-indexed* joint experience from the existential claim that **some** field unifies certain aspects. The [randomized pair–triple gate](TRIPLE_UNITY_RANDOMIZED_REPORT_PARTITION_GATE.md) explicitly warns that these targets differ. Its exclusive-partition inequality applies to a declared token partition with calibrated existential incidence, not automatically to Miller's nested, token-sharing subjects or to a single person's reports. Nothing in the present paper validates those assays or justifies extra invasive intervention.

## 5. Consequence for the battery

The constructive gain is a clear fork: the framework can investigate a nested-subject account using Miller's revised subject-relative boundaries, or retain a non-universal-subject ground and address its own non-circular constitution problem. Neither route is chosen by the four-spin dynamics or by the supplied paper. A real next advance needs an independently motivated constitution law or condition-valid experiential evidence capable of discriminating completed rival packages, including their subject identity, token identity, report and quality maps.

C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7; actual experience is valid data. B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open. No subject count, unique ontology, subject measure, felt-valence law, recovered physical theory or full battery pass follows from this audit.
