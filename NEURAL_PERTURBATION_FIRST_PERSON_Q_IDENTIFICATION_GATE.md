# Neural perturbation and first-person reports: does this identify `q`?

## Decision

**Stop the proposed mismatch-versus-unpleasantness comparison at the identification gate.** We found relevant perturbation studies, including an open iEEG-and-mood release and a thalamic-stimulation study reporting unpleasant experiences. None of the materials screened establishes a report-independent measurement of the interaction preference required by this project's `q`, nor a verified same-interaction, same-episode link to a direct unpleasantness rating. This is a bounded search, not proof that no suitable dataset exists.

Our fixed-before-row-inspection [metadata plan](RAO_IEEG_STIMULATION_SOURCE_READINESS_FIXED_PLAN.md) and [aggregate receipt](verification/rao_stim_metadata_readiness.json) give a concrete reason not to promote the strongest open candidate into a `q` test. In the pinned Rao conversion, **40 continuous-stimulation recording files from 15 people and five single-pulse recording files from five people share only three people**. Each of the 45 events files contains only one documented whole-recording-span row, not an annotated stimulation, pulse, or report event. Numerical BIDS onsets and durations exist but refer to recording spans; they are not evidence of when stimulation began or when a person reported mood. The three-person overlap is not established same-site, same-block, same-episode or sham pairing.

This does not mean raw artifacts cannot be used to reconstruct timing, that there was no sham, or that behavioral pairing is impossible. It means **we did not establish them**. We inspected neither signal values nor behavioral outcome tables, and did not run a predictive comparison. The paper's results are already published, so even a future fixed retrospective analysis could not honestly be described as prospective or blinded to all prior results. More importantly, valid timing and mood pairing alone would still not identify `q`.

## 1. What we would have to identify

The specific existing [four-spin physical gate](FOUR_SPIN_PHYSICAL_MISMATCH_MEASUREMENT_GATE.md) defines, for a designated edge, `q = (1 − τ s₀s₁)/2`: `s₀s₁` is the current relation and `τ` is the **preferred** relation. Its net-edge and separately designated target-channel versions are different estimands. A human-neural translation first needs an independently specified physical system, interaction target and variables; electrode voltages cannot simply be substituted for the toy spins. The field or episode claimed to underlie an experience is also not given by electrode placement.

An explicit *hypothetical* target for a future design could be a sign preference `τ_AB` for the **net effective interaction** between two physically defined, locally measurable state variables A and B, on a prespecified timescale. If and only if a separately validated binary interaction model is appropriate, define `x_AB = s_A s_B` and `q_AB = (1 − τ_AB x_AB)/2`. Calibrate the model and `τ_AB` on report-independent data or perturbations, with held-out physical checks and error bounds; transport sign conventions consistently. In a nonstationary, nonlinear, hidden-input neural system, a toy equilibrium cross-ratio is not automatically valid. Close-to-zero interactions may leave a preference unidentified; propagate an identified set rather than force a sign. `q_AB` is a proposed *physical* variable, not a measurement of feeling.

If the target instead is a particular connection or channel within that net interaction, it requires extra selectivity. The [imperfect-selectivity gate](FOUR_SPIN_IMPERFECT_SELECTIVE_INTERVENTION_GATE.md) shows why residual coupling changes must be independently bounded. Intracranial bipolar stimulation is a real physical perturbation but may affect many axons, local cells and distant circuits; it is not an intervention that removes only a designated A–B channel while holding the rest fixed. Raw evoked responses, power, phase lags and correlation are legitimate physiological features, but none alone is an independently identified preference. In the toy counterexample opposite-preference channels give the same passive distribution and dynamics, so observational abundance alone cannot settle a designated channel's sign.

Finally, a report links behavior and experience only under an explicit measurement model: mood, pleasantness, pain intensity and pain unpleasantness are not interchangeable. A separate `q`-to-quality hypothesis, the relevant experiencing subject and reporting reliability remain to be tested, not calibrated by choosing `τ` to fit the same outcomes.

## 2. Rao source and exact metadata check

The [Rao et al. paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.10.026), [Dryad release](https://doi.org/10.7272/Q6VD6WM2), [Zenodo replica](https://zenodo.org/records/5076490), and [pinned NEMAR conversion README](https://github.com/nemarDatasets/nm000380/blob/c4fb7e96fb314b1c222ab9a826ce2a6e565ff6e6/README.md) describe clinically implanted epilepsy patients, stimulation at OFC/other sites, self-reported mood and iEEG. The conversion's description, based on the paper, says a 0 mA sham occurred at the **start of each session**, active sites varied in order, participants were blind to condition, and verbal report and tablet Immediate Mood Scaler (IMS) followed stimulation onset. This supports a within-study sham but not a fully randomized active/sham time sequence or a demonstrated sham-to-each-active-file join. Expectation, order, prior stimulation, clinical state and drift remain possible competing explanations for simple active–sham differences. The exact original report-to-recording join is not checked here.

The release includes separate behavior tables (IMS, word valence and composite mood) and single-pulse datasets; they are not automatically pain-unpleasantness scales. The authors describe single-pulse evoked potentials before and after continuous stimulation in some subjects, a potentially useful physiological probe but not a selective-channel preference calibration. We used the converted task metadata, not a replay of the authors' signal processing or a full reading of their paper; the [release description](https://zenodo.org/records/5076490) identifies the tables and authors' signal artifact-detection scripts. We did not open behavioral scores or full continuous/SPS signal archives. The NEMAR conversion also excludes the natural-behavior ECoG archive because its time fields contain absolute dates; do not redistribute or publish those fields. NEMAR notes no stated recording reference/ground, one unreadable original file and three 3 mA filenames versus the paper's 1/6 mA description. These are caveats, not adjudicated errors in the original experiment.

## 4. Requirements before a fair comparison

1. **Physical specification and independent calibration:** specify the actual net interaction or channel, measurable states, timescale, readout errors and selection; establish preference independently of the target ratings, with adequate coverage, identifiable model class and sensitivity to hidden/common inputs and model drift. A selective-channel claim needs independent selectivity and residual-drift bounds. Otherwise stop or present an honestly broad identified set.
2. **Experience and join:** use separately elicited, clearly worded unpleasantness and intensity ratings (and relevant valence/context), with documented same-person, same-intervention and time alignment. Verify contact geometry, recording reference, artifact recovery, sham, missingness and adverse-event stopping without reconstructing protected dates in public output. Eligibility and preprocessing must be set before inspecting held-out outcomes.
3. **Fixed common-domain comparison:** if both gates pass, define a single eligible set and hold out participants or, where scientifically defensible, whole sessions/blocks; fit calibration and transforms without held-out reports, account for within-person clustering, and prespecify missing-data handling, uncertainty and primary outcome. Compare a stimulus-only baseline (site, dose/protocol, order, prior exposure and baseline report where available), a conventional physiological baseline (e.g. artifact-checked power/evoked response and local activity), and the calibrated `q` model on identical held-out cases. Report absolute performance, paired incremental performance and uncertainty, not just a favorable fit. Include negative controls, calibration uncertainty and a predeclared null/indeterminate outcome. Do not adjust for post-treatment mediators when claiming a total intervention effect; prediction and causal estimands must be separately stated.
4. **Interpretation:** even a genuine held-out improvement would be evidence for a predictive feature within the validated scope, not by itself a proof of phenomenal constitution, subject unity, the unique underlying interaction or the whole framework. A failure is informative only about the specified operationalization and its error bounds.

If only mood data, nonselective stimulation or reconstructed but unvalidated timing are available, an ordinary **retrospective mood-prediction** study could be worthwhile on its own terms after a separate fixed plan and provenance audit. It is not the requested `q`–pain-unpleasantness test. No such analysis was run here.
