# A concrete orientation-experience test: what existing TMS evidence can and cannot decide

## Decision

For the proposed visual-quality bridge, use **experienced line orientation**, not stimulus identity, forced-choice accuracy, or an undifferentiated “seen/unseen” label. The closest primary study examined here used **horizontal versus vertical** lines, not a small tilt versus vertical. Its results support treating report content and gradation carefully, but it does **not** supply the maintenance-preserving, distribution-removing intervention required to distinguish the repository's H_M1 and H_D1 local constitution hypotheses.

This is an application of existing primary research and established methodological distinctions—not a novel discovery of the effect, an independent reanalysis, or evidence selecting a whole-reality metaphysics.

## 1. The declared experiential target

Consider a subject presented with one line. Our nominated target is an episode whose visual character includes an experienced orientation distinction, anchored by the subject's own description in a well-observed baseline. We compare this qualitative/organizational *type* across baseline and intervention episodes, not the numerical identity of two temporally distinct experiences. Its content must be distinguished from the orientation of the physical stimulus: mistaking a vertical stimulus for a horizontal one would not imply that the stimulus was horizontal, and a correct guess alone would not prove an orientation was experienced.

For the frozen local comparison:

- **H_M1:** if the explicitly defined content-maintaining basis C and its relevant powers are genuinely preserved while the specified additional distribution is removed, the anchored target organization is preserved.
- **H_D1:** if those same premises hold, the specified additional distribution is necessary, so that organization is not preserved.

See the actual scope and P1–P6 contract in [MAINTAINED_DISCLOSURE_CANDIDATE.md](MAINTAINED_DISCLOSURE_CANDIDATE.md). The hypotheses are not exhaustive and do not represent all versions of IIT, workspace theory, physicalism or experiential monism. This contrast tests neither one versus many subjects nor the full qualitative identity law.

The baseline subject report is legitimate defeasible evidence: it is not an infallible window, but should not be discarded just because it is a report. The demanding task is to justify its relevance and errors for the **same experiential target under the perturbation**, not to assume it remains unchanged or demand an impossible independent oracle. Subjective judgment is not reducible by definition to correct guessing, decoding, or the physically displayed orientation.

## 2. Existing primary work and the finer distinction

**Boyer, Harrison and Ro (2005), [PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0505332102).** The orientation experiment recruited six participants and analyzed five, one having too few trials classified unaware. For TMS trials on which participants reported no awareness of the **orientation**, mean forced-choice accuracy was 75% (SD .09). The authors explicitly say they did not assess on every trial whether participants had no awareness of any stimulus versus awareness that something appeared without awareness of its orientation; some participants reported the latter. They proposed a V1-bypassing pathway, but the behavioral result alone does not identify that pathway or prove complete deactivation of V1.

**Koivisto et al. (2021), [Neuroscience](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroscience.2021.08.025).** The authors deliberately used similar horizontal/vertical targets and contrasted a binary orientation-visibility question with a four-level perceptual awareness scale (PAS). Fifty-three people were recruited, 36 passed localization/pretests and completed the experiment, randomized into two separate groups of 18 by scale. Short TMS delay was individualized as 60, 75 or 90 ms; a 155 ms delay and ipsilateral stimulation were controls. The authors note that their circular coil was not individually MRI-localized, the ipsilateral stimulation might itself suppress vision, and active/control block order was not fully counterbalanced. Response-order was counterbalanced, but the two rating scales were **between participants**, not two ratings on the same trial.

The graded instructions distinguished “nothing” (no awareness of stimulus presence), “something” (some stimulus impression without a clear orientation), “almost clear” and “clear” orientation. For short-delay critical TMS, the authors' binary-unseen comparison model gives about **65%** correct; when PAS respondents chose “nothing,” the model estimate was not distinguishable from 50% under their analysis. They also report above-chance accuracy when “something” was reported and discuss degraded visual cues as one possible source of performance.

These outcomes do **not** establish that every report of “nothing” perfectly tracks the absence of experience, that every “something” is an oriented cue, or that any TMS-induced blindsight is impossible. A result not distinguishable from chance is not, by itself, an equivalence finding or proof that the true accuracy equals chance. The paper's 90% Bayesian credible interval for the *fixed-effect logit intercept* in the PAS-“nothing” comparison is `[-0.18, 0.37]`, with median `0.096`. Its monotone inverse-logit transformation is approximately **[0.455, 0.591]**, median **0.524**. This is a transformation of a rounded published model summary, not a trial-level reanalysis or a confidence interval for a new sample or all participants. Its upper endpoint is compatible with appreciably above-chance performance within that fitted model; posterior support is model-dependent.

## 3. The key check: coarse labels can mix distinct targets

The distinction can be made explicit without pretending to fit the real study. Suppose a hypothetical coarse “unseen orientation” class contains equal numbers of:

1. no visually experienced stimulus, with orientation guesses 50% correct;
2. a visually experienced blur that does not count as a clearly seen line, with guesses 80% correct.

Then total accuracy in the coarse unseen class is `(0.5 × 0.5) + (0.5 × 0.8) = 65%`, even though accuracy is at chance in the stipulated first class. This is only a logically possible synthetic mixture: its frequencies and accuracies are invented, not estimates from the papers. The blur could carry an experienced orientation cue, or involve another cue; its actual content is itself an empirical and phenomenological question.

For one common population, the general identity is `a_coarse = w a_nothing + (1-w) a_something`, **if** coarse unseen consists exactly of these two categories and the same joint distribution applies. Koivisto's scales were assigned to different participants, so one cannot directly substitute their two groups' percentages into this identity and infer w. A scale can itself change response criteria; participant variability and other differences also matter. The point is not that the mixture has been measured but that an orientation-specific absence report does not logically establish the absence of *all* visual experience, and vice versa.

Ordinary verbal first-person evidence remains central: distinguishing the actual character of the blur from a hidden/nonvisual cue requires appropriately specific subject descriptions and additional measurements, with their limitations disclosed. Blindly treating either a “no clear orientation” rating or a correct choice as the experience would mistake an observation for the target.

## 4. Can the 2021 experiment test H_M1 versus H_D1?

| Requirement | Evidence provided by the study | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| **P1: a specified baseline target** | Orientation judgments and graded visual descriptions provide meaningful, defeasible subject evidence. A “something” rating does not specify its precise orientation, and an orientation-correct response need not be an experienced orientation. | Useful anchor, not a validated target-preservation assay. |
| **P3: preserve C** | Early visual cortex was deliberately perturbed; the study does not establish that a prespecified content-maintaining basis, including its counterfactual powers and feedback, remained equivalent. Coil localization was not MRI-guided. | Not established. |
| **P4: remove the additional distribution** | No direct selective removal of a named extra source-to-consumer distribution or exclusion of equivalent pathways is demonstrated. Perturbing the putative source is not selectively cutting a downstream consumer. | Not established. |
| **P6: calibrate target observation in the intervention** | Multiple reports, catch trials and signal detection are useful; however the paper does not provide independently warranted false-positive/false-negative bounds for preservation of our nominated qualitative target conditional on the P3/P4-valid trials. | Not established. |

The decisive condition on which M and D make opposing predictions therefore has not been verified. The paper's measurements do **not** decide either hypothesis, even if its reported psychophysics is accepted. The physical gates and observation-gate failure are separate: better grading does not prove a selective intervention, and better recordings do not make an experience assay error-free.

## 5. A feasible-next-step specification, not a completed protocol

A genuine separating experiment would need to fix, **before analysis**:

1. **Target:** one subject-specific horizontal-versus-vertical visual contrast, in a declared time interval and with an explicit standard for matching subjective clarity across distinct trials. Treat physical stimulus, experienced character, confidence, forced choice and rating as separate variables. Include catch trials and allow reports of non-oriented blur or other percepts.
2. **Competing mechanisms:** independently localize a candidate maintaining process C and nominate which additional consumers D requires, including relevant prior memory, feedback and alternative routes. Neither C nor D may be redefined after the outcome. These neuroscience choices remain hypotheses, not derivations of a phenomenal selector.
3. **Intervention and measurements:** design, under appropriate institutional safety and ethics review, an independently characterized manipulation of the specified distribution while recording sufficient temporal and spatial information to assess C and nominated consumers. A meaningful P3 requires bounded preservation of relevant state, dynamics and causal powers, not merely unchanged output of one decoder; P4 requires a justified upper bound for the specified causal effect and hidden coverage, not merely a nonsignificant fMRI difference. It is not established that this combination is feasible in humans.
4. **Observation:** obtain subject descriptions and predeclared behavioral/no-report corroboration and justify how their error bounds transport to the intervention and valid-physical-condition subset. A “no-report” measure is not automatically an independent gold standard; multiple measures can share errors. If bounds cannot be justified, report them as unknown.
5. **Predictions and uncertainty:** use the [robust separation criterion](ROBUST_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTRAST.md), participant-level dependence and simultaneous uncertainty for all physical and assay bounds. Check whether the study could distinguish M and D or reject both under shared assumptions. If the gates are not met, the result is non-identifying for this pair—not positive evidence for a preferred theory.

A future successful test could constrain a rule for *which physical organizations accompany or constitute the anchored local distinction*. It would not by itself explain why orientation feels like anything, establish a universal or plural subject, decide the intrinsic nature of the whole, or solve the problem of time. A physicalist, neutral monist, and experiential monist can agree on these local empirical predictions while disagreeing about their metaphysical interpretation.
