## Part I — State what incorporation would mean

### Step 1. Fix the scope of “all”

“Every possible world is actual” need not mean a plurality of possible worlds; a singleton satisfies the universal schema. This does not make every coherent mathematical framework an actual internal implementation. [The simulation/plenitude consolidation](SIMULATION_PLENITUDE_CONSOLIDATION.md) separates total actuality, modal alternatives, physical domains, and simulated environments.

The immediate scope is all the resources discussed: quantum information and computation, AQFT, causal sets, twistor constructions, topos methods, Langlands-type correspondences, fractals and scaling, computation and programming, complexity science, and systems science.

A correct account might incorporate every one of these. It need not make every mathematical structure physically actual or every interpretation of a framework true.

If “all mathematical frameworks” is meant literally without restriction, frameworks with incompatible axioms can be studied as separate formal systems. It does not follow that reality satisfies all their axioms in the same respect. Accommodating a mathematics as mathematics is weaker than identifying it as the actual world's constitution.

### Step 2. Keep the full target

The target includes:

- actual occurrence;
- public physical organization;
- qualitative experience;
- unity and individuation;
- laws, states, and realized histories;
- and justified epistemic weighting where needed.

A theory that integrates public physics while leaving its actuality and phenomenal connection unexplained is an important partial theory, not the completed account requested here.

### Step 3. Separate six kinds of claim

For each framework, distinguish:

1. its mathematical definitions;
2. its internal theorems;
3. a mathematical connection to another framework;
4. its physical interpretation;
5. evidence for that interpretation;
6. its ultimate explanatory sufficiency.

Success at an earlier item does not automatically establish a later one. This prevents mathematical sophistication from doing evidential work by association.

### Step 4. Do not give every framework the same job

Possible roles include:

- a representation of events and causal order;
- an algebra of observables;
- a state and transformation calculus;
- a geometric reconstruction;
- a logical or categorical setting;
- a duality between descriptions;
- an effective description at a scale;
- or a theory of organization and implementation.

These roles can overlap. They are not a predetermined assignment of fundamentality.

### Step 5. Define the reality/representation distinction

Write C for the concrete constitution sought. This is a placeholder for the explanatory target, not a definition that already explains reality.

Let M_i be a representation in framework i. A proposed interpretation associates suitable features of C with M_i. At this stage, neither C nor those associations have been constructed in full.

The commitment is methodological: several descriptions may concern the same actuality. The stronger metaphysical claim that one specified nature explains them all remains to be established.

### Step 6. Classify every proposed bridge

A bridge can be:

- an exact equivalence;
- an embedding;
- a restriction to a subsystem or domain;
- a controlled approximation or limiting procedure;
- an effective statistical description;
- or a speculative correspondence.

Do not call an approximation a duality, an analogy an embedding, or a speculative correspondence an identity.

### Step 7. Require overlap consistency

Where two presentations describe the same observable or operation, they must give matching predictions within their common domain, allowing a justified approximation error where appropriate.

Schematically, if translation T_ij connects models and p_i and p_j connect them to the same prediction space, require:

\[
p_j(T_{ij}(m))=p_i(m)
\]

in the exact case. An approximate relation needs a stated error measure, regime, and bound.

This is a consistency requirement, not a proof that the translations exist.

### Step 8. Require consistency around multiple routes

If a feature can be translated from i to k either directly or through j, the routes must agree in the relevant sense:

\[
T_{ik}\simeq T_{jk}\circ T_{ij}.
\]

Here the meaning of ≃ must be specified: equality, natural isomorphism, empirical equivalence in a domain, or controlled approximation.

Pairwise agreement alone need not establish a global model. A framework atlas therefore needs a common-model or compatible-reconstruction argument, not merely a drawing with arrows.

## Part II — Start from a genuine mathematical overlap

### Step 9. Use quantum information and operator algebras as a worked common domain

There is an explicit finite-dimensional connection, not merely a metaphor.

For a Hilbert space H, observables can be represented by an algebra B(H). A density operator ρ defines a state:

\[
\omega_\rho(a)=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho a).
\]

For an effect 0≤e≤1:

\[
p(e)=\omega_\rho(e).
\]

The same mathematical object can therefore be described through information-theoretic states and probabilities or through observables and positive state functionals.

This is an established overlap in the specified domain. It is not a derivation of the actual Hilbert space, state, dynamics, Born weighting from weaker premises, or consciousness.

### Step 10. Match transformations as well as states

A quantum channel acting on states has a dual action on observables. In finite dimensions:

\[
\operatorname{Tr}(\Phi(\rho)a)
=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho\Phi^*(a)).
\]

Trace-preserving completely positive maps on states correspond to unital completely positive dual maps on observables.

Thus two vocabularies can agree on interventions and predictions, not only on static states. This is a concrete example of the kind of bridge the larger programme needs.

### Step 11. Relate subsystem access to algebraic restriction

For H=H_A⊗H_B, restriction of a global state to observables a⊗1 agrees with the reduced density operator on A:

\[
\omega_\rho(a\otimes1)
=\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_Aa).
\]

This explains a precise relation between partial access and reduced description. It does not identify the restricted algebra with a conscious perspective or erase other concrete occurrences.

### Step 12. Extend cautiously toward AQFT

AQFT assigns local observable algebras to spacetime regions, with appropriate relations such as isotony and spacelike commutativity. States restrict to these algebras.

This connects observable organization with locality. But general QFT systems need not admit the simple finite tensor-factor treatment of step 11. The continuum and infinite-system issues must be handled rather than hidden.

The integration task is to specify the actual net, states, dynamics, matter content, and operational interpretation. Merely invoking AQFT does not supply them.

### Worked overlap: one state, two exact presentations

For two qubits, take

\[
\lvert\psi\rangle=(\lvert00\rangle+\lvert11\rangle)/\sqrt2,
\qquad \rho=\lvert\psi\rangle\langle\psi\rvert.
\]

The local algebras are generated by a⊗1 and 1⊗b. They commute because

\[
(a\otimes1)(1\otimes b)=a\otimes b=(1\otimes b)(a\otimes1).
\]

With Z=diag(1,−1), the state has

\[
\omega_\rho(Z\otimes1)=0,\qquad
\omega_\rho(1\otimes Z)=0,\qquad
\omega_\rho(Z\otimes Z)=1.
\]

Each reduced state is 1/2 times the identity. The joint state is not the product of those reduced states.

Here quantum-information and observable-algebra descriptions really do refer to the same explicit mathematical construction and give the same probabilities. Commuting local algebras do not require an uncorrelated joint state. This finite example is **not** a relativistic QFT, but it illustrates a genuine common construction rather than a verbal analogy.

Nothing in these equations identifies either algebra, the entangled pair, or the state functional as a conscious subject. That further identification is not contained in the bridge.

## Part III — Connect causal and geometric descriptions

### Step 13. Identify common causal content

Both relativistic continuum models and causal-set approaches organize which events can causally precede others. That is a potential overlap.

For suitable continuum spacetimes, causal order together with suitable volume information can constrain metric geometry under precise hypotheses. This makes an order/geometry bridge intelligible without making it universally automatic.

### Step 14. Do not infer a continuum from an arbitrary causal set

A locally finite partial order is not automatically a good approximation to a physically appropriate spacetime.

An actual integration must identify:

- the relevant class of causal sets;
- an approximation or reconstruction relation;
- dimension and volume information;
- suitable dynamics and matter;
- and the recovered predictions and errors.

This is an open integration obligation here. No unique actual causal set or full continuum limit has been derived.

### Step 15. Place twistors at a demonstrated geometric overlap

Twistor constructions provide precise links between complex-geometric data and spacetime/field descriptions in specified settings. For example, familiar flat complexified spacetime incidence constructions associate a spacetime point with a projective line in twistor space; physical reality conditions and domains matter.

A proposed common account could relate causal-order reconstructions to twistor descriptions through shared continuum geometric structure.

That would require an actual bridge. The current project has not demonstrated a general causal-set-to-twistor reconstruction, full gravitational dynamics, or a twistor origin of experience.

### Step 16. Allow revision of what is fundamental

The successful structure need not place causal sets “below” twistors or vice versa. They might instead arise as different representations, restrictions, or limits of something not yet identified.

A hierarchy chosen before the mathematics works would be an additional hypothesis. The integration should determine the roles rather than merely decorate a preferred ontology.

## Part IV — Accommodate logical and correspondence frameworks

### Step 17. Use category theory to organize presentations, not conjure their existence

Objects can represent theories, models, contexts, or processes; morphisms can represent translations, restrictions, or physical transformations. The choice must be typed and interpreted.

This language can express consistency among many frameworks. It does not guarantee that a compatible diagram has a realization, that it is unique, or that its realization is physically actual.

A higher-categorical treatment may be appropriate when translations themselves have equivalences and coherence data. Calling the resulting object an ∞-category is not explanatory progress without specifying that data.

### Step 18. Give topos methods a defined role

Topos methods can provide logical settings and ways to organize contextual descriptions, observables, or truth assignments under their particular hypotheses.

They need not compete with an operator-algebraic or geometric description at the same explanatory level. But the particular topos, interpretation, and physical payoff must be justified.

An internal logic is not automatically first-person phenomenality. Mathematical gluing or an internal point of view is not an occurrence of experience by definition.

### Step 19. Incorporate Langlands where an actual correspondence exists

Langlands-type programmes establish or conjecture particular relations among arithmetic, geometric, spectral, and representation-theoretic structures. Some connections to mathematical physics exist in special gauge-theoretic settings.

A complete account could use such a correspondence to relate two mathematical presentations of part of its physical organization.

But there is no justification here for treating Langlands as universal glue connecting every preceding framework, or for identifying arithmetic/automorphic sides with matter/mind. Its actual role could be important, indirect, or absent from a minimal physical formulation while remaining mathematically accommodated.

### Step 20. Distinguish incorporation from indispensability

The correct account could incorporate every named framework without every one being indispensable to every explanation.

For a framework claimed as indispensable, ask: what prediction, reconstruction, consistency condition, or explanatory result fails without it?

A mathematically available reformulation is not thereby a necessary ingredient of reality. Conversely, lack of a currently known role does not prove a framework will have none.

## Part V — Recover scales, organization, and implementation

### Step 21. Treat scaling as a map between descriptions

Coarse-graining and renormalization relate descriptions at different resolutions. They can explain why some large-scale behavior is insensitive to microscopic differences.

This can provide a principled route to multiple effective realizations. It does not establish that all microscopic differences are irrelevant to every target—especially a phenomenal target not included in the coarse-graining criterion.

### Step 22. Accommodate fractals without making everything fractal

Fractal and scale-invariant mathematics can describe particular patterns, approximate regimes, or critical behavior. A unified account may include them where its dynamics produce them.

It need not identify the whole universe, every conscious system, or every scale with an exact fractal. It must derive the applicable regime and its limits.

### Step 23. Identify stable physical organizations

Systems and complexity science can contribute models of feedback, persistence, maintenance, near-decomposability, adaptation, and boundaries.

These can explain how a recognizable organization persists despite exchanges with an environment. They need not posit a separate higher-level substance.

What they identify is initially a physical organization. Its connection to a unified subject remains an additional target.

### Step 24. Use actual implementation rather than description matching

Computation and programming contribute precise distinctions among specifications, state machines, interfaces, executions, and verification.

An actual process implements a specification only under a justified realization relation. Merely interpreting its output as a trace of another system does not give it all that system's intervention organization.

This is where the source/monitor distinction from the constructive research belongs. A passive copied trajectory and an independently organized implementation are not automatically equivalent realizers.

### Step 25. Preserve occurrence multiplicity

One process can have several descriptions. Several actual processes can share one abstract organization. A framework correspondence must not confuse the two cases.

States, encodings, sectors, histories, or alternative models are not automatically observer tokens. Equivalence of descriptions is not a license to quotient away genuine duplicate occurrences.

A successful integrated account must specify what its representation changes preserve, and what they omit.

## Part VI — Include phenomenality without defining it into the model

### Step 26. Keep the phenomenal target independent enough to constrain the account

Characterize warranted distinctions of quality, valence, temporal organization, and co-presentation. First-person evidence can be relevant and fallible; it must not be defined as whatever a chosen physical variable already does.

Content values, report behavior, episode incidence, and access reliability are different components. A theory that matches one has not thereby matched them all.

### Step 27. State the identity hypothesis without pretending it is proven

The candidate is that an actual conscious episode and its relevant concrete process are one occurrence, not two correlated events produced side by side.

The burden is to show why that identification is warranted and explanatory. Structural matching may support it but does not logically establish numerical identity or phenomenal sufficiency by itself.

Thin and thick approaches remain distinguishable: a substantive intrinsic basis cannot be claimed both as explanatory work and as no extra commitment.

### Step 28. Derive boundaries from target-relevant evidence, not connectivity alone

The research has already shown that:

- pairwise compatibility need not establish whole-episode unity;
- aggregate reports can conceal different proposed presentation histories;
- exact cores can merge under arbitrarily weak effective coupling;
- and compatible model families can disagree about boundaries.

A better candidate must explain the relevant episode contrasts across a justified temporal and intervention domain. Near-decomposability and maintenance can be investigated here, but they are not yet established unity laws.

No step in integrating AQFT, causal sets, topoi, or the other frameworks removes this obligation automatically.

### Step 29. Leave the range of realization genuinely open

A particular brain-process token can be identical with a particular episode without implying biological exclusivity.

For a proposed nonbiological realization, establish both:

1. physical realizability of the full relevant organization;
2. phenomenal preservation under that realization relation.

Neither “organizational” nor “intrinsic” settles both questions. Integration of mathematical frameworks is not by itself evidence that every implementation is conscious.

### Step 30. Do not use probability to generate subjects

A physical history measure, an evidence-conditioned inference, and a weighting over conscious centers are different things.

If a self-location problem is warranted, its occurrence domain and measure must be justified. Framework multiplicity, branch descriptions, alternative encodings, and hypothetical models must not manufacture additional observers.

Bostrom remains a conditional guide, not a compulsory simulation ontology or a derivation of substrate independence.

## Part VII — Complete the explanatory account, not just its diagram

### Step 31. Distinguish a common model from its actuality

Even a fully coherent common mathematical model would not, by that fact alone, show that it describes the actual world or explain why it is actual.

Empirical adequacy, identity with concrete actuality, and ultimate explanation are separate obligations. The project must address all three rather than asserting that consistency is existence.

### Step 32. Explain laws, states, and permitted alternatives

Recovering a mathematical form of dynamics does not explain the actual Hamiltonian, constants, initial/boundary conditions, or history structure.

An adequate whole-account must explain why its constitutive conditions obtain and how its actual facts follow or are otherwise accounted for. This is not permission to assume that determinism, necessitarianism, or a unique possible world follows merely from demanding intelligibility.

If the proposal uses branching, stochasticity, plenitude, or necessity, it must explain the role and scope of that ingredient rather than using it to hide the remaining question.

### Step 33. Include explanatory principles in the demand

The account must justify its use of admissibility, identity, modality, logic, interpretation, and explanation sufficiently for the claims it makes.

This does not require an earlier temporal cause of logic. It requires not exempting principles from scrutiny while declaring every explanatory condition paid.

A self-consistency equation, recursive definition, or fixed point is a mathematical construction. It is not automatically self-explanation.

### Step 34. Keep uncertainty explicit without replacing explanation by it

Where compatible models disagree, report the identification set and assumptions. Do not combine links from different alternative models into a single actual boundary.

But an honest identification set is not a completed metaphysical explanation. It marks a present limit; it does not prove that reality lacks a determinate answer or that the outstanding question is illegitimate.

### Step 35. Demand new explanatory consequences

The integrated account must do work that the separate frameworks plus independently supplied bridges did not already do.

Examples of genuine gains would include:

- deriving a physical parameter or allowed structure;
- proving a controlled continuum or geometric reconstruction;
- showing why apparently different laws are one constrained description;
- predicting independently warranted episode transformations;
- or explaining why a proposed realization preserves phenomenal character and occurrence identity.

A longer list of accommodated vocabularies is not the same achievement.

### Step 36. Apply a completion test

The account would earn the requested status only when it supplies, at the required scope:

1. a defined common constitutive basis;
2. explicit and justified links among the incorporated frameworks;
3. agreement in overlap regimes and evidence for the physical interpretation;
4. an account of actual occurrence and realization multiplicity;
5. a warranted explanation of qualitative character and episode unity;
6. justified probability and self-location where applicable;
7. and an explanation of the basis and principles themselves, with no remaining unpaid residue.

Nothing in this document establishes that all seven have been achieved. They state what remains to be constructed.

## The account in one paragraph

> **One concrete, internally articulated actuality can admit algebraic, causal, geometric, logical, informational, computational, and multiscale presentations. A successful theory relates those presentations through demonstrated structure-preserving translations and controlled limits, rather than making them separate substances or merely collecting their names. Stable organizations and actual realizations are features to derive within that constitution. Conscious episodes, where present, must be explained as concrete occurrences with qualitative and incidence structure—not assigned by a free label. The entire constitution, its actuality, laws, and explanatory sufficiency remain within the demand for complete intelligibility.**

This is the strongest constructive architecture we can responsibly formulate here. It accommodates the possibility that **all** the named frameworks belong to a correct account. It does not claim to have found that completed correct account.

Following that finite starting point, the broad architecture needs two continuing pieces of construction in parallel:

- **A cross-framework piece:** choose one defined overlap, formulate the source and target models, construct the translation, and prove which operations and predictions it preserves. The whole-account obligation must remain visible throughout. Local proofs can earn real progress without being relabeled completion.
