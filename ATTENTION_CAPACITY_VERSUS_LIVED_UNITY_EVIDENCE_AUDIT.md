# Attention capacity and lived unity: what the cited studies establish

## Bottom line

The studies cited in Lopez's [layered-unity proposal](LAYERED_UNITY_AND_SUBJECT_QUALITY_COMPARISON.md) give **relevant, task-specific evidence** about residual attention after callosotomy. They do not by themselves establish a single *experienced* centrality structure, the number of subjects, or the project's four-spin L/C incidence rule. That does not refute Lopez's proposal: she offers an additional philosophical argument connecting experienced attention to phenomenal unity, rather than simply defining a reaction-time effect as an experience.

The most informative source detail is that Ptito et al.'s four-stream design addressed a real within- versus across-hemifield **stream confound**, while its reported nonsignificant field comparison does not establish equivalent blink effects. Arguin et al.'s 500 ms arrow-cue result concerns a different attentional task and mostly **partial** callosotomies; it should not be treated as a direct, same-person test of the blink or as an experimentally measured universal timing threshold. These qualifications refine the empirical support without erasing the reported findings.

This is a retrospective, bounded source audit, following a [fixed plan](ATTENTION_UNITY_PRIMARY_EVIDENCE_FIXED_PLAN.md) written after the Arguin abstract and prior literature were known. No patient data, new interviews, neural recordings or experimental outcomes were acquired.

## 1. Source access and the actual observations

### Dual-task interference: Holtzman and Gazzaniga, 1982

[Holtzman and Gazzaniga](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.7146915), *Science* 218, 1325–1327, propose in the accessible abstract that demanding bilateral stimulation draws on a common processing-resource pool despite the surgery [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7146915). Lopez summarizes this as one side's difficult task worsening performance on the other. The abstract states a strong availability claim about hemisphere-specific information; it should be read as the authors' task interpretation, not a general proof that no information of any kind can cross in all callosotomy cases.

**Scope:** the publisher supplied the abstract but not the full article. We did not inspect its participant count, tasks, raw outcomes, exclusion decisions, uncertainty, or full controls; we therefore cannot independently evaluate the title's word “exclusively,” the magnitude of interference, or whether its design eliminates competing mechanisms. One common, capacity-limited mechanism is an explanatory hypothesis, not directly measured subjective unity. A shared bottleneck need not carry the stimulus contents whose conjunction is disputed.

### Attentional blink: Ptito et al., 2009

[Ptito et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301051109001239) used four simultaneous rapid visual streams, one per quadrant, in **one complete-callosotomy participant** and 22 neurologically intact participants. Two successive targets could occur in the same or different hemifields; when both required report, the second target could be harder to report at short intervals. Their abstract reports a comparable blink for the patient and controls, no significant variation by within/across hemifield, and a left-hemisphere processing advantage only in the patient [1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19539687/). Those are the authors' reported outcomes; “no significant variation” is not a demonstrated equivalence, and the patient is not an estimate of all patients.

The visible introduction explains why the four streams matter: a previous design placed within-hemifield target pairs in the *same* stream but across-hemifield pairs in *different* streams. Ptito et al. say their design permits a comparison using different streams **within** and **across** hemifields, addressing that particular confound. The visible discussion says overall report accuracy was lower for intra- than inter-hemifield targets, even while the authors characterize blink magnitude as comparable. Overall accuracy and a lag-dependent blink contrast are therefore not interchangeable.

**Scope:** we read the publisher's abstract, introduction and exposed participant/discussion snippets; the full methods, results, figures, numerical effect sizes, interval estimates, trial-level data and statistical analysis were unavailable. The preview does not establish the exact analysis subset or reported confidence intervals, so we do not calculate equivalence or a new effect size. The effect of a **first target on reporting a later target** is not a direct report that both were phenomenally conjoint at one moment. Sequential targets can yield important temporal-processing evidence without satisfying a simultaneous-experience target.

### Spatial orienting: Arguin et al., 2000

[Arguin et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028393299000779), *Neuropsychologia* 38, 283–291, studied **10 callosotomy patients**—seven with anterior sections of 50–80% and three reported as total—and **10 controls**. At a **500 ms** cue–target interval, two arrow cues preceded a lateralized visual target. The abstract reports that for a majority of the patient group, reaction times under divided cues were similar to valid focused cues and shorter than under neutral cues; intact controls instead showed a focused-cue advantage, with divided and neutral results similar [1](https://sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0028393299000779?via=ihub%3D). The exposed control result reports 319 ms divided versus 318 ms neutral. Similarity or a nonsignificant difference alone is not an equivalence test. The paper interprets its results as supporting divided visuospatial attentional control under these conditions; these are reaction-time findings, not a directly measured pair of separate phenomenal fields.

The introduction contrasts earlier short-interval and 1,500 ms studies and offers cue type and timing as **possible** explanations of their different findings. Those earlier studies had different designs and numbers of participants. The visible text does not supply a within-person, randomized dose–response comparison establishing exactly when any shared control emerges, nor a general cutoff at 1,000 ms. Lopez cites the earlier work for a longer-interval pattern; we did not independently inspect that 1984 full chapter, so cannot adjudicate her more specific interval claim. The fact that some people received partial rather than complete sections is important: no result for the seven anterior cases should be relabeled as a result from seven completely disconnected brains.

**Scope:** we read the publisher's abstract and introduction and exposed subject, result and discussion snippets; the full article, individual response tables, figures, detailed control procedures and trial data were not available. We did not independently verify fixation or rule out other causes of the cue effect. Patient overlap across these three studies is not established; do not add their patient counts as independent replications.

## 2. The inferential chain and what could break it

| Step | What this kind of evidence can support | Open alternative or limitation | What could discriminate, if feasible |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Task manipulation → behavioral contrast** | Measured RT or correct-report differences under cue, target or load conditions. Ptito's design specifically addresses one stream-layout confound. | Motor preparation, stimulus difficulty, fixation, response selection, memory, order, timing and missingness can influence a contrast; their impact on a particular study requires its actual methods. | Reproduce the same task with fixation and response controls, full condition tables, prespecified analyses, missingness and uncertainty; retain patient-specific results. |
| **Behavior → shared capacity/control mechanism** | Interference or cross-field timing constrains a family of functional explanations and motivates a shared bottleneck hypothesis. | Common arousal, shared output or memory stages, stimulus overlap, residual pathways or distinct mechanisms can also generate some interference. These are possible alternatives, not demonstrated confounds in these papers. | Compare independently specified predictions of bottleneck location, transfer and separate response/control mechanisms across randomized demands and timing; quantify instrument blind spots. Positive results need not reveal a unique mechanism. |
| **Functional control → experienced centrality / agency** | A genuine attentional capacity can be evidence relevant to a person's experience; first-person descriptions also carry evidential weight. | A shared computational or arousal resource is not automatically an **experienced** ordering of these particular aspects, and a sequential shift of control is not by itself a same-window conjunction. | Combine specific, time-anchored first-person judgments of focus/periphery and conjunction with separately recorded behavior, confidence and controls for delayed inference. Assess whether the questions are understood and whether probing alters the experience. |
| **Experienced centrality → unity** | Lopez presents a substantive sufficiency argument: when the relevant experiences form one *experienced* centrality system, their relational character can support a conjoint phenomenology. | The unity-producing principle and the presence of the relevant experiential relation are both debatable. An account that defines the one system using an already identified subject may explain unity within that subject without deriving subject incidence. | Defend and compare the principle with access-only, duplicated-field and partial-unity interpretations on the same target, using relevant firsthand evidence rather than assuming the conclusion. |
| **Unity → L/C physical selector and q-quality** | A particular justified conjunction could challenge a sufficiently specified exclusive boundary package. | Neither a genuine conjunction nor a resource bottleneck uniquely identifies a four-spin graph, its selected physical sets, physical preference `q`, or an unpleasantness law. | Fix and independently justify the physical domain, mapping and quality/report law before the relevant observations; otherwise stop. |

This chain explains why two kinds of skepticism must be kept apart. One can challenge the *empirical inference* from behavior to an experienced centrality structure while accepting the philosophical conditional “such a structure would unify these aspects.” One can instead grant the experiential structure and contest that conditional or the claim that it derives subject count. Neither response requires denying that an actual participant has an experience.

## 3. Return to the existing L/C decision

In the [common-domain table](LAYERED_UNITY_AND_SUBJECT_QUALITY_COMPARISON.md), `Jlocal` concerns a specified conjoint experience of detailed left/right aspects, while `Jglobal` concerns specified conjoint coarse aspects. The findings above classify **neither** target in that formal four-spin sector. Arguin measures a reaction-time effect for cueing; Ptito measures a report-performance pattern for **successive** targets. Holtzman's abstract proposes a common resource; we have not examined its complete task. None provides the separately specified same-window experiential conjunction, the designated population coverage for a negative, or a brain-to-spin mapping needed by the table.

In particular, preserved central interference is compatible with L-exclusive, C-full or C-layered **as physical behavior** if their physical and report laws are stipulated the same; it does not automatically predict that one of their experiential completions is true. A reliably warranted global conjunction by a mapped reporter would challenge the specifically exclusive L completion, but it would not by itself distinguish C-full from C-layered. Conversely, failure to answer does not warrant the negative target. This is a conditional comparison of packages, not a demonstration that all actual theories make the same predictions.

The [Pinto first-person reports](SPLIT_BRAIN_FIRST_PERSON_CONSTITUTION_COMPARISON.md) are separate evidence: the participants described whole-field experience and preserved ordinary unity. They are more directly relevant to lived experience than a blink score, but their main article does not establish a specific same-trial local/global conjunction or the toy physical mapping. A subject's experience is not reducible to the accuracy of a motor response; testimony also does not by itself enumerate inaccessible perspectives.

The q-quality hypothesis is separate again: none of these attention tasks independently calibrates the preferred physical interaction or measures q-linked unpleasantness. Pain intensity, unpleasantness, fear, anxiety, expected pain, mood and valence must not be substituted for one another or for visual centrality. Prediction of a report would not alone establish phenomenal constitution or subject boundaries.

## 4. A realistic next evidence gate

A worthwhile next step would require access to the **complete primary methods and results** of the relevant studies, or an ethically approved noninvasive follow-up with existing participants who consent. No new surgery is proposed. Before outcomes are accessed, specify the exact target and obtain authorized records or preregister a feasible design; the following is a research contract, not a validated clinical procedure:

1. In a separate report-accessible source domain, develop and evaluate questions about **experienced** centrality and same-window global/local conjunction. Use independent corroborating measures and interventions where ethically appropriate; report uncertainty, nonresponse, and possible memory and access errors. Do not define correctness solely by a participant's ability to do a comparison, or calibrate using L or C as the answer key. No infallible external gold standard is assumed.
2. Prespecify how those judgments transport to the **same target patient, task, time window, content and response mode**; attention, surgery, language, fatigue and memory can change report reliability. If transfer or negative-coverage assumptions cannot be defended, the result remains unknown rather than being imputed from the source-domain calibration.
3. Separately measure the functional contrast with within/across-field matched stream layout, prespecified target timing, cue conditions and baseline demands, and gaze, response and potential leakage controls. Model uncertainty and repeated observations within individuals. A nonsignificant difference is not equivalence; set any equivalence margin in advance and report whether the data are informative enough to meet it. Control burdens and stop when needed.
4. Specify rival physical and experiential packages, including potentially distinct bottlenecks, duplicated/global representations, access failures and content occurrence. Fix physical-to-field and report mappings before looking at the target outcomes; test their risky predictions rather than choosing a mapping after a result. A common bottleneck **with an unknown conjunction report**, or trustworthy conjunction evidence **without a justified L/C physical map**, leaves the boundary comparison unresolved. Even a well-supported conjunction would not by itself select an experiential rather than neutral metaphysical ground.

The current stop is therefore substantive: no calibrated same-condition conjunction assay, complete source review, or justified neural-to-four-spin incidence mapping is available here. A larger literature search or an extra toy model would not automatically fill those missing links. An author or library request for the full methods and permissible aggregate or trial records may be useful, but **no request has been sent**.

## Status

The experiential nature and self-sufficiency of the whole, genuinely plural subjects and local qualitative laws remain hypotheses. C3 is **full structural and experiential intelligibility**; B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open. This note does not claim a new empirical q, subject count, clinical outcome, full reading of Klinge's book, or substantive battery pass.
