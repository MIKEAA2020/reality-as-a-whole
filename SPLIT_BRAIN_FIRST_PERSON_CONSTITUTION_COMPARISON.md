# First-person unity against competing boundary rules: what a real case constrains

## Decision

The [subsequent private-subject identity audit](SPLIT_BRAIN_PRIVATE_SUBJECT_IDENTITY_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md) checks the 2023 DDC study's task-specific confidence and anecdotal first-person remark, and specifies the extra incidence and coverage needed to distinguish P from C-layered. The user subsequently supplied the 2017 supplement; its [first-person and methods audit](PINTO_2017_SUPPLEMENT_FIRST_PERSON_AND_METHODS_AUDIT.md) reads the actual questions and individual answers, qualifies the broad main-text summary and reports substantial eye-data exclusions.

**Later refinement:** the [layered-unity common-domain comparison](LAYERED_UNITY_AND_SUBJECT_QUALITY_COMPARISON.md) distinguishes global from detailed local conjunction. Its conditional challenge to an exclusive L package depends on the specified aspects and the absence of a cross-side field; neither form of conjunction is established in this clinical study.

Pinto and colleagues' 2017 study provides **actual first-person evidence**, alongside behavioral results, relevant to the project's subject-boundary question. The main article summarizes both participants as reporting whole-field vision and unchanged conscious unity. The subsequently inspected supplement gives the actual structured questions and differing qualified answers: DDV notes peripheral blurring and sometimes less clear left-sided sensation, and DDC mentions reduced left-hand control and an occasional involuntary right-arm movement. These reports deserve positive evidential weight but are not a directly calibrated trial-by-trial measurement of the four-spin model's L-versus-C target. The [follow-up](PINTO_2017_SUPPLEMENT_FIRST_PERSON_AND_METHODS_AUDIT.md) distinguishes the four-question account from the separately printed translated DDC interview; the supplement does not establish their relation to particular experimental trials.

On the existing four-spin sector, L and C do make opposite *conditional* claims about joint experience when each selected set is postulated to be exactly one unified field with all its specified contents. An independently warranted, same-window first-person conjunction could distinguish those **completed field packages**. The original toy has no such measured report, no established mapping to split-brain anatomy, and no model for the corresponding human intervention. Thus the 2017 findings neither validate C nor falsify L as defined. They do challenge an oversimplified clinical model that predicts rigidly lateralized response abilities.

## 2. Three kinds of evidence in the 2017 paper

**First-person reports.** The main article broadly summarizes both DDC and DDV as reporting whole-field vision, whole-body feeling and control, and unchanged conscious unity. The [supplementary answers](PINTO_2017_SUPPLEMENT_FIRST_PERSON_AND_METHODS_AUDIT.md) show important qualifications: DDV describes blurred peripheral angles and occasionally less clear left-side feeling; DDC speaks of reduced left-hand control and occasional involuntary right-arm movement and does not explicitly answer the whole-body-feeling question yes or no. DDV says he feels normal as before; DDC denies another presence. These are observations of what patients said about their lived experience, not merely performance scores, and favor taking preserved ordinary experience seriously. The supplement does not establish that the general reports refer to a particular 120 ms trial, to simultaneous individual stimuli, or to any specific identity of neurological substrates. The lack of an *apparent* other presence is not an omniscient census of inaccessible subjects.

**Single-stimulus detection and localization.** Experiment 1 tested both patients. A circle, when present, appeared for 120 ms; the patients reported presence and then location using blocked verbal, left-hand and right-hand response conditions. The article reports DDV's detection hits/false alarms as 100%/0%, and DDC's as 97.5%/7.7%; it reports localization across both fields with all response types. This is evidence against a strict behavioral prediction that a participant cannot report a left-field stimulus verbally or with the right hand, and vice versa. It is not, on its own, an observation that two separate lateralized stimuli were conjointly experienced in the same window.

**Two-stimulus matching and metacognition.** DDC alone completed Experiments 2–5. The authors report 55.4% average accuracy across visual fields versus 90.4% within one field for same/different comparisons, and report that the former was not statistically better than chance. Failure to reject chance does not prove zero information, and matching failure alone does not prove failure of conscious conjunction. In later single-stimulus tasks, higher confidence was associated with better performance; the detection task itself had a ceiling that precluded informative metacognitive assessment for that component. Accuracy–confidence association gives evidence of metacognition in those tasks, not a validated numerical error rate for a phenomenal-unity judgment.

The authors' interpretation is a preserved conscious unity despite divided perception. Alternatives include different combinations of residual pathways, action control, memory, task-specific processing and experiential organization. The paper describes eye tracking, exclusion of fixation failures and cross-cue precautions, and argues against several cue-based explanations. These controls matter; they do not prove that every transfer channel is absent. Non-significant response-type interactions are not equivalence tests. Results were obtained long after surgery and the two patients do not establish a universal postoperative outcome.

## 3. Make L and C answer the same first-person question

Take the existing four-spin `t=1/4` setup, one-update graph and selection rules from the [integrated candidate](INTEGRATED_EXPERIENTIAL_MISALIGNMENT_CANDIDATE.md). L selects the two outer pairs; C selects the four-spin component. Define a **local target**, not an existential census:

> `J_S=1` if a specified reporting subject S experiences designated left and right experiential aspects together in the same declared window, including their conjoint what-it-is-like. It is not enough that S can report each one on different trials or that a device can compare them.

To get opposite predictions, add all of the following **explicit package assumptions**:

1. Each selected physical set realizes exactly one unified field in the relevant window; no unselected encompassing field exists within the comparison. For L the fields are disjoint and there is no additional S containing both aspects; for C there is one relevant field S.
2. Both designated aspects are actually present in the relevant trial and map respectively to the two outer pairs. Under C they are aspects of S's one conjoint field. Under L each belongs only to its own pair-field. This phenomenological realization map is **added**, not output by the spin kernel.
3. The reporter is identified with the relevant field, and the target refers to that reporter, not to an existential statement about some unobserved field. The identity of a reporter across cases is not assumed merely from the word “person.”
4. The same target window, field selection, token/quality interpretation and report-access model survive the comparison. This is especially important because the project has shown that changing the influence horizon can change L's selection.

Under these stipulated assumptions, L predicts `J_S=0` for either pair-reporter and C predicts `J_S=1` for the component-reporter. A condition-valid, specific first-person occurrence of both aspects together would contradict this **exclusive L package**, not the mathematical L selection rule standing alone. A warranted absence of conjunction would challenge the completed C package, but absence is harder to establish: missing report, inability to compare, uncertainty or failed access are not sufficient. If overlapping fields, a third subject, partial unity, or context-dependent aspect identities are allowed, this simple two-way comparison may no longer apply.

This adds to, rather than replaces, the [original subject/record interface](SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION_INTERFACE_RESULTS.md). That interface's imagined 10%-error assay was stipulated, and its actual spin endpoint-agreement comparator was not a valid assay. Neither calculating L/C nor adding the same subjective labels to both cases yields an independently calibrated experience measurement.

## 4. Can the 2017 observations execute that comparison?

| Requirement | 2017 paper's main text | Status for this gate |
|---|---|---|
| Common physical domain and subject-rule input | Clinical patients with callosotomy; full physical dynamics, spin variables and influence graph not supplied. | **Not the four-spin sector**; no justified neural-to-spin map. |
| Positive first-person experience | Main-text summary of whole visual field and preserved felt unity; supplement records structured questions and qualified individual answers. | Genuine relevant evidence; mapping to a specified same-trial target not established. |
| Conjoint target with matched window | Experiment 2 presents two stimuli simultaneously, but its main reported outcome is matching performance; general reports are discussed separately. | A participant's reported ordinary unity is not automatically an item-specific trial-level `J_S`. |
| Reporter identity and coverage | The authors investigate responses and conscious unity; hypothetical inaccessible perspectives remain contested. | No independent mapping of a response channel to a uniquely counted subject-field. |
| Report calibration and error | Confidence–performance associations in other tasks and fixation control. | No validated class-conditional error bounds for `J_S` under the cross-field task. |
| Quality prediction | Visual shape/orientation, not the proposed q-linked unpleasantness. | No test of the E-L valence identity. |

A literal hypothesis that **a reporter cannot experience any bilateral field whatsoever in ordinary life** is in tension with the authors' reported first-person descriptions if those descriptions are understood literally. That is not the same as rejecting E-L: E-L never identified its toy pairs with cerebral hemispheres. Nor is it the same as demonstrating trial-specific bilateral phenomenal conjunction under brief controlled fixation. Conversely, a theory that insists every left-field stimulus must fail verbal detection conflicts with the reported detection results; it cannot be rescued merely by calling patients' subjective reports illusory. Distinguish that behavioral prediction from the hypothesis that separate phenomenological fields can communicate with a common reporting system.

## 5. A realistic discrimination contract, without a false gold standard

For future noninvasive, ethically approved work, a candidate comparison should first define what each theory predicts for a **particular** patient, target window and stimulus condition, including residual routes and reports. One would collect first-person conjunction judgments as well as separately recorded identification, confidence, gaze and response data, with within-field controls, simultaneous and sequential conditions, blinded cue monitoring, explicit uncertainty and nonresponse. Do not require any clinical intervention, and avoid treating poor task performance as proof of absent experience.

Before looking at relevant outcomes, a complete protocol must state:

- how both target aspects are made identifiable without assuming that the question itself produces or preserves their conjunction;
- how gaze, stimulus leakage, memory, attention, inference, verbal versus manual access, and communication across channels are evaluated in the actual window;
- what independently defensible experiential evidence would warrant a positive `J_S`, and what much stronger evidence, if any, would warrant a negative one;
- each candidate's fixed physical-to-field and physical-to-report mapping, including no-response and context dependence, rather than a report kernel chosen after seeing the result;
- which outcomes count against each full package and which would leave them unresolved, with a patient-specific uncertainty analysis and no unjustified pooling of repeated trials or patients.

A reliable direct first-person judgment can be important evidence without being declared an infallible external gold standard. Calibration against physical stimulus correctness alone is insufficient to measure conjoint phenomenology; calibrating by assuming C or L is circular. Positive conjunction would constrain a suitably specified exclusive-boundary package, but even a perfect test of subject boundaries would not automatically distinguish experiential from neutral grounding or identify numerical token sharing. The [pair/triple gate](TRIPLE_UNITY_RANDOMIZED_REPORT_PARTITION_GATE.md) concerns an existential target with different coverage requirements; its probability bounds must not be applied to this subject-indexed question without additional assumptions.

## 6. Implication for the framework

The positive lesson is that the project can take actual first-person reports seriously **and** make conditional boundary predictions that could fail. The limiting result is equally specific: no defensible map currently connects this clinical study to the four-spin L/C selection, its target-specific reported conjunction, or E-L's q-valence law. This is not a null empirical result and not evidence that split-brain patients lack experience.

The proposed experiential whole, plural subjects and full structural and experiential intelligibility remain research targets. B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open; this source reading, conditional comparison and earlier finite regressions do not constitute a full battery pass.
