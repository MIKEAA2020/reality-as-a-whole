# Kavuri et al.: supplied quantum-position-verification PDF audit

## Finding and source status

The supplied ten-page PDF, *Quantum position verification with remote untrusted devices*, by Gautam A. Kavuri and colleagues, describes a Bell-test position-verification experiment and conditional security results. Its central lesson for this project is useful but narrow: **timing and quantum correlations can constrain where a task was carried out under an explicit physical and adversary model; they do not locate a person's experience or determine how many subjects exist.** The PDF is not related to the preceding Shelat attention study except as another test of what physical records can and cannot establish.

The attached file, `10.1038_s41586-026-11247-3_ijfb.pdf`, has SHA-256 `a3f2d410c33f35ff649f317639f71c5ff980d3934a93cc10a4ce9768982f4d9d`. Its printed DOI is `10.1038/s41586-026-11247-3`, and it shows received 5 February and accepted 5 October 2026, but gives the publication date as **“xx xx xxxx.”** Its embedded creation timestamp is **7 November 2026**, later than the trusted current date **31 October 2026**. A timestamp can be wrong or refer to a later file version; this alone does not establish inauthenticity. It does mean that the provided file should be treated as a supplied document, not as independent proof of publication or availability as of the current date. Search results also surfaced November-dated publication and news pages, which cannot establish their availability before that date. An independent September 2026 [coherent-state QPV paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41567-026-03439-5) cites the Kavuri work as a preprint; it is a different experiment, not a validation of this PDF's counts. I read the supplied PDF's abstract, experimental section, figures and Methods; the important numerical discrepancy below was also checked on the rendered page 5. The PDF refers to separate supplementary proofs, experimental data at DOI `10.18434/mds2-4322`, and analysis code available from the authors on request. Neither the data nor code was obtained or replayed in this audit; no full supplementary proof was audited here. Its reported data, apparatus performance and security theorem remain **author claims**, not an independently reproduced experiment or a checked proof.

## What the protocol claims

Two secured verifiers, A and B, send randomly generated classical challenges toward a target region. A prover-side device combines them to choose a measurement setting; in the actual demonstration this function is XOR. At A a separate random setting is used to measure the other member of a photon pair. The prover returns its outcome to both verifiers. Time constraints and correlations from many trials enter a Bell-based test. Pages 2–4 describe a verifier separation of **195.1 ± 0.3 m**, approximately **250,000 trials/s**, detection efficiencies **above 81%** at both measured stations, and challenge arrivals within **3(2) ns** at the prover. The paper says no postselection or background subtraction was used in this demonstration.

Device-independent means the quantum source and measurement devices need not be trusted internally; it does **not** mean assumption-free. Pages 8–9 require secure classical computing, physically protected verifier labs, known verifier locations, sufficiently accurate timing, shielded measurement interfaces, appropriate input/setting distributions and a specified adversary class. Soundness depends on relativistic causal constraints and the treatment of entanglement at trial boundaries. In the no-prior-entanglement result, the source-entanglement location also has a stated boundary constraint. Quantum networks, timing channels and the full deployed security environment are not validated merely by the label “device-independent.”

The article distinguishes:

- **Basic implementation:** security against the stipulated class with no pre-existing adversarial entanglement at each relevant trial boundary, while allowing classical and quantum computation and communication during trials subject to the spacetime constraints.
- **Extended experimental analysis:** a bound of **8 × 10⁻⁶** on the adversaries' *average entanglement robustness over trials*. Robustness is a particular entanglement measure, not an ebit count or an empirically measured bound on all attackers. The paper estimates the honest source's trial-averaged robustness as **2 × 10⁻³**, 250 times that threshold, and describes an attack on the implemented protocol using two suitably prepared qubit pairs (p. 5).
- **Stronger theoretical variants:** more complex, longer challenge functions admit different restricted-resource security claims. The paper gives examples using 229 or 999 challenge bits and more trials, not an assertion that the single-bit XOR demonstration already defeated those classes (pp. 3, 8–9). The complete derivations are in supplementary sections not audited here.

The authors preconfigured their analysis using five calibration blocks, tested the procedure on that day's blocks, then reserved separate days for the basic and extended analyses. They report updating factors using prior fault-free blocks; fault-flagged blocks remained eligible to cause protocol failures but were excluded from factor updates (pp. 4–5). This distinction matters: removing unfavorable trials from the tested record would require separate justification. We have not verified the implementation of these rules.

## Statistical certificate and reported performance

A nonnegative test factor is evaluated on each trial and multiplied into a product `P`. If its conditional expectation under every permitted adversarial strategy is at most one given the past, the product is a nonnegative supermartingale. The paper sets the per-instance soundness parameter **δ = 2⁻⁶⁴ ≈ 5.42 × 10⁻²⁰** and passes an instance when `P ≥ 2⁶⁴`. This is an upper bound on false passes *under the model and assumptions*, not a posterior probability that a particular person is in a particular place, nor a guarantee against adversaries outside the class. The existence of a Bell violation by itself does not supply the paper's QPV-specific spacetime/adversary reduction.

The PDF reports 30 million trials for each basic instance and 60 million for each extended instance, with target honest success probability 0.97725. It reports **224 passes of 232 basic instances**. Its **96.5%** figure is not the conventional one-decimal rounding of 224/232 = **96.5517…%**, which rounds to **96.6%**; truncation or another reporting convention is possible, but the PDF does not explain it.

More seriously, the rendered PDF p. 5 literally says **“102 instances of which 103 passed”**, then gives **99.0%**. A count of passes larger than the number of instances is impossible. Reversing them would give 102/103 = **99.029…%**, which rounds to 99.0%, and an earlier report in this repository gave that order based on a previously inspected preprint. But arithmetic agreement does not establish the correct counts or the cause of the inconsistency. We do not silently correct the PDF; raw data or an independently verified authoritative correction is needed. The earlier repository's 102/103 claim is now explicitly qualified.

On p. 5 the authors report localization ratios, with parentheses denoting **one-standard-deviation standard uncertainties**, not 95% confidence intervals:

| Comparison | Reported ratio | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| One-dimensional, ideal classical comparator | 2.47(2) | Classical length divided by quantum length, with the stated ideal classical reference |
| One-dimensional, equal latencies | 4.48(2) | Classical length divided by quantum length under the specified latency comparison |
| Two-dimensional projection, equal latencies | 4.02(3) | Classical/quantum projected-area ratio |
| Three-dimensional, equal latencies | 4.53(5) | Classical/quantum volume ratio |

The different factors are not interchangeable. These are ratios of defined feasible target regions under specified geometry, latency and security conditions, not person-tracking precision in a deployed system. The theoretical loss-tolerance discussion involves heralding or commitment; the demonstration is not an arbitrary-loss, unrestricted-adversary deployment. The paper itself points to noise, speed, line-of-sight links and quantum-network development as practical constraints (pp. 5–6).

## Further checks and unresolved timing

An independent [small arithmetic and settings check](verification/kavuri_qpv_pdf_audit.py) enumerates all eight combinations of uniformly independent binary challenges and a verifier setting. XOR satisfies the paper's two displayed input-independence conditions in that ideal example; replacing XOR with AND fails the first condition even though the verifier setting remains conditionally independent. This is a **toy check**, not proof of random-number quality, implementation, device independence, or the security theorem. It also checks the reported percentages, robustness ratio and numerical value of δ. The earlier [causal-records toy](verification/qpv_causal_records_gate.py) illustrates that two colluding classical responders can mimic an honest central responder's XOR transcript within a time limit; it is not the paper's complete physical or statistical model.

The PDF's 3(2) ns arrival-offset claim matches the supplied repository LaTeX main manuscript. The earlier [QPV audit](QUANTUM_POSITION_VERIFICATION_AND_SUBJECT_LOCATION.md) reports that a previously inspected preprint/publisher supplement instead said 9(2) ns. That earlier account is not independent evidence that the supplementary material was available as of the trusted date, and no timing records were inspected here. We cannot decide whether the values refer to different measurements, versions, definitions or an error; neither their physical effect nor a correction is established.

## Implications for the reality-as-a-whole analysis

The strongest inference is operational and conditional: a successful aggregate record limits certain distributed spoofing strategies given the declared causal geometry, input assumptions and resource bounds. It is not an unrestricted demonstration that every individual trial's operation must be at a point, that all other attacks fail, or that a remotely controlled authorized device identifies its operator. As with Shelat's behavior, the observable is a record generated by a specific task, not a direct phenomenal report.

The protocol assumes an effective spacetime and light-speed causal structure; it does not derive spacetime from a timeless ontology. Matching records or secure physical access do not resolve whether there is one subject, two subjects, a locally exclusive stream, a layered stream, or a private local/global arrangement. Those competing experiential models need an independent bridge from physical operations and reports to subject ownership, same-window conjunction and qualitative experience. QPV neither supplies that bridge nor shows superluminal signalling, a universal subject, a simulator, or subjective unification.
