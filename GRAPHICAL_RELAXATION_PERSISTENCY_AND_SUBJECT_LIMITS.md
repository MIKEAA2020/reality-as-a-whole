## The useful established distinction

For a finite pairwise model, maximizing a score over all complete configurations can be rewritten as linear optimization over the realizable marginal polytope. Replacing that polytope by the larger locally consistent polytope provides an upper bound on the maximum; a fractional optimum can be impossible as any joint distribution. A feasible complete configuration gives a lower bound. If the bounds coincide, that configuration is certified optimal for the **specified objective and model**. A tighter hierarchy can close gaps at additional computational cost. Ordinary max-product on a graph with cycles is not generally an exact optimizer, and even a converged message-passing fixed point is not a generic certificate. These are known results, not new methods.

Optimality is not uniqueness. A strong-persistency statement says that **every** exact optimizer has the stated value for each fixed variable; weak persistency says that **at least one** exact optimizer is compatible with all fixed values. Ties matter. If one seeks a unique subject boundary from an optimization, obtaining a single optimizer or a weak certificate is not enough. The definition of the score and its input remains a separate scientific and philosophical assumption, even if optimization is exact.

## Exact counterexample to a literal strong-persistency claim

The visually checked mirror's Definition 8.1 and Proposition 8.4 on page 210 say, respectively, that strong persistency requires agreement with *every* exact optimizer and that any fractional solution of the binary first-order relaxation is strongly persistent. Under those literal definitions, ties give a counterexample. This flags a qualification or error in the displayed statement; it does **not** refute weak persistency or all stronger qualified theorems.

Consider four binary variables, a triangle of unit-weight disagreement edges among 1, 2 and 3, and a zero-weight edge from 0 to 1. The objective is the sum of the triangle's three disagreement indicators. Every complete assignment cuts at most two triangle edges, and there are 12 maximizing assignments: variable 0 may be either zero or one. In the local LP, set `x0=0`, give each triangle node a uniform singleton, and give each triangle edge half its mass on `01` and half on `10`. The edge from 0 to 1 is locally consistent with `x0=0` and a uniform `x1`. This feasible fractional point scores 3, which is the maximum possible in the relaxation because each edge disagreement is at most 1. It fixes variable 0 to zero even though an exact optimum with `x0=1` exists; weak persistency still holds because an optimum with `x0=0` also exists.

The point is not merely an arbitrary interior mixture: it uniquely maximizes the *different exposing objective* consisting of triangle disagreements minus `P(x0=1)`. Equality in its upper bound forces all triangle edges to disagree and fixes `x0`; local consistency on the odd cycle then fixes all remaining local tables uniquely. Thus it is an exposed fractional vertex, and it is an optimizer of the original unperturbed objective. This distinction avoids relying on a non-vertex tie. [`verification/lp_persistency_and_moment_gate.py`](verification/lp_persistency_and_moment_gate.py) checks local consistency, exact optima, objective bounds, and the finite witness; the vertex proof is the argument just stated, rather than an automated LP vertex enumeration. [Receipt](verification/lp_persistency_and_moment_gate.json).

For an applied selector, this is a warning about incorrectly eliminating tied candidates—not evidence that any of our physical candidate selectors has this particular LP form or degeneracy. To claim an invariant boundary, specify whether *all* optima agree, bound the effect of omitted context, and show that the scoring rule is physically and experientially justified.

## Moment positivity is not the same as the claimed eigenvalues

The visually checked page 247 also calls four atomic probabilities the eigenvalues of the monomial moment matrix. This is false as stated. For two independent fair binary variables, the four atom probabilities are all `1/4` and sum to `1`; the corresponding moment matrix `E[(1,X1,X2,X1X2)(1,X1,X2,X1X2)ᵀ]` has trace `9/4`. Its eigenvalues must sum to `9/4`, so cannot be those four probabilities. The finite gate constructs the matrix exactly. It is positive semidefinite because it is a Gram expectation.

The correct key relationship is **congruence**, not an assertion that eigenvalues are unchanged by a basis transformation: in the indicator basis, the matrix is diagonal with the atom probabilities; an invertible congruence preserves positive semidefiniteness and hence supports the stated feasibility equivalence. This correction does not undermine the full-moment positive-semidefiniteness result merely because the eigenvalue wording is wrong. Nor does a low-order positive-semidefinite moment matrix by itself establish a valid full joint law: the book's own section 9.2 supplies counterexamples and describes tighter hierarchies.

## Implications for a relational whole

Representation-invariant statistical constraints are useful, but neither feasible moments nor an optimal graph partition entail experience, necessary existence, or a uniquely selected subject. To advance the framework rather than merely compute a partition, a proposal must separately supply (1) an invariant physical or relational domain; (2) an explicit, justified candidate constitution rule; (3) certificates covering **all** admissible tied optima and bounded hidden context; and (4) experiential predictions and independent controls capable of distinguishing rivals. If multiple descriptions remain compatible with the observations, the framework must represent that underdetermination rather than silently select one. None of this establishes universal consciousness or temporal creation of possibilities.
