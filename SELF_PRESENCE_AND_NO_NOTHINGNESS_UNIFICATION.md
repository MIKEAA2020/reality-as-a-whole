# Self-presence and the impossibility of nothingness: one foundation or two?

## The question and result

Can experience's direct self-presence and the impossibility of absolute nothingness be shown to be two aspects of the **same self-existent actuality**, rather than independent assumptions?

The strongest result available from the user's own possibility–actuality principle is sharper than the earlier presentation of the problem:

> **If actual experience is concrete actuality, and strongest genuine possibility equals actuality for the relevant global descriptions, then neither the global absence of experience nor the global absence of concrete actuality is genuinely possible.** No external observer, substrate, creator, or process of actualization is needed for this conditional inference.

Furthermore, under the same modal reading, “there is some concrete actuality” and “there is some concrete experience” are not merely both actually true: their **existential truth values necessarily coincide**. But this still does not show that actuality's *intrinsic nature* is experience, or that the explanation of actuality and that of experience are the same. The principle makes true global claims necessary; it does not by itself identify what makes them true.

This identifies a useful division of labor. **Conditional on the user's strong principle, a separate proof that no absolute nothingness is genuinely possible is not required to establish that particular modal conclusion.** What remains is to justify the principle, establish experiential intrinsic nature and explanatory self-sufficiency, and recover subject plurality and physics. The conditional result is not a new theorem of modal logic or an independent proof of the principle. It is an application that integrates two strands previously handled separately.

## 1. Terms that do not introduce creation or an external viewpoint

Use descriptions of the **complete actual whole** throughout; local occurrences can have different characters without making globally contradictory statements true. Let:

- `E`: at least one actual concrete experience occurs somewhere in the whole. A human experience is enough for this datum; the definition does not assume that the whole is one subject.
- `C`: there is at least one actual concrete occurrence. “Concrete” does not require fundamental matter, spacetime, a process in external time, or physical substance. An actual experience counts as concrete, so `E → C`.
- `A(d)`: global description `d` is actually true of the whole.
- `P(d)`: `d` is genuinely possible in the user's **strongest** sense, not merely describable, mathematically consistent, compatible with partial evidence, or physically possible in an effective model.

The adopted principle is `P(d) ↔ A(d)` for global descriptions within its declared scope. This does **not** say every possible description is made actual, or posit other whole-worlds and a conversion between them. It rules out an unactualized description as genuinely possible. If `P` is instead defined for local realizations, indexed sectors, or a more restricted class of descriptions, the deductions must be rechecked under that definition; the global negative descriptions below cannot silently be substituted into it.

Here `¬E` means **no experience anywhere in the whole**, not that an individual is unconscious or that one time or region lacks experience. `¬C` means no concrete occurrence anywhere, not an empty vacuum. We do not treat either absence as a thing called Nothing.

## 2. Two proposed arguments, one conditional modal constraint

Take as datum `A(E)`. Since experience is concrete, `A(C)` follows. Classical negation and consistent global truth give `¬A(¬E)` and `¬A(¬C)`. Apply the user's principle to these two *negative* global descriptions:

```text
P(¬E) ↔ A(¬E)       and       P(¬C) ↔ A(¬C).
```

Therefore:

```text
¬P(¬E)              and       ¬P(¬C).
```

This is the direct inference: **no genuinely possible global absence of experience; no genuinely possible global absence of concrete actuality.** If the possibility operator is normal classical metaphysical possibility, these are `□E` and `□C` under the usual `□p ≡ ¬◇¬p`. No external possible-world ontology is assumed by writing the modal notation. The conclusion is relative to the stated *genuine*-possibility interpretation: it does not claim that the bare sentence `¬C` is syntactically contradictory in every formal logic.

This corrects the impression that we must first independently prove a no-nothingness thesis and then use it to get necessary experience. **On this adopted global principle and the actual experience datum, both consequences already follow.** We still have not justified the possibility–actuality principle by deriving it from the nature of experience. Nor has calling a description impossible supplied a positive account of why the whole has its particular nature.

### Existential coincidence under the same hypothesis

Both `C` and `E` are actually true. Consequently the global biconditional `C ↔ E` is actually true. If the principle also covers its negation, then:

```text
¬P(¬(C ↔ E)).
```

Equivalently, on the standard modal reading, `□(C ↔ E)`. It says that *there is some concrete occurrence if and only if there is some experience*. This is **not** the stronger thesis that every concrete occurrence is experiential, that every event is a subject, or that the fundamental nature of the whole is experience. And material biconditional—even when necessary—is not a constitutive identity or an explanation of either side.

The limitation is not special to experience: under the full global principle every actual global truth has the corresponding necessity consequence. Thus necessary co-occurrence of two actual facts alone cannot tell us which grounds which, or whether they are two ways of identifying one nature. This is why the sought positive bridge cannot consist solely of proving one more modal equivalence.

### Independent no-nothingness is a different route

One could instead begin by independently establishing that `¬C` is not genuinely possible and then argue that concrete actuality as such is experiential. If a warranted principle says `C → E` at every genuinely admissible global description, the conclusion is necessary `E`. This route does **not** establish the full possibility–actuality principle: necessary experience does not by itself make every true proposition necessary. Nor does it automatically establish a universal subject. The two routes therefore share a possible conclusion but have different explanatory burdens.

## 3. What self-presence contributes positively

In the experience of a sound, feeling or visual contrast, there is not merely a public description of a process. There is an actual qualitative occurrence. Its being experienced does not require a second internal observer to inspect a duplicate of it. This is a plausible one-level account of phenomenal self-presence, distinct from reflective self-judgment and from infallible knowledge of every property of the experience. Phenomenologists have developed related accounts; see the [SEP review](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/win2010/entries/self-consciousness-phenomenological/). It is a philosophical position, not a settled neuroscience measurement.

This provides a positive motivation for the experiential candidate:

1. **It starts from actual occurrence.** Unlike a merely formal specification, experience is not awaiting a separately supplied example to establish that at least one actual instance occurs.
2. **It avoids an extra phenomenal witness.** Explaining presence by positing a separate experience of the first experience would still require an account of the second. One-level self-presence offers a non-duplicative stopping point for *that particular* explanatory demand. Higher-order theories can avoid an infinite regress by treating some higher-order states as non-conscious; their viability is contested, not ruled out by the regress alone.
3. **It offers a non-additive ontology.** If the whole's intrinsic nature is experiential, finite experiences and the organization described by physics could be aspects of one actuality rather than independently complete realities that must be attached by a conversion. This resembles, but is not identical to, approaches discussed under [Russellian monism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/) and cosmopsychism.
4. **It makes self-existence a serious candidate rather than a verbal trick.** The proposal is not that an experience first exists and then makes itself exist. It is that its actuality is intrinsic to what it is. Any internal becoming or local succession is within the whole, not a precondition in external time.

These points are reasons to investigate the identity; they do not yet establish it. In particular, experience's being manifest *when it occurs* does not show that a particular finite experience is independent of the brain or of its other conditions. Self-presence, ontological independence, necessary existence, and full explanatory self-sufficiency are different claims. If “self-existent” is used, as the user intends, to **include** the last claims, it is incorrect to demand an external cause for an already self-existent whole; the task is to show that the actual whole has that nature rather than define it into existence.

## 4. The strongest remaining rival

Compare two proposed accounts of the **same actual history**, including the same real experiences, their directly present character, the same subject reports and the same public physical structure:

- **Experiential base:** the whole's intrinsically experiential constitution is fundamental; physical structure and finite experiences are constituted within it. An adequate account must say how, without just encoding the answers as labels.
- **Neutral base:** the fundamental constitution is neither itself an experience nor an external creator. Its organization constitutes the same actual experiences and physical structure. It is not a denial that experiences are self-present or real, and need not add a second independent substance. It owes an account of why that neutral constitution has the relevant phenomenal consequences.

Both can adopt the user's strong genuine-possibility principle and both can regard their own fundamental constitution as self-existent. They then agree on the conditional necessity of `E`, `C`, and `C ↔ E`. They disagree about **intrinsic nature and constitution**, including statements of grounding that are not part of the shared physical/phenomenal history. This is a comparison of formal accounts—not a claim that two incompatible wholes are both genuinely possible under the user's modal principle. At most one incompatible complete account is correct.

The experiential candidate has a serious advantage **if** it gives a genuinely intelligible account of qualitative character and physical organization without positing an additional neutral-to-phenomenal bridge. But the neutral rival may claim that a single constitution realizes experience intrinsically, without an external conversion process; it must then make that claim intelligible. The experiential account faces the symmetrical burden of explaining why its postulated experiential nature has the particular differentiation, physical powers, subject boundaries and qualitative contents that actually occur. Neither rival wins by counting the word “one” or calling the other's principles “extra” while treating its own unexplained constitution as free.

### Why direct acquaintance is not yet the decisive exclusion

Experiencing provides direct warrant that a concrete experiential occurrence is actual. On the preferred one-level reading it provides warrant for its immediate phenomenal presence. It does not automatically disclose every constitutive or dependence fact about that occurrence. A neutral-base account can accept the *same* presence while claiming that its constitution is more fundamental. An experiential-base account can insist that such a base cannot intelligibly constitute presence without being experiential; that is a promising substantive argument, but it needs to establish why rather than merely restate the preference.

This does not mean both accounts are equally good or that their difference is meaningless. It means the modal equivalences in section 2 and the bare fact of experience do not **by themselves** decide the issue. An independently defended account of constitution, or distinct testable consequences of sufficiently specified physical realizations, could make a difference.

## 5. The precise bridge to investigate

A candidate bridge must say more than “consciousness is self-present, therefore everything is consciousness.” The strongest form worth examining is:

> **Experiential constitution principle:** the complete actuality in virtue of which any genuinely experienced qualitative presence obtains is itself intrinsically experiential; there is no more fundamental, non-experiential constitution that completely accounts for that presence.

If justified for actual experience, this would support experiential fundamentality without requiring every physical subsystem to be a separate experiencing subject. Combined with a justified whole-constitution principle—that the relevant foundation of the actual whole is one and the same as the experiential foundation—it would support the user's whole-first target. Both clauses are substantive. The second does not follow merely from the fact that the experience belongs to the whole: a whole may contain or constitute differentiated aspects without every aspect exhausting its fundamental nature.

Three tests would count as actual progress on the bridge:

1. **Explanatory test:** show why a neutral constitution plus a fully explicit, non-circular constitution rule cannot account for the qualitative presence, while an experiential constitution can. The conclusion must not be built into a definition of “explain.” A demonstration of neutral failure is stronger than observing that its proposed rule has not yet been supplied.
2. **Positive construction:** specify an intrinsically experiential organization that recovers at least one nontrivial physical relation and at least one separately specified qualitative distinction without copying either from the desired output. State what is assumed, what is derived, and what evidence could challenge it. The existence of a neat mathematical encoding is not itself a constitution.
3. **Unity and plurality:** explain how genuine finite subjects are constituted. A universal experiencing subject with the user's intended capacities is an additional claim; an experiential whole or non-agentive background is not automatically that subject. Preserve the conditional non-optimality issue rather than calling the whole perfect by definition.

The same standards apply to the neutral rival. Failing the tests leaves the relevant claims open; it does not show that an experience-free reality or neutral grounding is genuinely possible.

## 6. Result for the whole-account battery

- **Established conditional on the adopted scope:** actual experience plus strongest possibility = actuality excludes both global absence of experience and global absence of concrete actuality. The existential claims have the same necessary truth value under the additional stated scope.
- **Not yet derived:** the possibility–actuality identity, an independently established constitutive identity of existence and experience, or why self-presence entails ontological independence and complete explanation.
- **Not supplied by these results:** a universal agentive subject, multiple genuinely constituted subjects, physical laws, Born weights, local spacetime, a subject measure, or an empirical selection between rival complete ontologies.

The existing [knowability/actuality gate](KNOWABILITY_AND_ACTUALITY_FOUNDATION_GATE.md) already contained the narrower necessity-of-experience implication; this report does not claim it as a new discovery. The [articulated self-presence analysis](ARTICULATED_SELF_PRESENCE.md) supplies the positive local starting point, and the [experiential-grounding rival](EXPERIENTIAL_GROUNDING_RIVAL_BATTERY_GATE.md) supplies a broader comparison. The result here is to **put them into one explicit inference** and show where equivalence of necessity ends and identity of nature begins. The original battery remains entry-incomplete; no finite regression can by itself pass this philosophical bridge.
