# A substantive constitutive argument: non-additive qualitative realization

## Result in brief

The strongest argument now available is not “integration produces consciousness.” It is a **conditional argument for non-additive constitution**:

> If phenomenal occurrences genuinely contribute to the effects through which we think, act, and know them; if those effects admit complete physical causal explanations; and if we reject systematic independent causal duplication, then phenomenal occurrences cannot be independent additional causes alongside those physical realizations. They must belong to the constitution, realization, or identity of the process already doing the causal work.

This constrains the relation sought. It does not establish that causal organization alone is sufficient for experience, identify particular qualities, select subject boundaries, or explain why the constitutive nature exists. The argument's premises are substantive and disputable; the field-theory constructions do not prove them universally.

## 1. Why this is a real bridge attempt

The earlier realized-process proposal only specified a candidate basis and demanded an episode selector Γ and qualitative identification χ. That left open why we should seek constitution at all instead of adding a separate phenomenal assignment.

This argument introduces an explicit reason: the relationship between experience, its causal relevance, and its physically realized consequences. Its conclusion is narrower than reductive physicalism and broader than a new psychophysical interaction law. It supports a **non-additive relation** while leaving several ways to specify that relation open.

No new word—“intrinsic,” “identity,” “qualitative,” or “realization”—counts as the missing explanation. The work is in the premises and the alternatives they permit or exclude.

## 2. State the premises precisely

### P1. Phenomenal occurrence

At least some qualitative episodes occur. This is an experiential starting point of the inquiry, not an inference from a synthetic consciousness label. It does not establish that every judgment about their content, cause, duration, or boundary is correct.

### P2. Relevant phenomenal efficacy

For at least the domain under discussion, some phenomenal differences genuinely contribute to differences in thought, action, or warranted knowledge of those experiences. The claim concerns an appropriate causal/explanatory dependence, not perfect report faithfulness or the thesis that every experience must produce an overt report.

This is stronger than “reports correlate with experience.” It is also not established simply by feeling that one's experience caused an action. A defender must address epiphenomenal alternatives and possible errors of causal interpretation. We do not treat it as an experimentally settled premise across all interventions.

### P3. Physical causal completeness for those effects

The relevant effects have complete physical causal explanations in the claimed domain. “Complete” here means no independent extra phenomenal cause is needed to complete the account of the effects—not merely that one physical factor has been found or that a model predicts a restricted dataset.

This premise does not say that a physical description lacks qualitative character, or that every higher-level explanation is eliminable. In a stochastic theory the causal explanation may concern transition probabilities rather than a deterministic sufficient cause. The exclusion argument must use a causation framework appropriate to that theory; a deterministic slogan cannot silently establish the stochastic case.

The limited free-field constructions do not prove universal physical closure. They demonstrate mathematical compatibility and controlled predictions in specified domains. The completeness premise remains stronger.

Predictive closure must also be distinguished from causal completeness. A physical model might predict the observed effects while leaving their constitutive interpretation unsettled. If “complete” is defined as already excluding every distinct phenomenal contributor, the conclusion has been assumed rather than established. The argument needs a defended causal completeness claim, not merely successful curve fitting or a definition of physics that includes whatever turns out to exist. This is a major unresolved premise, not a result of the mathematical tests.

### P4. No systematic independent causal duplication

We do not postulate two independent complete causes for the same effects merely to retain a separate phenomenal agent in addition to a causally complete physical process.

This is an explanatory nonredundancy commitment, not a theorem that all overdetermination is impossible. Coincident sufficient causes and legitimate explanations at different levels must not be confused with a systematic second independent causal channel. A realization relation may support distinct useful descriptions without producing causal duplication.

## 3. The argument

Assume P1–P4 and consider a relevant experiential contribution under P2.

1. The contribution cannot simply be omitted while retaining P2: that would deny the relevant efficacy rather than explain it.
2. Its physical effects do not require an independent extra cause if P3 is true.
3. Adding experience as another independent complete cause would incur the duplication rejected by P4, under the specified causal account.
4. Therefore the experiential contribution is not an independent causal addition to the physical realization.
5. Its causal relevance must instead be accounted for through a non-independent relation: token identity, constitution, realization, or another explicitly specified dependence compatible with P2–P4.

This is an elimination argument under stated commitments. It does not establish that all four premises are true, nor that the remaining options are one and the same theory.

In particular, step 5 does **not** license the inference:

> experience is not an independent extra cause; therefore every implementation of the same abstract algorithm has the same experience.

That conclusion needs a separate sufficiency and realization argument. The causal basis may contain material, temporal, state-dependent, or other features omitted from the algorithm.

## 4. Stress-test the conclusion against alternatives

| Position | What it does with the premises | What the present argument says |
|---|---|---|
| Experience is causally inert but correlated with physical events | Rejects P2 | Not ruled out by physical closure alone; owes an account of experiential knowledge and the correlations |
| Experience adds an independent influence missing from physical dynamics | Rejects P3 | Requires a richer causal theory; the present mathematical physics does not establish this route or exclude it universally |
| Physical and phenomenal causes independently duplicate one another | Rejects P4 | Remains a logical option if that explanatory cost is defended |
| Phenomenal occurrences are identical with the relevant actual processes | Retains the commitments through identity | Compatible with the conclusion, but must specify and justify the identities |
| Phenomenal properties are realized by physical processes without being independent causal agents | Retains the commitments through realization | Also compatible; strict property identity has not been established |
| One constitutive nature has physical and qualitative presentations | Can retain the commitments if the presentations do not introduce independent causal duplication | Compatible only when the shared nature and its consequences are specified rather than asserted |

These are conceptual escape routes, not numerical models whose assigned truth labels prove their metaphysical possibility. The table identifies what a position must defend. It does not assign probabilities or select a winner by counting assumptions.

## 5. Why causal exclusion does not finish identity

A higher-level explanation can be causally legitimate without adding an independent physical force. For example, an organization-level property may be realized by the very physical process that explains its effects. Whether a phenomenal property has that relationship is the hypothesis to justify; the general possibility prevents the exclusion argument from proving strict reductive property identity.

Likewise, discovering that two descriptions concern one cause does not derive why that cause has a particular qualitative character. The argument constrains **causal placement**; it does not supply χ.

Nor does it determine Γ. Several physical processes may contribute to one report or action. Their causal relevance does not prove they belong to one episode. A report about an episode and the episode's constitutive boundary are distinct targets.

Thus three levels must remain separate:

1. **Non-additivity:** experience is not an extra independent causal agent alongside its complete realization.
2. **Sufficiency:** a specified constitutive basis fixes phenomenal character and incidence.
3. **Identity and ultimate explanation:** why that basis is this experience, how it composes, and why that nature is actual.

The argument supports level 1 conditionally. It does not establish levels 2 or 3.

## 6. The evidence problem: don't use causal efficacy as a hidden report axiom

The source/monitor example shows the danger. In that model the report reads the source. If we assume that the target experience is whatever directly causes that report, we select the source interpretation by assumption.

P2 is weaker than universal report faithfulness, but it still needs a warranted application to a specific phenomenal contrast. Knowing that experience is sometimes causally relevant does not identify every cause of a report with an experience, nor does it guarantee access during an intervention that alters monitoring or memory.

A responsible use of the argument must therefore:

- declare the effects and phenomenal contrast in scope;
- distinguish causal relevance from mere correlation;
- justify the reliability of the experience/evidence relation in that domain;
- state the physical completeness assumption and its limits;
- avoid defining the target as the report and then claiming to discover the constitution of experience.

It remains possible to defend experiential efficacy through an identity theory rather than as an independent premise. But if the identity theory is itself what the argument is supposed to establish, using it as the sole warrant for P2 would be circular. That version must be presented as a mutually supporting explanatory proposal, not a deductive derivation from independently settled premises.

## 7. The positive constitutive principle this motivates

The resulting candidate is **non-additive qualitative realization**:

> The phenomenal occurrence is the qualitative occurrence of the realized process itself, or a non-independently realized feature of it; it is not a second observer or causal agent attached to a physical representation. A completed theory must identify the constitution that makes its physical powers, qualitative character, and episode incidence aspects of one actual process.

The disjunction between identity and non-independent realization is intentional. Removing it would overstate the argument. A later constitutive theory might justify one relation specifically.

This principle connects the physical and phenomenal targets more substantively than pure representational invariance. It says where to look and what explanatory duplication to avoid under P1–P4. It does not make “the process itself” a completed answer. That phrase still owes a specified constitutive nature and a law relating it to Γ and χ.

The phase-coherent and nonquasifree examples are relevant only as adequacy warnings: if a candidate's constitutive basis discards physically real distinctions, it needs an argument that those distinctions are irrelevant. They do not establish that any particular coherence or correlation is necessary for experience.

## 8. What survives the attempt

The inquiry now has a defensible **conditional bridge argument**, rather than another unmotivated declaration of unity:

- experiential efficacy plus physical completeness and nonduplication motivate non-additive constitution;
- they do not select abstract functional sufficiency, biological exclusivity, strict property identity, a qualitative map, or a subject boundary;
- refusing any premise is possible, but makes the rival explanatory commitments explicit.

This is a substantive narrowing of the proposed account only for those who can justify the premises in the relevant domain. It is not a proof of a unified theory and not an empirical consciousness discovery.

The remaining task is still positive: specify the constitutive nature and defend its qualitative and compositional consequences. Naming that nature “intrinsic” does not discharge the task. The zero-residue objective also requires an account of its foundation rather than exempting the proposed identity from explanation.

No new executable test suite is appropriate for declaring these philosophical premises true. The existing 1,048 tests remain tests of the specified mathematical constructions and conditional obstructions.
