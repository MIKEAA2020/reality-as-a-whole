# Placebo, nociception and pain: full-paper audit

## Source and scope

Botvinik-Nezer et al., [*Placebo treatment affects brain systems related to affective and cognitive processes, but not nociceptive pain*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50103-8), is a study of experimentally induced placebo analgesia, not a study of string vibrations or the intrinsic nature of experience. Read the article's complete substantive text, methods, captions and all four main tables; read the text and tables of the [Supplementary Information](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-024-50103-8/MediaObjects/41467_2024_50103_MOESM1_ESM.pdf), [peer review file](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-024-50103-8/MediaObjects/41467_2024_50103_MOESM2_ESM.pdf), and [reporting summary](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-024-50103-8/MediaObjects/41467_2024_50103_MOESM3_ESM.pdf), via publisher text extraction. Main figures 2–6 and supplementary figures 1–7 were visually inspected from the authors' [figure files at the pinned repository commit](https://github.com/rotemb9/paingen-placebo-fmri-paper/tree/da46f2f9739ad2e9eeb9658711582be6357c1026/figures); figure 1 was visually inspected only as a cropped, low-resolution search thumbnail, together with its complete caption and design description. Graph points were not independently digitized. Source citations in the paper are not thereby individually verified.

At the time of this paper audit, the project's prior analysis had inspected and computed from an author-provided condition-mean CSV, not trial records or raw MRI. A later [BIDS event provenance audit](PAINGEN_BIDS_TRIAL_ORDER_AND_MEAN_PROVENANCE_AUDIT.md) inspected the released event files and found internally paired ratings, but hundreds of differences between provisional trial averages and the condition export remain unresolved. The original analysis pipeline was not comprehensively audited or replayed, and the imaging, preregistration record and full OpenNeuro dataset were not independently inspected. The article states that data are available as [OpenNeuro ds004746](https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds004746.v1.0.0) and code in the [authors' repository](https://github.com/rotemb9/paingen-placebo-fmri-paper). The summaries below report authors' analyses, unless explicitly marked as ours.

## Design and analysis

The sample comes from a community twin cohort; 397 participants constituted the preregistered dataset. Two were removed for corrupt behavioral data and three more for inadequate complete condition sets. The authors report 392 for neuroimaging and 374 for behavioral analyses; these are different analysis samples, not independent cohorts. Family and participant effects were modeled to account for twin and repeated-measures structure.

The same inert cream was called an effective analgesic on one finger and a control on another. Suggestion and sham examples of other participants' ratings were followed by thermal conditioning: the placebo-site temperature was covertly lowered by 3.5 °C during conditioning. In the test, nominal heat levels 46.5, 47 and 47.5 °C and pressure levels approximately 6, 7 and 8 kg/cm² were applied to counterbalanced fingers, in control–placebo–placebo–control runs. Mechanical pain was not directly conditioned. Participants rated both pain intensity and unpleasantness, with their order randomized. The training analogy distinguished loudness from how much one likes a song. The intensity and unpleasantness ratings were highly correlated within participants: median trialwise r = 0.956 among the 372 with calculable correlations. This is not evidence that the concepts or experiences are identical.

There were 32 test stimuli, eight per run; each modality-by-treatment combination had eight stimuli distributed across three levels. The main Methods and authors' peer-review response describe 10-second stimulation and a 0.5-second cue; the reporting summary and figure 1 describe 12-second stimulation and a 1-second cue. Without checking the actual task files we cannot reconcile these descriptions. This discrepancy does not affect the published aggregate comparison by itself, but matters for any trial-level replay.

The fMRI analysis used fMRIPrep, 6 mm smoothing and a canonical hemodynamic response model with separate regressors for 12 stimulus conditions. NPS and SIIPS are weighted whole-brain pattern scores; region-of-interest scores are averages, while SIIPS subregions use local weighted dot products. These quantities are neither direct measurements of individual neurons nor interchangeable measures of the same physical quantity. Models used stimulus level and placebo condition, with interaction tested and removed from the reported mean-effects model if not significant. Two-sided tests generally use uncorrected p < .05, with within-family-of-regions Bonferroni notes. Bayes factors compare specified alternative and null models and priors, not the probability that nociception itself is absent.

Preregistration followed preprocessing and preceded the full hypothesized-outcome analysis, but **behavioral results, NPS and Bayes-factor analyses were not preregistered**. The NPS analysis had already been run on part of the sample before preregistration; the complete-sample test was run afterward. Seven of the SIIPS subregions were additional and not preregistered, and one of seven nociceptive regions was not preregistered. The reviewer-requested standardized NPS–SIIPS comparison was added on revision. A priori and preregistered are not synonyms.

## Results and what they do—and do not—show

| Outcome | Thermal | Mechanical | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intensity rating | placebo mean 0.129; control 0.183; d = 0.53 | placebo 0.097; control 0.134; d = 0.34 | Mean reported pain decreased in both modalities; scale is not linear, so numbers are not percentages. |
| Unpleasantness rating | placebo 0.109; control 0.162 | placebo 0.095; control 0.130 | Supplement reports lower unpleasantness in both; this is a distinct report, though highly correlated with intensity. |
| NPS | placebo coefficient −0.030, 95% CI [−0.101, 0.041]; BF10 0.044 | 0.008, CI [−0.064, 0.081]; BF10 0.036 | Evidence favors the specified null over a wide-prior alternative for the group-level NPS effect; not proof that all nociceptive processing is unchanged or that every individual has zero effect. |
| SIIPS | coefficient −0.129, CI [−0.195, −0.063], d = 0.19 | −0.112, CI [−0.185, −0.039], d = 0.15 | A distributed pain-predictive pattern decreased; it is not a pure unpleasantness meter or an identified physical mismatch q. |
| Direct standardized SIIPS–NPS contrast | 0.100, CI [0.010, 0.189], p = .030 | 0.124, CI [0.036, 0.211], p = .006 | Supports a difference between these standardized pattern-score responses, under the analysis and standardization used; not a direct comparison of all nociception with all affect. |

The mixed-model coefficients are standardized within modality; reported means are on the 0–1 labeled magnitude scale, whose anchors are quasi-logarithmic (0.061 weak, 0.172 moderate, 0.354 strong). Reported d values do not themselves adjust for family structure. Unpleasantness means are from the supplement, and the prior project calculation below uses a different pair-weighted estimand and sample; their numerical differences are not a contradiction.

For thermal stimulation, none of seven prespecified nociception-associated ROI means showed a significant placebo decrease. For mechanical stimulation six of seven showed **increases**, while the periaqueductal gray did not. These are localized BOLD associations, not direct proof of increased peripheral nociception. The authors propose prediction error and altered threat-related analgesia as possible explanations, not established mechanisms. The NPS group effect may be near zero while some regions increase: a weighted aggregate is not each of its components.

The SIIPS was designed to predict pain above stimulus intensity and NPS, but itself rose with stimulus intensity in both modalities. Authors explicitly caution against equating SIIPS with affect: high intensity–unpleasantness correlations and imperfect pain specificity leave its precise evaluative contribution unresolved. In thermal higher-level ROIs, only posterior left dlPFC survived the stated within-set Bonferroni correction; mechanical effects included this region and bilateral nucleus accumbens. Many local results were weaker or inconsistent across modalities. SIIPS's placebo Bayes factor was sensitive to the model including an interaction: supplementary table 3 gives BF10 about 93.9 without versus 1.1 with interaction for thermal, and 30.4 versus 0.352 for mechanical. These compare different models and should not be misread as a standalone test that an interaction exists.

Within-person control–placebo differences in both NPS and SIIPS correlated with reported analgesia. Such associations do **not** establish mediation or show that participants with stronger placebo responses causally reduced these markers: different finger sensitivity, measurement variation and time effects can jointly influence neural and rating differences. Behavioral contrasts correlated across modalities, whereas the neural contrasts did not reach significance across modalities; this is not proof of zero stability. Authors suggest independent classification of responders in a separate session with other skin sites.

## Peer-review and limitations

The reviewer challenged whether comparison of one significant and one nonsignificant result justified a difference, and the authors added a direct standardized contrast. The reviewer also raised low ratings, scale training versus verbal/trackball and unlabeled measurement, few repetitions, symbolic-conditioning demand characteristics, individual differences, and misuse of “calibration.” Authors clarified that familiarization was not individual stimulus calibration, documented the training scale and instructions, and pointed to stimulus-intensity sensitivity of NPS and ratings as positive controls. The reviewer ultimately considered the clarifications and new contrast strengthening, but retained concern about inconsistent rating methods.

Positive stimulus sensitivity shows signal is measurable, not that every small intervention effect or its individual heterogeneity can be detected. Bayesian comparisons depend on priors, models and numerical approximation (some supplementary BF error estimates are appreciable). Fixed run order, distinct fingers, combined suggestion/conditioning and nonblinded experimenters constrain causal attribution to components. The paper argues counterbalancing skin assignment and symmetric run order reduce average site/order confounding, not that individual differences are identified. Demand-related changes in reporting remain a possible contributor; similar effect sizes to earlier studies and a nonsignificant expectation–SIIPS correlation do not eliminate it. Conversely, questioning exact report fidelity is not a reason to deny or dismiss experienced pain.

Other authors' limitations include predominantly white adults, generally less-than-moderate pain especially under mechanical stimulation, possible signal dropout with multiband acceleration, canonical hemodynamic response choice, and sensitivity to analytical pipelines. Pattern markers may miss changed spatial topographies, are not complete or equally accurate for every individual, and SIIPS specificity for pain is less established. The paper does not independently intervene on SIIPS or establish it as a mediator.

## Consequences for the project

1. **Our restricted external-stimulus proxy is inadequate as a unique predictor of reported unpleasantness.** In the same study's author CSV, our [matched-cell analysis](MATCHED_STIMULUS_UNPLEASANTNESS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_BRIDGE.md) found 2,155 complete person × modality × nominal-level condition pairs and a mean control-minus-placebo difference of 0.04466 on that scale; its bootstrap and oracle error floor have the limited meanings documented there. The present article's manipulation and results support that interpretation, but nominal matching is not independent verification of delivered waveforms or identical afferent activity. The retrospective calculation is not an independent replication.
2. **This is not a test of intrinsic misalignment.** Neither NPS nor SIIPS measures the proposed physical/relational mismatch q. Lower report with a near-null group NPS does not falsify a candidate that permits q to vary with expectation, nor does it verify one. If q is defined solely by nominal external stimulus and its claimed mapping requires the same reported output, that restricted operational claim faces the paired data, subject to report and measurement limitations. Prediction errors discussed by the authors are a distinct theoretical construct, not measured string-vibration mismatch.
3. **Do not upgrade a report prediction into an experiential bridge.** Experience remains a valid datum; self-report is evidence about it but can be affected by interpretation and response processes. Even a well-validated physical predictor would not by itself derive why a state is unpleasant, who experiences it, or why one structure rather than an empirically indistinguishable rival has a given phenomenal character. B4, B5 and B10 remain open; C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7.
4. **Next discriminating design.** Operationalize q independently of reports and predeclare its scale, invariances and direction of prediction; measure actual stimulation and skin sensitivity, randomize/counterbalance site and treatment order, separately manipulate expectation and stimulation, and use independently assessed pain reports plus behavioral and physiological outcomes. Test held-out people, families, modalities and settings; compare q against stimulus, condition, NPS and SIIPS baselines, including reliability and missingness. Independently classify responders on other sites or sessions before estimating individual brain–behavior effects. Such tests could reject a specific operational proposal, but would not alone establish a metaphysical identity or full experiential intelligibility.

The results favor a context-sensitive, multilevel empirical account over a simple equation of delivered stimulus with discomfort. They do not decide whether relational misalignment grounds unpleasantness, whether all genuine possibilities are actual, or whether a self-sufficient experiential whole exists.
