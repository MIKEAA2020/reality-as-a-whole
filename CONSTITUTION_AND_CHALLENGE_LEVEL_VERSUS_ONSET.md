# One constitution, one rival, and a decision that could challenge both

## The answer in brief

The most useful next test is not another physical echo calculation. It is to take **one existing psychophysical candidate**, expose the exact identity it asserts, and compare it with an experience-respecting rival at a place where they genuinely disagree.

The existing E-L candidate says, in a restricted four-spin sector, that a selected subject's designated unpleasant-versus-neutral contrast **is** its current internal mismatch `q¹`. A rival says the corresponding contrast is **onset** of mismatch, `q⁰=0, q¹=1`. Both have the same physical system and the same proposed subject rule. They make opposite experiential assignments when mismatch persists (`1→1`).

The decisive point is that the physical equations determine the mismatch history, but **do not themselves determine which of those two assignments is the felt one**. The proposed identity would not need a force to connect a physical state to a separate feeling. But it needs substantive support for identifying *this* state with *this* quality, just as the specific physical and geometrical premises of the relativity derivation need support.

We can state a quantitative challenge. Among trials *classified* as persistent mismatch, let `ρ` bound errors in the physical history and `η` bound errors in the recorded quality contrast. If `ρ+η<1/2`, the two completed candidates have disjoint ranges of possible positive-report rates, even without assuming independent errors. An observed intermediate rate can challenge **both**. Existing code supplies an exact toy physical-noise illustration, not a real sensor or a validated phenomenal reference.

**Verdict:** this is an end-to-end *conditional test specification*, not a completed real-world test or a demonstration of experiential identity. The real bottleneck is to identify the physical relation and the subject's episode in an actual system, independently of the quality data used to test it. [Executable checks](verification/constitution_challenge_decision_test.py).

**Access correction:** the [Ventura full-text reassessment](VENTURA_HARD_PROBLEM_FULL_TEXT_AND_ACCESS_REASSESSMENT.md) makes explicit that, when the complete physical description also fixes a common report law, one report cannot reliably match both incompatible quality assignments on their disagreement histories. The earlier same-spin-kernel comparison does not prove that both assignments have reliable access or the same complete subject/report constitution.

## 1. The one candidate, with all parts visible

This is a deliberately narrow use of the earlier [integrated experiential-misalignment candidate](INTEGRATED_EXPERIENTIAL_MISALIGNMENT_CANDIDATE.md), not a new complete theory or an alteration of a frozen dossier. It uses the existing **classical four-spin sector**, not the quantum echo from the immediately preceding passes. Both address the same constitutive question and the earlier coordinate/access cautions inform this test, but no physical mapping or derivation between the two models is claimed. The four-spin sector is chosen because its pre-existing, explicit quality assignment and history rival make the decisive disagreement and a possible human-facing challenge more concrete.

**Ground.** E-L hypothesizes a self-sufficient whole whose intrinsic nature is experiential. It does not posit an external creator, universal observer or process that attaches feelings. Neither the finite dynamics below nor this test derives that ground, establishes its actuality, or proves that the whole has one subject.

**Physical domain.** Its worked sector has four signs `sᵢ∈{−1,+1}`, a specified equilibrium distribution, and an explicitly chosen random-site heat-bath update. The pair `(0,1)` has a preferred sign relation `τ=+1`. For the two ordered states define

```text
qᵏ = (1 − τ s₀ᵏs₁ᵏ)/2,     k=0,1.
```

`q=1` means the selected pair's present relation differs from the stipulated preference; `q=0` means it agrees. This is *current state mismatch*, not minimum frustration, energy dissipation, a stimulus intensity or a neural measure. The finite spin model is not asserted to be literally realized in a person or derived from the whole.

**Subject and episode.** At the model's specified one-update causal grain and `t=1/4` coupling, its existing L rule selects `{0,1}` as one of two local pair candidates. For this quality test, E-L **postulates** that this pair constitutes one actual subject and that the ordered states belong to the relevant episode. L and that postulate do not establish a real subject or persistence. At a different causal horizon, the same L calculation can select the whole rather than the pairs; there is no established universal grain ([horizon audit](INTEGRATED_SUBJECT_HORIZON_AND_VALENCE_AUDIT.md)).

**Quality identity.** On the designated domain, E-L postulates a binary contrast `U` of unpleasant versus neutral:

```text
E-L-level: U = q¹.
```

The claim is **identity**, not that q pushes, generates or externally produces an additional mental object. The word “unpleasant” is a substantive first-person reference, not a label fixed by the spin algebra. This deliberately restricted contrast does not represent all qualities, degrees of suffering, or mixtures of pleasure and pain. If the target episodes cannot be adequately described as binary, this specific test must stop or be redesigned rather than force them into the categories.

**Incidence and measure.** The toy equilibrium and chosen update give sampling probabilities for physical histories. Neither a sampled transition nor a probability is, without further justification, the actuality or census of an experience. The candidate's subject-selection, persistence and ground are not fixed by measuring q.

## 2. The experience-respecting rival

Hold the same spin law, preferred relation, L-selected pair, target episode and postulated field fixed. Change only the proposed quality assignment:

```text
E-L-onset: U = 1 exactly when q⁰=0 and q¹=1; otherwise U=0.
```

This is a sharp formal rival, not a claim that real affect is exclusively onset-driven. It says an onset is unpleasant and that the *designated contrast* is neutral on persistent mismatch; it does not say a subject or all experience disappears. It is not a coordinate change of the level assignment: with physical states and the neutral/unpleasant reference held fixed, the assignments differ on a real, positive-weight set of histories. In particular, rescaling the quality coordinates as in the [echo coordinate analysis](ZERO_SR_ECHO_COORDINATE_EQUIVALENCE_AND_CONTENT.md) cannot turn persistent mismatch into onset while preserving the same quality labels and decoding.

Both hypotheses are proposed descriptions, not a proof of distinct complete possible realities under the user's strong modal thesis. The same physical readouts have been stipulated in the underlying model; a real report channel sensitive to the quality could produce different observations and must be included as additional structure rather than silently assumed unchanged.

### The exact internal comparison

For equilibrium at middle coupling `t=1/4` followed by one update, the existing [verified history law](CURRENT_MISMATCH_VERSUS_HISTORY_EXPERIENTIAL_GATE.md) gives:

| Physical history `(q⁰,q¹)` | Model probability | Level `U` | Onset `U` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `00` | `295/448` | neutral | neutral |
| `01` | `41/448` | unpleasant | unpleasant |
| `10` | `41/448` | neutral | neutral |
| **`11`** | **`71/448`** | **unpleasant** | **neutral** |

Thus `71/448≈15.85%` is the **toy physical probability of a disagreement**, not the probability of pain or of a real subject. The result is rederived from the existing stationary distribution and transition kernel in the accompanying tests. It is independent of the names of the two spin signs: transforming `sᵢ` and the preferred relation `τ` together leaves `q` and the history assignments unchanged. A change of preference without transporting it is a change of physical model, not mere redescription.

This is what the arithmetic establishes: *given* either identity postulate, the claimed contrast at each history follows. It does not establish either postulate. Compare SR: a rod worldtube, Lorentz geometry and a simultaneity relation determine the contracted length without external squeezing; unlike our present quality assignment, the relevant physical and measurement relations are independently supported. The missing work is not to find an outside force but to establish the appropriate physical–experiential constitution and why it applies.

## 3. A test that can reject a package

Suppose a real candidate implementation and target episode have first been justified. Let `C=(q⁰,q¹)` be the true physical history, `H` its independently measured classification, `U` the specified quality contrast, and `Y` the person's recorded, anchored answer (`1`=unpleasant, `0`=neutral). Consider the population classified as `H=11`, with nonzero probability. Suppose the following **conditional bounds in that population**, justified independently of the quality hypothesis being evaluated:

```text
P(C≠11 | H=11) ≤ ρ,
P(Y≠U   | H=11) ≤ η.
```

No independence between the errors is required. First-person experience is evidence in its own right; η concerns the interpretation and recording of the *specified contrast in the specified window*, not a claim that subjects cannot know their experience. It cannot be justified just by assuming the level or onset assignment is true. If the only established outcome is the answer Y itself, one may instead test operational hypotheses *defined in terms of Y*; the conclusion is then about answers, not an independently calibrated phenomenal identity.

Write `r=P(Y=1 | H=11)` and `b=ρ+η`. By a union bound, for the two exact quality identities:

```text
level implies r ≥ max(0,1−b),
onset implies r ≤ min(1,b).
```

If **`b<1/2`**, these ranges do not overlap. This is a sufficient conditional discrimination criterion, not a necessary one. When the full true-history mixture `p_ab=P(C=ab|H=11)` is independently identified, the underlying positive-quality fractions are `p_01+p_11` for level and `p_01` for onset. With only a common bound η, their report-rate ranges are disjoint if **`p_11>2η`**. This is the sharper result already developed in the [assay-error gate](HISTORY_MISMATCH_FIRST_PERSON_ASSAY_ERROR_GATE.md). Neither bound exists for real human data yet.

### A fully labeled hypothetical worked example

Reusing the [physical calibration construction](FOUR_SPIN_PHYSICAL_MISMATCH_MEASUREMENT_GATE.md), suppose the toy model's preference is estimated independently from 19 perfectly read independent equilibrium states, each spin reading in the subsequent episode has independent 5% error, and the calibration outcome is independently drawn for each episode. The existing exact model gives:

```text
P(H=11) ≈ 0.15509,
ρ = P(C≠11 | H=11) ≈ 0.17041,
p_11 ≈ 0.82959.
```

Assume additionally—not observe—that a defensible target-specific bound is `η≤0.10`. Then `b≈0.27041`, and:

| Completed package | Allowed positive-report rate `r` in classified `11` |
|---|---:|
| Level | at least `0.72959` |
| Onset | at most `0.27041` |
| Either exact package | **neither** permits the open interval between these bounds |

The checks independently reconstruct the physical history and noisy posterior using exact fractions. These are **illustrative model numbers**; the 19-state calibration failure is averaged across repeated independent calibrations. A single shared erroneous calibration can contaminate every trial and requires a different conditional and clustered analysis. The numerical bound on η is purely invented and may be exceptionally difficult to establish.

A stronger, additional *common report channel* assumption gives point predictions, not implied by the error bounds alone. If in every true history the false-positive rate is `0.10` and sensitivity is `0.90` under both packages, the same toy posterior yields `r_level≈0.80426` and `r_onset≈0.14059`. Real report errors may depend on history, context, person and model, so these point values cannot be treated as empirical estimates. Without an informative channel—for example sensitivity and false-positive rate both `1/2`—the two output rates are equal despite different stipulated qualities.

## 4. What an actual outcome would mean

For a fixed, independently justified `b<1/2`, construct a simultaneous uncertainty interval `[L,U]` for r using a prespecified procedure appropriate to the design, as well as uncertainty regions for the calibration bounds. If b is known, the logical rules are:

- `U < 1−b`: reject the **completed level package** under the physical, target and assay assumptions.
- `L > b`: reject the **completed onset package** under those assumptions.
- If both inequalities hold, reject both exact packages; a mixture, a different quality relation, or a failed assumption is among the possibilities.
- If neither holds, the comparison is inconclusive. Failure to reject is not proof of an identity.

An observed contradiction never identifies *which* component failed automatically: physical measurement, selected episode, subject, the quality identity, report calibration or sampling assumptions may be wrong. If calibration is estimated, use a joint confidence region and reject only when it excludes the relevant combined admissible region; do not spend a nominal 5% error budget separately on every component while claiming a 5% total error.

### Finite-sample arithmetic, not a human sample-size recommendation

For illustration only, assume `b≈0.27041` is known, selected reports are independent identically distributed, and a fixed `n=36` classified-11 episodes are available. The two-sided Hoeffding radius at 95% is about `0.22635`. If `18/36` are positive, the interval is approximately `[0.27365,0.72635]`, strictly inside the gap: **both** exact packages are rejected at the stated simultaneous-coverage level. Extreme outcomes `0/36` and `36/36` reject level and onset respectively. This is a decision illustration, not a guarantee of power; outcomes near a boundary can be inconclusive. The threshold is chosen in advance, and there is no repeated peeking or optional stopping in this calculation.

The toy `P(H=11)≈0.15509` implies that **294 independent episodes** give at least a 95% probability of obtaining 36 or more classified-11 episodes (293 do not), by the exact binomial tail. This assumes the identical independently calibrated physical model for each episode, no dropouts, and selecting a fixed number without outcome-dependent inclusion. Nineteen separate calibration states per episode would itself require 5,586 independent calibration states in this idealization. These assumptions and costs are not justified for an actual study; repeated trials from one person, a common calibration, missing responses, finite calibration uncertainty or drift require cluster-aware design and a new calculation. The 95% probability of enough episodes is **not** a 95% probability of rejecting a false theory. No empirical precision or safety claim follows.

## 5. A realistic translation: prerequisites, controls and stopping rules

This is a study-development plan, **not a preregistration, completed experiment or instruction to induce pain**. An ethical human study would use an approved, informed-consent protocol, minimal-risk individually tolerable stimulation, stop rules and appropriate clinical oversight. If the physical and experiential prerequisites cannot be met, do not run or report this as a test of the q identity.

### Stage A — independently specify the physical candidate

Before inspecting test outcomes, a physical theory and domain expert would have to specify:

1. The physical degrees of freedom and interaction that correspond to the candidate pair, the admissible subject/episode boundary, and how their measurements relate to state variables. The four spins are not a neural map.
2. The preferred relation and how it is identified **without using the held-out unpleasantness ratings**, including rival channels, confounding, measurement units, time resolution, sensor error and uncertainty. Net coupling need not identify a separately preferred channel; selective intervention requires an independently validated exclusion or residual-effect bound.
3. The two physical windows, the transformation from continuous/noisy observations to `(q⁰,q¹)`, and the positive-overlap criterion for all history cells. Freeze the pipeline and calibration data before held-out evaluation. If the system is not binary, justify coarse-graining or declare this specific binary candidate inapplicable; do not tune thresholds until it agrees with ratings.

The previously analyzed PAINgen condition table does **not** provide those physical variables, preferences or a validated mapping. Its condition averages can inform baselines but cannot execute this q test; its provisional trial reconstruction also has unresolved provenance discrepancies. Temperature, fMRI signatures, EEG amplitude, intensity ratings and expectation are not q by definition. We do not currently have a demonstrated real-world implementation of Stage A.

### Stage B — define and anchor the experience independently

Select a prespecified current-episode **unpleasantness** question and neutral reference, time window, response handling and exclusions in consultation with participants and relevant experts. Separately collect intensity, relief/pleasantness, uncertainty and context so they are not silently equated with unpleasantness. Pilot feasibility and interpretation without reusing held-out outcomes to define q. A single report may be important first-person evidence but does not automatically supply an independently validated error probability, solve every response bias, or establish the subject's persistence. Repeated or multiple proxies may share biases; agreement alone is not a gold standard.

Specify what independent evidence or sensitivity assumptions would support η in the actual target conditions, and report a range if it cannot be bounded. If binary quality coding is inappropriate or η and physical uncertainty cannot separate predictions, **stop with an inconclusive identity test**; do not set the assumptions to the values that favor a result. Report direct experience evidence and limitations rather than discarding it.

### Stage C — compare alternatives fairly

If Stages A and B are met, use ethically approved randomized history protocols to seek overlap among the four measured histories. Specify baseline and alternative predictors in advance, including current mismatch, onset, a mixed level-plus-history model, stimulus/context and established physical predictors where available. Match reporting, analysis flexibility and training resources; evaluate on held-out participants or independent cohorts. A design whose observed `11` group is too rare or lacks a validated classification is inconclusive, not a negative finding.

Randomizing the protocol does **not** automatically randomize q: q is measured after assignment, and conditioning on it can introduce selection bias. Prespecify how missingness, clustering, calibration uncertainty, attention, arousal, expectations, tissue exposure, report timing and history effects are addressed. An assignment effect is an effect of the whole assigned protocol; isolating the causal role of q requires additional justified assumptions or selective convergent interventions. Where possible, include manipulations designed to change q with different side effects and verify selectivity rather than assuming it.

An empirical disagreement supports or challenges a **completed operational package** in its domain, subject to its measurement premises. Prediction and intervention can increase confidence that a particular physical relation tracks a quality; they do not by themselves prove that this relation is what the quality *is*, pick a unique subject boundary, or establish the experiential ground of the whole.

## 6. The deeper why: what is and is not answered

The candidate makes a genuine constructive move: if the identified mismatch is the experienced contrast, then its being unpleasant is not something an outside agent must add. The proposed identity could be an ontological identity even if it was discovered empirically rather than deduced from ordinary meanings. But our demonstration presently stops at the following unresolved dependency:

```text
self-sufficient experiential whole          proposed, not derived
  → physical laws, admissible grain          not yet derived from that ground
  → pair/subject and episode                  L and persistence postulated, not established
  → physical mismatch history                 derived within stipulated finite model
  → specific felt contrast                    level identity postulated, rival also fits physics
  → report and actual incidence               independent access and measure not established
```

That is not a declaration that the open premises are ultimate bedrock. Nor does it prove that no deeper unified constitution could determine them. The next substantive inquiry, if a defensible human physical mapping cannot yet be built, is to derive or justify **why this relation and this grain, rather than onset or another relation, are constitutive of the indicated quality and subject**. Claims that “mismatch feels bad” or “a whole experiences itself” merely repeat the proposed conclusion. The neutral-ground rival, if constructed to have the same physical and phenomenal facts, would also not be eliminated by this experiment alone; both accounts owe a coherent grounding explanation.
