# Wave-function realism, relational structure and plural experience

## What the supplied paper establishes

Alyssa Ney, [*Three arguments for wave function realism*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13194-023-00554-5), examines whether a quantum state should be understood as a **field on a high-dimensional configuration space**. Her answer is notably cautious: none of the three arguments establishes that this is the true ontology, although she regards the framework as practically useful and worth developing. It is not the same position as denying quantum reality, and it is not a conclusion that the universe has one consciousness.

I read all 18 pages of the [supplied paper](sources/uploads/Three-arguments-for-wave-function-realism.pdf), including footnotes and references, using extracted text, and visually checked pages 1, 5–7 and 15. The two displayed state equations on page 5 were checked against the page image. The article includes no scientific figures or experimental dataset; the small page-1 image is publisher branding. This is a philosophical assessment, not a new experiment or a proof that one interpretation is true. The article itself cites many further works that I have **not** fully inspected; selected additional sources and their scopes appear below. The supplied PDF is unchanged, SHA-256 `d1d6f810abe30650487c4f59ac7dd2df1a061bb3678cfc7b3463b5e3b9e9761a`. It identifies its license as [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

## The three arguments and Ney's assessment

1. **Prima facie argument:** wave functions are central to state specification and dynamics. This is a reason to take the mathematics seriously, but not enough to infer that it denotes a literal field in high-dimensional configuration space. A state vector, density operator or law-like object are different ontological interpretations of overlapping formal tools.
2. **Entanglement argument:** independently specified low-dimensional, single-particle position distributions miss correlations in entangled states. A field on configuration space captures them. But Ney points out that this does not uniquely favor that ontology: relational holism, spacetime-state realism, multi-field and primitive-ontology proposals also encode the distinction, with different commitments. It is not correct to say that *every* ontology based in low-dimensional space is restricted to independent local probability distributions.
3. **Separability and locality:** the configuration-space field promises a picture where facts and dynamics are separable/local in that high-dimensional space. Ney regards the simplicity and intuitive appeal of such a picture as practical reasons to investigate it, not as decisive evidence that it is fundamental. These are locality and separability **relative to the proposed fundamental space**; they do not automatically become ordinary spacetime locality. She notes that extending the locality story to collapse theories such as GRW is controversial and requires further argument. On her account spacetime-state realism is nonseparable in spacetime; primitive-ontology variants can instead retain local beables while accepting nonlocal dynamics.

Ney explicitly says her view falls short of belief that wave functions really are fields in configuration space; she is still a scientific realist about well-supported aspects of quantum theory. She also distinguishes a configuration-space field from a ray in Hilbert space. A Hilbert-space-first approach needs its own argument for recovering physical parts and ordinary spatial structure. Neither its apparently minimal description nor the configuration-space proposal removes the explanatory cost of recovering observations, records, and—in our project—experience.

## An exact qualification of the paper's two-state illustration

Ney's example (pp. 5–7) compares an entangled state with a product state. Adopt two orthonormal alternatives per subsystem:

- first subsystem `|0⟩=|↑,A⟩`, `|1⟩=|↓,B⟩`;
- second subsystem `|0⟩=|↑,C⟩`, `|1⟩=|↓,D⟩`.

In these labels the equation **as printed** is `|ψ+⟩=(|01⟩+|10⟩)/√2`; the alternative is `|++⟩=(|00⟩+|01⟩+|10⟩+|11⟩)/2`. The paper describes a spin singlet initially but prints a plus sign for the post-device state. The standard singlet has a minus sign in the usual common spin convention; relative phases acquired by the apparatus or choice of branch conventions could change the printed expression, but Ney does not spell out those phases. We use the equation as printed and do not infer a source error solely from that sign.

Both printed states assign probability 1/2 to each local position alternative. Their **joint** position distributions differ: the entangled state has support only on `01` and `10`, while the product state assigns 1/4 to each pair. This supports Ney's point about the inadequacy of a description retaining only independent local position probabilities.

A useful boundary: the complete one-subsystem reduced density matrices are **not** the same in this particular comparison. The entangled state's are `I/2`, while the product state's are `|+⟩⟨+|`. If the spin–path coherence in each two-level sector is retained and a suitable local coherent operation is available, their single-subsystem `X` expectation values are 0 and 1 respectively. Therefore this specific pair does not demonstrate indistinguishability by *all* local measurements, only by the stated position-probability fields. Ney's separate, general nonseparability claim does not depend on overstating this particular example.

## Stronger control: even all one-subsystem states and the joint position statistics can agree

Consider additionally `|ψ−⟩=(|01⟩−|10⟩)/√2` in the **same fixed apparatus and basis conventions**. Standard density-matrix arithmetic gives:

| Fixed physical description | `ρ₁`, `ρ₂` | Joint position probabilities `00,01,10,11` | `⟨Z⊗Z⟩` | `⟨X⊗X⟩` |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| `ψ+` | both `I/2` | `0, 1/2, 1/2, 0` | −1 | +1 |
| `ψ−` | both `I/2` | `0, 1/2, 1/2, 0` | −1 | −1 |
| `++` | both `|+⟩⟨+|` | `1/4, 1/4, 1/4, 1/4` | 0 | +1 |

`X` and `Z` are Pauli observables in the **specified spin–path two-level subspaces**, not necessarily a bare spin measurement or an easy laboratory operation. The `X⊗X` distinction requires retaining and coherently recombining the relevant alternatives and comparing joint outcomes; irreversible records or decoherence could prevent this operation in practice. Both one-subsystem outcome distributions remain unchanged between `ψ+` and `ψ−`. Their joint coherence cannot be reconstructed from those marginals, nor from the one joint position distribution alone. This standard quantum fact is not presented as a new theorem; [the exact arithmetic checker](verification/ney_relational_correlations_gate.py) applies it to Ney's example and records the stated results.

It is essential not to confuse an **active** phase intervention with a change of notation. A physical `Z` operation on the second subsystem changes `ψ+` to `ψ−` while leaving the first subsystem's reduced state unchanged. Holding both physical measurement settings fixed, the joint `X⊗X` correlation changes sign; there is no signal in the first subsystem's local statistics. A passive basis redescription transforms the state **and measurement operators together** and leaves all predictions unchanged. Thus a candidate based on “phase mismatch” must say whether it refers to a relative phase against independently specified physical interactions/reference operations or merely to a basis convention. A bare sign or coordinate phase is not an experiential quality.

## Consequences for the proposed framework

**1. One physical whole is not one universal subject.** A single wave function, ray, global state, or relational whole does not on its own specify a subject, bind every content into one experience, or rule out plural subjects. Conversely, plurality does not follow merely from the existence of spatial regions or tensor factors. This paper leaves the subject question open; it does not answer it in either direction. Our candidate retains plural subjects and perspectives without assuming a universal consciousness.

**2. Relational structure is a live physical interpretation, not a consequence proven by entanglement.** Ney's discussion shows how nonseparable relations can represent what independent local fields miss; it also shows why that does not uniquely refute a high-dimensional field rival. Her spacetime-state realist comparator takes spacetime regions as given, whereas our target treats spacetime as emergent. Adopting its mathematics by analogy does not derive spacetime, local algebras, measurement outcomes or relativistic gravity. The strong possibility-equals-actuality premise does not follow from the existence of components in one superposition: amplitudes and configurations cannot simply be counted as actual worlds or experiences.

**3. A concrete constraint on the mismatch bridge.** An experiential selector or valence score depending *only* on the two one-subsystem density matrices, or additionally on their joint position probabilities, necessarily returns the same output for `ψ+` and `ψ−` when all its other inputs are fixed. If a future independently justified theory predicts different experience for these states, that restricted selector cannot express the prediction; if it predicts the same experience, this control alone does not refute it. Adding `X⊗X` can distinguish the physical states but does not show either has a subject, a feeling or discomfort. A useful subject rule also needs the relevant processes, contexts and admissible intervention algebras, not merely a list of correlations. The existing E-L selector uses a stipulated transition kernel and mismatch bit; this is a **requirement for a proposed quantum implementation**, not a demonstration that E-L itself fails the test.

**4. Substrate independence requires transport of the whole test.** If two descriptions are claimed to describe the same physical situation, map states, dynamical laws, admissible operations, relevant subsystem/subject candidates and phenomenal assignments together. Invariance under a genuine redescription avoids an arbitrary choice of notation. It does not establish that every formal repartition is a physically equivalent relabeling, that the relevant factorization is uniquely determined, or that the same functional organization necessarily feels a certain way. See the prior [duality selector audit](STRING_DUALITY_SUBJECT_SELECTION.md) and [quantum-access gate](QUANTUM_ACCESS_STRUCTURES_AND_SUBJECT_COUNT_GATE.md).

**5. Compare complete accounts, not labels.** A fair comparison with high-dimensional field, Hilbert-space-first, relational and primitive-ontology alternatives must count their assumptions about laws, preferred structure, records, subject constitution, experiential character, self-location and spacetime recovery. An austere physical state plus an unexplained experience map does not satisfy C3, **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, any more than an unexplained subject map appended to a relational ontology does. Genuine experience is a datum; strong illusionism that denies it is not an eligible solution. Elegance/parsimony is C7 and must be assessed jointly with all the other criteria, not by counting symbols or choosing separability in advance.

## Next decisive bridge

On the **same** candidate physical domain, specify (a) the observable algebra and physically available coherent interventions, (b) the subject selection rule and its domain/overlap, (c) an independent physical mismatch variable and its transformation law, and (d) distinct experience-respecting valence mappings. The fixed-phase pair above is a necessary bookkeeping control for any rule claiming sensitivity to such coherence. A subsequent [quantum mereology stabilizer gate](QUANTUM_MEREOLOGY_STATE_STABILIZER_GATE.md) tests whether the starting state and Hamiltonian uniquely specify the factorization used by such a rule. Then identify independently calibrated reports or intervention predictions that separate those mappings without defining mismatch from the report to be predicted. The existing [affect/mismatch gate](AFFECT_PREDICTION_ERROR_AND_MISMATCH_BRIDGE_GATE.md) spells out a crossed-history design; it has not yet identified or validated the physical mismatch. A positive physical distinction cannot, by itself, prove a phenomenal identity or subject boundary.

This leaves B4/B5 subject and quality constitution, B6 quantum recovery, B7/B8 spacetime and continuum, B9 subject measure, B10 empirical discrimination, B11 alternatives and B12 parsimony open. Nor does the paper establish self-existence or plenitude. B3 remains only the previously identified conditional inference.
