# Benovsky on dual aspects, mineness and no-self: full-book audit

## Decision

Jiri Benovsky’s proposal is a serious rival and useful pressure test for the project, especially its treatment of subjectivity without an extra inner observer. It distinguishes **a lived, first-personal occurrence** from an additional enduring bearer, and favors non-experiential proto-mentality over tiny conscious subjects. Neither his no-bearer view nor our field-first candidate is thereby established. Eliminating a separate subject can remove a particular subject-*summing* problem; it does not automatically explain why any particular arrangement is genuinely experienced, what it is like, or how concurrent and successive occurrences are individuated.

A small formal comparison below shows that, even granting generic first-personal character and specified parallel continuation links, more than one symmetric diachronic grouping remains available. The comparison is about the information supplied to a toy model, **not** a denial of experienced unity, a proof of multiple metaphysical possibilities, or a new account of personal identity. It adds the specific distinction between Benovsky’s generic mineness and a continuant-link rule to the existing [field-lineage analysis](FIELD_LINEAGE_PERSISTENCE_AND_FISSION_GATE.md).

## 2. The book’s position, without collapsing its components

| Commitment | What Benovsky proposes | What does not follow automatically |
|---|---|---|
| **Proto-mentality** | Fundamental constituents are proto-phental, but need not have phenomenal experiences or be micro-subjects (pp. 2–5, 21–22). | All matter thinks or feels, or a physical model has thereby shown any phenomenal occurrence. |
| **Dual-aspect monism** | A single underlying phental reality is accessible from mental and physical perspectives. In ch. 3, aspects are expressly **not properties or higher-order properties**, and should not be ontologically reified (pp. 15–19). | That naming one underlying entity explains why a particular quality is present or why one physical process is identical to this experience. |
| **Mineness** | An invariant, primitive subjective aspect accompanies qualitative experience; it is not a separate self-quale or a higher-order self-representation (pp. 27–33). | An explicitly perceived ownership relation, a separately existing ego, or a complete count of concurrent and successive subjects. |
| **No-self / no distinct bearer** | Rejects an ontologically reified bearer distinct from experiential states, while retaining subjectivity and experiential occurrence (pp. 35–43). | That all experience is illusory, that nobody suffers in the ordinary sense, or that all accounts identifying a subject with an experience are refuted. |
| **General eliminativism** | More radical, optional macro-object eliminativism says fundamental entities are arranged toothachewise instead of composing a further macro toothache (pp. 6–7, 45–46). He distinguishes it from the no-bearer proposal. | That phenomenal pain may be removed from the explanandum and replaced solely by behavioral or verbal dispositions. |
| **Cosmopsychism** | Explores an existence-monist / cosmo-proto-psychist mirror and considers, without deciding, whether it and bottom-up eliminativism might be terminological variants (pp. 47–49). | That any whole is itself a conscious cosmic subject, or that all whole-first theories are his existence monism. |

There is a textual nuance in his optionality claims: general elimination of macro objects is optional for a dual-aspect panprotopsychist, but he says no-subject-as-bearer is needed for his favored solution to the hard subject combination problem (pp. 24–25); he presents the stronger general elimination as what removes the remaining general composition problem (pp. 45–46). Neither is a license to call this framework’s existing experiential-ground proposal his theory.

## 3. What the argument buys—and what it still owes

**Avoiding versus solving.** On Benovsky’s proposal, there are no micro-experiences or micro-subjects, so the demand to explain how many *micro-subjects* merge into a macro-subject does not arise. That is a genuine conditional dialectical benefit. But panprotopsychism still needs an account of how non-experiential proto-character and its organization constitute an actual phenomenal occurrence. The physical brain/solidity comparison (pp. 3–5, 22–23) shows that wholes need not replicate microscopic features; it does not by itself show why an arrangement is felt, or establish a psychophysical identification. Chalmers distinguishes subject, quality and structure problems in the [previous full-paper audit](CHALMERS_COMBINATION_FULL_PAPER_AND_STRUCTURE_AUDIT.md). Different variants carry different burdens; the argument against summing is not a general impossibility proof for every panpsychist account.

**Aspects and causation.** Benovsky deliberately avoids a second property ontology: the ‘aspects’ language marks different perspectives on a single phental item, and he holds that a mind-to-body interaction between two separate things is a misconstruction (pp. 17–19). If the identity claim is granted, it avoids adding a second independent causal event *merely because there are two descriptions*. It does not, by itself, explain the specific first-/third-person correspondence or determine the causal role of fine-grained phenomenal differences. Nor does his explicit rejection of **physical** closure in favor of phental closure prove any novel empirical force.

There is a further clarification to request, not a contradiction established by this audit: if aspects are epistemic perspectives, and microscopic entities are not themselves experiencers, are the ‘proto-mental aspects’ intrinsic categorical features, possible perspectives of other observers, or some other feature? Benovsky stresses both ubiquitous proto-mentality and non-reified aspects; a complete account needs to say how these claims fit. The [later full-preprint comparison](TOOTHACHEWISE_PARAPHRASE_AND_REAL_ASPECTS_AUDIT.md) analyzes Le Bihan’s related but contested argument about aspect realism. Calling something ‘phental’ does not independently demonstrate its experiential capacity or make our physical-to-phenomenal bridge noncircular.

**Mineness is not a unique identifier.** Benovsky takes mineness to be phenomenally generic while treating numerically different occurrences as distinguishable partly through their physical aspects and causal streams (pp. 29–30). This is more nuanced than simply claiming that all people have the same experience. It still invokes a correspondence between physical individuation and experiential token individuation; a space-time label alone does not establish which contents form one lived field or which separate fields exist. His account is compatible with considering experiential tokens distinct without postulating an extra bearer; it does not by itself supply a law selecting those tokens or a continuous person.

**First-person evidence remains.** His word *mineness* is not automatically Guillot’s stronger phenomenal presentation of ownership. Our [givenness audit](FIRST_PERSONAL_GIVENNESS_AND_FIELD_INDIVIDUATION_AUDIT.md) distinguishes for-me-ness, self-awareness and experienced ownership and reports Zahavi’s different terminology. A real experience, including a genuinely felt togetherness where present, is evidence to respect; a theory may dispute whether it contains a separate bearer without declaring the episode unreal. Conversely, reports of familiarity or continuity are not infallible metaphysical identity detectors.

**Eliminative paraphrase has a burden.** Benovsky’s broader account insists on retaining macro first-personal experience, while his optional ‘toothachewise’ paraphrase eliminates a composite entity (pp. 7, 46). It is natural to read the paraphrase as intended to preserve phenomenology, but the short text does not itself give a complete phenomenal-preserving semantics. A viable translation must state what makes the arrangement *actually painful*, how qualitative and jointly experienced contrasts are preserved, and how instances are individuated. If the translation only preserves a physical arrangement and pain-report dispositions, it has not yet justified preservation of felt pain. This is a challenge to the adequacy of a proposed paraphrase, not a claim that Benovsky intended to deny experience. The [follow-up comparison](TOOTHACHEWISE_PARAPHRASE_AND_REAL_ASPECTS_AUDIT.md) distinguishes primitive preservation from a derived or independently warranted identity.

## 4. What the perceptual example supports

Benovsky uses apparent motion and visual object tracking to make the appearance of endurance compatible with impermanent states (pp. 36–39). Scholl’s review describes the tunnel effect: spatiotemporal continuity can lead observers to treat a visually changed object as persisting, and a *temporal same-object advantage* concerns better change detection when successive displays support a persisting object representation. The review also describes fission costs and possible splitting/copying of object-file information, and cautions that unusual hypothetical scenarios and task demands can mislead philosophical judgments.

This is evidence about **visual tracking and perceptual representation**, not a direct experiment determining whether a first-person subject endures. It supports the possibility that perception constructs apparent continuity and that continuity intuitions can be fallible; it does not establish that every appearance of self-continuity is generated by the same mechanism, that all perception of persistence is mistaken, or that no person persists. Nor does an apparent-motion illusion make the experience of apparent motion itself illusory. Benovsky’s inference from these studies to a no-enduring-self metaphysics is a philosophical extension, not the experiment’s result.

## 5. A small invariant-mineness grouping gate

This is a formal illustration, not Benovsky’s model and not an empirical test. Take four **stipulated occurrence tokens** in two modelled temporal slots:

```text
A₀ ──→ A₁        B₀ ──→ B₁
```

The arrows are independently given, parallel continuation-support edges; all four tokens carry the same abstract generic first-personal marker `g`. The physical/causal input, the four tokens, their time indices and their qualitative labels are held fixed. We **do not** claim that writing `g` creates experience or specifies all phenomenal facts, nor assume that these tokens have or lack real conjoint phenomenology. The temporal slots are local model indices, not a universal clock.

Let a *bookkeeping partition* group occurrences proposed to belong to the same continuant across the two slots. This partition is an extra modelling relation: in a no-bearer view the singleton version can mean only that no trans-token continuant is asserted; the blocks need not be objects or extra subjects. Impose: (i) at most one occurrence from each slot per block, and (ii) any two-token block must follow one of the given arrows. Then

- all four tokens separate: `{A₀},{A₁},{B₀},{B₁}`;
- both paths linked: `{A₀,A₁},{B₀,B₁}`;

are different admissible groupings. Both preserve the same `g`, same physical input and full exchange symmetry `A↔B`. Two intermediate groupings link only one path but are exchanged with one another. With the stated restrictions there are exactly **four** groupings, of which **two** are exchange-invariant. Without the edge restriction there are **seven** time-admissible partitions; without either restriction there are **15** partitions of four labeled tokens.

For `n≥1` parallel indistinguishable paths, if each eligible edge can be independently linked, there are `2ⁿ` groupings. Invariance under every permutation of the `n` paths leaves exactly two: link none or link all. Proof: permutation-invariant subsets of a transitive `n`-element set are only the empty set and the whole set. The [verifier](verification/benovsky_mineness_partition_gate.py) explicitly exhausts `n≤4`; the general result is the orbit argument, not an extrapolation from samples.

The inference is deliberately narrow: **generic `g` and the stated physical/edge data alone do not select a unique grouping**, even with symmetry. A theorist can add an edge-sufficiency principle, adopt no continuants, allow an identity primitive, deny the partition framework, or supply more detailed phenomenal temporal-unity facts. Each changes the premises. The model does not show those completions are genuinely possible or empirically indistinguishable once their full consequences are specified. In particular, genuine experienced diachronic unity is not equated with a report or assumed away. The earlier [lineage/fission gate](FIELD_LINEAGE_PERSISTENCE_AND_FISSION_GATE.md) analyzes a different, branching constraint and overlapping persons; the present construction isolates the information lost when generic mineness is used as if it uniquely identified a continuant.

## 6. Project integration and next obligations

1. **Rival on equal terms:** compare no-bearer experiences, the [field-first identity](SUBJECTIVE_CHARACTER_AND_NONCIRCULAR_FIELD_BRIDGE.md), an independently existing subject, and neutral-ground rivals. All must retain warranted phenomenal occurrence. A no-extra-bearer thesis is distinct from wholesale denial of experience and need not be rejected just because it is austere.
2. **Specify the bridge:** independently fix experiential contrasts and physical descriptions; explain why an organization is *this felt occurrence*, not merely how a report can be predicted. The [color-metamer gate](COLOR_METAMER_PHENOMENAL_PHYSICAL_BRIDGE.md) illustrates why physical mixing and colorimetric matching cannot simply be identified with complete felt experience. Benovsky’s paint analogy is illustrative, not a derivation of phenomenal mixing.
3. **Individuate unity and persistence separately:** say whether intrinsic for-me-ness entails conjoint unity, which events belong to a field, whether tokens can be shared, and whether a continuant exists. The [first-person audit](FIRST_PERSONAL_GIVENNESS_AND_FIELD_INDIVIDUATION_AUDIT.md) and the [lineage gate](FIELD_LINEAGE_PERSISTENCE_AND_FISSION_GATE.md) already distinguish field identity from temporal lineage and persons.
4. **Do not infer a cosmic experiencer:** Benovsky’s existence-monist, priority-monist and cosmopsychist alternatives have different commitments. A whole with locally first-personal occurrences is not thereby one further cosmic subject; an existence-monist can reject parts altogether. Any claimed equivalence of top-down and bottom-up descriptions needs an explicit translation preserving ontology and phenomenal facts.
