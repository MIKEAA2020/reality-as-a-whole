## 2. Algebraic plenitude, not modal realism

In algebraic quantum field theory (Haag–Kastler), the quasilocal algebra 𝒜 of observables has many unitarily inequivalent representations (sectors, folia) corresponding to different global states (different vacua, different temperature sectors, different charges, different CC values, etc.). The **universal representation**
$$\pi_u = \bigoplus_\omega \pi_\omega$$
(direct sum of GNS representations over a sufficient family of states) decomposes 𝒜 into sectors; every locally quasi-equivalent sector appears as a direct summand of π_u(𝒜)''.

**Theorem (Doplicher–Haag–Roberts; Araki):** Every irreducible representation satisfying the DHR selection criterion (localized endomorphisms = charges) is a subrepresentation of π_u.

Concretely this means: **every sector consistent with the net of observables ACTUALIZES as a piece of the universal von Neumann algebra π_u(𝒜)''**. There is no additional step of "creation," "actualization," or God choosing one world. All sectors are real — they are disjoint representations (no bounded observable connects them; observers in different sectors cannot communicate), but they are all actual pieces of one mathematical structure.

This is NOT David Lewis's modal realism:
- Lewis: infinitely many *concrete, disconnected* worlds, each as real as ours, with no shared structure.
- Algebraic plenitude: ONE net 𝒜 with many *representations*; all sectors share the same laws (the net structure); they are different vacuum states/charges/CC values of the same theory, not causally sealed duplicate universes.

Lewis's view multiplies concrete entities without necessity. Algebraic plenitude asserts only the universal algebra (which we already need for AQFT), with all its folia — no extra ontology.

## 3. Stability selection of d=4

Every dimension d is *consistent* as a formal QFT (QFT exists in any dimension). But not every dimension supports stable, record-bearing finite observers. Four structural criteria select d=4 uniquely (verifier #78):

Key reasons:
- **Ehrenfest/Bertrand:** V(r) ∝ 1/r^{d-2}; only in d=4 does this yield stable closed elliptical orbits (necessary for atoms and planetary systems). In d>4, all circular orbits are unstable to small perturbations; structure cannot persist.
- **Huygens' principle:** massless fields propagate sharply (with δ-function support on the light cone, no residual tail) only in even spacetime dimensions d ≥ 4. For finite observers to have sharp causal records (distinct memories, well-defined signals), Huygens is required.
- **Chiral fermions:** the SM is chiral (parity-violating); chiral Weyl spinor representations require even d and KO-dimension compatibility with the real structure J (KO-2 for Minkowski CPT).
- **Polarization counts:** 2 graviton helicities and 2 photon helicities — the observed counts — match only d=4.

**Only d=4 satisfies all five.** This is not a post-hoc explanation; each criterion is a dynamical prerequisite for the existence of finite record-bearing observers (atoms, sharp signaling, chiral chemistry, propagating forces with observed helicities).

## 4. Weinberg anthropic selection on Λ_CC

Weinberg (1987) showed that galaxies (and hence observers) can only form in sectors where
$$|\rho_\Lambda| \lesssim (10^{-3}\text{eV})^4.$$
If ρ_Λ were positive and larger than this, the universe would enter exponential expansion before gravitational collapse could assemble bound structures; if negative and larger, it would recollapse too fast. Within plenitude, all values of Λ_CC (from all consistent Weyl² running/conformal-symmetry-breaking patterns) actualize in different sectors; observers find themselves in the tiny Weinberg window because structure formation (and hence observers) only occurs there.

Observed ρ_Λ ≈ (10⁻³ eV)⁴ = 10⁻⁴⁸ GeV⁴ is within the Weinberg bound (P4). This is a **selection effect**, not a fine-tuning. It is no more mysterious than finding ourselves on a planet with liquid water rather than on Venus or Pluto.

## 5. Why "grass is blue" worlds aren't separate sectors

A common confusion: if all possibilities actualize, does that mean there's a world where grass is blue, where you're the Pope, where the Confederacy won the Civil War? **No.** These are not *sectors* (disjoint representations); they are *contingent microhistories within our sector*.

- Sectors differ by VACUUM STRUCTURE — representation-inequivalent states (different CC, different gauge symmetry breaking patterns, different particle masses).
- Contingent facts (who won a war, biological adaptation, historical accident) are configurations of matter WITHIN our vacuum. They are possible in the sense of being allowed by the laws, but only one history is actual in our sector. Plenitude does not assert that every historical contingency branches into its own world — that is Many-Worlds branching, which is a separate interpretive question. Plenitude asserts that every SECTOR (representation) is actual.

This resolves the "blue grass/Pope" paradox: plenitude ≠ "everything conceivable exists." It means every CONSISTENT VACUUM OF THE NET exists — a much smaller set, constrained by the structural conditions in §3.
