# Reality as a whole: plenitude as a candidate explanation

## The strongest surviving thesis

> The target is a completely intelligible account of reality as a whole. Bostrom’s argument is a starting heuristic, not a constraint requiring the final theory to remain about simulations. The useful insight is that a world can be real without being fundamental, and that observers are internal to the reality they investigate. A candidate completion takes reality to comprise every admissible coherent structure, with laws understood internally rather than imposed by an external designer. It seeks to remove an unexplained selection of one ultimate world. But its central principle—that coherence suffices for actuality—must itself be justified, and the account must explain phenomenality, observational probabilities, and its own explanatory sufficiency. Internalizing an unanswered question does not answer it.

The strongest surviving proposal is therefore **whole-inclusive structural plenitude as a research hypothesis**, not a demonstrated zero-residue ontology.

## 1. The objective has a strict priority order

The project asks for:

1. Maximum intelligibility: no unexplained residue concerning reality as a whole.
2. Among accounts that achieve that, minimum departure from the useful starting framework.

These are not competing goals to average together. Fidelity to Bostrom must yield where it prevents explanatory completion. Nor is it responsive simply to say that Bostrom’s argument cannot establish a theory of everything: the request is for a reconstruction beyond what the original argument establishes.

Nevertheless, an objective is not its own realization. Priority for complete intelligibility permits substantial revision; it does not certify a proposed revision as complete.

A proper comparison should distinguish:

- the stated explanatory target;
- a candidate account of how to meet it;
- the argument that the candidate actually succeeds;
- and only then the extent of its departures from the starting point.

No candidate in the supplied exchange has been shown to pass the third stage. “Minimum changes conditional on success” therefore remains an optimization problem, not an established result.

## 2. What survives from the starting point

The final theory need not retain a simulator, a hierarchy of artificial worlds, or a prediction that we occupy one level rather than another.

The durable insights are narrower:

- **Non-fundamental need not mean unreal.** An implemented process can be real even if another process realizes it.
- **Observers are internal.** Their evidence and models arise within reality rather than from an external standpoint.
- **Implementation and fundamentality differ.** Explaining a world through another implementation level does not automatically explain the whole containing both.
- **First-person probability needs care.** Population ratios do not automatically yield a uniquely justified credence without assumptions about evidence and observer weighting.

These insights can motivate a broader account without forcing computation to be the ultimate ontology. Structure, process, computation, and concrete implementation are related notions, not synonyms. If the candidate includes all coherent structures, it must explain whether noncomputable structures are included and what role computational language still plays.

The correct center of gravity is reality, not the question “Am I simulated?” The latter becomes an optional local inquiry within the larger framework.

## 3. Totality blocks an external addition, not every unexplained fact

If reality means everything that exists, any existing explainer is already included within it. An “outside reality” containing more existing machinery is not outside reality in the stipulated sense.

This is a useful constraint against endlessly relocating the explanatory burden. But it does not prove that the whole is self-explanatory.

> Nothing existing lies outside total reality; it does not follow that nothing within total reality remains unexplained.

A particular universe can be externally implemented by another system while both belong to the whole. The totality need not have an external implementation. These are compatible claims.

The surviving global requirement is that any complete explanation must account for the whole without depending on an excluded, unexplained addition. It need not present that explanation as an earlier efficient cause: essential, constitutive, or holistic explanation remains open for investigation.

A brute stopping point is excluded by the project’s standard, but a genuinely explanatory necessary nature is not. Calling a structure “necessary” supplies no proof; conversely, necessity should not be ruled out as explanatory in advance.

## 4. Plenitude is the substantive candidate

Let \(\mathcal A\) denote a properly specified class of admissible structures. A schematic plenitude principle says:

\[
\forall S\in\mathcal A,\quad \operatorname{Actual}(S).
\]

The intended advantage is clear. A selective ontology must explain why these possibilities are actual and others are not. A plenitudinal ontology denies that there is such a selective boundary within its admissible class.

This can remove one kind of unexplained choice. Our laws would not be the sole laws chosen for ultimate realization. They would characterize our world within a more comprehensive reality.

The strongest charitable motivation is:

> If no independently justified distinction explains why one admissible possibility should be actual while another is excluded, plenitude offers a uniform alternative to arbitrary selection.

That is a serious explanatory strategy. It is not yet a proof. Uniform inclusion is one proposed policy; the argument must establish why it is true and why its admissibility criterion is not itself an unexplained selector.

## 5. Coherence does not automatically explain actuality

The decisive claim is not merely that structures are internally consistent. It is that every admissible coherent structure is actual in the relevant ontological sense.

These must be distinguished:

- **Syntactic consistency:** no contradiction is derivable in a specified formal system.
- **Model-theoretic satisfiability:** a theory has a model under a specified semantics.
- **Metaphysical possibility:** the structure could genuinely obtain.
- **Actuality:** it does obtain, in the sense asserted by the ontology.

Relationships among these depend on further assumptions. A formal consistency result does not, without an argument, establish concrete actuality. Identifying actuality with mathematical existence is itself a proposed ontology, not a consequence of the word “coherent.”

The candidate therefore needs to explain:

1. Which logic, semantics, and admissibility criteria are used, and why.
2. Whether “all structures” means a set, a proper class, or something else.
3. Whether isomorphic presentations count as one structure or several actual occurrences.
4. Whether actuality is mathematical existence, concrete existence, or a new identification of the two.
5. Why the plenitude principle holds necessarily rather than being an unexplained universal assertion.

Different structures may contain incompatible laws without making the total account contradictory, provided their domains are appropriately distinguished. But combining them into one allegedly interacting physical whole would require additional specification.

Likewise, our world’s being included does not show that it is a “region” of a common physical space. Inclusion in a plenitude is not automatically causal or spatial connectedness.

## 6. Plenitude is not the only conceivable alternative to arbitrary selection

A selective account might propose a uniquely explanatory necessary structure. It might fail, but its failure has not been shown merely by noting that a brute selection would be inadequate.

Other global proposals might appeal to a different account of necessity, an experiential ultimate, or a holistic explanatory relation. The present exchange does not establish that these fail or require more unexplained assumptions than plenitude.

Thus the defensible comparison is:

> Plenitude can avoid an unexplained choice among admitted structures. A genuinely explanatory unique-world account could instead explain that choice or show that there was no genuine alternative. Both must demonstrate their explanatory adequacy.

The fact that plenitude is expressible in one short principle does not make it the smallest substantive commitment. Description length, ontology size, and explanatory economy are different measures. A short sentence can encode a very strong postulate.

## 7. Phenomenality remains a constitutive obligation

The proposal refuses to leave experience outside reality. That is necessary but not sufficient. Calling experience an “intrinsic aspect” of a process gives it a place in the vocabulary; it does not yet explain why the relevant process is experiential.

A successful account must specify:

- which structures contain conscious occurrences;
- what fixes their qualitative character;
- how subjects are individuated and unified;
- whether structural duplicates necessarily share phenomenal character;
- and why the proposed relation holds rather than merely being stipulated.

No general computational identity, biological exclusivity, or ubiquitous phenomenality is established in this attachment. Those would be further proposals, not consequences of plenitude.

There are at least two distinct obligations:

1. Explain how a formal or structural description relates to concrete actuality.
2. Explain how the relevant actuality is experiential.

Declaring every coherent structure actual does not independently settle either identification. If phenomenal structure is included among the admissible fundamentals, its relation to physical structure still needs explanation under the zero-residue standard.

## 8. Self-location does not automatically recover prediction

Plenitude can change “Why are these the only actual laws?” into “Why do our observations have this character within the plenitude?” That is a legitimate change in the question. It is not its complete answer.

If many observer-containing structures fit our past evidence but predict different futures, prediction requires justified weighting or some alternative rule of inference. A count over descriptions can change when the same structure is represented repeatedly. A count over abstract isomorphism classes can ignore physical multiplicity. Neither is automatically the right observer measure.

A schematic centered framework may distinguish worlds or histories \(\omega\), centers \(c\), and evidence \(D\). But writing

\[
\operatorname{Cr}(A\mid D)
\]

presupposes a credence assignment; it does not derive one from plenitude.

A local use of “I” need not invoke a lottery assigning an extra ego to a body. Population-level questions and predictions across indistinguishable centers may nevertheless require weights. These issues must be assessed separately rather than declaring all self-location solved or all self-location a lottery.

The strongest surviving probability claim is therefore conditional: recover observer-centered inference from a justified measure and an explicit treatment of evidence. No such derivation appears in the attachment.

## 9. The zero-residue success test

A developed version of this candidate must satisfy the following together:

| Obligation | Required achievement |
|---|---|
| Scope | Define the whole and the admissible class without hidden exclusions |
| Actuality | Justify why admissible coherence suffices for actuality |
| Non-arbitrariness | Explain the admissibility criterion and plenitude principle themselves |
| Phenomenality | Explain experience and subject individuation rather than label them intrinsic |
| Probability | Recover evidentially justified predictions without arbitrary representation counting |
| Global closure | Explain the framework, not merely keep all questions inside it |
| Comparative minimality | Show why equally successful alternatives require greater departures or assumptions |

The account need not have a finite humanly surveyable derivation to be true. But uncertainty about our understanding cannot count as a demonstration of its completeness. Equally, failure to have established it is not a proof that complete intelligibility is impossible.

## Final formulation

> Starting from the possibility of real but non-fundamental worlds, pursue an account of total reality whose explanatory basis is internal to the whole. Structural plenitude is a candidate: every properly specified admissible coherent structure is actual, so no arbitrary selection of just one world is required. Its explanatory merit depends on defending that coherence–actuality principle, characterizing experience, and recovering observation and probability without unexplained choices. These tasks are not peripheral problems made harmless by placing them inside the totality; they are constitutive conditions of success. Bostrom remains a heuristic source, while complete intelligibility governs the reconstruction.

**Audit verdict:** The priority ordering and shift from simulation-centered inquiry to whole-reality explanation survive decisively. Plenitude survives as a motivated candidate for avoiding selective actuality, not as a necessary consequence of maximum intelligibility or a demonstrated minimum-change solution. Neither strict impossibility of zero residue nor successful achievement of it has been established.

### Relation to the other supplied exchanges

This proposal is a distinct global branch. It is not identical to an unbounded implementation fixed point, a neutral-monist intrinsic ground, or an experiential self-explanatory ultimate. Those approaches might share commitments to non-external explanation, but they have different substantive premises. The local process–phenomenality programme can be investigated within several such frameworks; success locally would not automatically establish plenitude globally.
