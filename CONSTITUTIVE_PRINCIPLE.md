# The missing bridge: realization-sensitive qualitative constitution

## Direct identification

The missing principle is a **realization-sensitive law of qualitative constitution and composition**:

> Some concretely realized processes constitute phenomenal episodes through their intrinsic qualitative-relational organization. The same constitutive nature must explain their publicly accessible physical powers and, where an episode exists, its qualitative character and unity. Which processes constitute episodes, and which constituents belong to one episode, must follow from that nature rather than from an externally assigned phenomenal label, a chosen description, or an arbitrary integration threshold.

This names the explanatory job and a candidate identity hypothesis. It is **not an established law**, and the inquiry has not discovered its specifying equations or an independently justified sufficient condition. Calling the physical and phenomenal “two aspects” does not itself perform the missing explanatory work.

The proposal does not assert that every process is conscious, that elementary events are little subjects, that biological tissue is uniquely privileged, or that abstract computational equivalence suffices. Those are distinct further claims requiring evidence and a composition theory.

## 1. Why this principle, rather than another stress-limit theorem?

The mathematical sequence establishes restricted links among dynamics, states, correlations, and observables. It shows that concrete differences can disappear under a representation or limit:

- equal energy and stress need not identify a state;
- equal one-particle covariance need not identify higher correlations;
- coherent and incoherent preparations can differ locally;
- a mathematical model of an occurrence is not another occurrence merely by specifying it.

These results rule out several *shortcuts to a constitutive inference*. They do not prove that coherence, ultraviolet structure, or a particular higher correlation is necessary for consciousness. Nor do they show that a full physical description must omit experience: the demonstrated nonidentifiability concerns particular incomplete data and models, not every possible complete physical theory.

Thus the missing inference is not another link from a lattice current to an energy density. It is an independently justified identification of the concrete organization that constitutes a qualitative episode, including its incidence and boundary.

## 2. A precise schema, with the missing content exposed

[Common-constitution closure](COMMON_CONSTITUTION_CLOSURE.md) now states the exact fiber criterion for a target to factor through a proposed physical description, applies it to recorded reports, and separates representational sufficiency from intrinsic determination. A physically proven reduction preserves a phenomenal target only with the further independently warranted target-preservation premise.

Let X denote a fully specified candidate **concrete realization**, not just an abstract program, a Hamiltonian formula, or an observable summary. Its candidate specification must state its actual history, constitution, relevant dynamics, and realization relations; whether a particular feature is relevant is itself to be justified.

Seek a unified constitutive theory C with two explanatory presentations:

\[
P_C(X)=\text{public physical organization and predictions},
\]

\[
E_C(X)=\{(e_\alpha,Q_\alpha,I_\alpha)\}_\alpha.
\]

Here eα is a constituted episode, Qα its qualitative organization, and Iα the incidence of actual constituents in that episode. E may be empty. No global partition into disjoint, timeless subjects is assumed; overlap, temporal extent, or exclusion would need to be settled by the theory. Incidence is an explanatory target, not a freely supplied label.

The demand is that P and E be constrained consequences of one specified constitution, rather than taking an already complete physical theory and appending an arbitrary map to experiences. Their sharing the symbol C is not evidence that such an explanation has been supplied.

If X is represented only by a structural physical model and a new variable ξ is introduced as its “intrinsic nature,” then ξ must do independently justified explanatory work. An unconstrained ξ can merely relocate the annotation problem. Conversely, a successful theory might identify qualitative nature with an adequately specified physical process, without adding a separate hidden variable. The schema does not settle that ontological choice in advance.

## 3. The candidate principle has three substantive clauses

### A. Realized constitution, not description

A specification, simulation, or mathematical presentation does not constitute an episode solely by referring to the relevant organization. A candidate realization must actually instantiate whatever powers and relations prove constitutive. A simulated process may qualify if its implementation really supplies them; neither automatic inclusion nor automatic exclusion follows from the word “simulation.”

Constitutive equivalence must be distinguished from matching outputs, matching stress, sharing a universality class, or implementing a selected abstract transition table. If the theory eventually identifies some functional organization as fully constitutive, that identification needs justification rather than exclusion by definition.

### B. Qualitative identity with explanatory content

Where an episode occurs, its qualitative character is hypothesized to be the intrinsic character of the realized organization, not an extra observer attached to a representation. The theory must specify which character and why: red rather than green, pain rather than a mere pain report, this differentiated content rather than another.

Structural correspondences among physical and phenomenal distinctions can constrain the proposal. They are not by themselves a proof of qualitative identity. A map that merely renames physical states “red,” “pain,” or “experience” fails this clause unless the identification is justified.

### C. Non-arbitrary composition and individuation

The same theory must explain why certain realized constituents jointly constitute one episode, why other processes do not belong to it, and how this incidence changes through time. Mutual coupling, information integration, maintenance, recurrence, or self-modeling may be relevant, but none has yet been shown sufficient.

In particular, an arbitrarily weak nonzero coupling cannot be promoted to a proven merger of subjects by defining a connected causal graph. The work has exposed that inference as an unpaid bridge. A threshold also cannot be chosen solely to produce an intuitively desired boundary. A sharp transition, graded relation, contextual individuation, or some other structure would need independent constitutive justification.

## 4. What would turn the proposal into a discovery?

A serious candidate must satisfy all of the following, not merely receive a name:

1. **Specify the constitution.** Give actual variables, admissible realizations, dynamics or constraints, and composition conditions. State what is primitive and what is derived.
2. **Recover public physics.** Reproduce established physical predictions in the claimed domain. The free-field results supply limited consistency checks, not derivations of all physical law.
3. **Constrain phenomenal identifications.** Explain qualitative contrasts and episode incidence using more than an arbitrary annotation of an unchanged physical model. Use first-person phenomenal distinctions and their experimentally anchored relations without assuming perfect report faithfulness.
4. **Survive realization tests.** Distinguish specification from occurrence, copying from implementation, altered content from monitoring, observational matching from constitutive equivalence, and weak interaction from demonstrated subject unity.
5. **Risk exclusion.** State which physically and phenomenally interpreted alternatives the principle rules out and why. If every possible physical history and phenomenal assignment fits, it has not identified constitution.
6. **Address the foundation.** Explain why this constitutive nature and its laws are actual or sufficient. A primitive identity may be a valuable local hypothesis, but it is not automatically the zero-residue whole-account.

A candidate may constrain physics, phenomenal identity, or both through an explanatory identity without predicting a new force. New physical effects are not required by definition. But if it makes no new physical predictions, its identity claim still needs a noncircular warrant; physical fit alone cannot select arbitrary phenomenal annotations of the same physical model.

## 5. The decisive comparison to pursue

The immediate research object should be competing **specified constitutive models**, not another generic measure of complexity. A useful comparison matrix would ask each model to determine:

- what actual process, rather than its monitor or report, constitutes a content contrast;
- what changes qualitative content while preserving reporting capacity, and what changes reporting while preserving the relevant process;
- whether duplicated or differently implemented organizations constitute one, several, or no episodes;
- whether and why an added weak connection changes episode incidence;
- which implementation details can be varied without altering the putatively constitutive organization;
- which physical and phenomenal distinctions would exclude the model.

These are research obligations, not a claim that clean independent interventions already exist. The earlier annotation-equivalence result remains important: within a model where phenomenal assignments change no physical predictions, merely collecting more of those same physical predictions cannot select the assignment. One needs additional constitutive constraints, warranted phenomenal identification, or a richer theory—not a larger enumeration of the same underdetermined setup.

## 6. Current verdict

**The best identified missing principle is qualitative-relational constitution with an independently justified composition law, sensitive to actual realization.** It fits the surviving thesis and explains what the physical constructions still lack. The exact content that would make it true, selective, and explanatory remains undiscovered.

This is stronger than saying “everything is one” and more demanding than assigning consciousness to complexity. It is also more limited than claiming that intrinsic nature has already unified physics and phenomenology. The physical constructions narrow admissible presentations and expose information loss; they do not yet establish which concrete nature constitutes experience or why that nature is actual.

No new executable theorem or empirical consciousness finding is claimed by this assessment. The existing 1,012 research tests remain checks of the specified constructions, not tests of a completed constitutive principle.
