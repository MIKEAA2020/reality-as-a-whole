## 2. A typed claim/reference channel on that same history

PSR's S2 evaluation correctly warns that a content label, even preregistered, does not by itself say what it is **about**. We can avoid that particular formal defect in the sphere model by explicitly fixing the following binary protocol *before applying its match rule*:

* **Target:** the physical question `B=+1 or B=−1` for the same supported system. The reference instrument directly reads the B observable in a specified joint readout; its target is the B sector, not an answer defined by the echo record. The tested B projection is unchanged through the echo on the actual states. This is an independently specified physical observable in the model, not independent experimental evidence.
* **Content:** `c∈{+1,−1}` means the proposition **“the B-sector outcome is c.”** No free content-to-target lookup is fitted to a desired correctness result.
* **Claim channel:** in the normal decoder, read the echo memory and output content `c=r`. In the inverted-decoder control output `c=−r`. These are operationally defined physical output channels; calling them propositions is the model's declared semantics, not proof that a conscious subject asserts or understands them.
* **Decision:** for an eligible same-run, same-tick pair, `Correct(c,b) ⇔ c=b`; `Error(c,b) ⇔ c≠b`. The binary target and both outputs are total on this declared domain. Reference and claim are jointly readable commuting outputs in this finite idealization. The protocol uses a specified accessible interface, not the assumption that a surface-confined inhabitant can access an off-surface process.

The direct B target and the echo record are different observables, although the pulse correlates them. The strong correlation is **a result of the fixed interaction and the ready state**, not independence of the outcomes. Merely calling a record public does not make it a public witness: a real-world protocol would still need physical implementation, provenance and independent audit.

At tick 10 the joint normal table is `(c,b)=(+,+):1/2`, `(−,−):1/2`, with the other two cells zero. Both true and false assertions have positive-weight cases in the specified physical model: the inverted decoder yields the two mismatch cells `(+ ,−)` and `(−,+)` at weight `1/2` each. These are model cases, not observations from an implemented experiment. It is the **same B target**, not one target designated true and another designated false.

The table is unchanged in the existing clean and thermal auxiliary preparations. It explicitly yields the following controls:

1. **Decoder shift:** invert the claim decoder while holding the B reference fixed. At tick 10 all verdicts change, rather than remaining correct by definition.
2. **Target shift:** keep the normal decoder and instead compare against `−B`; all tick-10 verdicts change. This is a different target question, not a gauge transformation.
3. **Passive redescription:** relabel both claim and target signs together. Correctness is unchanged. Relabeling only one side is not the same operation.
4. **Ready-state dependency:** if the echo memory begins `r=−1` and the normal decoder is not changed, the pulse yields `r=−b`, so it is wrong on every supported input at tick 10. The complete cycle returns that dirty memory to `r=−1`. Thus blank readiness is a real input, not a label that can be inserted after the result.
5. **Clock and erasure control:** the ready memory is correct with probability `1/2` before the pulse, becomes perfectly predictive at tick 10, and returns to `1/2` after uncomputation. The record is therefore neither a permanent trace nor a claim about the physical state at an arbitrary clock reading.

These results repair a **particular content/reference ambiguity in an ideal finite model**. They do not themselves meet the PSR S2 evaluation's actual public-witness, acquisition, provenance, team-independence or preregistration requirements. They also do not prove general truth, semantic understanding, or subjective experience.

## 4. Put the measure on the same candidate, not on a separate example

For the proposed two quality laws and the same tick-10 support, physical Born weighting gives

```text
P_phys(v_now ≠ v_echo) = 1/2,
E_phys(v_now) = E_phys(v_echo) = 0.
```

If the actual experiences correspond exactly to these occurrences, and if their first-person incidence weights **are** these physical effect weights, then the same numbers are the conditional incidence results. But neither identity follows from the circuit. For an explicit alternative on the same positive support, multiply B-positive weights by `3/2` and B-negative weights by `1/2`. This is normalized and leaves `s` counterparts equally weighted, so both signed sums remain zero, but its candidate-quality disagreement is `1/4`. This is an **alternative proposed incidence measure**, not an alternative physical Born law or a laboratory prediction of this state.

The positive route is already available: the [effect-algebra reconstruction](ZERO_INCIDENCE_REFINEMENT_AND_BORN_MEASURE.md) shows that a normalized, additive, effect-noncontextual incidence assignment on all effects, together with certainty of the specified pure whole state, forces its Born weights. The task is to justify those premises **for subject incidences**, not to repeat the theorem as though the subject/event identity were proved. A mixed-state preparation or a restriction to one accessible algebra needs separate analysis. Symmetric signed zero alone is not enough; the counterexample above makes this concrete in the same echo experiment.

The competing F2/F3 completions have the same physical predictions by construction. A calibrated report on the `v_now≠v_echo` cases would discriminate them only if the physical–experiential connection, access, reporting channel, and error bounds were independently established. This is the right target for a real bridge, not an inference that a qubit is a subject or that a positive sign erases a person's negative experience.
