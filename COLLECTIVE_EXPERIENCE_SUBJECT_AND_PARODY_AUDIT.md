# Collective experience, subject multiplicity, and the moral-parody challenge

## Decision

Bohn's collective-property proposal gives the project a serious way to avoid building a human subject out of already conscious microscopic subjects. It does **not**, without additional individuation principles, identify how many lived perspectives there are, even if one accepts that a particular plurality collectively instantiates a genuinely phenomenal property. Conversely, a first-person field-first view secures a subject only by taking a first-personal occurrence as basic; it still owes an account of why these fields, qualities and physical realizations obtain.

A further challenge comes from Zach Blaesi's *The Moral Parody Argument Against Panpsychism*. Its relevant lesson is not that experience is unreal. It is that an explanatory-gap argument plus a general ban on brute emergence may overgeneralize, and that moving an ordinary property to a basic plurality does not automatically explain either its distribution or its higher-level grounding. The preferred experiential whole must answer the **same explanatory standard** it applies to its competitors. This is a substantive constraint on the research program, not a newly established bridge or a battery pass.

## 2. What a collective phenomenal property must do

Let `P` be the same fixed plurality of physical realizers and `M` a genuinely experiential property type, such as a specified felt quality. Write `M(P)` for collective instantiation, not for each member's separate instantiation. Even before considering persistence or interventions, four claims must be distinguished:

1. **Type:** `M` is the kind of phenomenal character involved.
2. **Bearer:** the plurality `P` collectively instantiates `M`.
3. **Occurrence:** a particular instance of `M` occurs, perhaps identical with or grounded in other instances under a specified account.
4. **Perspective:** a subject has a lived occurrence with that character; which other contents are jointly present and how many such perspectives there are are further questions.

Taking `M` to be *genuinely* experiential matters. One cannot consistently read a stipulated phenomenal occurrence as merely an external physical tag and then infer that experience has been explained. But neither does the fact that there is experience, on its own, settle how a plurality is a subject, the number of subjects, or whether two descriptions refer to one occurrence. The local datum includes experience **for** a perspective, not just a public correlate. A theory that asserts an unowned phenomenal character must explain how it relates to that datum rather than redefining the datum away.

A defender may **identify** the plural instantiation itself with a lived perspective; that is a real philosophical proposal, not a grammatical error. It requires a principle saying when one such instantiation is one perspective, what its content and numerical identity are, and how it relates to other instantiations. The plurality need not be an additional plural object, and identifying a perspective with it need not automatically posit a further inner observer. But neither plural syntax nor an anti-observer regress supplies the individuation principle.

## 3. One bearer-extension, different subject counts: a narrow counterexample

Fix one state of the existing four-spin system and the full plurality `P={0,1,2,3}`. Hold the physical state and dynamics fixed, and suppose the only recorded plural quality fact in this comparison is `M(P)=true`. Introduce two distinct *candidate singular composites* `a` and `b`; stipulate the same being-grounding relation from `P` to each. Do not assume that either is actually a subject just from being a composite. For this comparison alone, assume that a candidate is a subject of this `M` exactly when it singularly instantiates `M`.

| Interpretation | `M(P)` | `M(a)` | `M(b)` | Count of these candidate M-subjects |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | true | true | false | 1 |
| B | true | true | true | 2 |

Both obey the literal one-way TDP implications `M(a) → M(P)` and `M(b) → M(P)`, and both respect the additional premise that **at least one** of the two candidates is an M-subject. They agree on the fixed physical description, the assumed being-grounding links, and the recorded plural property extension. They differ in their **singular phenomenal facts**. Thus this is not a claim that the complete experiential worlds are identical. It is a counterinterpretation to the inference from the specified plural fact, being-grounding links and one-way TDP **alone** to the number of singular subjects. Bohn's full metaphysics could reject one or both interpretations on additional grounds.

The hypothetical common ground is intentionally strong: one plurality fully grounds the being of both candidates. Whether two distinct conscious composites can share precisely that full ground is controversial, and no claim is made that actual subjects do so. The argument is semantic bookkeeping, not a demonstration of metaphysical possibility, a new empirical finding, or a derivation of subject count. Its value is to locate the missing premise. If a theory instead states that precisely one subject exists for each `M(P)`, it has added a **uniqueness** principle. If it equates the plural instantiation with a subject rather than positing singular composites, it must still determine what counts as one instance and whether a single plurality can realize distinct perspectives. If it denies the shared-ground premise, it must say what differentiates or individuates the grounds.

Even adding Bohn's stronger intended explanation—that the singular `M` fact is grounded in the plural `M` fact, not merely accompanied by it—does not by itself state a uniqueness rule. Whether one grounding fact could ground two distinct singular phenomenal facts is a substantive question; a theory may forbid it, but must say why. The table alone does not establish that either extension has a coherent fact-grounding semantics or meets that stronger claim. Nor does the count above rule out further subjects outside `a,b` or a plural subject identified with `P`.

This differs from the earlier [Bohn gate](BOHN_PLURAL_COLLECTIVE_CONSCIOUSNESS_FULL_PAPER_AUDIT.md): there the two local pair facts were fixed and the *whole-plurality* property varied. Here the whole-plurality property and the candidates' being grounds are fixed, while candidate **singular** phenomenal facts vary. It also differs from the [facet gate](RELATIONAL_WHOLE_SUBJECT_FACET_CONSTITUTION.md), which assumes a family of joint content states and studies possible owners. Neither construction licenses treating a property extension as a subject-counting law.

## 4. A conditional bridge and its cost

The following package would make the sought inference precise for a restricted domain:

- **Existence:** every relevant collective `M` occurrence is itself, or constitutes, at least one lived M-perspective.
- **Instance individuation:** a principled, physically and phenomenally meaningful rule identifies which collective occurrences are numerically one; `P` and the property type alone need not do this.
- **Unique ownership:** each such occurrence belongs to exactly one subject, and every subject to be counted has exactly one such occurrence in the specified window.
- **No extra bearers:** the declared domain includes every relevant M-perspective; varying quality, concurrent multiple experiences and changing realizers are handled rather than ignored.

If these premises hold, the number of relevant subjects equals the number of relevant collective occurrences. This is a simple bijection, not an independent discovery of either number. Existence and unique ownership are separate: a shared occurrence could, under an alternative ontology, be present to several perspectives. Likewise one subject might have several relevant occurrences in a window. The [shared-token analysis](SHARED_EXPERIENCE_VERSUS_DUPLICATED_TOKENS_GATE.md) explains why matching local reports and shared physical support do not alone decide numerical token identity; it also sets out a conditional way to challenge specified rivals. None of these alternatives is assumed possible merely because we can write it down.

A potentially economical **field-first** rival takes an actual first-personal field occurrence to be a thin subject and identifies its qualitative organization with the field itself. This does not require an observer behind the field. It can satisfy existence by definition, but it does not derive the existence, multiplicity, unity or realization of those fields from a subject-free plural physical base. Conversely, Bohn can treat the collective consciousness fact as basic and accept its distribution as partly brute; that is a substantive ontological alternative, not automatically a failure by his own more modest explanatory standards. Neither approach has yet earned the project's stronger target of full structural **and experiential** intelligibility.

For E-L, replacing `q=1` with the words “collectively instantiates M” supplies no new reason why the selected pairs are the perspectives, why mismatch has that character, or why a whole subject is absent. A genuine completion must say what makes the plural property first-personal and must determine incidence, quality, and realization together, while distinguishing these from persistence. The [episode reformulation](EPISODE_INCIDENCE_REFORMULATION.md) explicitly cautions against presenting another graph or label as that completion.

## 5. Blaesi's parity challenge, accurately scoped

Blaesi reconstructs a master argument for **constitutive smallist panpsychism**: experience cannot be fully grounded in purely microphysical facts, dualist emergence is unacceptable, and a restricted choice of alternatives leaves basic experiential properties of ultimates. He constructs a parallel argument for *panmoralism*, with hypothetical fundamental normative properties of ultimates grounding ordinary moral facts. He treats the parity as a challenge to the **argument**, not as a direct proof that panpsychism is false; he discusses ways to accept the normative view, reject moral realism, break parity, or give a different argument. The premises and restricted alternatives are controversial, including non-panpsychist Russellian views he later discusses.

His section 6 distinguishes panmoralism from Bohn's **collective pluralized pannormism**, which assigns ordinary basic normative properties collectively to pluralities, not hypothetical micro-normative properties to individual ultimates. He raises conditional putative counterexamples to a general trickle-down principle, including the subject issue, and then sets them aside to press comparative costs even if the principle survives. He argues that collective properties may add basic ingredients rather than play the individual microphysical roles; that necessary or lawlike connections to their distribution need explanation; and that a higher-to-lower grounding connection may itself be unexplained. His essence-based dilemma and supervenience assumptions are contested, not conclusions established here. In note 38 he explicitly raises the ambiguity of grounding a thing's *being*: an interpretation that quantifies over how the thing is can make the premise question-begging against some rivals. This matters directly to any attempted whole-to-part TDP argument.

The project can grant the challenge without treating felt pain as merely a moral judgment. Experiential acquaintance and moral reasoning have different evidential profiles: a particular experience is locally present even when its report or interpretation is mistaken; whether an act is wrong typically requires evaluative and factual assessment. **That is a proposed epistemic asymmetry**, not yet a proof that the relevant metaphysical inferences or grounding arguments lack a moral parallel. Local presence by itself neither establishes that experience is fundamental, nor refutes a warranted physical identity, nor proves a unique necessary whole. A defense that simply repeats “experience is real” would not answer Blaesi's overgeneralization objection, because his own starting point accepts ordinary experience.

A disciplined response has three parts:

1. State exactly which phenomenal premise is warranted by the local datum, and do not infer fundamental experiential ingredients merely from the failure of a particular physical description to capture feeling.
2. Compare a neutral-ground identity, experiential-ground identity, and plural-primitive proposal on the **same** requirements: what determines qualitative organization, subject individuation, physical correspondence, and the necessary status of each bridge. Count unexplained identity, grounding and selection principles on every side, including ours.
3. Give a positive reason for any proposed asymmetry strong enough to block the particular parity argument; alternatively accept a normative analogue and assess its costs, or use a different independently defended argument. C3's full intelligibility remains an aspiration until that account is actually supplied.

**Subsequent operational comparison:** [Jointly controlled action versus a lived perspective](ACTION_UNITY_JOINT_CONTROL_VERSUS_LIVED_PERSPECTIVE.md) makes a common action depend on both physical mismatch inputs and contrasts that physical result with stipulated L-like and C-like phenomenal assignments. Action-level joint computation does not itself supply subject individuation or the valence law.

## 6. Evidence and next discriminating work

First-person qualitative occurrence is legitimate evidence, not an optional report variable. To distinguish constitution candidates empirically, however, one needs more: a specified jointly experienced target and window; safely manipulable physical conditions; an independently defensible account of access, reporting and error; and rival predictions that remain distinct under those conditions. Agreement of physical histories or local reports does not itself decide numerical ownership. Neither this audit nor the previous finite constructions provides a calibrated subject-count assay or establishes real collective-subject duplication.

The immediate constructive task is to formulate a **noncircular individuation principle** that explains one first-personal field and distinguishes a second field from a redescription, while allowing warranted qualitative articulation, real physical realizations and possible changing support. Its positive and negative boundary cases must be derived or independently motivated, not chosen to favor L or C. Then compare its explanatory commitments against field-first and neutral-ground alternatives, including Blaesi's parity challenge. An argument yielding only a conditional bijection or a finite model is useful constraint, but is not that principle.
