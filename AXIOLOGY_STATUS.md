# Axiology: consolidated argument, rival accounts, and next decisive work

## Executive assessment

The privation proposal deserves serious conjectural standing. Its strongest current content is that actual powers are positive and their wrongness can consist in missing due integration, rather than an additional opposing substance. The corrected empathy example is an improvement: destructive direction names an outcome, while missing due regard identifies the proposed privation.

However, several claims previously discussed as one package need separate support. In particular, the later qualitative-value hypothesis is an assistant-proposed extension, not something the user has already established or necessarily accepted. Denying independent evil substance does not itself establish either that all badness is privative or that normative Good explains existence.

The leading account form remains self-explanatory qualitative process monism. Constitutive axiology and strict axiarchism are increasingly specific candidate extensions, not discoveries of completed constitutive laws.

## Necessary occurrence: the current preferred route

[The necessary-occurrence candidate](NECESSARY_OCCURRENCE_CANDIDATE.md) distinguishes necessary concreteness of any instance from existence of an instance. It also tests the S5 route: possible necessity is stronger than mere possibility. The preferred starting point is known actual occurrence, investigating its nature rather than trying to produce existence from an abstract definition of Good. Its necessary character remains to be justified; this is an epistemic starting point, not exemption from the no-brute-facts objective.

## Self-explanatory actuality: current boundary

[The self-explanation audit](SELF_EXPLANATORY_ACTUALITY_AUDIT.md) tests bare self-support, unique global coherence, and a necessary determining ground. Self-support need not exclude nonoccurrence; unique abstract structure does not by itself establish concreteness. A necessary ground G plus a necessary implication from G to particular actuality P entails necessary P, but neither philosophical premise has been established. This is a standard conditional modal result, not discovery of the ground. The strongest contrastive reading of complete intelligibility favors investigating such a determining constitution without proving it from the no-brute-facts slogan alone.

## Actualization result: even a unique optimum needs a bridge

[The bridge audit](AXIARCHIC_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md) grants a complete value ordering with one uniquely best candidate and shows that an unconstrained realization predicate can still mark either candidate as actual. This applies the prior actuality diagnostic, not a new general theorem. A principle making only the best realizable excludes inferior realization but does not alone establish any realization; a stronger bridge supplies that missing premise and itself needs explanation. The preferred whole-account research direction is an intrinsically concrete common constitution, without treating the phrase “necessarily concrete” as proof. Value's constitution and existence's explanation should now proceed in parallel.

## Refined candidate: actual qualitative-relational fulfillment

[The standing and fulfillment analysis](ACTUAL_FULFILLMENT_AND_STANDING.md) distinguishes welfare, standing, and specific claims. Under the valid-payment premise, inducing satisfaction without the required transfer does not discharge the claim. Thus the richer candidate concerns actual powers and legitimate relations, not only current pleasant experience. Standing and legitimacy remain normative hypotheses requiring grounding; listing them is not a completed explanation. The previous qualitative-value proposal concerned at least some value and must not be silently read as a reduction of all value to felt valence.

## Current research preference: qualitative fulfillment

[The foundation comparison](FOUNDATION_OF_DUE_REGARD.md) tests goals/needs, rational agency, and qualitative fulfillment as grounds of due regard. Goals and functional standards support qualified evaluations but do not alone establish interpersonal reasons. The tested agency argument needs both a move from instrumental to unconditional worth and a move from agent-relative to interpersonal authority. Qualitative fulfillment most directly targets the intended intrinsic-value account and is the preferred candidate extension, not a proved winner. The reason-giving identity itself remains unestablished, and complete intelligibility cannot be defined so as to assume it.

## Worked local result: an applicable entitlement

[The due-regard argument](DUE_REGARD_ARGUMENT.md) supplies a concrete conditional case. Under a valid agreement and applicable entitlement rule, qualifying completed work entails payment being due. Withholding it can be analyzed as missing due integration, regardless of empathic feeling. The general rule is assumed, not derived from logic; legal consistency is not moral legitimacy, and an agent rejecting the rule is not thereby logically contradictory. This closes a local application question, not the authority or actualization questions.

## 1. The charitable reconstruction

The user's argument contains two strands:

1. **Goal reversal:** what frustrates one goal may serve its opposite; unqualified badness cannot mean disadvantage to every possible goal where the admissible goals include reversals.
2. **Positive being and privation:** actual capacities and activities are positive; apparent badness concerns missing fulfillment or integration, such as agency exercised without due regard for others.

The first strand is conditionally rigorous and symmetric between instrumental Good and Bad. The second supplies an ontological and evaluative hypothesis. It does not follow from goal reversal alone.

This distinction does not dismiss the second strand. It prevents its conjectural support from being confused with a proof obtained for a different predicate.

## 2. Three levels often conflated: substance, property, and relation

“No separate substance of evil exists” is weaker than “every bad-making feature is a privation.”

An account could deny an evil substance while holding that an actual experience possesses intrinsically negative valence. Another could regard wrongness as a relation between an action and the legitimate claims of affected beings. Neither must posit particles or a separate realm of evil.

These rival accounts might be wrong, but the absence of evil substance does not rule them out. Conversely, a privation theorist can acknowledge all actual processes and analyze their badness through missing due fulfillment. The dispute concerns the constitution of bad-making features, not whether harmful events occur.

Likewise, calling a property negative does not itself posit a negative substance. The physics analogy is strongest as a warning against multiplying substances; it is not a proof of the evaluative analysis.

## 3. The dependency map

### D1. Actuality has positive powers

Supported by the actual occurrences being discussed. “Positive” here means actual rather than absent; intrinsic normative worth has not yet been established by this vocabulary.

### D2. Every actual being has goodness insofar as it has being

A coherent goodness-of-being thesis. If Good simply names positive actuality, it is definitional. If it attributes genuine intrinsic worth, it is substantive and requires justification. It does not mean every actual act is morally justified or useful for every goal.

### D3. All badness is a privation of due fulfillment

A stronger hypothesis than denial of evil substance. It needs a warranted standard of what is due and coverage of apparently countervailing cases. Its success cannot be established by renaming every disfavored condition a deficiency.

### D4. Fulfillment supplies interpersonal reasons

The proposed axiological identity hypothesis: actual qualitative fulfillment has significance that is not created by another agent's chosen goal. It offers a possible foundation for due regard. It is not entailed by D1, the goal-reversal theorem, or biological function alone.

### D5. Value helps determine reality's constitution

Stronger than D4. Anonymity and Pareto improvement leave conflicting value rankings open; the exact example is given in [the constitutive-value development](CONSTITUTIVE_VALUE_PRINCIPLE.md#12-an-underdetermination-result-fulfillment-does-not-yet-select-actuality).

### D6. Value is indispensable to concrete existence

Strict axiarchism. A shared root of existence and value does not itself establish this asymmetric explanatory dependence. Necessary existence, if established, would also need to be necessary concrete occurrence rather than uniqueness of an abstract structure.

The missing implications are not proofs that D2–D6 are false. They show why the present derivation does not yet establish the complete package.

## 4. Three decisive adversarial cases

### Case A: the informed exploiter

An agent understands others, plans coherently, and deliberately disregards their welfare. This blocks a deduction of moral concern from thin agency or accurate representation alone. It does not block a thicker normative constitution, but that constitution must be independently justified.

The privative diagnosis can be missing practical regard, not absent cognitive empathy. Whether that regard is due is the substantive question.

### Case B: normally functioning aversive experience

Protective pain can occur through functioning systems, without a missing empathic faculty or an obvious malfunction. Its aversiveness is an actual qualitative feature. A privation account must identify the relevant deficiency—such as threatened bodily integrity or impeded fulfillment—and show why that analysis captures the evaluative feature rather than simply accompanying it.

This is not a proof against privation. It prevents “all unpleasant experience is a broken function” from doing the explanatory work. Pain, suffering, wrong action, and moral evil must not be treated as interchangeable categories. A functionally useful pain can have aversive character without being an all-things-considered wrong.

### Case C: competing undominated fulfillments

Arrangements (2,0), (1,1), and (0,2), interpreted as stipulated ordered fulfillment levels, can all be undominated. Symmetric lower-level-first and higher-level-first rules rank them differently while respecting Pareto improvement. Additional constitutive priorities are needed for selection; coherence alone does not supply them.

## 5. Comparative assessment of three routes

### Route P: privative constitutive goodness

Positive actuality has intrinsic worth; badness is deficient due fulfillment. This most directly preserves the user's proposal. Its main burden is a noncircular account of due fulfillment that covers the relevant cases.

### Route Q: qualitative-value identity

The condition of an actual qualitative perspective constitutes at least some intrinsic value and supplies prima facie reasons for treatment. This gives due regard more specific content and supports conditional realization neutrality. Its main burden is why qualitative condition has interpersonal reason-giving authority, rather than merely causal or first-person significance.

Route Q is compatible with P only if its account of negative evaluation can genuinely be analyzed privatively. Compatibility is not established simply by saying both concern experience. Q must not be quietly substituted for P and then reported as the user's argument.

### Route N: nonaxiarchic self-explanatory monism

One constitution explains physical occurrence, experience, and perhaps value, but value is not an independent or indispensable actualizer. This remains a live rival. The failure to prove strict axiarchism does not refute common constitution or intrinsic value.

The inquiry does not yet justify a calibrated probability ranking among these completed accounts. P and Q have clearer formulations than before; strict axiarchism has a more explicit explanatory burden.

## 6. What the comparison currently favors

For moral wrongdoing, P's strongest formulation is missing due practical regard rather than missing empathic feeling. Q could help ground that regard, but does not yet prove its authority. The informed-exploiter case therefore favors this formulation over a theory that treats all wrongdoers as unable to understand others. It does not decide between normative realism and its rival.

For aversive experience, Q directly acknowledges actual felt character. P must explain the proposed positive fulfillment whose privation constitutes the negative evaluation. If “missing well-being” is defined only as “whatever suffering is not,” the analysis is circular rather than explanatory. An independently specified condition—such as bodily integrity, integrated control, or undisturbed functioning—would be stronger, but none has yet been shown to capture every relevant case.

Calling an experience bad *for its subject in a respect* does not make it bad for every possible goal. Thus Q need not contradict the goal-reversal result. It can nevertheless conflict with a strong reading of D3 if negative valence is intrinsic but not privative. Denial of an evil substance does not resolve that conflict.

For ultimate selection, neither P nor Q currently determines a unique arrangement. Both need additional justified constraints. Their possible combination is therefore best treated as a **division-of-labor conjecture**: Q grounds the significance of affected perspectives; P analyzes failures of due regard as privations. This is a more specific candidate, not an established reduction of all badness to privation.

The user’s stronger universal privation proposal remains a separate live hypothesis. Narrowing the working candidate to moral failures must not be reported as having proved that stronger proposal.

## 7. Positive next work

The next targeted argument should compare P and Q on **what makes a fulfillment due**, using at least the three adversarial cases above. A candidate must specify its standard before assigning verdicts and explain:

- the bearer and circumstances of the standard;
- why it is not merely an arbitrarily selected end;
- whether it is functional, phenomenological, or normatively authoritative;
- how conflicts and different subjects are treated;
- what makes a failure privative rather than merely redescribed as a privation.

Only after this should a further actualization claim be credited with normative support. An independent ultimate-constitution argument can proceed in parallel, but no actualizing principle has been derived from the analogy.

Another finite simulation with supplied welfare scores would not resolve these questions. Neither would adding more names to the conjecture. The needed progress is a justified constitutive principle with consequences that distinguish it from the live rival.

## 8. Result of this consolidation

What has strengthened is the explicitness of the privation conjecture and the account of missing due regard. What has been conditionally established is the goal-reversal obstruction, the limited product-order consequences, and the repair constraint under stronger axiarchic premises. What remains unestablished is the normative identity and its role in concrete actuality.

**Unfinished is not automatically likely wrong. But identifying a promising account is also not the same as supplying its missing derivation.** This assessment preserves both distinctions.

No new empirical result, executable model, or increase in the research test count is claimed.

### Aspect-relative privation: targeted stress test

[The privation stress test](PRIVATION_STRESS_TEST.md) distinguishes positive actuality from due fulfillment indexed to bearer, respect, and circumstances. Functioning protective pain does not alone refute this richer theory: successful warning and a local cost can coexist. Mere absence of pleasure, however, does not distinguish neutral experience from suffering. A nontrivial theory must independently specify the positive fulfillment and justify why it is due, rather than define Good as not-Bad and report an explanation. The cruelty example remains strongest as missing practical integration of legitimate claims, not absent empathic machinery. These are conceptual compatibility and discrimination results, not a derivation of universal privation or axiarchic actuality.
