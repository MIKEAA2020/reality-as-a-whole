# Klinge on emergent consciousness: a challenge to constitution and causal closure

## Assessment

Fabian Klinge’s *Panpsychism and the Emergence of Consciousness* offers a serious alternative to constitutive panpsychism. His central proposal is that microsubjects and their experiences **causally produce** a new macrophenomenal occurrence that is fundamental, rather than composing or grounding it as a derivative phenomenal whole. The micro-experiences remain. He argues that this avoids certain combination arguments while permitting genuinely effective macrophenomenal causation.

This matters to the project: taking experience seriously does **not** by itself entail that a local experience is constituted by smaller experiences, or that a common experiential ground uniquely fixes subjects and qualities. Klinge provides an experience-respecting competitor. But his causal-emergence proposal does not automatically satisfy the project’s demand for full experiential intelligibility: its special producing powers, phenomenal inheritance rule, pairing structure and causal laws also need explanation. His proposals are substantive metaphysical hypotheses, not established neuroscience or a derivation of the project’s `q`-to-quality relation.

A small independently specified causal model below clarifies one of his strongest distinctions: having an immediate micro-level sufficient cause is different from having a closed evolution from an earlier microstate. This formal distinction is useful, but neither confirms nor refutes his detailed theory.

## 2. The actual proposal

| Question | Klinge’s proposed answer | Cost or unresolved premise |
|---|---|---|
| **Micro–macro relation** | A structured microphenomenal base causally produces a separate, fundamental macrophenomenal event; constituents survive rather than fusing away (pp. 182–183). | The existence of such micro-experiences and the non-constitutive production law are not demonstrated. |
| **Dependence** | Special context-dependent micro-powers jointly produce phenomenal events. Longer states are sustained through continuing production of short events (pp. 91–96). | The powers, conditions, temporal grain and relation between a succession and one enduring episode need justification. |
| **Mental efficacy** | A macrophenomenal property affects the dispositional profiles of microintentions, indirectly changing physical effects (pp. 187–190). | Klinge explicitly rejects stronger microphysical causal closure; physical consistency and testability remain substantive obligations. |
| **Pairing** | A separate two-layer intrinsic framework pairs a macroquale with its own microphenomenal base; it acts on microintentions in that base (pp. 197–200). | Exclusive pairing and collective action are assumed in the framework; they are not supplied by the word “intrinsic.” |
| **Subject and quality selection** | A speculative IIT-inspired rule applies maximal integration/exclusion to emergent macroconsciousness, while microconsciousness persists (pp. 207–211). A further law must specify inherited character. | The author expressly presents this as an exploration, not a verified law; it is not our four-spin L rule or IIT 4.0. |

In his account microintentions are not ordinary human deliberations: they are primitive, simple dispositions or urges that mediate between micro-experience and microphysical behavior (pp. 171–174). His electron-repulsion/micro-pain example is an **illustrative analogy**, not evidence that electrons feel pain or that their charge changes due to experience. The proposal also differs from Benovsky’s non-experiential proto-mental base and no-reified-bearer strategy, discussed in the [Benovsky audit](BENOVSKY_NO_SELF_DUAL_ASPECT_FULL_BOOK_AUDIT.md).

## 3. What emergence does—and does not—explain

Klinge’s move is a genuine alternative to *summing* or composing microsubjects: if the macro-occurrence is a new effect rather than the sum of micro-occurrences, the demand to explain that particular subject-summing operation does not arise. This does not establish the occurrence merely from a diagram. One still needs a specified producing base, a law connecting that base to **this** felt character, an account of the occurring subject, and a reason for that law and its conditions. Klinge explicitly regards a causal explanation in terms of the producing powers as the appropriate explanation for a fundamental but dependent outcome (pp. 98–100); the project’s C3 demands more than a stipulated causal regularity if the law and phenomenal nature remain unexplained. That is a difference in explanatory standards, not a claim that ordinary causal explanations are worthless.

There is a precise pressure point in his sustainment account. He initially frames strong dependence as the emergent being unable to persist without its base; he then says a short event, once produced, exists independently for roughly 100 ms, whereas a longer state is sustained by repeated production (pp. 91–92). A short lifetime alone does not logically imply simultaneous dependence on a base. Continuous production can explain the continuation of a *series* if the series criterion is fixed; it does not automatically establish the simultaneous dependence, unity, or personal persistence of each event. The 100 ms figure is Klinge’s illustrative assertion here, not an independently validated universal experiential time quantum. This is an unresolved choice of event and dependence semantics, not a proof that causal emergence is incoherent.

### Revelation, constitution and the whole

Klinge endorses a strong *Direct Phenomenal Transparency*: direct acquaintance with experience is taken to reveal its complete essence and, with further grounding assumptions, its dependence relations. He uses this to press both micro-constitutive and certain cosmic-subsumption accounts (pp. 154–166). He also presents and rejects Roelofs’s response that knowledge of a potential ground is required to recognize a grounding relation (pp. 158–160). This is a serious argument, but the strong transition from **experience is given** to **all objectual and constitutive facts are introspectively accessible** is a substantive premise, not a consequence we have established. Rejecting that transition does not license denying experience or claiming an opaque grounding solves the problem.

His cosmopsychist discussion concerns specific accounts of an encompassing **cosmic subject** and its phenomenal parts. Our current candidate posits a fundamentally experiential whole with genuinely plural subjects, **not** one universal subject. Therefore his argument does not automatically refute it. The project must still specify how the whole’s intrinsic character relates to real individual fields, why those fields and qualities occur, and how relations between subjects and physical spacetime arise. His closing discussion emphasizes that a monistic account of spatiotemporal relations remains unfinished and considers a cosmic subject as a possible route (pp. 217–220). That route cannot simply be imported as the user’s preferred ontology.

## 4. Immediate causes versus physical closure: a checked illustration

Klinge distinguishes his narrow **Completeness**—a physical effect has an immediate sufficient microcause—from stronger microphysical **closure**, which excludes non-microphysical intervention in the microcausal chain. He argues that his downward influence on a microintention changes which effect it produces without adding a second independent sufficient cause of that effect (pp. 188–190). He explicitly acknowledges that his proposal violates the stronger closure claim. Figure 7.2 represents macro-influence on the microcausal arrow rather than a second direct arrow into its effect.

The following **new toy is ours, not Klinge’s equation**. Let an earlier microvariable `B`, a macro-variable `E`, a subsequent micro-disposition `D` and an outcome `Y` each be binary:

```text
D = B XOR E
Y = D
```

For every setting, `D` fixes `Y` at the subsequent step. This depicts an immediate micro-level cause of `Y`, with the macro variable acting upstream through `D`; it does not place two independent sufficient causes directly on `Y`. Yet if `E` is separately varied while `B` is held fixed, then `B` alone cannot determine `D` or `Y`. Immediate micro-sufficiency and closure of the earlier micro-transition are **different claims**. Whether `D` really counts as a complete physical cause in Klinge’s ontology is a further interpretive question, and whether a real physical theory is closed depends on the full relevant state and its laws, not an arbitrarily selected bit.

Now impose the natural-regime rule `E = B`. The model then yields `D = Y = 0` for both values of `B`. A rival with the same recorded `B` and `E` but equations `D = 0`, `Y = D` has the **same complete recorded tuples in that regime**. The models differ under a stipulated surgery setting `E = 1−B`: the first gives `D = Y = 1`, the rival still gives zero. This formal exercise illustrates why observational agreement on the restricted regime cannot determine the proposed macro effect.

**Important limit:** the surgery violates the stipulated natural relation `E = B`. It is a mathematical intervention, not evidence that one can physically or metaphysically alter a dependent experience while its complete base is fixed. If no such intervention is admissible, the toy’s surgical distinction cannot be taken as an operational test. Likewise it does not prove that Klinge’s actual model is observationally equivalent to a closed physical theory, or that either model is genuinely possible under the project’s strong modal commitments. ## 5. Evolution, IIT and empirical discriminability

Klinge proposes that evolution could select organisms in which macrophenomenal structure is appropriately fitted to psychological awareness, because his macroqualities have downward effects on behavior (pp. 203–206). This is not an argument that experience is merely a report, nor the simpler objection that an epiphenomenon cannot evolve: he explicitly distinguishes the possibility of a correlated by-product. His account depends on a lawful inheritance rule, differential reproductive consequences of its downward effects, and enough relevant variation; selection could favor what affects fitness, but those premises do not by themselves show a unique subjective structure or derive a particular pain quality. His stronger structural-coherence claim is not validated simply by the existence of verbal reports.

Klinge suggests downward influence might be hidden within chaotic neural dynamics, and labels his proposed mechanics speculative (pp. 190–191). Chaos makes prediction difficult; it does not by itself prove that intervention is undetectable in principle or that any observed deviation is mental. Conversely, calling the proposal unfalsifiable does not follow solely from its novelty. A discriminating empirical model would need an independently specified interaction, conservation and dynamical constraints, explicit rival predictions, measurable targets, and intervention or observation assumptions that do not beg the question. A statistical association between EEG and ratings supplies none of that automatically.

His modified IIT sketch proposes macro-selection by local integration maxima while maintaining microphenomenality; it does not identify an actual emergence law, and he expressly declines to assert that IIT or his modification is correct (pp. 207–211). On p. 210 he objects to one micro-latent disposition sustaining two distinct macrophenomenal properties at once, suggesting one cause cannot simultaneously produce distinct effects. That is not a general principle of causation: a single alarm activation can cause a light to flash and a bell to sound. Even a model with `A = Z` and `B = 2Z` allows one input to affect two distinguishable outputs. The mere fact that bases share a constituent also does not make the *complete* producing conditions identical. Klinge may intend a narrower constraint specific to phenomenal powers, but that constraint needs its own argument; the counterexample does not show that overlapping subjects actually exist or settle the phenomenology.

The project’s [phase-lag gate](OBSERVED_PHASE_LAGS_VERSUS_PHYSICAL_FRUSTRATION_GATE.md) and [physical mismatch measurement gate](FOUR_SPIN_PHYSICAL_MISMATCH_MEASUREMENT_GATE.md) likewise distinguish an observed neural statistic from independently identified interaction preferences. Neither Klinge nor this toy establishes the candidate’s `q`, the existence of microsubjects, or a first-person bridge.

## 6. Consequences for the current candidate

1. **Keep emergence as a live rival.** A non-constitutive, experience-respecting alternative exposes an assumption in the project’s field-incidence and quality rules; comparing predictions and explanatory costs must not presume that those rules follow from experiential ground.
2. **Require a local production/constitution account either way.** An experiential whole, a non-constitutive emergence law, or a no-bearer description must each say what makes a particular occurrence felt and how simultaneous and successive occurrences are individuated. Neither the label “fundamental” nor “one whole” derives this.
3. **State causal and temporal assumptions separately.** Immediate sufficient causes, closure across complete states, lawful emergence, possible interventions and local temporal order are distinct. A fundamentally timeless whole would need an internal account of the causal and succession relations in any adapted emergence story; Klinge’s temporally phrased production does not by itself supply it.
4. **Retain the battery’s open verdicts.** No empirical physical–phenomenal anchor, subject-incidence derivation, measure or complete recovery of physics follows. B4–B5/F2, B9/F3, B10 and full structural **and experiential** intelligibility C3 remain open.
