# Actual fulfillment, welfare, and standing

## Direct result

The candidate should not identify fulfillment with pleasant experience, satisfaction reports, or the belief that one's goals have been achieved. At least the entitlement example concerns actual relations and powers, not only their appearance to the subject.

The most promising formulation is **realized qualitative-relational fulfillment**: actual beings, their qualitative condition, their agency, and the legitimate relations in which they stand. This extends the axiological candidate beyond a purely hedonic theory without asserting that every actual relation is morally authoritative.

The result is a conceptual distinction and a conditional argument about claims. It is not a proof of moral realism, a new empirical finding, or an actualizing principle.

## 1. A sharper version of the money example

Assume the valid payment obligation used in [the due-regard argument](DUE_REGARD_ARGUMENT.md). Compare:

- **Payment:** the worker receives the due funds through a transfer that satisfies the agreement.
- **Appearance:** the worker is led to believe payment occurred, but no such transfer takes place; the outstanding claim to payment remains.

The cases need not differ immediately in the worker's felt satisfaction. This is a local conceptual comparison, not a claim that two complete physical histories can be identical in every experience or causal effect while all relevant relations differ.

Under the retained entitlement rule, appearance does not discharge the obligation. The worker's satisfaction is neither the specified payment nor the due relation itself.

Therefore, if the normative account is to preserve the valid entitlement, its conception of fulfillment must include more than the current feeling that the entitlement has been fulfilled.

This is an exact conditional conclusion. A theorist may reject the rule, argue that the circumstances defeat it, or adopt a purely experiential criterion that refuses it. Those are substantive alternatives, not logical contradictions. The comparison does not independently establish which ultimate theory is true.

## 2. Three kinds of significance

### Welfare

How the being fares, including qualitative benefits and burdens. Even this need not reduce to a single pleasure score. The account must specify which conditions constitute welfare and how they relate to felt valence, agency, and actual circumstances.

### Standing

Whether the being is a subject whose interests and agency count in their own right, rather than merely an instrument in someone else's project. Standing is the proposed reason not to erase the affected perspective.

This is a substantive normative notion. Real perspectivehood does not by logic alone prove a particular level or kind of standing. The constitutive identity hypothesis is intended to explain that connection.

### Specific claims

What is due in a particular relationship or situation: for example, an entitlement under a valid agreement. A claim can depend on actual commitments, qualifying acts, and legitimate authority. It need not be equivalent to the welfare difference produced by honoring it in one isolated case.

These categories can interact without being identical. Improving someone's current mood does not automatically discharge a specific claim; frustrating a desire does not automatically violate a right; recognizing standing does not imply satisfying every demand.

## 3. The next pressure test: pleasure from mistreatment

Suppose one agent takes pleasure in exploiting another. The pleasure is an actual qualitative occurrence. On a theory of positive being, its occurrence has actuality; the agent may also receive a prudential benefit in some respect.

That does not settle the action's moral evaluation. The affected being's standing and claims are not automatically canceled by another's pleasure. Nor has the current theory supplied an aggregation rule proving that any amount of pleasure can outweigh any violation.

Conversely, the absence of such an aggregation rule is not yet a proof of an absolute prohibition. The account needs justified constraints on how these categories interact.

A privative analysis can locate a missing integration of the affected being's standing, while retaining the positive actuality of the pleasure, agency, and action. This is stronger than calling pleasure unreal or assuming every wrongdoer is unhappy.

## 4. Why good intention or empathy is insufficient

An agent can accurately imagine another's welfare and fail to realize what is due. They can feel concern while substituting reassurance or a convincing appearance for an actual obligation. Another agent can lack strong empathic feeling yet fulfill the obligation.

The relevant integration is therefore not merely cognitive representation or affect. It concerns the practical relations that the action actually realizes.

This connects the axiological investigation to the repository's standing realization distinction: a description, report, or represented fulfillment need not instantiate the relevant condition. It is an analogy about constitution, not proof that the same physical law governs consciousness and moral claims.

## 5. The revised candidate

> Actual qualitative beings have forms of fulfillment grounded in their condition, powers, and legitimate relations. Their significance is not bestowed solely by an observer's project. Due practical organization acknowledges their welfare, standing, and applicable claims. Wrongdoing can consist in a privation of that realized integration, even where actual powers succeed and someone experiences pleasure.

The adjective **legitimate** is not a completed foundation. It marks the further requirement to justify the relevant relation, not simply to find it in an existing institution. Slavery, coercion, or exploitation cannot be vindicated merely by their having a consistent legal form.

The revision makes explicit what the broader qualitative-value candidate should mean. The earlier proposal claimed that at least some value is constituted by qualitative condition; this analysis does not refute that narrower claim. It prevents the proposal from silently expanding into the unsupported thesis that all value is determined by current felt valence.

## 6. Consequences for privation

### What the richer account explains in outline

A wrong can involve a missing relation of due acknowledgment, not just a missing pleasure or defective organ. Thus the bearer of the privation can be an action's practical organization or an interpersonal relationship.

This accounts for the user's correction: acquisition can be positive while the missing integration of others' legitimate needs and shares constitutes its proposed defect.

### What it does not prove

It does not establish that all wrongs have the same form or that every negative valence is reducible to a relational deficiency. It also does not derive standing, establish a complete distributive rule, or justify every entitlement.

The wider the proposed positive fulfillment becomes, the more important the anti-circularity requirement is: the theory must specify its constituent goods and their authority rather than include every favored verdict under “proper integration.”

## 7. Consequences for whole-account explanation

The richer candidate is not “maximize pleasant states.” It is a common-constitution programme for actual qualitative-relational beings, including the norms of their legitimate interaction.

Under complete intelligibility, the ultimate account must explain the relation among welfare, standing, and claims. Merely listing all three as independent brute axioms would not satisfy the objective. They could be different expressions of one constitution, but their unity remains to be justified.

A shared constitution of physical powers, experience, and value is compatible with the leading monism. It does not yet show that value selects the physical laws, determines the subject-incidence structure, or explains concrete existence. Those remain stronger axiarchic requirements.

## 8. Current positive advance

We have identified a nontrivial constraint on the proposed value theory:

> If a valid claim requires an actual relation, a merely represented or pleasantly believed fulfillment does not discharge it.

This gives the privation account a more concrete target: missing actual integration, not an absent sensation or lack of empathetic information.

The next foundational argument must explain why actual qualitative beings have standing and how legitimate claims arise from their constitution and relations. A purely instrumental account and a purely hedonic account remain intelligible rivals, but neither should be mistaken for the richer candidate now being developed.
