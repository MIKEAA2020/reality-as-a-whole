# A real witness and a boundary test for the maximal rule

## Verdict

The first-person evidence we already have does not establish the specific simultaneous bilateral conjunction used to distinguish the three candidate packages. It does, however, provide meaningful evidence against a **stronger, simpler** claim that the verbal reporter has no visual experience on the left side under ordinary viewing. Separately, the proposed maximal-connectivity rule has a serious conditional problem: if applied indiscriminately at an interpersonal causal scale, reciprocal interaction joins two people into one strongly connected component and therefore selects **one** subject where the ordinary distinct-subject premise says there are two.

These are not two proofs that the framework is false. They identify a claim already challenged by an actual report and a further, concrete burden on our own candidate rule. Neither establishes the number of subjects in a split-brain patient, finds a new biological mechanism, or solves experiential constitution. The mapping from the toy graph to human brains remains missing.

## 1. An actual first-person report, and its exact target

The main article by [Pinto et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/aww358) reports that both participants, DDC and DDV, indicated they saw their entire visual field, including both sides of fixation. The project's prior [supplement audit](PINTO_2017_SUPPLEMENT_FIRST_PERSON_AND_METHODS_AUDIT.md) records the individual structured responses more cautiously: **DDC** reported seeing well on both sides, similarly before and after surgery; **DDV** reported peripheral blurring. Their bodily and control answers differed too. The supplement was read in that prior work; it was not recovered anew in this pass, and we do not have the raw interview audio or trial-level records here. The authors' main article was rechecked in the [repository version](https://pure.uva.nl/ws/files/21255810/Split_brain.pdf), including methods, results and discussion.

Consider a deliberately **strong clinical hypothesis H**: “After this patient's surgery, the verbally reporting experiencer never has any visual experience of the left side, even during ordinary unrestricted viewing.” H is a literal, universal no-experience claim about that reporter; it is **not** attributed to Sperry, a published contemporary theory, or all two-subject accounts. Applied to DDC, his reported bilateral sight is evidence against H, if his answer refers to his own actual visual experience on the left and is correctly communicated and interpreted. It does not depend on his being able to report absence correctly on every occasion. The account of ordinary sight deserves positive weight; it is not a formal proof of infallibility, and the exact circumstances and window of his answer are not specified.

This is a **retrospective challenge**, chosen after seeing the study, not a preregistered test of H. It rejects neither every version of lateralized processing nor the idea of another subject. Both eyes and head can move during ordinary viewing, input can be duplicated across time, and surviving pathways or later processing may contribute. Saying “left side” in a natural-language account does not establish isolated, 120-ms input to one hemisphere. These possibilities do not make DDC's report unreal; they show why H is much stronger than the real scientific alternatives.

## 2. What the actual experiments add—and do not add

Pinto et al.'s experiment 1 presented either a circle for 120 ms or no circle; the two patients responded verbally or with either hand in separate blocks. The authors report detection and localization across visual fields and response types. Their overall detection figures were **97.5% hits / 7.7% false alarms** for DDC and **100% / 0%** for DDV. These are the article's pooled summaries, **not** left-field verbal cell rates; the [supplement audit](PINTO_2017_SUPPLEMENT_FIRST_PERSON_AND_METHODS_AUDIT.md) records substantial eye-data exclusions and response-mode details. We have not independently recalculated the tests or established that absent and present trial samples are matched in all relevant respects. The published findings challenge the strict operational prediction that left-field targets cannot be reported verbally in these patients under the tested conditions. They do not identify the neural route or prove that every detected stimulus was consciously experienced.

The specific question from the previous proposal was stricter:

> Did one specified reporter experience two specified left- and right-side details **together in the same brief window**, in a condition mapped to the candidate's two local supports?

The general bilateral-sight answer does not explicitly answer that trial-specific question. In [Pinto et al. (2023), experiment I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10667445/), DDC judged whether two simultaneously presented shapes were the same and rated confidence. The paper reports high confidence on 52% of cross-field versus 21% of within-field trials, despite cross-field accuracy not significantly exceeding chance. Those ratings concern confidence in a **comparison**, not an explicit report that both particular shapes were simultaneously experienced together. Its experiment II used sequential displays and cannot simply be substituted for a simultaneous-experience test. Both findings are meaningful; neither provides the proposed conjunction witness, a false-positive bound for it, or a subject census.

| Claim to assess | Evidence in this case | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| DDC, the verbal reporter, never visually experiences the left side under ordinary viewing (H) | His reported bilateral sight | **Challenged**, conditional on ordinary interpretation and communication; not a new clinical measurement. |
| Left-field targets cannot be reported verbally under the 2017 experiment's conditions | Reported cross-field detection and localization | **Challenged as an operational claim** by the authors' findings; route and experiential status remain open. |
| No subject ever experiences the two specified briefly presented details together | The available testimony is not a trial-specific conjunction report | **Not identified** by these records. Poor matching is not proof of absent conjunction. |
| Exactly one subject, or three including private local subjects, was present | Neither task identifies the full population or private occurrence identity | **Not identified**. Ordinary personal unity is not a complete census; extra subjects are not established either. |

This distinction keeps the positive report rather than dismissing it, but also avoids promoting an ordinary-vision statement into the missing trial-level result. No numerical false-positive bound or new participant data was obtained.

## 3. A problem for our own maximal-connectivity candidate

The previous [three-candidate test](SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION_THREE_CANDIDATES_CRITICAL_TEST.md) defined M on a small graph: each maximal strongly connected component is one field, with no additional proper nested fields. Its scope was explicitly the two invented conditions; it was **not** a finished universal law. What if we try the tempting generalization “apply the same rule to every causal graph at every scale”?

Assume, for a particular episode, the following independently stated premises:

1. Two people, A and B, are **numerically distinct experiencing subjects** during an ordinary conversation. Their distinctness is a substantive premise here, not derived from their two bodies, names or response channels.
2. A legitimate causal representation includes disjoint supports for A and B. Each support is strongly connected internally, and there is a directed causal path from A's support to B's and a directed path from B's to A's within the specified effective graph. These could represent reciprocal communication, but the existence and relevance of such paths must be established in a real case, not inferred just from the word “conversation.”
3. The generalized M applies to that very graph and time/causal horizon; its exclusion clause says a maximal component supplies one field and no separate subject wholly contained in it.

The union of the two supports lies in one strongly connected component: any point in either support can reach a point in the other via an internal path, the cross-path, and another internal path, and can return. It remains contained in one maximal component even if other nodes join it. Thus generalized M's exclusion clause conflicts with premise 1. The contradiction follows from the premises; it is **not** an empirical determination of the relevant graph, a proof of separate subjecthood, or a discovery about actual conversations. A time-unrolled graph can be acyclic, and feedback edges depend on coarse-graining, interventions, thresholds and horizons. We have not measured them here.

The challenge is consequential because plausible interpersonal interactions create a need for a **principled scale and boundary**, rather than automatically making all communication a phenomenal merger. It is not enough to stipulate “use the brain graph” if the theory has not explained why that graph rather than a larger, smaller, differently timed or coarse-grained realization is constitutively privileged. Equally, selecting a particular graph can be a substantive and defensible hypothesis; the point is to specify and justify it, not to forbid any fixed-scale model.

### Available repairs and what each costs

- **Restrict admissible realizations.** Identify a non-arbitrary intrinsic physical scale, causal closure or other condition that admits an individual brain but excludes the coupled interpersonal system. Show that it is independently specified and robust to appropriate redescriptions, and say what happens at ties. A verbal label such as “organism” alone is not an explanation.
- **Weight and time the interaction.** Replace mere reachability with a quantitative, temporal integration criterion. Explain the units, interventions, threshold or graded treatment; then test transitions and separate high-dimensional internal influence from low-capacity external channels. No particular integration metric or subject transition follows from this suggestion.
- **Permit nested fields.** Allow individual subjects even when an encompassing component exists. That avoids the immediate exclusion conflict, but must explain whether and when the additional collective field exists, how subjects and private or shared occurrences are individuated, and why arbitrary subdivisions do not proliferate.
- **Reject the distinct-subject premise in the target case.** This avoids the logical contradiction but is an extraordinary substantive cost, not a free implication of graph connectivity. The project requires genuine plurality, and a successful account would have to explain where that plurality occurs and why it does not correspond to the apparently distinct people in the case.

Our verdict is to **reject the unrestricted all-scales version of M as a viable project principle under the stated distinct-person and graph premises**. Keep the original finite M as a diagnostic model only. This is a conditional constraint, not proof that every sophisticated maximality, exclusion, global-workspace, or integrated-information theory makes the same prediction; their actual realization rules and metrics must be analyzed on their own terms.

## 4. Next decisive step

Do not keep multiplying unconstrained toy graphs. A serious candidate must now provide a **realization rule before a subject selector**: what physical degrees of freedom, interventions and time horizon define its admissible system, how the rule treats nested and coupled systems, and how it preserves a warranted ordinary plurality without assuming the conclusion. Then it must make a specific prediction for an independently measured case and state how a reporter's experience is anchored. If no candidate can state those ingredients without circularity or arbitrary exceptions, report that failure rather than declare a solution.

For the present clinical case, the best answer remains: the actual reports and cross-response results constrain strong lateralized claims; the target simultaneous-conjunction and subject-identity questions remain unresolved. This is genuine pruning and a clearer design requirement, **not yet non-incremental progress on the central qualitative bridge**. No participant contact, clinical intervention or raw-data reanalysis was performed.
