# Pain intensity and unpleasantness: a common-interface benchmark

## Result

In an exploratory reanalysis of the PAINgen placebo study's condition averages, a contemporaneous **intensity report** is a strong held-out-family predictor of the separately prompted unpleasantness report. On 4,380 complete condition means, a condition-only linear model has row RMSE **0.15369**, while adding intensity reduces it to **0.05714**. Adding both available fMRI pattern scores to the intensity model changes RMSE only to **0.05690** in this fixed comparison; its MAE becomes slightly worse. An exploratory cluster interval for this RMSE change crosses zero.

This is a practically useful **report-based comparator**, not an independent physical measure of experience. It does not show that sensory intensity and unpleasantness are the same experience, that either prompt captures its target without error, that fMRI is uninformative in every model, or that the project's physical mismatch is absent. Both targets are post-stimulation, condition-averaged reports; using intensity makes this a contemporaneous cross-report prediction, not an advance forecast. Experience remains a datum, and reports are evidence about it rather than the definition of it.

## 1. Why this comparison matters

The [toothachewise paraphrase audit](TOOTHACHEWISE_PARAPHRASE_AND_REAL_ASPECTS_AUDIT.md) asks a proposed translation to preserve relevant qualitative distinctions rather than simply predict pain behavior. The [earlier unpleasantness benchmark](PAINGEN_UNPLEASANTNESS_BASELINE_AND_MISMATCH_GATE.md) compared condition and two fMRI signatures, without including the separately elicited intensity report. This follow-up asks how much that **available rival report** explains under the same family split, and whether the signatures add predictive information over it under a simple, fixed model. The point is to expose a demanding baseline and the limits of treating a successful report predictor as a constitutive explanation—not to rank phenomenal ontologies by prediction error.

Botvinik-Nezer et al., [*Placebo treatment affects brain systems related to affective and cognitive processes, but not nociceptive pain*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-50103-8), already reported that the two ratings are highly correlated, with median within-participant trialwise `r = 0.956` among 372 participants for whom it was calculable. That is the authors' trialwise result, **not** our correlation below, which uses changes between condition means. Their experiment asked intensity and unpleasantness separately, randomized their order, and trained the distinction using a loudness/liking analogy. [Our full-paper audit](BOTVINIK_NEZER_PLACEBO_FULL_PAPER_AUDIT.md) records the source, conditioning, scale, imaging, reviewer concerns, and differences in descriptions of timing. No raw trials or scans were replayed here.

The available author CSV averages ratings by participant, modality, nominal level and treatment. The means represent separate prompts on the same nominal experimental conditions; we did not independently reconstruct which trials contributed to each mean or their precise timing. Matching is by coded nominal level, not independently verified equal delivered inputs, finger sensitivity, neural state or physical mismatch. NPS and SIIPS are author-derived fMRI pattern scores, not independent `q` measurements or pure intensity/affect meters.

## 2. Fixed retrospective comparison

The [analysis plan](PAINGEN_INTENSITY_UNPLEASANTNESS_HELDOUT_PLAN.md) was written before running this particular calculation, but **after** the source publication and prior analyses of the same data. It is retrospective and not blinded, prospectively preregistered or an independent replication. The [code](verification/paingen_intensity_unpleasantness_gate.py) checks the pinned author table's length and SHA-256, and writes an aggregate [receipt](verification/paingen_intensity_unpleasantness_gate.json), not participant records. Our prior [source cross-check](PAINGEN_UNPLEASANTNESS_BASELINE_AND_MISMATCH_GATE.md) matched both rating fields against the authors' behavioral export, but the two exports share provenance and do not verify raw trial collection.

The file has 4,687 rows, 392 people and 243 families. Requiring finite intensity, unpleasantness, NPS and SIIPS leaves **4,380 rows, 376 people and 237 families**; 307 rows lack each of the two ratings and none lacks either signature. Missing-field counts can overlap; they are not additive. There are 2,225 person × modality × level cells with at least one eligible row, including **2,155 complete control/placebo pairs** and 70 with one eligible condition. Cells with neither eligible condition are absent from that 2,225; the earlier [matched analysis](MATCHED_STIMULUS_UNPLEASANTNESS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_BRIDGE.md) counted 2,352 candidate cells from the unscreened table. The complete-pair set includes 369 people and 236 families. Selection may matter.

All four ordinary least-squares models use the same rows and deterministic five family-held-out folds. Each has 12 condition-cell intercepts; additional predictors enter with global slopes and training-fold standardization. There is no participant-specific fitting on held-out families, but these are the same participants and study already explored in the project. Relative to an entirely new cohort, protocol, scanner or clinical population, transport remains untested.

| Predictors of condition-mean unpleasantness | Row RMSE | Row MAE | Family-weighted RMSE | Family-weighted MAE |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Condition | 0.15369 | 0.11310 | 0.15836 | 0.11580 |
| Condition + NPS + SIIPS | 0.14409 | 0.10603 | 0.14753 | 0.10823 |
| Condition + intensity report | 0.05714 | 0.03330 | 0.05886 | 0.03424 |
| Condition + intensity report + NPS + SIIPS | 0.05690 | 0.03349 | 0.05845 | 0.03442 |

Family-weighted RMSE is the square root of the mean of each family's mean squared error; family-weighted MAE averages family MAEs. The input uses the study's 0–1 labeled magnitude scale, which is not a cardinal unit of pain or suffering. The intensity model changes row RMSE by about **−62.8%** relative to condition alone on this dataset, not relative to a prospective clinical baseline. The full-minus-intensity row RMSE difference is **−0.000245**; the family-resampled 95% percentile interval, holding the already fitted out-of-fold predictions fixed, is **[−0.000634, +0.000135]**. It crosses zero and omits refitting variability; shared training sets across folds mean it is not a complete cross-validation uncertainty interval or equivalence test.

Unclipped predictions below zero occur in 0, 84, 179 and 220 rows, respectively; none exceeds one. Clipping was not part of the fixed comparison. This is a limitation of the linear benchmarks, not observed negative pain. The intensity predictor is measured in the same experimental conditions as the target, while NPS and SIIPS summarize concurrent neuroimaging; neither comparison justifies interpreting these scores as a pre-stimulus forecast. Shared rating instructions and response tendencies can contribute to predictive performance; they do not establish that the reported experiences are unreal.

## 3. Matched treatment changes: dimensions are related, not proven identical

For each complete pair, `dU` is control minus placebo unpleasantness, and `dI` is control minus placebo intensity. These are changes in numeric report means, not interventions on only one phenomenal dimension. Condition-mean contrasts and their correlations are not the authors' trialwise within-person correlations.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| All | 2,155 | 0.04466 | 0.04640 | −0.00174 | [−0.00439, 0.00104] | 0.893 |
| Thermal | 1,080 | 0.05258 | 0.05365 | −0.00108 | [−0.00473, 0.00271] | 0.883 |

Intervals resample whole families with all included cells, retaining their repeated measures; they concern these pair-weighted, complete-case, scale-dependent descriptions. The interval crossing zero does not establish equivalence, a selective affect change, or unchanged sensory experience. Equal numbers on distinct prompts do not by themselves constitute equal subjective magnitudes. Mean differences may conceal heterogeneity and are influenced by scale nonlinearity, common response processes, treatment context and missingness. The pair differences are not a randomized test separating suggestion, conditioning, skin site and order.

Subtracting the two **out-of-fold row predictions**, without refitting to the contrast, yields all-pair RMSEs of **0.13598** for condition, **0.13304** for condition + signatures, **0.06161** for condition + intensity and **0.06160** for the full model. This last numerical improvement is about **0.000005**, with all-pair MAE worsening from **0.03639** to **0.03716**. These are descriptive same-study predictions, not evidence that any individual treatment effect is recovered accurately or that MRI patterns have zero incremental value under other designs. A different weighting, nonlinear model, task or physically identified predictor may behave differently.

## 4. Consequence for a genuine bridge test

1. **Separate targets before modeling.** A theory that proposes an account specifically of unpleasantness must preserve the distinction from reported pain intensity, other felt qualities and their temporal conjunction. A dataset with both prompts is better suited than one that asks only intensity, but condition averages do not validate a precise simultaneous trial-level phenomenal target.
2. **Do not confuse the comparator with an independent physical anchor.** Intensity is another subjective rating made in the same task; it is valuable as a stringent prediction benchmark but can share response noise and conceptual overlap with unpleasantness. A model that accurately predicts one from the other has not established their experiential identity, measured the structure that constitutes either, or identified an experiencing field. Nor does a residual automatically isolate a pure affective experience.
3. **State what the physical candidate adds and how it is identified.** This table contains no independently identified channels, preferred offsets, selective perturbations or subject boundary for the project's mismatch `q`. Defining `q` from intensity, unpleasantness, NPS or SIIPS would make this particular test circular. A future study must specify those quantities without using held-out unpleasantness, validate temporal alignment and perturbation controls, and compare with stimulus, context, independent physiological measures and both with/without-intensity baselines. The [phase-identification analysis](OBSERVED_PHASE_LAGS_VERSUS_PHYSICAL_FRUSTRATION_GATE.md) explains why an observed lag alone is not a preferred interaction offset.
4. **Distinguish explanatory and predictive aims.** The intensity baseline is appropriate for testing **incremental prediction conditional on an intensity report**, but it is not a fair replacement for a model that must work without that report or before either rating. Compare both conditions separately. A useful increment over intensity would still be evidence about this operational model, not proof that the physical predictor is identical with, or explains why there is, felt unpleasantness.
5. **Demand independently justified contrasts.** Reconstruct time-aligned trial provenance and rating order, assess missing trials and scale reliability, and use held-out families, sessions and a new protocol where available. Seek interventions capable of changing an independently measured candidate apart from plausible competing pathways. Do not call a nonsignificant difference equivalence or require a constructed subject-object to preserve genuine experience.

The original authors' study and the present calculations do not discriminate Benovsky's, Le Bihan's, experiential-ground, physical-identity and experience-respecting dualist accounts as such. Without distinct fixed predictions or independently supported constitutive commitments, these remain philosophical rivals rather than empirically distinguished models.

The default fetch is limited to one 3,728,915-byte pinned GitHub file and verifies its SHA-256. The verifier checks keys, family grouping, training rank and fold separation; the tests exercise score weighting, pairing and cluster bootstrap logic. Neither validates the authors' raw measurements, verifies experiential identity or reruns the imaging pipeline.

No independently measured mismatch, subject boundary, unique ground or causal mediation is established. The specific conditional baseline is new analysis of existing data, not a new pain experiment or placebo discovery.
