# Pain affect, sensory intensity and the experiential-misalignment proposal

## Finding in brief

The supplied article gives relevant first-person and brain-imaging evidence for **distinguishing pain unpleasantness from reported sensory intensity**. It does not identify the physical state that constitutes unpleasantness, measure the proposed internal mismatch, or settle whether experiential ground is fundamental. Its selection and measurement design also make a stronger conclusion—perfectly preserved sensory experience while affect alone changes—unwarranted.

This matters to the candidate: the same external painful stimulus can be accompanied by different reported unpleasantness, and a sensory-intensity rating alone need not characterize the affective report. But a candidate in which internal mismatch changes with hypnosis remains possible; neither the study nor the present project has independently measured that mismatch.

## 1. What was done

- Volunteers were screened for hypnotic suggestibility and a **robust prior modulation of pain unpleasantness**. Eight participants were selected for PET; five had one experimental session and three had two, totaling **11 sessions, not 11 independent participants**. The screening supports feasibility in this selected group, but it limits generalization and means the study did not prospectively sample the full range of hypnotic responses.
- During each PET scan, participants immersed a hand for 75 seconds in neutral or painfully hot water. The heat temperature was individually adjusted to obtain an initial pain rating of 40–80 and ranged from 46.5 to 47.5°C. The experimental comparison uses the designed temperatures; it does not establish that every bodily and neural input was unchanged.
- Conditions included alert control, hypnosis control, and hypnotic suggestions for increased or decreased unpleasantness. To limit carryover, alert control always came first, then hypnosis control, then suggestion conditions. Neutral/hot presentation in control states and blocks of high/low suggestion scans were counterbalanced across individuals. This is not a fully randomized order of all conditions; attention, suggestion, expectations, habituation, carryover and report processes remain possible influences.
- After scanning, participants rated intensity and unpleasantness on separately anchored numerical scales. The investigators allowed scores beyond 100 when appropriate to avoid a ceiling. These are reports of experience, valuable evidence without being a mechanically infallible access channel or a measure of all aspects of experience.
- The investigators used H₂¹⁵O PET to measure regional cerebral blood-flow changes and individual MRI for anatomical alignment. Pain-related images involved a painful-minus-neutral comparison. They searched selected SI, SII, anterior cingulate cortex (ACC) and insula regions. The method is **indirect, spatially and temporally limited**; it is not a measurement of neural oscillatory phase relations or the candidate's `q`.

## 2. What the paper actually reports

The high- versus low-unpleasantness suggestion conditions had mean unpleasantness ratings **81.4 versus 45.0**, reported as significantly different (`P < 0.001`). Mean intensity ratings were **78.0 versus 71.2**, a difference the paper reports as not statistically significant. These are different measures and selected participants; the summary is not a report of each individual's experience or a calibrated statement of equal sensations. In particular:

> A statistically nonsignificant intensity difference is **not evidence that intensity is exactly equal**, nor a demonstrated equivalence within a prespecified tolerance. The means differ by 6.8 scale points; the paper does not establish that all sensory qualities, attention, bodily states or neural patterns remained fixed.

The investigators report that the directly compared pain-related activation was greater in ACC under increased than decreased unpleasantness suggestion (`P < 0.02`), while the SI comparison was not statistically significant. The article also reports a positive residual ACC blood-flow–unpleasantness association after accounting for interperson variability, intensity and session (`r = 0.419`, `P = 0.005`), with no statistically significant association in their tested SI and insular comparisons. These are the authors' results, not an independent reanalysis. A significant association in one region and a nonsignificant association in another is not, on its own, a statistical demonstration that those associations differ. Nor is a nonsignificant result proof of zero activity or an absence of affective involvement.

The authors report no significant pain-evoked SII peak in the two suggestion conditions and discuss possible attentional or habituation explanations. Lack of a detected peak is not proof that SII was inactive. The paper reports a multiple-comparison-corrected threshold for its specified activation searches; do not assume that this automatically proves every subsequent volume-of-interest contrast or regression independently replicated.

### Figures and table, inspected

- **Table 1** lists thresholded pain-related activation peaks and stereotaxic coordinates for the stated conditions. It is not a table of individual outcomes or directly a test of whether two conditions differ. Reported peaks and their `t` values must not be compared as if a difference in statistical significance were itself a significant between-condition difference.
- **Figure 1** overlays group PET maps, averaged across 11 sessions, on one person's MRI; red circles show volumes of interest. These are pain-related contrast images and slices centered on relevant activation peaks, not images of pain itself. Color differences by eye are not a substitute for the reported comparison or an independent replication.
- **Figure 2** plots residual ACC blood-flow measures against unpleasantness ratings, with a fitted line and a peak-location inset. There is substantial scatter and the figure uses scans from a small, selected set of people; points must not be treated as independent persons. The plot does not show that ACC activity alone uniquely determines unpleasantness or that the relationship holds in other conditions.

## 3. What this means for the proposed bridge

### Three different claims

1. **Evidence:** after a suggestion intervention in selected volunteers, reported unpleasantness changed substantially and the study detected a relationship between this contrast and ACC PET activity, whereas the tested intensity contrast was not statistically significant.
2. **A plausible interpretation:** affective and sensory dimensions can be modulated differently and involve partially distinct, interacting processes. The paper itself emphasizes interactions between these regions; it does not establish a completely independent affect circuit.
3. **Not established:** ACC blood flow *is* unpleasantness; activity in ACC is necessary or sufficient for all unpleasantness; a particular neural phase mismatch exists or is intrinsically unpleasant; or there is a single universal subject. PET association after a suggestion does not identify the causal contribution or constitutive status of any single area.

The distinction supports taking the subjective quality seriously rather than collapsing it into a stimulus description or a general notion of information. It does not show that a general first-person intuition uniquely selects any one physical realization. The matching problem is still two-sided: physical relations need an independently justified subject boundary and experiential character, while reported feelings must not be dismissed merely because their physical readout is imperfect.

### Same input, three experience-respecting hypotheses

The following are **competing interpretations**, not models fitted to the PET data:

- **Sensory-only:** within a defined scope, unpleasantness is fixed by a narrowly specified sensory-intensity state. Different mean unpleasantness with a nonsignificant mean intensity contrast puts pressure on an overly simple version, but does not falsify every such rule: the intensity distributions, unmeasured sensory dimensions and measurement error are not shown to be identical.
- **Internal-mismatch:** hypnosis changes an independently defined subject-internal `q`, and `q` is related to unpleasantness. This is compatible with the observed pattern, but the article does not measure `q` or show that ACC activity is `q`.
- **Alternative experiential constitution:** a different contextual neural organization, rather than the proposed mismatch, accompanies and constitutes the affective contrast. This also respects experience; the same PET/report observations do not discriminate it from internal mismatch.

No interpretation can gain a free bridge by calling a physical proxy “unpleasantness.” The previously studied [matched-stimulus placebo data](MATCHED_STIMULUS_UNPLEASANTNESS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_BRIDGE.md) reach a related but separate operational constraint, using another experiment and condition means. These studies are complementary—not a joint identification of `q`, a calibrated shared assay or proof of substrate independence.

## 4. What would discriminate the mismatch claim?

A positive test needs (i) a subject-local physical mismatch and preferred relation specified independently of the target unpleasantness ratings, (ii) measurements adequate to estimate it under each condition, (iii) competing models for stimulus, sensory intensity and context, (iv) held-out subjects/conditions and uncertainty that reflects repeated observations, and (v) a separately justified experiential report interface. Varying suggestion alone is not a selective intervention on mismatch. Treating intensity as a post-intervention covariate also needs care: controlling for it is not automatically a causal mediation analysis.

A specifically measured mismatch could make predictions that differ from context or sensory baselines. A reliable failure of a prespecified prediction would challenge that **specified** identity under the assay assumptions; a successful prediction would support the empirical model without by itself deriving why it feels unpleasant. This is a practical target, not an instruction to induce pain or a claim that this small PET study supplies the necessary data. Any new human study requires ethical oversight, safe limits, informed consent and immediate withdrawal.

## Battery status

The article is genuine evidence relevant to B5, but it does not close the qualitative bridge or B4/F2 subject constitution and persistence. It supplies no derivation of an experiential self-sufficient whole, genuine possibility as actuality, quantum physics, local time, gravity or subject measure. C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7; neither the external battery nor the internal substantive battery is passed on this basis. No universal consciousness or external creator is assumed.
