# Privation and the conjecture that Good actualizes reality

## Assessment

The privation idea can strengthen the **formulation** of an axiarchic account. It suggests an asymmetry: Good could be positive constitutive fulfillment, while evil is a defect, deprivation, or disordered exercise of actual powers, rather than an independent opposing substance.

That is a serious conjectural development, not a proof of axiarchism. It does not yet establish that normative Good is identical with coherence, that Good explains concrete existence, or that actual suffering follows intelligibly from the proposed foundation.

## 1. What the physical analogies support—and what needs correcting

Darkness ordinarily means an absence or low level of relevant illumination, not a substance of dark particles opposing photons. But darkness is relative to an environment, detector, spectral range, and threshold. The experience of black or darkness can involve actual perceptual processing; phenomenal darkness is not simply the absence of every phenomenal occurrence.

The heat/cold analogy is useful but less exact. In thermodynamics, heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference, not a material substance in a body. Cold conditions have objective physical descriptions, and low-temperature physics and cryogenics study them. Temperature, internal energy, and heat transfer must not be conflated. A cold system has a physical state; it is not simply unreal.

The general lesson is therefore not “only the positively named side is real.” It is:

> Opposed descriptions need not denote opposed fundamental substances. Some contrasts are differences, limits, or absences within one positive physical organization.

That is compatible with absences being causally and explanatorily relevant. Oxygen deprivation, for example, is not a separate destructive substance, but its consequences are real. Avoiding a substance ontology for a deficit does not make it insignificant.

Whether moral evil has a corresponding privative analysis is a philosophical hypothesis, not a result of thermodynamics or photon physics. The analogy motivates a candidate ontology; it does not establish the moral premise.

## 2. The privation hypothesis made substantive

A promising version is:

> Good is the fulfilled exercise and integration of constitutive powers appropriate to actual beings. Evil is a privation of due fulfillment or integration in the exercise of those powers, rather than an additional fundamental kind of substance. Destructive direction describes what occurs; a privative account must identify what relevant fulfillment or integration is missing.

On this view, a harmful action can be entirely real while its wrongness is privative. It uses actual capacities—agency, intelligence, physical power—against the fulfillment of beings capable of being harmed. The theory need not say that nothing happened or that harm is imaginary.

But “appropriate” and “fulfillment” carry real explanatory burdens. A cancer can proliferate while damaging an organism; an oppressor can realize a plan while destroying another person's agency. Mere persistence, local success, coordination, or complexity cannot automatically count as Good.

The theory must identify whose capacities matter, what constitutes their fulfillment, and how conflicting claims are resolved without arbitrary preference. Otherwise the normative content has been hidden in those words rather than explained.

## 3. Why this is a better axiarchic candidate than bare “Good = consistency”

Logical consistency rules out contradiction. It does not distinguish a flourishing system from a coherently organized system of exploitation. Both can be described without formal contradiction.

A privative account can aim at a richer notion of **constitutive coherence**:

- capacities are genuinely realized rather than merely represented;
- their exercise sustains rather than arbitrarily destroys relevant beings;
- differentiated perspectives can flourish without being erased for a falsely uniform whole;
- coordination is not purchased simply by suppressing the claims of those affected;
- qualitative well-being, suffering, agency, and their relations are included in the target rather than left outside a purely structural optimization.

These are candidate normative commitments, not definitions already derived from physics. Their integration would have to be justified. The point is to identify the sort of account needed, not to announce that every listed commitment is already entailed by one abstract coherence principle.

This gives the axiarchic conjecture a more concrete direction: positive actuality and its fulfilled organization may have an intrinsic evaluative aspect. It avoids needing a second metaphysical substance of evil. But it must still justify the link from constitutive fulfillment to normative value.

## 4. Three claims that must not be collapsed

### Claim A: evil is not an independent fundamental substance

This is the ontological economy suggested by the analogy. A privation theory is one way to explain moral asymmetry without two ultimate opposing powers.

A deprivation presupposes an actual bearer and a standard of fulfillment or a capacity whose fulfillment is missing. This gives the privation account an important asymmetry: failure is intelligible relative to positive constitution, rather than requiring an independent negative substance. But it does not establish that a perfectly fulfilled Good already exists as a separate actual entity. A standard or possible fulfillment must not silently be converted into an actual perfect foundation.

The traditional idea of “goodness of being” must also be distinguished from the moral goodness of every actual action. If “good” means only “exists positively,” then identifying being and good has not yet explained normative value. If it means fulfilled being with genuine value, the constitutive and normative identity is substantive and needs justification.

### Claim B: Good is intrinsic to fulfilled actuality

This is a stronger evaluative-constitutive identity: value is not an external observer's arbitrary approval, but belongs to the fulfillment of actual beings and their relations.

It is not entailed by A. An account can reject evil substance while treating value as relational, response-dependent, or otherwise not an intrinsic aspect of actuality. The identity needs its own argument.

### Claim C: Good explains why reality exists and has its constitution

This is axiarchic actualization. It claims that the fulfilled, intelligible, valuable constitution is not merely something that exists, but the reason for its own actuality.

It is not entailed by either A or B. A world could contain real capacities, their fulfillment, and privative harms while those facts are produced by a nonaxiarchic physical constitution. That conceptual alternative is enough to show that the proposed inference needs another premise; it is not proof that axiarchism is false.

Likewise, the absence of a separate substance of nonbeing does not establish that being is self-explanatory. “No opposing entity prevents it” is not an actualization principle.

## 5. The candidate bridge to the whole-account

The most promising strengthened conjecture is:

> Reality is not created by an external evaluator choosing the best available object. Its positive constitution, intelligibility, and intrinsic value may be inseparable features of one self-explanatory actuality. Good is fulfilled qualitative-relational organization; evil is an internal deprivation or disorder of finite modes, not a second ultimate principle.

The intended dependence is constitutive, not necessarily temporal. “Good actualizes reality” need not mean that Good existed earlier and caused a universe to appear. It could mean that the ultimate reason for actuality is inseparable from its fulfilled nature.

This fits the non-additive account: causal structure, qualitative character, and perhaps value would be presentations of one concrete constitution rather than three substances linked by unexplained laws.

But the word **perhaps** marks a substantive additional identity. A completed theory must establish why these aspects are one nature, why that nature is actual, and why its finite articulation has the observed features. Adding value to a dual-aspect account cannot itself count as proving the new identity.

The conjecture also does not license “all possible things must exist,” “more quantity of experience is always better,” or “every locally organized process is morally good.” Those require separate premises and may conflict with the differentiated claims of finite perspectives.

## 6. Suffering and wrongdoing remain serious constraints

Calling suffering a privation does not make its felt character an absence of phenomenality. Pain is an actual experience. Some pain can have a protective function; that does not make every instance good or eliminate its aversive character. Pleasure can accompany cruelty; positive felt valence is not identical with moral goodness.

The candidate must therefore distinguish:

- the actuality of a process;
- its felt valence;
- functional success relative to a system;
- moral or axiological fulfillment across affected beings.

The fact that an event is actual does not automatically make it morally justified. “Reality is Good at its foundation” cannot be used as a shortcut to “whatever happens is good.”

A strongly axiarchic account owes a particularly demanding explanation of finite deprivation, suffering, and destructive agency. It must show why they occur within the proposed foundation without postulating a brute failure, appealing to an unexplained permission, or declaring them necessary after the fact. Apparently gratuitous suffering is genuine pressure on a claim of comprehensive goodness, not merely another item to relabel.

This debt may be unfinished rather than fatal. But the theory must confront it, and the privation vocabulary alone does not close it.

## 7. How far this should promote the conjecture

The proposal deserves promotion from an unspecified reference to “the Good” to an **articulated privation-based axiarchic conjecture**. It has a clearer intended ontology and a plausible route to avoiding a dualism of ultimate Good and evil.

What has improved is explanatory structure and motivation. We have not acquired empirical evidence that value actualizes existence, a derivation of normative fulfillment, or a demonstrated answer to suffering. An increased conceptual promise should not be reported as a calibrated increase in probability.

The strongest core of the existing account remains non-additive qualitative process monism. The new extension asks whether normative Good is an intrinsic aspect of constitutive fulfillment and ultimately part of reality's reason for being. It remains a serious candidate extension, not a result already contained in the darkness or heat examples.

## 8. The questions that would make the extension explanatory

A developed theory must answer:

1. What positive constitutive features make an actual being fulfilled, and why do they have normative significance?
2. How do the claims of different perspectives fit together without defining one perspective's destruction as a benefit to the whole?
3. What is the actualization principle connecting this fulfillment to existence rather than merely to evaluation of an existing world?
4. Why does this constitution follow rather than a different coherent and apparently valuable one?
5. How does finite privation arise and why is its actual extent compatible with the proposed foundation?
6. Why are that principle and its normative aspect themselves intelligible without residue?

These are positive research tasks for the conjecture. An account that answers them would be substantially more than a privation analogy. An account that merely repeats “Good is actuality” would not yet have paid them.

## Verdict

**Yes: privation helps develop the axiarchic hypothesis.** It supplies a disciplined asymmetry between positive constitution and its failures and suggests a non-dualistic place for value within the proposed whole.

**No: it does not by itself establish that Good actualizes reality.** The promising conjecture is that fulfillment, intelligibility, intrinsic value, and actuality belong to one constitution. Their claimed identity and actualizing role are precisely what must still be explained.

No new empirical finding or executable theorem is claimed by this assessment.

### Goal-relativity and the authority of the normative standard

[The goal-relative value argument](GOAL_RELATIVE_VALUE_ARGUMENT.md) establishes a conditional obstruction: if the admissible goals include every evaluator's reversal, no outcome is strictly worse—or strictly better—than another for every goal. This does not make relational badness incoherent or equate normative objectivity with service to every possible goal. It does require an axiarchic account to justify its normative standard rather than silently privilege one end. Goal-relativity alone neither establishes privation theory nor yields an asymmetry favoring ultimate Good; unrestricted reversal applies equally to Good.

## The more exact parallel: missing integration, not merely destructive direction

The user's correction improves the analogy. An eye has actual structure and powers while lacking an appropriate visual function. Similarly, an act of cruelty exercises actual capacities—agency, intelligence, pursuit of an end—while, on the proposed privative account, lacking due regard or integration of the affected being's needs and standing. Calling its direction “destructive” describes the action or result; it does not yet identify the privation that constitutes its proposed wrongness.

Seeking money or power need not itself be wrong. In the proposed analysis, the defect can lie in pursuing those ends without the regard owed to others' needs, legitimate claims, and fair shares. Not every frustrated preference or unmet demand is thereby a wrong; the relevant standards need justification.

“Lack of empathy” is an example, not a universal psychological diagnosis. An agent may understand another's suffering, or possess empathic capacities, while overriding appropriate concern. Conversely, someone can act fairly without strong empathic feeling. The relevant proposed privation is of due regard in practical integration, not necessarily of an emotional faculty. Empathy can also be selective and needs integration with fair treatment.

The harmful act and suffering remain actual. The claim concerns the constitution of wrongness, not the nonexistence of the deed or a complete causal explanation of its occurrence. The remaining normative question is why the missing regard is due; describing its absence does not by itself derive that standard.

### Privation requires a justified standard of what is due

[The due-absence analysis](CONSTITUTIVE_VALUE_PRINCIPLE.md#13-due-absence-and-the-test-of-fulfilled-agency) distinguishes mere absence from missing fulfillment due to a particular bearer in particular circumstances. Privation depends on a positive standard; it does not independently generate that standard's authority. Thin agency, accurate understanding, and effective goal pursuit are compatible with deliberate exploitation, so morality cannot be derived merely by calling agency “full.” Instrumental success, factual functional roles, and normative authority must remain distinct. The constitutive-value identity remains a serious hypothesis requiring an explanation, not an established deduction or an automatically disconfirmed proposal.
