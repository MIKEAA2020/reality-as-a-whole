## 2. Step 1: "Nothing exists" is self-refuting

Suppose for contradiction: *nothing exists.* Then:
- There are no laws, no logic, no mathematics, no truths.
- But the proposition "nothing exists" is itself a (putative) truth; it uses the structure of predication, identity, negation, and quantification; it presupposes the semantic framework that allows it to be meaningful.
- If nothing existed, the proposition could not exist or be true. The assertion undermines its own preconditions.

This is a transcendental argument (not an ontological argument from definition): *any coherent thought whatsoever* — even the thought "nothing exists" — requires a framework (logic, structure) to be the thought it is. That framework cannot be "nothing"; something exists necessarily.

The necessary existent cannot be "our universe" (contingent, could have been different — physicists routinely consider other matter contents/dimensions). It must be the underlying structure that makes any possibility possible.
