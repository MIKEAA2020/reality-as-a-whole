## 2. The strongest precise version of the experiential cancellation argument

Suppose, for this argument, that a finite whole has real local experiences `e_i`, each with a **well-defined signed contribution** `q_i` to one objective, additive experiential quantity. Assume the whole's total is `Q=sum_i q_i=0` and at least one `q_i>0`. Then at least one `q_j<0`: if all other terms were nonnegative, the sum would be strictly positive.

This establishes a real conditional parity with a signed energy balance. It does not require a distinct anti-experience substance or a universal subject. But the crucial assumptions remain: why this Q measures experience itself rather than one feature of it; why the signs, magnitudes, cross-subject comparison and sum are objectively defined; why Q is zero; and whether some compensating term is non-experiential. If a relevant experiential contribution can be positive but the total includes an independently constituted negative term, the accounting may balance without that term being a negative experience. Whether such a separate term is self-subsistent is another question, not something settled by calling it separate.

For an infinite whole, the total also needs a well-defined composition or measure. Unrestricted cancellation of divergent positive and negative contributions is not an established value. A countably additive measure, if used, must be justified and need not be a probability measure over subjects.

### Valence is a lead, not the full identity

Pleasure and suffering are real, familiar contrasts; an ordered signed scale can represent their direction. But a zero **net score** does not mean no experience: the illustrative scores `(+1,-1)` sum to zero while both occurrences remain real. Nor does it imply that one person's pain has disappeared or been morally compensated by another's pleasure. A neutral experience can also be actual despite receiving zero valence.

Even signs and ordering alone do not fix a sum. The strictly increasing, sign-preserving transformation `f(x)=2x` for `x>=0` and `f(x)=x` for `x<0` maps `(+1,-1)` to `(+2,-1)`, changing the sum from zero to one. A physical or metaphysical quantity could have a privileged cardinal scale, a common zero and justified weights, but that must be established; ordinal valence labels alone cannot provide it. An empirical study of separate positive and negative affect scales reports consequences for mixed-valence measurement [3](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613475456); I checked its abstract, not the full data. It illustrates a measurement issue, not a proof that experiential quantity cannot exist.

## 3. Was your gravity point correct?

**Yes, as the motivating physical picture.** In Newtonian mechanics a bound pair has potential energy `U=-GMm/r` when the zero is chosen at infinite separation; forming a bound system can lower its energy relative to separated constituents. Positive matter energy and a negative gravitational contribution are precisely the intuitive ingredients invoked by zero-energy-universe hypotheses. They are not merely metaphors. Brown and York's gravitational energy construction, in its stated setting, includes a negative binding contribution in the Newtonian limit [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9209012); I checked the abstract.

The important distinction is between this real binding effect and a demonstrated unique whole-universe total. The reference level and treatment of the gravitational contribution matter, especially in a dynamical cosmology. Local gravitational binding occurs, but that alone does not measure the energy of the whole or establish that its total is zero. Our [FLRW analysis](ZERO_TOTALITY_FLRW_CONSTRAINT_AND_ENERGY.md) exhibits a zero Hamiltonian constraint with positive scalar energy density, but the constraint is not automatically a uniquely defined observable total energy.

### The chat's “30% plus 70%” assertion

DeepSeek says that a Newtonian sphere accounts for 30% of the required negative energy and that the remaining 70% **must** come from relativistic spacetime curvature. The export provides only unresolved `[reference:n]` markers for this; it does not provide inspectable source URLs. I could not establish that 70% assertion from the chat.

There is a conditional Newtonian calculation yielding 30%. For a uniform sphere of mass M and radius R, `U=-3GM²/(5R)`. With the special choices `R=c/H` and `rho=3H²/(8 pi G)`, its mass is `M=c²R/(2G)`, so `U=-3Mc²/10`. A corresponding nonrelativistic homologous expansion gives `T=3Mc²/10`, hence `T+U=0` **without including rest energy**. Valev reports those two 30% figures in a Newtonian cosmological calculation [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.2726); I checked its abstract and opening derivation, not all observational assumptions.

This is not a derivation of an additional negative `0.7Mc²` from general relativity. `R=c/H` is a stipulated Hubble-radius scale, not generally identical to the particle horizon; at that radius the assumed Hubble speed reaches c, limiting a nonrelativistic kinetic-energy estimate. The calculation does not define or measure the entire universe's relativistic energy. If rest energy is added to the stated Newtonian bookkeeping, `Mc²+T+U=Mc²`; one cannot infer the missing compensation from that arithmetic alone.

## 4. Does making energy meaningful require changing GR?

**Not in general.** GR already supplies useful energy notions under specified circumstances; changing the theory is not a prerequisite for every well-defined energy. Brown–York quasi-local energy is defined for appropriate bounded regions, and the cited construction approaches ADM energy at spatial infinity under its asymptotic assumptions [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9209012). These definitions involve choices and conditions; not every quasi-local proposal applies to every surface or has the same limit. A particular stationary spacetime may also possess a useful conserved energy associated with a time-translation symmetry.

The difficult, narrower question is whether the **actual cosmological whole** has a unique, physically meaningful, globally conserved energy of the kind your argument requires. There is no general answer just from the words “diffeomorphism invariant.” One must specify the spacetime, boundaries or their absence, observers, observable and normalization. The absence of a generic global time-translation symmetry matters for ordinary global conservation arguments, but does not mean that no global or quasi-local energy can ever be defined in GR.

An emergent-spacetime theory could instead provide a more fundamental quantity and derive suitable GR limits. It does not automatically do so: the quantity, its zero, objective interpretation and relationship to measurable local energy still need to be specified. Your “everything is energy” idea is a substantive unification proposal to test on those terms, not a slogan or an assumption that GR is final.

## 5. Preferred foliation versus emergent time

The chat treats these as two exclusive ways to solve one problem. The more useful distinction is among **three** different moves:

| Approach | What it does | What it does not automatically do |
|---|---|---|
| A physically preferred foliation | Adds or gives physical status to a particular slicing or clock structure. Some theories can express this with covariant fields; a physical preferred frame can nevertheless have observable symmetry consequences. | Merely writing equations covariantly or obtaining a Schrödinger parameter does not explain why that foliation exists, recover all GR tests, or solve experiential time. |
| Relational clock or gauge choice within a covariant theory | Describes one variable when another internal variable has a specified value. A practical coordinate/gauge choice is not automatically a new physical preferred foliation or a breaking of the theory's diffeomorphism symmetry. | Does not by itself provide a clock valid globally, resolve quantum clock ambiguities, or explain lived succession. |
| More fundamental/emergent spacetime | Seeks to recover spacetime, physical clocks and their dynamics from a deeper account, potentially without fundamental external time. | Does not solve the problem simply by declaring time emergent; the underlying theory, recovery and empirical tests are required. |

These are not mutually exclusive: an emergent theory may yield an effective preferred foliation, or a relational clock may be useful both in GR and in an emergent description. Using York time as a gauge or relational variable is not identical to asserting that York slices are a physically fundamental preferred foliation; global validity of any particular clock or slicing is an additional question.

The problem of time includes more than the frozen equation: observables, clock choice and consistency, quantum clocks and backreaction, and recovery of ordinary physics and experienced succession. Höhn, Smith and Lock show equivalences among relational approaches **within specified relativistic settings and frequency sectors**, not a complete quantum-gravity solution [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00580). Carlip and Hu propose a covariant canonical approach and explicitly do not claim to have quantized full gravity [1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.10272). I checked their abstracts and the opening discussion; neither establishes the experiential bridge.

**My assessment for your aim:** prefer a self-subsistent whole with internally defined relational time as the research direction, and investigate emergent spacetime where it provides a concrete recovery of physics. Use a foliation or clock as a calculational tool when justified, without assuming it is fundamental. A physical preferred foliation remains a candidate only if its origin and observational consequences are explained; it is not ruled out merely by its name. The chat's claims that emergence already offers a complete solution, or is the only possible foundational route, go beyond what has been shown.
