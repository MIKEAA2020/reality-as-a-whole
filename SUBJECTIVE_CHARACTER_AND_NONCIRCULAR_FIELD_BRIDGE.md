# Subjective character, unity, and a field-first bridge

## Result

**Related no-bearer rival:** The [Benovsky full-book audit](BENOVSKY_NO_SELF_DUAL_ASPECT_FULL_BOOK_AUDIT.md) examines primitive generic mineness without a separately reified subject. It preserves experiential occurrence while leaving phenomenal constitution and individuation to be explained.

**Important qualification:** [The first-personal givenness and field-individuation audit](FIRST_PERSONAL_GIVENNESS_AND_FIELD_INDIVIDUATION_AUDIT.md) compares Guillot, Zahavi's reply, and Barbieri's distinct state-priority critique. The field identity below avoids a particular two-item explanatory cycle only by positing first-personal fields; it does not solve their individuation or temporal scope. Distinct nested-window field tokens cannot also be numerically the same thin field-subject under that identity.

The [subsequent first-person constitution comparison](SPLIT_BRAIN_FIRST_PERSON_CONSTITUTION_COMPARISON.md) tests this field-first proposal's boundary interface against Pinto et al.'s 2017 clinical report, distinguishing genuine general first-person evidence from the unestablished trial-specific and physical-to-spin mappings.

A positive starting point is available: a conscious episode is not just a list of qualities; it is qualitatively present **as an experience**. The experienced togetherness of distinguishable contents is real evidence to be accommodated. It need not be explained by positing a second inner observer. But the step from this local datum to a law selecting *which* subjects exist, and which physical states have which felt character, remains unproved.

Alberto Barbieri's 2025 account helps pinpoint the remaining step. It proposes that the *subjective character* of concurrent experiences grounds their phenomenal unity. Crucially, its explanans already refers to **one and the same subject** being aware of those experiences. That is a serious candidate account of unity *for an already identified subject*, but not by itself a subject-constitution rule for the current project. It is not thereby refuted: these are different explanatory jobs.

A field-first alternative can avoid an extra observer and avoid taking subjectless experiences as its starting ontology, by treating concrete, first-personal fields as basic and identifying a minimal momentary subject with each such field. This is a coherent **research proposal under its stated assumptions**, not a derivation of fields or a determination of the true ontology. Its progress is an explicit dependency structure and a non-circular stopping point for one question; its cost is to assume actual subject-involving fields and their individuation. It does not give the hoped-for subject-free derivation, physical correspondence, or full experiential intelligibility.

## 2. What Barbieri contributes

Barbieri distinguishes *qualitative character*—the specific way an experience feels—from *subjective character*, roughly its being experientially given to its subject. He assumes, rather than establishes, a contested view on which both belong to phenomenal character. His preferred `SA1` employs a modest inner-awareness formulation: awareness of an experience as the particular experience it is, rather than necessarily an explicit or reflective judgment that it is mine. It does **not** require an infinite sequence of higher-order observers as the definition of the proposal.

The manuscript's claim is that, when multiple experiences are concurrent and belong to the same subject, their subjective character supplies an intelligible ground of their being experienced together. It argues that mere simultaneous instantiation of qualitative properties would not do so. The author explicitly adds **concurrency and same-subject unity** as further necessary elements (footnote 24), and in footnote 25 defends the proposed grounding direction by appealing to a bottom-up priority of individual experiences over the overall experience. These are substantive commitments. He also assumes the phenomenal unity thesis for his inquiry, while noting that it has not been conclusively proved.

There is a real philosophical gain in this proposal: it tries to explain unity in a feature of experience itself rather than replacing it with access, physical correlation, or an unexplained physical graph. The argument's success is contested. In particular, the jointness claim cannot simply be moved into a premise and then counted as its independent explanation; nor must a competing account deny that experienced unity is real. A subjectivist may reasonably hold that first-personal presence is intrinsically unified, but then owes a defense of that strength of presence.

## 3. The exact bridge in the argument

For a declared local window, let `A(s,e)` mean subject `s` is experientially aware of experience/aspect `e` as that particular experience; let `J(s,e,f)` mean `s` experiences `e` and `f` **together**, with conjoint phenomenology. Let concurrence and being experiences of `s` be separate premises. In skeletal form the proposed move is:

```text
A(s,e) and A(s,f) and same-subject and concurrence
                    + a subjective-character-to-jointness principle
                 => J(s,e,f).
```

The principle after the plus sign is where the explanatory work occurs. Merely using the same symbol `s` in two statements does not make the conclusion a theorem of ordinary logic. A schematic interpretation could assign both `A(s,e)` and `A(s,f)` true while `J(s,e,f)` is false; this illustrates the formal gap **only**. It does not show that such a subject or awareness is genuinely possible, or that Barbieri accepts that interpretation. If `A` is understood strongly enough to *include* one unified field and hence J, the implication follows by that richer meaning, but then the proponent needs to defend why this is what subjective character amounts to and why it grounds rather than redescribes jointness.

A weaker `A` gives more distance between explanans and explanandum, but the additional jointness principle must be argued for. A stronger `A` may capture the lived phenomenon more faithfully, but its explanatory independence from J becomes the question. This is an explanatory dilemma, not a proof that all accounts of awareness are circular. Barbieri explicitly argues for the stronger connection and for its priority; simply labeling it circular would overlook that argument. His use of already identified subjects also avoids claiming to derive them in the first place.

Most importantly, first-person evidence of a conjoint field bears directly on `J` in a particular case. We must not discard that datum because it doesn't by itself select a *general* grounding direction. Equally, a single case doesn't establish the necessity of `J` for all concurrent experiences, numerical sharing between subjects, or a universal field.

## 4. A field-first construction with costs exposed

Here is the minimal alternative **we propose for evaluation**, inspired by the broader top-down and bundle literature, not attributed as Barbieri's view:

1. **Concrete phenomenal base.** At an appropriately specified local scope, posit a collection `F` of actual, concrete, intrinsically first-personal field-occurrences. An occurrence has its qualitative and conjoint character intrinsically; differences within it may be real aspects without assuming independently existing atom-experiences. A field is not a representation on paper, a physical graph, or an unexperienced quality. No global clock is presupposed by a local scope.
2. **No separate reader.** Do not add a distinct observer to make the occurrence present. Stipulate the *thin momentary subject* of a field to be that very concrete field, considered under its first-personal aspect—not the abstract set of its contents. Thus `thin_subject(f)=f` is an explicit identity proposal, not a process by which a field creates a subject. It is not a definition of a persisting person, psychological self, or body.
3. **Individuation.** Distinct field tokens are not assumed identical merely because their contents look alike. Nor does the existence of a perceived aspect imply an independent token-field subject for that aspect. A complete theory must specify which occurrences count as distinct and how such an identity is warranted. In a restricted variant one may disallow shared aspect tokens between distinct fields, but this is an extra anti-sharing premise, not a consequence of first-person presence.
4. **Whole and relations.** The actual whole includes these field occurrences and whatever other intrinsic or relational facts the theory needs; their collective presence does not itself make the whole one further conscious field. This is compatible with investigating a timeless whole and plural local experiences, but no self-sufficiency or global modality result follows from the local construction alone.

### What it buys and what it costs

This alternative avoids the particular circle “derive a subject from co-consciousness, defined as co-experience by that same subject.” There is no extra subject variable used to assemble the input: a concrete first-personal field is the primitive. It can also take an actual lived conjunction seriously without first imagining fully independent micro-subjects.

But it **does not derive subjecthood from subject-free ingredients**. The phenomenal base already has the first-personal character that makes it a plausible subject-candidate; the identity is a philosophical commitment, not an independent test of whether something is an experiencer. If a critic holds that an experience must be *had* by a distinct bearer, the proposal must explain why an occurrence itself can be the bearer, rather than merely repeat the identification. A thin-field proponent may reject that demand, but that is an ontological disagreement, not a solved empirical question. O’Conaill's abstract records a related concern about a different, capacity-based theory; it is not a refutation of every field-first ontology.

The primitive must be **contentful**, not just a symbol `f` declared to be an experience. For a real candidate, the intrinsic character and its differentiated-yet-conjoint organization need to be specified in a way that respects experience as data. And if grounding explanatory self-sufficiency requires every primitive to be intelligible from the whole, treating fields as basic is a remaining debt, even if the local identity is non-circular. The number and identity of fields are still inputs; a suitable rule must decide when one field rather than two is actual. No claim of actual subject count follows.

## 5. The four-spin comparison: where the construction stops

At the same `t=1/4` sector and original one-update query, L selects the two outer physical pairs. Compare these possible **research descriptions**, not established genuinely possible global worlds:

| Question | Field-first + L proposal | What is actually supplied |
|---|---|---|
| How many fields? | Two actual thin fields, one associated with each selected pair. | **Assumed** mapping from a physical criterion to first-personal field occurrences. L alone selects physical sets, not fields. |
| What is each field like? | One designated felt contrast tracks the corresponding `q=0/1`. | **Assumed** q-to-quality identification. A field is not made unpleasant by writing “unpleasant” beside q. |
| Is there an encompassing field? | None asserted for this local comparison. | A restriction on the description, not evidence that no other subject exists. |
| Is subject identity across time fixed? | No. A thin field only describes a local occurrence. | A separately defended persistence and temporal-extent law is needed. |

The same field-first proposal can be paired with the E-C connected-component selection rule, which proposes one field at `t=1/4`; that alternative still owes its own quality law. Calling both candidates experiential does not choose between their boundary rules. Likewise, actual lived unpleasantness does not fix whether `q=1`, `q=0`, a context-dependent quantity, or some other physical feature corresponds to that felt contrast. A reversal is an alternative formal assignment, not evidence it is metaphysically possible or a valid description of the same experience.

There is a genuine positive hypothesis here—**intrinsically first-personal fields rather than subjectless felt tokens or a universal subject**—but it moves the foundational starting point: plural subject-like fields are primitive inputs, whereas E-L aspires to explain plural local subjects through the intrinsic whole. L and q have not been derived from that whole, and introducing a primitive field for each L output would merely repackage those postulates. Nothing in the construction shows that the whole has *only* field-occurrences, that every physical realization is experienced, or that a whole composed of fields is self-sufficient.

## 6. What could make the proposal more than a stopping point?

A useful next argument must bridge **one specified link** without building the conclusion into the premise:

- **Local unity:** defend a phenomenological principle that subjective character makes particular contents conjoint, distinguishing individual from genuinely joint awareness and justifying the explanatory direction. State its domain, counterexamples and whether same-subject identity is an input.
- **Individuation:** give an independently motivated criterion for distinct concrete fields, accounting for qualitatively identical cases, holistic contexts, possible overlapping tokens and alternatives to thin subjects. Distinguish a conceptual criterion from evidence for a specific count.
- **Constitution:** explain, on an explicitly defined physical domain, why a given relational configuration is *this* first-person field rather than merely sharing its functional structure. A principle phrased only as “the structures that make a field are fields” is not enough.
- **Quality and evidence:** anchor a specified qualitative contrast in warranted first-person evidence, define a physical predictor independently and test rivals under interventions with validated report/access models. Even a successful prediction need not settle metaphysical grounding direction. Preserve failures and calibration uncertainty rather than treating silence as absence of experience.
- **Persistence and foundations:** specify temporal extent, fission and lineage separately, then defend how the resulting fields and physical relations belong to a self-sufficient whole satisfying the modal and other requirements. Neither local self-presence nor a constructed collective implies those results.

C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7. The present analysis contributes a precise candidate stopping point and identifies its cost, not a new proof of a subject or qualitative bridge. B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open; no finite formal check or literature citation turns them into a full battery pass.
