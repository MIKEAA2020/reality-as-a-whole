# From goodness of being to axiarchic constitution

## Working conjecture

Reality's actuality, intelligibility, qualitative nature, and intrinsic value may belong to one self-explanatory constitution. Good would not be an external chooser or a separate causal force. Badness would be a deprivation or disorder within finite articulations of positive actuality, not a second ultimate substance.

The proposed identity is substantive. Defining Good as existence does not establish normative value; defining it as complete intelligibility does not explain why complete intelligibility is valuable or actual. This document develops the candidate without claiming to have derived it.

## 1. Separate the proposed dependencies

The proposal has four stages:

1. **Positive constitution:** actual occurrences and powers, rather than a second negative substance, supply the bearers of both fulfillment and deprivation.
2. **Constitutive fulfillment:** some ways of exercising those powers fulfill their bearers; others damage, frustrate, or disorganize them.
3. **Intrinsic value:** fulfillment has genuine value not exhausted by success relative to an arbitrarily adopted goal.
4. **Self-explanatory actuality:** the complete constitution, including its value, accounts for its own existence and articulation.

Privation motivates stage 1 and a way of framing stage 2. It does not establish stages 3 and 4. Universal goal reversal does not provide the missing implication; it blocks unconditional instrumental rankings in both directions.

## 2. A proposed positive standard

The candidate standard is not maximum power, complexity, survival, computation, pleasure, or uniformity considered in isolation. Each can occur at the expense of affected beings. Nor is formal consistency alone sufficient: destructive projects can be internally consistent.

A stronger candidate is **mutually intelligible fulfillment of real qualitative perspectives and their constitutive powers**. Its intended features are:

- actual experience matters, not merely its description;
- the affected perspective is included, rather than evaluated solely as an instrument for another;
- capacities for agency and understanding count without making every chosen project authoritative;
- coordination does not count as fulfillment merely because dissenting perspectives have been suppressed;
- a local gain is not automatically a global improvement when its costs are displaced onto others.

These are proposed substantive constraints. They do not yet determine a complete ordering, resolve every conflict, or establish a unique world. Their normative authority remains to be justified. A theory that merely places the word “proper” before its favored capacities has not supplied that justification.

One possible supporting route is to investigate whether the reality and significance of qualitative perspectives impose standards that any adequate account must acknowledge, regardless of a particular agent's goals. This is a research route, not a proof: an epistemic requirement to describe suffering accurately does not by itself yield a moral requirement to prevent it.

## 3. The actualizing bridge

The strongest candidate is not:

    This world would be good; therefore it exists.

Nor:

    There is no independent substance of nonbeing; therefore being must exist.

Neither inference is valid without additional premises.

Instead, the intended claim is:

> A complete constitutive nature is not an abstract description waiting for an external act of instantiation. Its explanation would show why the distinction between a merely possible description and concrete actuality does not remain an unexplained extra fact at the ultimate level.

That is the target, not its achievement. Mathematical completeness, logical consistency, or the inclusion of the word “actual” in a definition does not supply this explanation. Any successful candidate must give a reason why its constitutive principle applies to concrete reality and excludes a merely descriptive interpretation.

On the axiarchic version, intrinsic value is an inseparable explanatory aspect of this nature. On a nonaxiarchic rival, value might be a consequence of the constitution without explaining its actuality. The axiarchic version must distinguish itself from that rival by more than vocabulary.

If the same proposed constitution and existence explanation work unchanged after removing its value claim, normative Good has not yet been shown to do explanatory work as an actualizer. Conversely, indispensability within a proposed derivation would be evidence about that derivation, not automatically proof that its premises describe reality.

## 4. What the word “Good” would have to contribute

The extension earns its place only if it helps explain something not already explained by the nonaxiarchic common-constitution account—for example:

- why this form of qualitative organization is constitutively privileged;
- why the claims of affected perspectives are non-arbitrary;
- why the complete constitution is determined rather than left among unexplained alternatives;
- why its actual finite articulation has its particular limitations and possibilities.

A mere renaming of positive being contributes no additional selection or actualization principle. A substantive identity may contribute, but its necessity must be independently argued rather than assumed in the desired conclusion.

## 5. Finite privation is not yet derived

Differentiation alone does not entail suffering. Being finite does not by itself entail cruelty, extreme pain, disease, or their observed distribution. An account must not move from “finite beings cannot possess every perfection” to “these particular harms are necessary.”

Likewise, local deprivation need not mean the whole suffers a deficiency in its existence, but that observation does not justify local harm. A completed whole is not automatically a morally perfect whole. An agent's sadistic pleasure and a victim's suffering cannot be resolved by counting both as positive occurrences.

A privation theory must explain what is deprived or disordered in aversive experience, while acknowledging that the experience itself is positively actual. “Pain is merely absence of experience” would be false. Some pain's protective role does not justify all suffering.

A serious candidate must address actual harms with a stable principle, not declare each harm necessary because it happens. Apparently gratuitous suffering is counterpressure on a comprehensive-goodness hypothesis. An unfinished explanation is not automatically a refutation, but the difficulty cannot be removed by terminology.

## 6. Comparative standing

The development improves the specificity of an axiarchic completion. It does not yet show that this completion is more likely than a nonaxiarchic self-explanatory qualitative process monism.

What the privation idea positively offers is a unified ontology: real powers and perspectives, their fulfillment and failures, without a competing ultimate evil substance. What the specifically axiarchic claim adds is that intrinsic value belongs to the reason for actuality. That addition is the central conjecture to test, not a theorem already won by the privation analysis.

## 7. A disciplined next target

The next philosophical target is a **constitutive-value principle**, not another simulation that assumes value weights in advance. It should state:

1. Which features of actual qualitative life ground value.
2. Why those features supply reasons beyond arbitrary chosen goals.
3. How different perspectives and their conflicts are treated.
4. Whether the principle constrains ultimate constitution or merely evaluates outcomes within one.
5. How it explains actuality without silently assuming an actualizer.
6. What a nonaxiarchic rival leaves unexplained that this principle resolves.

Until such a principle is justified, the appropriate status is: **an articulated and serious axiarchic conjecture, with an explicit actualization debt**.

## Conclusion

The strongest present formulation is:

> One concrete nature constitutes actuality, intelligibility, and qualitative perspectives. Its fulfillment may possess intrinsic value, while badness is privative rather than a rival substance. The axiarchic conjecture is that this value is inseparable from the nature's self-explanation—not an externally imposed purpose.

This is a positive candidate, not a completed solution. Its central research problem is why constitutive fulfillment is normatively authoritative and why that value contributes to existence itself.

### Candidate constitutive-value principle

[The constitutive-value development](CONSTITUTIVE_VALUE_PRINCIPLE.md) proposes three explicit constraints: invariance under arbitrary renaming, prima facie intrinsic significance of real qualitative perspectives, and preference for improvements that worsen no affected perspective. These are proposed normative commitments, not deductions from description alone. Given fixed perspectives and fulfillment preorders, their product supplies a rigorous partial ordering and finite nonempty feasible sets have undominated members; no unique best world or actualization result follows. The central normative debt is the authority of qualitative significance and fulfillment standards. The specifically axiarchic debt is why this value contributes to concrete existence. Suffering remains a constraint on the candidate, and changing subject populations remain outside the fixed-domain ordering result.

### A discriminating constraint on fulfillment axiarchism

[The repair analysis](CONSTITUTIVE_VALUE_PRINCIPLE.md#11-the-repair-constraint-a-consequence-with-discriminatory-content) separates privation and goodness of being from the stronger claim that actuality must be undominated in justified fulfillment. Under the latter claim, an independently established admissible improvement removing deprivation with no relevant loss contradicts the actual arrangement's required status. Mere imaginability does not establish such an alternative. Defining admissibility as the actual arrangement alone makes undominated status trivial; an independently derived unique constitution would still need to establish intrinsic value and concrete actuality. Missing explanations, apparent repairs, and established counterexamples remain different epistemic cases.

### Why undominated fulfillment does not determine actuality

[The underdetermination argument](CONSTITUTIVE_VALUE_PRINCIPLE.md#12-an-underdetermination-result-fulfillment-does-not-yet-select-actuality) gives an exact counterexample: abstract arrangements (2,0), (1,1), and (0,2) are all Pareto-undominated. Symmetric lower-level-first and higher-level-first lexicographic rules both respect strict Pareto improvement but disagree about their ranking. These are stipulated ordered levels, not empirical welfare measurements. Thus non-arbitrary identity treatment and undominated fulfillment do not themselves supply a complete value ordering or select actuality. The result leaves privation intact but requires a justified priority principle or independently determining concrete constitution for stronger axiarchism.
