## Result

The previous all-sector construction implemented a genuine noncommuting sphere instrument only on the original two-dimensional code; its 254-dimensional complement used a commuting fixed-setting surrogate. We can now replace most of that surrogate **without changing the original paired lab input state** and while preserving the finite model's three directional charges and declared local lab energies.

There are two different, explicitly constructed choices:

1. **Exact qubit instrument:** use equivalent-representation pairs to put the original noncommuting qubit sphere POVM on 190 of the 254 complement dimensions. Together with the original code, that is **192/256 = 75%** of the original state's lab weight. The remaining 64 dimensions use a neutral scalar readout.
2. **Wider, unsharp sphere instrument:** use exact qubits where multiplicities are even, and higher-spin directional POVMs where they are odd. This covers **229/254** complement dimensions with noncommuting lab effects. Together with the original code, that is **231/256 ≈ 90.23%** of the original state's weight. The remaining 25-dimensional block uses a neutral scalar readout.

These are alternatives, not cumulative coverage. Higher-spin readouts are genuinely noncommuting but **not** the same sharp rank-one qubit instrument: their record correlations have reduced visibility. The weights above are Born weights in the existing maximally paired input, not counts of actual subjects.

The two constructions have the same three setting marginals as the geometry, survive the actual old relative-clock selection and the later reference/record-memory history, and retain its paired record–work result. But they do **not** produce the same full joint table as the old complement surrogate, or as each other. The physics of the instrument matters.

## 1. The representation hidden in the existing charges

The one-site lab space is four-dimensional. Its existing three Hermitian generators are \(X_i\). Define

\[
 R_1=-i[X_2,X_3],\quad R_2=-i[X_3,X_1],\quad R_3=-i[X_1,X_2],\qquad
 A_i=(R_i+X_i)/2,\quad B_i=(R_i-X_i)/2.
\]

For the actual matrices, \(A_i\) and \(B_i\) are two commuting spin-half algebras, and \(X_i=A_i-B_i\). The test constructs an explicit one-site basis that turns them into \(\sigma_i/2\otimes I\) and \(I\otimes\sigma_i/2\). Thus the existing four-site lab is equivalent, as a representation, to four A-spin-halves and four B-spin-halves. This is a mathematical factorization of the stipulated four-level representation, not a claim that its factors are new physical particles or subjects.

Four spin-halves have total-spin multiplicities \(m_0=2,m_1=3,m_2=1\). Hence each pair \((j_A,j_B)\) has irrep dimension \(d=(2j_A+1)(2j_B+1)\) and multiplicity \(m=m_{j_A}m_{j_B}\). The verifier builds the coupled-spin basis explicitly, checks all 256 columns are orthonormal, and locates the existing code inside the four-dimensional \((0,0)\) multiplicity space.

| \((j_A,j_B)\) | irrep dimension \(d\) | multiplicity after removing the original code | complement dimension \(md\) | qubit-paired dimension |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| (0,0) | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| (0,1) | 3 | 6 | 18 | 18 |
| (0,2) | 5 | 2 | 10 | 10 |
| (1,0) | 3 | 6 | 18 | 18 |
| (1,1) | 9 | 9 | 81 | 72 |
| (1,2) | 15 | 3 | 45 | 30 |
| (2,0) | 5 | 2 | 10 | 10 |
| (2,1) | 15 | 3 | 45 | 30 |
| (2,2) | 25 | 1 | 25 | 0 |
| **Total** | | | **254** | **190** |

Operations on multiplicity spaces commute with the complete algebra generated by the three \(X_i\), hence with each total lab charge. The previously declared lab energies \(H_A,H_B\) vanish on the entire complement, so these complement operations commute with those energies as well. That strong degeneracy is helpful but is a limitation: it does not demonstrate an energy-exchanging finite-reference measurement in the complement or conservation of omitted control/interaction energies.

## 2. Construction A: maximal exact qubit support under the stated symmetry

Within a given irrep, pair its equivalent multiplicity copies and use the pair index as a logical qubit. On all 190 paired complement dimensions, the verifier constructs physical operators \(\widetilde X,\widetilde Y,\widetilde Z\) with

\[
 [\widetilde X,\widetilde Y]=2i\widetilde Z,\qquad
 \widetilde X^2=\widetilde Y^2=\widetilde Z^2=Q_{190},
\]

and checks that each commutes with all three charges and both declared lab-energy operators. The original three effects are lifted as

\[
 E_i=q_i(I+n_i\cdot\sigma),\qquad
 q=(1/8,2/5,19/40).
\]

Their square-root Kraus operators sum to the active projector when squared. Bob's corresponding axis measurement is implemented in the **conjugate physical multiplicity basis**, with the logical rotation that yields the same singlet statistics. Neutral-pointer isometries can be completed blockwise to unitaries commuting with the same charges and energies; the original code keeps its existing finite-reference implementation.

This 190 is not an arbitrary sample: under a local, charge-commuting **full qubit factor** with the existing code left intact, an irrep of multiplicity \(m\) can support at most \(2\lfloor m/2\rfloor\) equivalent copies. Summing these gives 190. The statement is about this representation and class of exact qubit factors; it is not an upper bound on all noncommuting POVMs, as construction B shows, nor on schemes using additional charged resources.

## 3. Construction B: noncommuting unsharp directions on 229 dimensions

Instead of pairing and leaving odd copies unused, on each \(m=3\) and \(m=9\) multiplicity space use its spin-\(s=(m-1)/2\) matrices \(J_a\), normalized by \(L_a=J_a/s\). These are just a deliberately chosen representation on the multiplicity space; they are not identified with the physical lab charges. Use

\[
 E_i^{(s)}=q_i(I+n_i\cdot L),\qquad
 F_r^{(s)}=(I-r\,(b\cdot L)^T)/2,\qquad r=\pm1.
\]

Because every direction is unit and \(n\cdot L\) has spectrum in \([-1,1]\), the effects are positive. Because \(\sum_iq_i n_i=0\), the three Alice effects sum to identity. Different directions give noncommuting effects, checked directly in the physical 256-dimensional lab. Bob's transpose is needed for the actual maximally paired input; it is not an assertion that transposition is a physical time reversal.

The \(m=9\) block has dimension 81, the two \(m=3\) blocks have dimension 45 each, and the even blocks carry exact qubit instruments on 58 dimensions. Thus the genuinely noncommuting complement coverage is

\[
 81+45+45+58=229,\qquad (229+2)/256=231/256.
\]

The 25-dimensional \((2,2)\) irrep has multiplicity one. By irreducibility and Schur's lemma, every lab-only effect commuting with all three lab charges is scalar there. A neutral external pointer may still have its own dynamics, but it cannot turn that block into a noncommuting **lab** POVM within this charge-preserving class. A different algebra, altered sector decomposition, or charged reference could change the conclusion; none is supplied by this calculation.

The higher-spin joint table is explicitly

\[
 p_s(i,r)=\frac{q_i}{2}\bigl(1-r\,\eta_s\,b\cdot n_i\bigr),\qquad
 \eta_s=\frac{s+1}{3s}.
\]

Thus \(\eta_1=2/3\) on the two \(m=3\) blocks and \(\eta_4=5/12\) on the \(m=9\) block. The exact qubit singlet table has \(\eta_{1/2}=1\). These visibility changes are a substantive difference, not a numerical defect or a gauge transformation.

## 4. The actual paired input and coherent measurement

The earlier lab input has amplitude matrix \(M=(CODE\,J\,CODE^T+D)^T/16\), with \(D=I-PCODE\). It is **not replaced** by a newly postulated singlet in each physical sector. The two code dimensions and every complement block are mutually orthogonal on Alice, with corresponding conjugate blocks on Bob. Restricting the original maximally paired complement to a block automatically gives a maximally paired multiplicity factor and a paired irrep spectator. The logical basis and Bob's measurement are chosen to extract the required correlations from that same input.

For the qubit construction and for every higher-spin block, the verifier applies the actual lifted Kraus operators to the physical \(256\times256\) paired-state matrix. It checks the **entire Gram matrix** of the six joint pointer amplitudes against the compressed state used in the downstream circuit—not just outcome probabilities. This establishes equality up to an environment isometry for the subsequently applied lab-independent clock, work, target and reference-memory operations. It also checks the physical charge balances and declared lab energies of the input blocks. Orthogonal lab-sector records make the displayed coarse outcome tables sums of their sector contributions; this is not a claim that the original state was an incoherent sector mixture.

The input, choice of equivalent-copy pairing, higher-spin embedding, measurement settings and physical apparatus remain specified structures. The result does not derive their existence or preferred selection from zero itself.

## 5. Clock, work and the remaining incidence question

Both options retain the geometry's unequal marginal \(q\) and vector balance. In the original 16-label relative clock, each sector gives total weight \(3/16\) to the old three-label window. In that window, the event old record \(r=+1\), target \(t=-1\) has full Born weight \(3/64\), and the target perfectly records the earlier binary work relation \(h\). These statements hold for the new complete code-plus-complement models, including their scalar remainders.

The test then attaches the same reference and later memory interaction used in the previous all-history construction. For **all 16 old-clock labels**, all constituent types and the sampled new relative phases \(0,\pi/4,\pi/2\), it checks the three reference-orientation zero constraints, the geometric-value-weighted complementary constraint and the pairing involution. It also checks both new-clock eigencomponents. The all-phase extension follows from the prior operator commutators and isometry intertwining, not from assuming three samples cover a continuum. As before, the joint reference/record/work readout is not automatically accessible to either confined observer.

The strengthened construction also confirms that a geometric marginal and a paired work value do not uniquely fix felt valence. Put \(d_i=b\cdot n_i\), and consider the two *candidate* signed variables \(d_i r\) and \(d_i j\), where the verified work witness is \(j=rt\). The latter has zero mean by the target pairing; their signs disagree on exactly half of the physical joint weight. For the full old-clock average, the new wider unsharp construction has

\[
 \langle d r\rangle=-97727/972800\simeq-0.1004595,\qquad
 \langle d j\rangle=0.
\]

The exact-qubit option instead gives \(\langle d r\rangle=-161/1280\simeq-0.1257813\), while the earlier surrogate gave \(-38157/965200\simeq-0.0395327\). Their different joint values are a useful test: preservation of the same geometric weights and paired work zero does **not** force a common direction–record coupling. The calculations do not establish that either candidate is experienced, or that an assumed negative value constitutes suffering. If an independently justified bridge identifies a negative local value with suffering, global cancellation would not erase that local experience.

## 6. What this advances and what it does not

This replaces most of a classical placeholder by explicit, noncommuting, charge-preserving measurements on the **same all-sector physical lab input** and transports them into the existing relational history. It also isolates an exact 25-dimensional obstruction to charge-commuting lab noncommutativity in this model, and shows that exact-qubit coverage and wider unsharp coverage are different, calculable tradeoffs.

It does not derive the Hilbert spaces, charges, lab Hamiltonians, complement degeneracy, apparatus, pairings, POVM, reference, clock, or old initial state from self-sufficient zero. The declared lab energy and relational constraints are not a proof of objective global cosmic energy zero, and the accounting does not include all control and interaction energy. Neither mathematical amplitudes nor pointer labels establish actual subject incidences, felt valence, an equal-experience mirror, or a preferred incidence measure. In particular, the explicit alternatives show that a substantive experiential/individuation bridge is still needed; they do not show that such a bridge is impossible. Necessary plenitude and determinism are not settled here.
