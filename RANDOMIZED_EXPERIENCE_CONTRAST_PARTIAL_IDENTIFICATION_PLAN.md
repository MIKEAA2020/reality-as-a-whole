# Plan: an intervention contrast with an imperfect experiential channel

This retrospective plan extends the [feedback and phenomenal bridge audit](FEEDBACK_REALIZATION_AND_PHENOMENAL_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md). It is an invented population example, not preregistration or a study. Derivations and premises are fixed below as an illustrative decision gate.

1. Specify binary potential experiences and an observed report under a randomized, ethically feasible hypothetical intervention. Derive a positive population causal contrast only under a common, target-valid sensitivity and false-positive rate; give sharp numerical bounds over explicitly stipulated rate ranges.
2. Construct an exactly observationally equivalent model in which the intervention changes the report channel rather than experience, while each channel remains positively associated with the target. Keep the first-person anchor and experiential error assumptions explicit.
3. Show that even a known population contrast does not identify individual responders: construct two potential-outcome couplings with the same intervention-arm distributions but different benefit and harm rates. State consistency, randomization, interference and subject/target assumptions.
4. Check all arithmetic and witness distributions independently with exact rational calculations and tests; relate this example to, rather than duplicate or supersede, prior audit and calibration gates. Refresh inventories, run selected regressions and deliver the report.
