# A constitutive-value principle: positive development and explanatory limits

## 1. The next commitment

The privation proposal should develop as **constitutive goodness**, rather than as an unexplained optimizer selecting a universe. The candidate is that positive actuality and the fulfilled organization of its differentiated beings have intrinsic value; failures are defects within that organization, not a second negative substance.

This does not mean everything serves every goal, or that every actual action is morally justified. Nor does it identify goodness with maximum power, successful domination, computational complexity, or mere logical consistency.

The next task is to articulate a value principle whose content is not just “the things we favor are good.” The following is a substantive candidate, with its assumptions explicitly visible.

## 2. Three proposed requirements

### V1. No arbitrary identity privilege

An evaluation must be invariant under a mere renaming of affected beings that preserves all evaluatively relevant features and relations. A different name alone cannot change a verdict.

This is a constraint against arbitrary labels, not a proof that all beings have identical capacities, interests, or entitlements. It also does not establish which features are evaluatively relevant. That specification must not hide an arbitrary preference behind a new label.

### V2. Non-erasure of qualitative perspectives

A real qualitative perspective supplies prima facie evaluative significance in its own right. Its significance is not exhausted by its utility to another perspective's project.

This is the central normative conjecture. A complete descriptive theory must acknowledge real perspectives, but descriptive acknowledgment alone does not entail this evaluative principle. The proposal is that qualitative reality and significance are constitutively connected; it is not a deduction of an ought from the requirement to describe accurately.

V2 does not mean that every chosen goal is authoritative, that every capacity must be maximally exercised, or that every experience is equally valuable. Distinguishing a being's fulfillment from arbitrary project success remains essential.

### V3. Undominated fulfillment

Fix a domain of affected beings and sufficiently specified constitutive standards. If one physically admissible arrangement improves fulfillment for at least one being, worsens it for none, and introduces no omitted relevant loss, it is better in that respect.

This is a candidate Pareto-style normative principle. It is plausible and more precise than the instruction “maximize coherence,” but its normative authority and the underlying fulfillment standards are not established by notation. The clause about omitted losses must be specified in advance, not used after the fact to protect every existing arrangement.

Together, V1–V3 propose that fulfillment is positive, that real perspectives cannot be dismissed through arbitrary identity privilege, and that improvement need not require a preselected cosmic goal. They are a candidate constitutive-value principle, not a complete ethics or an actualization theorem.

## 3. A rigorous conditional consequence

Let I be a fixed set of affected perspectives, X a set of feasible arrangements, and each relation <=_i a preorder describing fulfillment for perspective i: reflexive and transitive. The notation x <=_i y means y is at least as fulfilled as x for i.

Define:

    x <=_P y iff for every i in I, x <=_i y.

This relation is reflexive and transitive because every component is. If x <=_P y and not y <=_P x, then y dominates x in the resulting strict product ordering. Calling this an improvement adopts V3; the order-theoretic facts alone do not establish its normative authority.

The strict dominance relation is transitive and irreflexive. For transitivity, x <=_P y <=_P z implies x <=_P z. If z <=_P x, transitivity would imply y <=_P x, contradicting strict dominance of y over x. Thus x is strictly below z. Irreflexivity follows immediately from the definition.

For a finite nonempty X, at least one undominated arrangement exists: if every element were strictly dominated, following dominating successors would eventually repeat an element, contradicting transitivity and irreflexivity. This establishes neither a unique best arrangement nor that an undominated arrangement fulfills everyone completely. Infinite domains need further conditions even for existence of an undominated member.

These are elementary conditional order results, not discoveries of moral or psychophysical laws. No executable model or new test result is claimed.

## 4. Why opposing goals do not automatically reverse this evaluation

The earlier goal-reversal proof allowed every evaluator and its negative as equally admissible. V1–V3 instead propose constraints on evaluative authority. A goal of harming another does not automatically become an equally authoritative constitutive standard merely because it can be formulated.

This does not refute the goal-reversal theorem. It challenges the applicability of its unrestricted domain to normative value. The restriction needs justification; V2 is where that justification must ultimately be supplied.

Conversely, frustration of a destructive goal does not automatically establish a Pareto improvement. The agent may incur real costs. The candidate must distinguish project frustration, loss of morally relevant agency, qualitative harm, and the effects on other beings without defining every desired verdict into the standards.

## 5. Partial order is progress, not a cosmic selection principle

The proposed framework can identify some improvements without arbitrary numerical weights. It will generally leave conflicts incomparable. Incomparable does not mean equal in value, and undominated does not mean morally perfect.

It also does not resolve:

- cases where benefit to one accompanies harm to another;
- whether and how to aggregate across perspectives;
- changes in which subjects exist;
- whether creating additional fulfilled lives improves a world;
- unique ranking of entire possible realities;
- the grounding of the component fulfillment relations themselves.

An account of subject incidence cannot be replaced by treating I as eternally fixed. A whole-account must eventually address changing populations and the constitution of perspectives; the fixed-domain formal result does not settle those questions.

This is precisely why an axiarchic account cannot jump from “there are objective improvements” to “there is one best possible universe, and goodness makes it exist.”

## 6. A new contrastive test for goodness of being

Suppose Good means only positive actuality. Any concretely actual world, including one containing severe deprivation, has positive actuality. Consequently, goodness in that sense alone does not distinguish which world should exist or explain its particular structure.

Suppose instead Good means fulfilled constitutive organization. The predicate can now distinguish arrangements, but its normative authority and relation to existence need explanation. The value principle is meaningful only if its standards are not silently rewritten to fit whichever arrangement is actual.

A fully justified necessary constitutive principle could, in principle, determine reality without comparing an external menu of possible worlds. That would avoid a cosmic optimizer. It would still need to show why its necessity concerns concrete occurrence rather than merely the consequences of an abstract axiom system.

Therefore the most promising axiarchic direction is an **identity account**, not an unexplained value-to-existence causal arrow. Actuality, intelligibility, qualitative nature, and value would have to be one necessarily concrete constitution. Saying this identifies the candidate; it does not yet supply that constitution.

## 7. Suffering remains a discriminating constraint

V2 means that suffering cannot be dismissed solely because an external observer or a supposed whole benefits. V3 allows some improvements to be specified without assigning numerical prices to every perspective. Together they make the Good conjecture more demanding rather than immunizing it.

If a supposedly necessary harmful arrangement has a genuinely feasible alternative that improves one perspective without any relevant loss, the claim of complete fulfillment is challenged. Showing that the alternative is infeasible requires an independently supported constitutive constraint, not the bare fact that it did not occur.

The deprivation analysis remains compatible with pain being an actual event. What is claimed to be privative is the relevant loss or disorder of fulfillment, not the occurrence of the experience. Whether that account captures every negatively valenced experience is itself a question, not already settled by calling evil a privation.

## 8. Current verdict

We now have a more specific candidate bridge from positive constitution to value: no arbitrary identity privilege, intrinsic significance of real qualitative perspectives, and undominated fulfillment. The framework supplies a conditional partial ordering, not a complete ethics or a unique universe.

The central unproved claim is V2 and the associated constitutive standards. The central axiarchic debt is why this value is inseparable from the reason for concrete actuality. Neither debt makes the candidate automatically false, but neither is closed by the elementary ordering proof.

The leading architecture remains self-explanatory qualitative process monism. **Constitutive goodness with privative failure** is a coherent and increasingly articulated extension. **Good as the demonstrated reason for existence** remains the stronger conjecture.

## 9. Why regard might be due: constitutive interests and reasons

The corrected privation analogy identifies a missing integration of affected beings, rather than merely a destructive result. The next question is what makes that integration due.

### Constitutive standards need not be chosen projects

An organism's dependence on oxygen does not originate in a decision to pursue oxygen. Likewise, an eye's visual function is not assigned afresh by its possessor's current preferences. There can be factual, constitution-dependent standards relative to which a process sustains, impairs, or fulfills a being.

Such facts do not establish an unconditional moral obligation to sustain every function. A person may reject continued survival, a medical procedure may intentionally disable a function, and the unrestrained exercise of one capacity may damage another being. These examples separate constitutive needs from chosen goals without deriving moral authority from biological function alone.

The promising route is therefore not “every capacity should be exercised.” It is that the constitution of qualitative beings supplies interests and forms of fulfillment that are not created merely by an external evaluator's preference.

### The candidate bridge: an affected perspective supplies a reason

A proposed constitutive-reasons principle is:

> A being's qualitative flourishing or deprivation supplies a prima facie reason concerning how that being is treated, whether or not another agent chooses to adopt its welfare as a goal.

The reason is prima facie: it can conflict with other reasons and does not automatically dictate one action. Pain's aversive character, its biological function, an agent's endorsement of it, and its all-things-considered value must remain distinguishable. Nor does every frustrated desire establish a deprivation that others are obliged to prevent.

This principle would ground due regard in the affected being rather than in an observer's optional project. It is a substantive normative-constitutive conjecture, not a deduction from the bare existence of needs. Under the no-brute-facts objective, it must ultimately be explained through the same constitution as qualitative occurrence and agency, not installed as an unexplained axiom.

### Symmetry constrains privilege but does not prove impartial morality

If two relevantly identical cases receive different evaluations solely because of arbitrary names, the difference lacks the proposed relevant ground. This supports the no-arbitrary-identity requirement.

But the rule “each agent privileges that agent's own interests” is itself invariant under a consistent renaming of agents and patients. An agent-relative egoism is therefore not refuted by permutation symmetry or accused correctly of formal contradiction on that basis. Likewise, an agent can coherently acknowledge another's suffering while denying that it supplies a reason for the agent.

The bridge must instead defend the intrinsic reason-giving significance of the affected perspective. If another's welfare supplies a reason, an agent's refusal to adopt that welfare as a goal does not erase the reason. Whether it does supply such a reason is the substantive disputed premise.

### The privation thesis after this clarification

On the proposed account, an acquisitive action uses actual powers. Its wrongness, where present, consists in missing integration of reasons grounded in the legitimate interests of affected beings. The missing regard need not be a missing empathic feeling or lack of information. It can be a failure of practical acknowledgment despite understanding.

This distinguishes the actuality of the deed from the proposed constitution of its wrongness. It does not yet establish that every wrong has this analysis or settle the content of legitimate claims and fair shares. The possibility that certain harms require additional evaluative structure remains a question for the account.

### Explanatory priority, not yet actualization

The resulting proposed order is:

    concrete qualitative constitution
        -> constitutive interests and fulfillment
        -> reasons for due regard
        -> practical failures understood as privations of integration.

The first arrow has descriptive content; the second is the central normative bridge. The diagram is a programme, not a proven derivation. The final step applies the privation interpretation to those failures.

Even success here would establish an account of value within reality before establishing value as reality's actualizer. The further axiarchic identity must explain why this same value belongs to the reason for concrete existence rather than being only a feature of its finite articulation.

## 10. The explanatory fork: goodness of being versus axiarchic selection

Two distinct explanations have been running together:

1. Why value belongs to concrete reality rather than being an external label.
2. Why value accounts for the existence and particular constitution of reality.

A successful answer to the first does not automatically answer the second. The privation thesis has its most direct application to the first.

### The counterfactual discrimination test

Consider descriptions of three possible arrangements: extensive flourishing, mixed flourishing and deprivation, and extensive deprivation. Their physical or metaphysical possibility is not assumed established; they serve to distinguish the candidate concepts.

If goodness means positive actuality, each arrangement would possess that goodness insofar as it were actual. This sense of Good does not by itself discriminate which arrangement obtains. If Good means integrated fulfillment, the arrangements can differ in value, but one still needs both a justified standard of fulfillment and a principle connecting that standard to actuality.

Thus neither the denial of an evil substance nor the goodness of being alone entails an optimally fulfilled reality. A deprivation can be real as a deficiency of an actual bearer without being an independent negative entity. Its cause can also be an actual positive process; privation analysis does not require harm to lack causal explanation.

### The stronger constitutive alternative

The most economical candidate is not a value principle acting on an independently constituted reality. It is one self-explanatory nature that grounds causal powers, qualitative perspectives, and their value together. On this version, no additional moral force pushes possibility into actuality.

The name **constitutively axiological process monism** distinguishes this extension from strict selection-based axiarchism. It is a candidate explanation of value's place in reality. The further claim that value is indispensable to existence itself remains stronger.

If the ultimate constitution is independently shown to be necessarily concrete and intrinsically valuable, its existence and value would have a common explanation. It would not follow merely from that common explanation that value asymmetrically produces or grounds existence. Identity and causal/explanatory priority must not be conflated.

### The hard requirements are now separate

A completed account must establish:

- **qualitative constitution:** what constitutes actual perspectives and their fulfillment;
- **normative identity:** why that fulfillment has intrinsic reason-giving significance;
- **modal concreteness:** why the ultimate constitution cannot remain merely a possible description;
- **finite articulation:** why its actual organization contains these particular limitations and harms.

No one item substitutes for the others. Uniqueness of an abstract model does not establish its concrete instantiation. Logical consistency does not establish moral perfection. Global unity does not erase the distinct claims of finite perspectives. The absence of a substance of nonbeing does not establish that nonexistence is impossible.

### Comparative conclusion

The user's proposal gives a coherent route to goodness as intrinsic to positive fulfilled being and badness as privative. The next justified promotion is to an explicit **constitutively axiological extension** of the leading monism, not a claim that the argument has already shown Good selecting or necessitating a world. Stronger axiarchism remains a live conjecture, but needs an explanatory contribution that the shared-constitution account alone does not provide.

## 11. The repair constraint: a consequence with discriminatory content

The next step is to derive a constraint on the stronger axiarchic hypothesis rather than repeatedly rename its explanatory target.

### Three theses with different commitments

- **Privation:** wrongness or badness consists in missing or disordered due fulfillment, rather than an independent negative substance.
- **Goodness of being:** actual beings have positive actuality and, on the proposed evaluative identity, goodness insofar as they are actual.
- **Fulfillment axiarchism:** the reason for concrete actuality excludes a strictly better admissible arrangement, where better is assessed by independently justified constitutive standards.

The third thesis is stronger. Neither of the first two entails it. An account can accept privation while denying that the actual whole is undominated in fulfillment.

### Conditional repair lemma

Let A be the actual arrangement, F an independently specified set of admissible arrangements, and < a strict improvement relation grounded in the constitutive-value principle. Assume A is in F and fulfillment axiarchism requires A to be undominated in F.

If B is in F and A < B, the hypothesis is contradicted: A is dominated by B, contrary to its required undominated status.

For the Pareto-style case, fix the affected perspectives and their justified fulfillment standards. If B removes a deprivation for at least one perspective, diminishes fulfillment for none, and loses no other relevant value, V3 gives A < B. Fulfillment axiarchism then rules out A.

The reasoning is elementary, but its application is substantive. The admissibility of B, the completeness of the comparison, and the authority of the fulfillment standards are premises requiring support. Mere imaginability of B does not establish admissibility.

### The resulting principle

> A reality whose actualization is explained by undominated fulfillment cannot contain a deprivation that admits a genuinely admissible improvement with no countervailing loss of fulfillment or other justified value.

Call this the **no gratuitous deprivation constraint**. Here gratuitous means removable without relevant cost, not merely emotionally distressing or currently unexplained. The constraint does not deny that conflicts can make some deprivation unavoidable. It also does not establish that any particular harm is unavoidable.

### A comparison that clarifies the stakes

Consider two descriptions with the same affected beings, legitimate achievements, and relevant future consequences, except that one includes an additional episode of severe suffering and the other does not. If both arrangements are genuinely admissible and the suffering supplies no compensating value, the suffering-containing arrangement is dominated under the proposed principle.

This is a conditional comparison, not a demonstration that such globally matched alternatives are physically or metaphysically possible. Real analgesia, suffering, learning, agency, and later consequences involve empirical and constitutive details that cannot simply be stipulated away in an actual case.

The point is that privation vocabulary does not protect the stronger hypothesis from the comparison. A removable deprivation counts against complete fulfillment precisely because it is a deprivation.

### Finite limitation is not a universal defense

Finitude can explain the inability to realize mutually incompatible capacities simultaneously. It does not by itself explain the intensity, distribution, or duration of suffering. Likewise, the existence of some useful warning pain does not entail that every instance of pain, or every increment of severity, is necessary.

A proposed necessity must be supported by the independently justified constitution. “It happened, so the Good must require it” would make the explanation circular. Compensating goods cannot be invented after every challenge without a stable account of why they exist and how the dependence works.

### The singleton-domain problem

If admissibility is defined as “whatever the actual world contains,” then F = {A}. A is trivially undominated, whatever it contains. This supplies no discrimination and no evidence that value explains its actuality.

A genuine necessitarian theory could independently establish that only A is possible. That would not automatically be vacuous: a substantive constitutive derivation can explain uniqueness. But undominated status would then follow trivially from uniqueness and would not by itself establish goodness or show that value did the actualizing work.

The theory must therefore show either that value is indispensable within the independent constitution argument, or that it places genuine constraints not already fixed without it. A unique abstract structure also still requires an explanation of why it is concrete.

### What this advances

The privation account remains coherent even if the actual arrangement is not maximally or undominatedly fulfilled. Fulfillment axiarchism, however, now carries a precise comparative burden. A well-supported admissible repair would be a counterexample, not merely a missing derivation.

This separates three epistemic situations:

1. No adequate account of a harm's necessity has yet been supplied: an explanatory debt.
2. A strongly supported costless repair appears available: counterpressure, with strength depending on the support for admissibility and value comparison.
3. A genuinely admissible dominating repair is established under the theory's justified standards: contradiction of the undominated-actuality version.

Thus the user's central distinction remains intact: unfinished is not automatically wrong, but a live conjecture must allow facts or arguments to count against it.

The next decisive work is not an additional toy simulation. It is an independently justified constitutive-value principle and a noncircular account of the admissible arrangements to which the repair constraint applies.

## 12. An underdetermination result: fulfillment does not yet select actuality

The repair constraint excludes dominated arrangements under the stronger hypothesis, but it does not select among undominated alternatives. This is a structural limitation, not simply an uncompleted calculation.

### A small exact counterexample

Fix two affected perspectives with comparable ordered fulfillment levels solely for this example. Consider the feasible arrangements:

    A = (2, 0)
    B = (1, 1)
    C = (0, 2).

The numbers stipulate three ordered levels for an abstract counterexample; they are not empirical measurements of experience, nor a proposed cardinal welfare scale. Cross-perspective comparison is granted for the example, not established for actual subjects.

All three arrangements are Pareto-undominated. A improves the first component relative to B but worsens the second; C reverses those advantages. No candidate improves one component while preserving or improving the other.

Two symmetric evaluative rules remain available:

- **Lower-level priority:** sort the levels from lower to higher, compare the lower first, and use the higher to break a tie. This favors B.
- **Higher-level priority:** sort from higher to lower, compare the higher first, and use the lower to break a tie. This favors A and C over B.

Both rules are invariant under exchanging the names of the two perspectives. Both respect strict Pareto improvements on the general two-coordinate domain: if neither coordinate falls and at least one rises, neither sorted order statistic falls and at least one rises, so either lexicographic rule ranks the improvement higher. Both register the second perspective's condition when the first priority level is tied.

These are not endorsements of either moral theory. They establish that anonymity and Pareto improvement alone do not determine a unique value ranking. V2's prima facie significance does not supply the missing comparison unless its content is strengthened to include a particular priority principle. Doing so is possible, but the priority then requires justification.

The result is not about merely selecting between differently named copies. B and the pair A/C differ in their distribution of fulfillment; they remain structurally distinct even if A and C are identified under a relabeling.

### Consequence for the whole-account

Even granting the privation analysis, intrinsic significance, cross-perspective comparison, and admissibility, the present constraints do not establish which kind of fulfillment is ultimate. The stronger axiarchic claim therefore needs more than a ban on gratuitous deprivation.

Possible additions include priority to the worst-off, a justified aggregation rule, rights constraints, or a constitutive argument that only one arrangement is genuinely possible. No such addition is established by the present counterexample, and none is supplied by the bare absence of an evil substance.

Under the user's demand for complete intelligibility, an additional rule cannot simply be installed as a brute preference of the whole. Its authority and applicability must belong to the explanation. Likewise, a probability distribution might explain a distribution of alternatives without by itself explaining why one particular outcome obtains under a fully contrastive demand.

### The positive consequence: two projects should be pursued separately

**Constitutive value:** explain how fulfillment and deprivation belong to the nature of real qualitative beings. This can succeed without proving a unique complete ranking of possible realities.

**Axiarchic actuality:** explain why value determines or indispensably grounds this reality's actuality. This requires the constitutive-value account plus a justified determination/actualization argument.

A shared necessary constitution of existence and value could ultimately unify these projects. That remains a substantive identity hypothesis. It must derive the particular normative relations and concrete actuality, not merely assert that goodness and necessity coincide.

### Current strengthened verdict

The privation proposal is not refuted by this underdetermination result. What is refuted is the inference that the present weak constraints already select an actual world. The gap is structural: more than one evaluative ordering satisfies them.

The next justified target is the source of the priority or compatibility relations among goods. Another generic assertion that the whole is coherent cannot settle this example, since both conflicting rules are coherent and symmetric. A successful continuation must either justify a stronger normative principle or show that the concrete constitution independently resolves the relevant alternatives.

## 13. Due absence and the test of fulfilled agency

### Not every absence is a privation in the evaluative sense

A stone's lack of vision is not blindness. A person temporarily asleep need not be acting wrongly because they are not currently exercising empathic attention. Nor is every unmet desire a deprivation that someone else is obliged to remedy.

The relevant structure is therefore not merely:

    a capacity or activity is absent.

It is:

    a fulfillment is due to this bearer in these circumstances,
    and that fulfillment is absent or impaired.

One can represent the proposed evaluative privation as D(b,p,c) iff Due(b,p,c) and not Realized(b,p,c), with appropriate treatment of impairment rather than simple binary absence. This representation is a conceptual schema, not a derived theory of Due. A lack of empathic feeling can explain a psychological fact without by itself establishing moral wrongness. The account needs to show why practical regard is due in the particular case.

Thus privation is not the independent source of normative standards. It explains the dependence of badness on a prior standard of positive fulfillment. The asymmetry is important, but the standard's authority must be explained separately.

### Thin agency does not by itself establish moral integration

The preceding section proposed that recognizing others may belong to fuller agency. This must not be silently treated as proved. Consider an agent who:

- understands the affected beings and their suffering;
- reasons consistently about means;
- pursues a coherent personal objective;
- exploits others deliberately.

No contradiction in this thin description establishes that the agent is ignorant or instrumentally irrational. Moral deficiency is compatible with considerable agency and effectiveness. Calling the agent's agency incomplete *in a morally thicker sense* is a substantive normative claim, not a consequence of the thin definition.

This conceptual counterexample does not refute moral realism or a thicker constitutive theory. It blocks the inference from accurate understanding plus effective agency alone to due concern. A successful theory must explain the thicker standard rather than insert it into “full.”

### Three sources of standards must be distinguished

1. **Instrumental standards:** a process succeeds relative to an adopted end.
2. **Functional standards:** a capacity fulfills a constitution-dependent role, as in visual or biological functioning. There can be factual grounds for this role without a person's present endorsement.
3. **Normative authority:** a fulfillment supplies reasons for action or treatment, including reasons that constrain adopted ends.

The existence of the second prevents all evaluation from being reduced to momentary preference. But it does not by itself establish the third. An evolved function or a statistically normal capacity is not automatically morally authoritative. Multiple capacities also interact and can conflict.

### The resulting order of inquiry

The correct development is:

    justify positive fulfillment and its authority;
    identify what regard that fulfillment requires in context;
    explain wrongdoing as a failure of that due integration.

It is not:

    find an absence;
    call the absence evil;
    infer the authority of its opposite.

The user's proposal supplies a serious dependency thesis: badness is not an independent negative substance. It does not yet supply all the content or authority of the positive Good on which the privation depends.

### A substantive candidate, not an unexplained axiom

The strongest existing candidate remains that concrete qualitative beings possess forms of fulfillment whose significance is intrinsic to their nature. Appropriate practical regard would acknowledge that significance. This principle must be constitutively explained under the no-brute-facts objective; merely declaring intrinsic significance is not enough.

A completed account might ground functionality, qualitative fulfillment, and reasons together. Alternatively, a rival might accept functional privations while denying stance-independent normative reasons. The privation schema alone cannot choose between those accounts.

No argument presently supplied establishes that normativity or concrete existence follows from thin agency, absence of evil substance, or complete descriptive coherence alone. This is an explicit boundary of the derivation, not a verdict that the stronger identity conjecture is likely false.

## 14. A specific identity hypothesis: qualitative fulfillment and value

Continuing requires selecting a candidate, not claiming another deduction from privation alone. The candidate to develop is an **axiological identity hypothesis**:

> The fulfillment of an actual qualitative being is not merely something an external evaluator happens to approve. At least some of its value is constituted by the being's realized qualitative condition itself. Its fulfillment and deprivation supply prima facie reasons concerning its treatment.

The last sentence makes this stronger than a descriptive theory of preferences. It is explicitly part of the hypothesis, not a proven consequence of the first sentence.

### Three levels must remain separate

1. **Unchosen qualitative orientation:** an experience can be aversive or attractive without an agent first choosing a project under which it is so. This does not identify every pleasant experience with moral goodness or every painful experience with an all-things-considered wrong.
2. **Reason-giving significance:** the qualitative condition supplies a reason for or against something, rather than merely producing approach or avoidance. This is an evaluative identity claim, not a deduction from behavioral motivation.
3. **Interpersonal authority:** the reason is not erased when another agent declines to adopt the affected being's welfare as a goal. This is stronger than the description that the experience matters from within its own perspective.

The candidate embraces all three, but level 1 alone does not establish levels 2 or 3. A thermostat's regulatory behavior does not establish qualitative experience or normative value. Conversely, absence of an adopted goal does not establish absence of qualitative valence.

### The internal adversary

A coherent rival can accept actual experiences, aversive character, functional needs, and privations of those functions while denying stance-independent interpersonal reasons. The bare descriptions supplied so far do not contradict that rival.

This does not show that intrinsic value is impossible. A true necessary constitutive identity might rule out the rival. It shows that the identity has not yet been justified by merely redescribing aversion, goal frustration, or missing empathy.

The hypothesis must therefore explain why qualitative condition and significance are one nature, not add a brute rule that observers must care. Under complete intelligibility, an unexplained evaluative axiom would fail the same objective as an unexplained psychophysical bridge.

### A useful conditional consequence: realization neutrality

If the value is constituted by the actual qualitative condition, changing the material label alone cannot change that value while preserving its entire relevant qualitative and relational constitution. Realized nonbiological subjects, if such subjects exist with the relevant constitution, cannot be discounted merely because their material is unfamiliar.

This is not a claim that current software has experiences. It depends on actual constitution, not matching descriptions or reports. Nor does it imply identical treatment in different relationships or circumstances: those relations can be evaluatively relevant.

The principle also gives a possible adversarial test: an account that assigns different intrinsic significance solely to biological versus nonbiological labels while stipulating all relevant constitution unchanged needs an additional justification.

### The relation to Good and actuality

The positive target is a common constitution C that explains concrete occurrence, physical powers, qualitative perspectives, and value together. This is not an assertion that the word C supplies that explanation; C remains to be identified and justified.

A shared root does not entail an asymmetric chain from value to existence. Strict axiarchism must show that value is indispensable to why C is concrete or determined, rather than merely a consequence of C. A constitutively axiological monism can be true even if strict selection-based axiarchism is false.

### Current commitment

The identity hypothesis is the strongest specific normative extension now worth developing. It has a clearer content than “Good is coherence”: qualitative fulfillment is intrinsically significant and supplies reasons not created by another agent's optional goal. It is compatible with a privative interpretation of wrongness, provided that interpretation captures the actual failures in question.

Its status is a substantive conjecture. No proof has yet excluded the internal adversary, derived an actualizing principle, or shown that every form of value reduces to qualitative welfare. No new empirical result is claimed.

### Consolidated status and a compatibility test

[The axiological status assessment](AXIOLOGY_STATUS.md) distinguishes denial of evil substance, universal privation, qualitative-value identity, value-based determination, and concrete actualization. The qualitative-value hypothesis is an assistant-proposed extension, not a deduction already established by the user's argument. It can help ground due regard while privation analyzes failures of that regard, but compatibility with a universal privation thesis remains unproved, especially for aversive experience occurring through normally functioning systems. The assessment compares the informed exploiter, protective pain, and competing undominated fulfillments; it does not claim a new completed explanation or numerical likelihood ranking.

### Aspect-relative privation: targeted stress test

[The privation stress test](PRIVATION_STRESS_TEST.md) distinguishes positive actuality from due fulfillment indexed to bearer, respect, and circumstances. Functioning protective pain does not alone refute this richer theory: successful warning and a local cost can coexist. Mere absence of pleasure, however, does not distinguish neutral experience from suffering. A nontrivial theory must independently specify the positive fulfillment and justify why it is due, rather than define Good as not-Bad and report an explanation. The cruelty example remains strongest as missing practical integration of legitimate claims, not absent empathic machinery. These are conceptual compatibility and discrimination results, not a derivation of universal privation or axiarchic actuality.
