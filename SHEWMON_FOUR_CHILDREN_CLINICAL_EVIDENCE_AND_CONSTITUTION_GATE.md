# Shewmon's four children: clinical evidence and the constitution gap

## Source and reading scope

The user supplied [*Consciousness in congenitally decorticate children: developmental vegetative state as self-fulfilling prophecy*](sources/uploads/shewmon1999.pdf), by D. Alan Shewmon, Gregory L. Holmes and Paul A. Byrne ([publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0012162299000821)). The complete 11-page article text, case descriptions, discussion and captions were read. I visually inspected the printed CT and MRI figures 1, 3 and 5 and the accompanying photographs on the rendered pages, but **not** the underlying scans, recordings, videotapes or medical records; the low-resolution printed figures cannot establish residual functional connectivity or substitute for neuroradiological assessment. The study cites many earlier studies whose full texts were not independently reviewed. The supplied PDF's SHA-256 is `71a0e72bac353b93c93ca31110dc629a6d7f9de61e145f619243cbf04b3a8195`.

**Conclusion:** The paper offers detailed evidence of variable, context-sensitive responsiveness and learned discriminations in four children with very severe congenital brain malformations. Its direct and caregiver observations make indiscriminate, confident predictions of permanent unresponsiveness untenable as a clinical practice; they provide meaningful grounds for taking the possibility of experience seriously. All four had some reported cortical remnants. The case series does not demonstrate that these remnants were functionally irrelevant, that any identified subcortical network was sufficient for experience, or that a particular intervention caused the children's development. It does not justify dismissing their possible feelings or care needs.

## 1. What was actually observed

The authors reviewed records and visited the children at home; their report combines parents' and therapists' histories, clinical records, and observations during home visits, some videotaped. The cases came to the authors' attention in different ways, not through a defined consecutive population. They were 5–17 years old at the study visits, and the same foster/adoptive mother cared for subjects 1 and 2. This is not a prevalence study or a controlled trial.

| Case | Anatomy as reported | Examples, with observation source and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |

No autopsy was performed. | At the home visit the authors observed visual tracking and one author saw the child move around obstacles; a caregiver described movement through an open door toward the porch. Therapists reported differentiated responses to familiar music and other stimuli. Individual subjective qualities were not directly reported. |

The article regards observed interaction as evidence of consciousness, while acknowledging that reflective self-awareness is empirically uncertain and that external behavior is not a simple test of it (pp. 370–371). A child's inability to provide a conventional verbal report does not make their possible experience or preferences negligible. At the same time, clinical descriptions of smiles, orienting, distress or learning do not specify their felt quality with certainty.

## 2. Residual anatomy, plasticity and causality

The authors openly state that **none of the four was absolutely devoid of cortical tissue** (p. 371). They distinguish severe tissue loss or dysgenesis from maximal hydrocephalus, where cortex may be compressed rather than largely absent. Their inference is that different residual regions in different children are unlikely to explain all the reported functions and that shared brainstem/diencephalic structures are plausible substrates. They state that logistical and economic obstacles prevented PET or functional MRI that might clarify the anatomy–function relationship. Visual inspection of reproduced scans here does not resolve it either.

The proposal that early damage allows subcortical systems to acquire functions usually associated with cortex—called **vertical plasticity** by the authors—is a hypothesis supported by suggestive comparisons and cited animal work, not an experimentally established mechanism in these four cases. The fact that abilities developed during affectionate care is compatible with benefit from that care, but there was no randomized or otherwise adequate counterfactual comparison to show that care caused the observed trajectories or that all similarly situated children would develop the same capacities. Selection, maturation, medical treatment, health changes, seizures, sensory ability and assessment context all matter. Respectful support and avoidance of unwarranted pessimism do not depend on claiming that causal uncertainty has been resolved.

### A small identification check: common structures are not automatically sufficient

**Conditional toy illustration, not a model fitted to patients.** Suppose each of four observed individuals really has experience, each has a shared brainstem feature `B = 1`, and each has at least one of four different retained or network features `C1, C2, C3, C4`. Two hypotheses can both agree with all four positive observations:

```text
H1: E = B
H2: E = B AND (C1 OR C2 OR C3 OR C4)
```

They differ on an unobserved configuration with `B = 1` and every `Ci = 0`. The observation that `B` is common, while the individual `Ci` vary, does not by itself choose between the hypotheses; neither differing prediction is established for an actual patient. The `Ci` are abstract features, not claims that particular remnants are functional or that the paper measured them. Conversely, the toy does not disprove subcortical sufficiency. It separates *plausibility* from an identified causal or constitutive rule, while taking the assumed experiences in the observed cases seriously.

## 3. The music hypothesis and experience

Responses to music provide potentially valuable individualized observations. The subject-2 contrast between a familiar therapist singing and an author playing a song, and the subject-4 contrast between keyboard music and favorite recorded music with live maracas, combine performer, modality, familiarity and arrangement. Thus they do not determine which feature explains the response, whether the response is specifically musical, or what it feels like. Even carefully matched auditory preference would not establish that physical consonance, synchrony or a mismatch quantity **is** pleasantness or unpleasantness. The [mismatch calibration-transport gate](EXPERIENTIAL_MISMATCH_CALIBRATION_TRANSPORT_GATE.md) applies only when its independently justified calibration assumptions hold; this case series does not supply those assumptions.

An ethically bounded comparison could use familiar, non-distressing sounds chosen with caregivers, counterbalanced and repeated across days and observers, separating performer, loudness, tempo, instrumentation and familiarity as far as practicable. It should report unscorable sessions and prespecify behavioral and physiological outcomes rather than forcing a conclusion about awareness. Only consented and appropriate observation is contemplated, never withholding needed support or inducing injury or distress. Even a reproducible preference remains indirect evidence about experience, not a validated measure of felt quality in this population.

## 4. Relevance to the framework

These cases strengthen the empirical demand that a theory not equate consciousness with high intelligence, speech, a typical cortical volume, or a single superficial behavioral response. They favor comparing explicit alternatives about subcortical support, residual tissue and distributed pathways rather than treating absence of one familiar anatomical pattern as proof of absent experience. They are consistent with, but do not establish, substrate independence.

The project's target remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7: intrinsic experience and genuinely plural subjects in a self-sufficient timeless relational whole, without an assumed universal subject or external creator. These case observations help constrain candidate mappings and ethical inference; they do not derive why a relational organization has experience, its qualities or its subject boundary.
