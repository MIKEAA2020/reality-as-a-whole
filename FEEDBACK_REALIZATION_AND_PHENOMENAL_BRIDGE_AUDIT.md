# From adaptive organization to an experiencer: a bridge audit

## Bottom line

Feedback and adaptive maintenance can be established as physical causal features under suitable interventions. They do **not**, by themselves, establish that the process is experienced, what it is like, how many subjects exist, or whether a subject persists. A bridge theory must add explicit incidence, individuation and quality principles and connect them to first-person anchors with a defensible error model. Competing principles can agree on known anchors and physical observations while disagreeing elsewhere. They can become empirically discriminable only when they entail differing observations under additional, independently defensible observation assumptions; otherwise their disagreement remains unresolved by those observations.

This follows the [chemotaxis audit](BACTERIAL_CHEMOTAXIS_ORGANIZATION_AND_EXPLANATION_AUDIT.md): its bacterial pathway is a real regulatory subsystem, not an established subject. The finite comparison below is invented and is neither a biological model nor evidence for or against machine or bacterial consciousness. It also does not decide Jaworski's physicalism-versus-hylomorphism dispute.

## 1. A controlled replay comparison

Consider four binary inputs `u₀…u₃`, initial memory `m₀ = 0`, and an error/output `eₜ = uₜ XOR mₜ`. In the feedback device the next memory is `mₜ₊₁ = mₜ XOR eₜ`; thus absent internal intervention it equals the current input. In the replay device `mₜ₊₁ = nₜ`, a fixed prerecorded schedule, with `n = (0,1,1,1)`; it ignores the present error and input when updating memory. The output becomes zero on a constant input after one update in the feedback device. This is a small tracking/error-correction system, **not** the biochemical integral-control model in the chemotaxis report.

| Input history | Feedback output | Replay output | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0,1,1,1` (nominal) | `0,1,0,0` | `0,1,0,0` | All displayed input, memory and output values match. |
| `0,1,0,0` | `0,1,1,0` | `0,1,1,1` | The final error returns to zero only in feedback. |

The exhaustive calculation covers all 16 four-step input histories: output histories match for exactly the two with first three inputs `0,1,1`, and differ for the other 14. The fourth input may be either value because its effect on memory would occur *after* the observed window. The count depends on this finite design and window; it is not a general probability of detecting replay. A single nominal trace does not identify the update mechanism. A carefully chosen input perturbation can distinguish these two specified rivals, assuming the input is set as intended and the relevant outputs are measured reliably; it does not eliminate every alternative mechanism.

A further distinction matters. A third device updates `mₜ₊₁ = uₜ` directly. It produces the same memory and output trace as the feedback device for **every** one of the 16 allowed input histories without internal interventions. Their causal graphs still differ: if one can independently clamp the computed error node at time 1 to zero under nominal input, the feedback outputs become `0,0,1,0` while direct-copy outputs become `0,0,0,0`. This assumes a clean intervention on that node, the specified update equations remain valid under intervention, and the node has a comparable operational identification in each device. Such assumptions are not licensed by observational data alone. The test is not equivalent to observing a naturally occurring change and, in an actual system, invasive manipulation can have side effects and different levels of description may permit multiple models.

Executable construction and full receipts: [script](verification/feedback_replay_phenomenal_bridge.py), [independent formula and intervention tests](verification/feedback_replay_phenomenal_bridge_test.py), [JSON](verification/feedback_replay_phenomenal_bridge.json). These are checks of stipulated mathematics, not evidence for the physical or experiential status of an actual system.

## 2. An explicit underdetermination witness

Use the same hypothetical domain of physical realizations for both candidates. For each realization define `F` as the stipulated presence of some specified, sufficiently rich recurrent functional organization; `B` as possession of a stipulated biological substrate property; and `E` as the presence of **at least one local subject having experience**, not merely the claim that the underlying whole is intrinsically experiential. A real theory must define and operationalize `F` and `B` far more precisely; the four-bit device above is not asserted to satisfy the rich `F` criterion.

- **Functional incidence candidate:** `E = F`; the specified organization is necessary and sufficient independently of substrate.
- **Biology-required incidence candidate:** `E = F AND B`; that same organization is necessary but its biological realization is also required.

Both are explicitly *experience-respecting*: they assign experience to an anchored human case stipulated to satisfy `F = B = 1`, and neither denies first-person experience. Both can be embedded as alternative local-subject principles within a view that the wider whole is intrinsically experiential; neither follows from that view. For a hypothetical nonbiological realization with `F = 1, B = 0`, the first says `E = 1` and the second says `E = 0`. For `F = 0`, both say `E = 0`, although neither rule is justified as a real universal necessity. A biological condition is an illustrative substantive rival, not a claim that biology as such has been shown necessary or that humans lack experience outside a narrowly defined toy feedback condition.

Hold fixed the same physical realizations, input–output traces, interventions, and physical records under both assignments. Then those observations and a shared human positive anchor are logically insufficient *by themselves* to choose the local-subject label for the nonbiological case. The script enumerates the three stipulated label cases; it does not construct a human or actual conscious artificial realization. Nor does consistency with one anchor make either rule a plausible, complete, or empirically supported theory. Additional anchors, mechanistic constraints, and a sound observation bridge could exclude one. If a rival promises distinct reports or measurement outcomes, it must specify why and with what errors; adding an untestable label to identical predicted records would not supply such a difference.

## 3. The missing principles are different questions

| Target | Additional commitment needed | What a test can and cannot do |
|---|---|---|
| **Incidence** | A selector Γ specifying which realized processes have episodes, including boundary and substrate requirements. | Perturbations can establish candidate physical features and human report associations. They do not alone prove that Γ applies to an unanchored target. |
| **Subject count and unity** | Rules for ownership, unity, overlap and partition, including whether multiple streams can share one subject. | Validated within/between-field tasks may discriminate specified partitions under their calibration and exclusivity assumptions. Correlations or a connected graph alone do not count subjects. |
| **Quality and content** | A map χ from realized episodes and context to phenomenal qualities, with standards for relevant qualitative similarity. | Controlled discriminations and reports can constrain χ where anchored and calibrated, not give direct external access to another's private quality. Incidence alone does not specify what it is like. |
| **Numerical episode identity** | Criteria distinguishing one episode token from a duplicate with the same content or structure. | Matching functional or report profiles does not by itself establish that two descriptions refer to the *same* token, rather than qualitatively similar tokens. |
| **Subject persistence** | Diachronic identity conditions for gradual change, interruption, component turnover and branching/fission. | Continuity of memory, structure and behavior can be evidence under a stated rule, not an independent proof of personal identity. If one predecessor branches into two successors, ordinary one-to-one numerical identity cannot make both distinct successors identical to that predecessor; the proposed account must say what follows instead. |

In particular, a yes/no incidence rule does not tell us whether there is one subject or many, which experiences belong together, which quality occurs, or who continues over time. First-person occurrence is a datum for the experiencing subject, not a report inferred from behavior; communication, recall, attention and interpretation may nonetheless be mistaken or incomplete. One's own anchor does not directly reveal which other systems experience or what their experiences are like. This is an epistemic asymmetry, not a reason to deny others' experience.

## 4. What a serious test programme would require

1. **Specify rivals before looking at target outcomes.** State realization boundaries, causal variables, Γ, subject partition and χ, alongside conditions under which each rival changes its prediction. Avoid defining a positive subject as merely whatever a report detector labels positive. Specify rival alternatives and potential confounds; exact computational copying, lesion or turnover in humans is not assumed feasible.
2. **Anchor and calibrate where ethically and practically possible.** In consenting human participants with first-person access, use tasks and independently checked reports as fallible observations. Estimate false-positive and false-negative rates for the particular target, task, state and intervention, with uncertainty; distinguish failures to report from absence of experience. Repeated within-person assessments and controls can constrain reliability, but no external report is an infallible ground truth. A human anchor supports a local mapping only under further assumptions connecting experience, recall and measurement.
3. **Identify physical causation, not just correlation.** Predefine input perturbations and, where safe and justified, targeted interventions and controls. Check alternative mechanisms, measurement drift and intervention side effects. Treat evidence as supporting causal models in their tested domain, not as establishing experiential incidence. The Boolean example's clean clamp is an idealization, not an authorization for invasive experimentation.
4. **Derive and compare observable consequences.** For each rival, combine its incidence and quality claims with an explicit report/measurement model, including nonreporting cases, pre-registered contrasts, uncertainty and feasible sample sizes. If predictions overlap under credible error and confound ranges, report non-identification rather than choosing the preferred metaphysics. A negative or absent report need not imply absence of experience; an uncalibrated positive proxy need not prove it.
5. **Do not assume transport.** Explain why calibration in one population, substrate, state or task applies to another, and seek target-relevant validation and sensitivity analyses. For nonreporting or nonbiological targets with no independent target-valid anchor, explicitly state the remaining gap; a source-domain fit alone does not close it. If no potential observation can distinguish the proposed rules under the chosen assumptions, label the claim currently underdetermined rather than pretending it is a test result.
6. **Bound the ethics.** Obtain appropriate consent and independent review for human studies; avoid unnecessary risk or distress. For potentially sentient nonhuman or artificial targets, apply precaution proportionate to plausible welfare risks rather than using uncertainty as permission to cause harm. Do not create distress, invasive lesions, or irreversible branch/termination experiments simply to settle metaphysics. Stop or redesign if safety, consent, intervention specificity, calibration, or target validity is inadequate.

The resulting evidence can incrementally constrain rival theories, but bridge premises themselves remain contestable. Even successful human predictions may leave qualitative mapping and substrate extrapolation open, and some identity questions may be underdetermined by all proposed tests rather than merely awaiting larger samples.

## 5. Relationship to the existing gates

- [Realized-process candidate](REALIZED_PROCESS_CANDIDATE.md): supplies a proposed physical realization basis but leaves Γ and χ as obligations. The toy isolates a causal comparison; it does not fill those maps.
- [Experience-assay latent identifiability](EXPERIENCE_ASSAY_LATENT_IDENTIFIABILITY.md), [rivalry random audit](RIVALRY_RANDOM_AUDIT_EXPERIENCE_BRIDGE.md) and [mismatch calibration transport](EXPERIENTIAL_MISMATCH_CALIBRATION_TRANSPORT_GATE.md): formalize limitations of proxies, rival predictions and target-domain error transport. The present witness makes a simpler common-domain incidence disagreement explicit, rather than improving those bounds.
- [Phenomenal unity and plural-subject gate](PHENOMENAL_UNITY_AND_PLURAL_SUBJECT_IDENTIFICATION_GATE.md) and [triple-unity report partition gate](TRIPLE_UNITY_RANDOMIZED_REPORT_PARTITION_GATE.md): give conditional partition tests under specific ownership, exclusivity and measurement assumptions. This audit does not treat those premises as verified or replace their quantitative results.
- [Plural experiential ground](PLURAL_EXPERIENTIAL_GROUND_SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION.md) and [robust experiential contrast](ROBUST_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTRAST.md): emphasize experience-respecting alternatives and discrimination requirements. The binary witness illustrates only one incidence gap; it does not resolve the constitution of the ground or of multiple perspectives.

**Status:** the feedback/replay result and label disagreement are proved only within their stated finite definitions. Real-world applicability, experiential bridge principles, target calibration, subject number, phenomenal qualities and persistence remain open.
