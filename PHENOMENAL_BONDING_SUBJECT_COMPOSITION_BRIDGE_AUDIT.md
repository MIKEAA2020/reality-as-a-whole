# From experienced unity to subject constitution: the missing inference

## Decision

The [Bohn plural collective consciousness audit](BOHN_PLURAL_COLLECTIVE_CONSCIOUSNESS_FULL_PAPER_AUDIT.md) examines a different strategy: basic collective consciousness properties rather than a bonding relation among microsubjects. It clarifies the remaining plurality-to-perspective and grounding obligations without treating Bohn as a bonding theorist.

The [subsequent subjective-character analysis](SUBJECTIVE_CHARACTER_AND_NONCIRCULAR_FIELD_BRIDGE.md) considers a positive, field-first alternative: its no-extra-observer identity avoids one circularity, but it starts with first-personal fields and does not independently derive their physical selection or qualities.

Ordinary first-person experience can give genuine evidence that contents are experienced together. It does **not by itself establish** that those contents belong to additional smaller subjects, that they are numerically shared between subjects, or that the subject is constituted by them. Conversely, it does not show that such composition is impossible. The dispute between S Siddharth and Gregory Miller makes this distinction explicit and corrects the tempting inference that a positive conception of *intra*-subject unity automatically gives an independently established *inter*-subject bonding relation.

For the present E-L candidate, this is not simply another name for a micro-subject combination problem: E-L does not postulate that fundamental physical particles are subjects. The direct challenge is nevertheless serious. E-L's L rule outputs physical subsets and its q rule assigns a proposed unpleasant/neutral contrast; neither determines why the subsets are subjects or why q has that felt character. An experiential ground and familiar lived unity motivate the inquiry, but do not yet fill that gap. B4–B5/F2 remain open.

## 1. The actual exchange and source coverage

- **Siddharth, [“Against Phenomenal Bonding”](https://doi.org/10.31820/ejap.17.1.3) (2021):** we read all substantive main HTML text and notes. Siddharth challenges role-only bonding and argues that an analogy from familiar co-consciousness fails to explain subject combination. The 2021 paper relies on intuitions of ontological unity and token privacy; its Euclidean volume example illustrates the risk of stipulating an incoherent relation, not a proof that phenomenal sharing is inconsistent in the same way.
- **Miller, [“A Reply to S Siddharth’s ‘Against Phenomenal Bonding’”](https://doi.org/10.31820/ejap.18.1.4) (2022):** we read all substantive HTML text and notes. He disputes the strength of the simplicity and privacy intuitions, distinguishes the subject-summing explanatory gap from mereological impossibility arguments, and replies that the co-consciousness relation can indirectly relate subjects by directly relating their experiences. His alternative introspective route is explicitly conditional: **if** phenomenal bonding and composite subjects obtain, introspection of one's own unity would also introspect relations between experiences of subject-parts. He does not present that conditional alone as an independent observation that bonding is true.
- **Siddharth, [“Are Composite Subjects Possible?”](https://doi.org/10.31820/ejap.20.1.9) (2024), pp. 205–229:** we read the complete PDF text returned in eight extracted chunks, including notes and references, but did not visually inspect its pages. It distinguishes a *general* question about what composite subjecthood is and whether it is possible from a *special* question about which relations would compose subjects if it is possible. Siddharth defends an epistemic primacy principle for intuitions drawn from human subjectivity and argues that a special-condition proposal alone does not settle the general question. He acknowledges his own case depends on inadequately substantiated intuitions and can face a question-begging objection.

- **Hedda Hassel Mørch, [“Why Consciousness Can’t Overlap”](https://philarchive.org/archive/MRCWCC), draft:** we read the fetched extracted text including §§3–7 and notes; the returned final chunk ends within the conclusion, so this is not a complete full-paper audit. The draft argues that phenomenal overlap is contradictory under a particular deflationary definition of subjects as exclusively bounded unified bundles; under a substantive account it instead objects to overlap on parsimony grounds. It explicitly acknowledges Miller's subject-indexed reply and challenges the deflationary status and motivation of alternatives. The draft should not be treated as an established published result.

We did not independently read the complete Goff/Roelofs works or Miller's 2017 *Dialectica* paper for this audit. Their positions above are reported as characterized in the exchange, not silently attributed as independently checked primary arguments. The previously supplied [Miller de-combination paper](MILLER_SUBJECT_PARTHOOD_AND_DECOMBINATION_AUDIT.md) and [Chalmers manuscript](CHALMERS_COMBINATION_FULL_PAPER_AND_STRUCTURE_AUDIT.md) were separately read with the scopes stated there. ## 2. The exact inferential step

Distinguish these claims in one local window:

- **U:** a particular subject S has a genuinely conjoint experience containing aspects A and B. This is a first-person experiential claim, not a claim that a report is infallible or that A and B are independently existing atoms.
- **P:** there are *distinct* subjects `s_A` and `s_B`, each experiencing an aspect token also experienced by S; appropriate subject-parthood and numerical token identity obtain.
- **B:** the relevant inter-subject relation holds between those subjects, directly or indirectly through their experiences, and is sufficient to constitute S.

Miller's conditional introspective argument has the form: if P and the relevant bonding account hold, then one's access to U can also be access to the relation as an inter-subject relation. That is a legitimate **conditional interpretation**; it is not the inference `U ⇒ P ∧ B`. The missing P and the sufficiency/grounding claim in B need independent defense. Siddharth's further demand that a positive account answer the general composition question is a contested standard for intelligibility, but the missing entailment is a concrete issue whatever label one gives the overall problem.

### Same subject-level datum, different ownership hypotheses

Use the common interface of the [Miller audit](MILLER_SUBJECT_PARTHOOD_AND_DECOMBINATION_AUDIT.md), in a restricted local window. Stipulate that in both descriptions S experiences A and B together and has the same accessible qualitative and report facts for that conjunction.

| Description | Fact about S's experience | Further subject claims |
|---|---|---|
| **Only-S package** | S experiences A and B together. | No subject-parts are posited for this interface. |
| **Nested package** | S experiences the same A and B together. | `s_A` and `s_B` are distinct subject-parts, sharing those very tokens with S; the further subject-parthood and bonding/constitution facts are postulated. |

This is an abstract comparison of claims, not a demonstration that either complete ontology is genuinely metaphysically possible, that a human experience has atomic pieces, or that its local reports exhaust all evidence. The two descriptions are stipulated to agree on U and the specified S-accessible facts; they disagree about P and B. Thus, **on that limited information**, U does not logically select P or B unless a further premise says it does. A different evidence base or an independently justified law might distinguish the packages. S could have access to other aspects in a complete account; we have not proved universal experimental indistinguishability.

The nested description genuinely uses **numerical token sharing**, not merely equal-looking experiences. Stating that S owns both tokens and that further subjects own them does not itself prove the latter are S's proper parts. Nor does a stipulated formal set of owners establish subjects, subjective character or experiential bonding. A holistic view on which phenomenal character changes with context may also challenge whether a token shared across different fields can retain the relevant character; that needs argument rather than an implicit assumption of context independence.

This is not a refutation of Miller's analogy. It shows what an analogy and the conditional introspective proposal leave open: a concept can be extended and a prima facie objection answered without establishing that the target relation obtains or entails a new subject. It also is not an inference that the simple-subject or token-privacy theses are true merely because alternative composition is unproved.

## 3. What unity and privacy do—and do not—license

The following should not be conflated:

1. **First-person unity:** several contents are experienced together. This is a meaningful datum, though the completeness and boundary of any particular report may be uncertain.
2. **Mereological simplicity:** a subject has no proper parts. This is a stronger ontological thesis, not the same as phenomenological unity.
3. **Token privacy:** no single experiential occurrence can belong to two distinct subjects. This is also stronger than my not having access to another person's experiences.
4. **Exclusive maximality:** a unified field is a subject only if it is not part of a larger unified field, under a specified unindexed unity relation. This rule forbids nested subject-fields by construction; it is not the bare claim that each field has definite boundaries.

Miller gives reasons to challenge the last three as assumptions; he does not thereby disprove them. Siddharth (2024) argues that human-subject intuitions have epistemic primacy unless a positive alternative is supplied. That is a defensible proposed **burden-of-proof principle**, not an automatic logical derivation of universal metaphysical simplicity or privacy from an individual's first-person unity. Equally, saying an alternative is merely *formally describable* is not a sufficient answer to Siddharth's demand for real subject-level intelligibility. Both burdens remain live.

Mørch's sharper objection deserves an explicit formulation. Suppose unindexed `U(x,y)` means that tokens are unified, and a subject-field F requires that its members are mutually unified **and** no member is unified with a token outside F. If `AB` and `ABC` are both subject-fields, `U(A,C)` follows from the larger field and `¬U(A,C)` from the smaller: contradiction. This is a valid conditional argument, not a proof that its unindexed exclusion premise is mandatory. Similarly, a partial overlap between `AB` and `BC` produces `U(B,C)` and its negation. Mørch argues that simply dropping exclusion leaves the many-subject view less compelling than an exclusively bounded bundle; that comparative conceivability judgment is substantive, not the contradiction itself. In her substantive-subject alternative, overlapping subjects are not contradictory but she argues they add redundant entities. Whether extra subjects have independent explanatory work, and whether counting them violates parsimony, cannot be settled merely by counting labels.

Miller's subject-indexed UBIT revision addresses the incompatibility argument by allowing `U_W(A,C)` while denying `U_L(A,C)`. Mørch contends that using already distinct subject indices risks presupposing substantive subjects, or amounts to removing the unindexed exclusion condition from a deflationary account. This is a real grounding-direction challenge: defining a subject in terms of a relation that itself requires that same subject as an unexplained parameter can be circular. Miller need not accept her taxonomy, but must explain what fixes the indices without presupposing the subjects he is trying to constitute. His 2018 footnote 31's one numerically identical unity relation is not a proposal to double qualitatively distinct unity relations, an alternative Mørch separately considers. A non-nested alternative does not follow merely from this unresolved challenge.

The [finite facet analysis](RELATIONAL_WHOLE_SUBJECT_FACET_CONSTITUTION.md) makes the structural cost explicit: maximal fields alone, nested fields and exclusive ownership follow from different added rules. Its maximal facets may still overlap and share tokens: maximality *by inclusion in a complex* is not automatically Mørch's cross-field no-external-unity condition or exclusive ownership. The finite representation does not independently resolve the subject metaphysics.

## 4. What this means for the proposed E-L bridge

The existing candidate gives a definite *special-case physical rule*: from the four-spin kernel and chosen intervention horizon, L selects certain physical sets; a further postulate identifies their q contrast with unpleasant/neutral experience. At `t=1/4` and the original one-update query, it selects two pairs. The physical dynamics and the experimentally unanchored experiential interpretation are distinct components of the hypothesis.

To convert that into the demanded constitution account, four independent links must be defended:

1. **Experiential base and ownership:** say in what sense the whole's intrinsic nature is experiential without claiming an already unified universal experiencer or leaving an actually felt episode with no subject. Explain how the local subject is present, not merely assign it a label.
2. **Incidence:** explain why the declared physical/intrinsic relation constitutes **these** perspectives and their boundaries, and why the alternative connected-component rule, unselected overlapping subjects or partial unity are not equally eligible. A physical-maximization rule, or choosing no overlap, does not by itself answer this.
3. **Qualitative reference:** explain why the selected q-state is this *felt* unpleasant contrast rather than its reversal, a different affect or no specified quality. First-person unpleasantness is evidence; a chosen q label is not its measurement.
4. **Transport and testing:** specify equivalence, contextual sensitivity, persistence, and a common-domain mapping to calibrated reports and independent physical measurements. A single report correlation, successful relabeling check or selected coarse Markov kernel does not provide these links.

For comparison, E-C uses the same physical sector and experiential-ground hypothesis with a connected-component subject postulate; N-L shares E-L's physical and experiential output stipulations but proposes a neutral basis. Each is incomplete and must pay its own explanatory costs. E-L is not disqualified by being incomplete, and N-L is not automatically viable simply because its outputs were matched by construction. Where the rival packages use the same physical/report law, those records do not decide the grounding claim; if completed packages predict different records under defensible interventions and validated experiential readouts, they can be compared. A mathematical counterdescription is not itself proof of a genuinely possible alternative under the user's strong plenitude thesis.

## 5. What would change the result

A non-circular advance would provide at least one of the following, on the **same** declared domain:

- An independently motivated experiential constitution principle that fixes a subject's ownership and quality from prior intrinsic facts without presupposing that very subject or a synonymous subject-forming relation. Explain how the principle meets, rejects with reasons, or revises the unity/privacy challenge. Establish its scope, non-overlap or nesting implications, and limitations.
- Condition-valid first-person evidence plus an independently grounded physical and report model yielding **different risky predictions** for completed E-L and an experience-respecting rival, with controls for access, memory, attention, context and token identity. A single person's co-presence judgment is not automatically a calibrated assay for unseen subject-parts or their shared tokens.

Until then, bonding, L selection, relative boundary, a non-subject experiential ground, and neutral constitution are research proposals with different costs—not proofs, empty by definition, or decisive failures merely because their opponents reject them. Experience is accepted as a datum. The seven criteria apply jointly; C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**. This analysis does not determine subject counts, genuine modality, measure, fundamental physics or a full battery pass.
