# Post-trial felt valence: provenance, stimulus baselines and the EEG access gap

## What is new

[Chen et al., *A Large Finer-grained Affective Computing EEG Dataset*](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-023-02650-w) describes an experiment much closer to the needed outcome than song preferences chosen before recording. After each video, participants rated their **subjective experience while watching**, including valence on a continuous 0–7 scale. EEG was recorded during viewing. The [pinned BIDS conversion](https://github.com/nemarDatasets/nm000112/tree/2577b0bbc089b49e828cd98d80445133634b6d34) provides all 123 participants' converted event tables with video spans and reported scores; the converted EEG recordings themselves are annex pointers and were **not downloaded**.

We audited all event tables and established useful no-EEG baselines, following a [fixed exploratory plan](FACED_POSTTRIAL_REPORT_BASELINES_FIXED_PLAN.md) written after inspecting one participant's structural sample but before inspecting the other 122 tables. As an additional aggregate provenance check added after the first complete baseline run, all 28 converted video-mean valence scores agree within rounding with the paper's supplementary table. This increases confidence that the converted *aggregate* valence values are consistent with the publication, but cannot verify which individual report was paired to which individual EEG segment.

This is a film-viewing study, not the proposed musical interaction experiment; a single post-video rating does not reveal the time course of experience. No physical interaction preferences, independently identified frustration, relevant interventions, neural analysis, or experiential identity law have been demonstrated. The next step is an actual bounded EEG–report check with original-source alignment and controls, not a conclusion that these baseline numbers validate the framework.

## What was checked

The paper describes 123 participants, 28 common video clips and post-clip ratings of eight emotions plus valence, arousal, familiarity and liking. Intended positive, negative and neutral videos were organized into blocks, with order randomized within the design. The publication treats valence 0 as very negative and 7 as very positive. This report uses **the participant's valence**, not the stimulus's assigned emotion, as the target. The source report describes remembered or summarized experience of the video; it is not independent of recall, wording, context or reporting behavior.

We read all 123 event TSVs and EEG JSON sidecars from a GitHub source archive of 402,639 compressed bytes, and verified each relevant content file against its Git blob in the pinned tree. All 123 had exactly 28 nonduplicated, nonoverlapping video spans, valid 0–7 valence values, and consistent per-video target labels: 3,444 participant–video rows. Event onset/sample fields agreed within half a sample, and spans passed the stated recording-duration check. This is internal metadata consistency, **not** verification against recorded triggers, playback time or EEG bytes.

The converter's [source code](https://github.com/nemarDatasets/nm000112/blob/2577b0bbc089b49e828cd98d80445133634b6d34/code/faced.py) describes loading each `After_remark.vid` and joining the video identifier to spans parsed from 101/102 event markers. It silently skips some malformed original rating records and overwrites repeated video keys; we do not know whether that happened in conversion, because we have not inspected or replayed the original MATLAB rating files and event BDFs. The converted files' 28 published valence means match [supplementary table S4](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41597-023-02650-w/MediaObjects/41597_2023_2650_MOESM1_ESM.docx): all 28 are within 0.005 of the two-decimal values, with maximum absolute difference 0.004961. This is not an independent cohort, and aggregate agreement does not exclude individual swaps or common errors.

A material metadata inconsistency needs resolving before EEG interpretation: all 123 BIDS JSON sidecars identify the manufacturer as **Biosemi**, whereas the paper's methods identify a **NeuSen.W32 (Neuracle)** system. We have not established the cause. Likewise, the conversion README's citation names do not match the paper's authors; the paper and dataset description give Chen, Wang, Huang, Hu, Shen and Zhang. We cite the paper and its DOI rather than relying on the README's mistaken author line. The BIDS dataset description declares CC BY 4.0; separate original-data and stimulus terms must be checked before reusing or distributing the original recordings or films.

## What stimulus information predicts

Within each video, the population standard deviation of the 123 valence ratings averaged **1.527** across videos. The population standard deviation of the 28 video-mean ratings was **1.304**. These are different descriptive quantities, not components of a fitted variance model; neither establishes why people differ or quantifies measurement error.

The fixed baseline analysis predicts the **individual post-video rating**. Grand and group means are fitted using training ratings only. Errors are averaged over the 3,444 held-out rows; lower is better. There is no EEG feature in any model.

| Split and predictor | MAE | RMSE |
|---|---:|---:|
| New person, known video: training grand mean | 1.6777 | 2.0177 |
| New person, known video: intended valence category mean | 1.2565 | 1.5672 |
| New person, known video: video-specific mean | 1.2357 | 1.5552 |
| New person **and** new video: training grand mean | 1.6777 | 2.0176 |
| New person **and** new video: intended valence category mean | 1.2552 | 1.5683 |

New-person splits use five fixed participant-ID folds. The crossed analysis uses those folds and three category-stratified video folds; a training row shares neither a held-out person nor a held-out video with its test intersection. Each row is tested once and there were no fallback predictions. It does **not** establish generalization to arbitrary films, settings or populations: it is retrospective on 28 selected videos and one participant sample, and the 15 training sets overlap. These are descriptive point estimates without uncertainty intervals. Intended category is information supplied by the experiment, not an EEG-derived measure; its use requires that equivalent pre-existing category information would actually be available in a future deployment.

For this specific experiment and split, an EEG predictor should be compared against the same-row category baseline, and, for known films, the video mean. Beating a grand mean alone would not show neural information beyond stimulus identity. Even beating these baselines would establish at most improved prediction of *reported* valence under the evaluation conditions—not an assay of a preferred physical phase or an explanation of experiential character. An EEG model must avoid placing windows from one participant–video trial into both training and test; any feature selection, scaling and tuning must be fitted only on training data.

## Comparison to existing work

The FACED authors already computed within-person EEG spectral-power correlations against self-reported ratings and trained EEG classifiers. Their cross-subject classifier scores in the paper target **stimulus-assigned categories**, not our continuous valence target; their within-person classification splits time samples from each video across training and test. We did not replay either analysis and our MAE/RMSE cannot be directly compared with their classification accuracies. Their design supports testing neural correlates, not inferring that a neural pattern is experience itself.

A related musical alternative, [Turchet et al., *Emotion Recognition of Playing Musicians From EEG, ECG, and Acoustic Signals*](https://doi.org/10.1109/THMS.2024.3430327), paired EEG during musical performance with post-performance ratings of both felt and communicated valence/arousal. Its [data record](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10396364) advertises the ratings, recording files and audio features, but only as a large ZIP in the examined listing; no recordings or ratings were downloaded here. The musicians were performers rather than passive listeners, were asked to convey assigned emotions, and the paper reports felt and communicated ratings were strongly associated. Its classifier used intended emotion categories, not direct continuous felt-valence regression. The paper's subject-dependent train/test split is described in terms of segments of the same trials; the description does not establish that trials were grouped across splits. Its impressive reported score therefore cannot be assumed to generalize to unseen performances. This is a caution based on the reported split, not a replay or a finding of confirmed leakage.

## Source access, audit scope, and next gate

For Chen et al. we read the paper's HTML main prose and figure captions, and the supplementary DOCX text/tables, not the figure images, underlying original MAT ratings, original event files, BDF signals, experiment code or original stimulus clips. The dataset's converted event/sidecar files and relevant conversion code were inspected, not the original conversion execution. For Turchet et al. we read the dataset page's description and part of its ZIP listing and the paper's abstract and methods sections in extracted PDF text; later sections, figures, original questionnaire, dataset and code were not fully inspected. The NEMAR description provides direct HTTPS access routes, but those hosts are outside this workspace's permitted outbound host list. The smallest BDF pointer observed is subject 038, **97,616,448 bytes**, SHA-256 `a3f52d2d71201151939b332bdd1de5530d60dcae557c8c3e0aa3d673b15749d3`; the pointer—not the recording—was checked against its Git blob. The full subject 000 pointer is 468,584,448 bytes. No BDF download or acquisition-header inspection was performed. Do not imply that the file itself is verified or that a successful range request is known to be available.

A defensible continuation would first resolve original-to-converted report pairing and hardware/timing metadata, and test selective access to a genuinely pinned EEG object under a bounded transfer budget. Then freeze one signal feature and controls and use subject- and video-disjoint evaluation against the above baseline; document any differences from the prior music-specific statistic. A subject–quality bridge still requires independent arguments and evidence beyond prediction. B4, B5 and B10 remain open; C3 is **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with the other criteria, and first-person experience remains a valid datum.

## Reproducibility

[Program](verification/faced_report_baseline.py), [tests](verification/faced_report_baseline_test.py), and [receipt](verification/faced_report_baseline.json) contain splits, aggregate output and input SHA-256 hashes. Five targeted tests passed. The first implementation run encountered a file-size guard and stopped; the disclosed bounded amendment raised that guard without changing endpoints or eligibility. The later supplementary comparison was added after the first complete baseline run; no score model was revised. This is a fixed retrospective analysis, not an externally registered or independent replication. The source archive and `tree.json` are ignored local files. Re-fetch with `gh api 'repos/nemarDatasets/nm000112/git/trees/2577b0bbc089b49e828cd98d80445133634b6d34?recursive=1'` and `curl --max-filesize 12582912 https://codeload.github.com/nemarDatasets/nm000112/tar.gz/2577b0bbc089b49e828cd98d80445133634b6d34`, saving them as `.arena/faced/tree.json` and `.arena/faced/metadata.tar.gz`, then run the script. It reads archive entries without extracting paths or executing repository code.
