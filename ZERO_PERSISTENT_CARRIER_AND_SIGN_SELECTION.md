# Can subject persistence determine experiential sign?

**Result.** On the existing internal-reference history, a gauge-compatible pair of sphere-sector labels persists through the original sign gate. That persistence alone does **not** choose between the conserved-sphere and disturbed-record quality assignments. A stronger, explicitly stated stability principle *does* select the sphere assignment on the accessible part of a restricted binary-observable class. But an explicit memory-dependent rival satisfies the same persistence, stability and mirror-balance conditions. Thus the positive selection theorem is real but scoped: to turn it into a constitution of experience, the subject boundary and admissible intrinsic observables must themselves be determined.

This is a finite structural test, not a claim that sectors are conscious, that Born weight is subject incidence, or that the circuit is a brain. It continues [the common-domain quality comparison](ZERO_INTERNAL_REFERENCE_SUBJECT_VALENCE_COMPARISON.md), uses exactly the [original gate](ZERO_INTERNAL_CONTEXTUAL_REFERENCE_AND_RECORD_TRADEOFF.md), and is checked by [the verifier](verification/zero_persistent_sphere_subject_selection_test.py).

## 1. A compatible persistent carrier rule

In the existing valid sector let P be the validity projector, B the Bob sphere sign, Zj the old record, M the sign pointer, O the internal sign observable, and G̃ the dressed gauge. O²=P and [O,B]=[O,G̃]=0. The original sign-writing unitary is

```text
N = (P−O)/2,
W = (I−N)⊗I_M + N⊗X_M.
```

Define the two nonzero, orthogonal sector projectors

```text
Π_b = P(I+bB)/2,       b = +1 or −1.
```

They sum to P, commute with G̃ and with W, and have exact unitary transport W(Π_b⊗I_M)W†=Π_b⊗I_M. The same statement holds through the later intervention below. All 16 existing relative-clock inputs have conditional Born weight 1/2 in each b sector. The verifier also checks the original old clock, work and diagonal G constraints under the operations used here.

**Conditional subject rule:** designate the sphere factor B plus the pointer M as a carrier and track its b sector through this episode. Treat each chosen, registered b-sector incidence as retaining its identity when the operations preserve Π_b. This is a precise transport rule on the actual Hilbert space, not the older Hamiltonian/graph selector and not a derivation of a conscious subject. The sectors are alternatives of one sphere factor, not two spatially disjoint subsystems; interpreting them as distinct actual subjects, or deciding how many incidences occur in a coherent state, needs further premises. M is only assigned to this candidate boundary by stipulation, and no automatic communication with Alice or another realm is presumed.

The two previous signed-quality bridges remain

```text
v_B = κ s b,       v_j = κ s z,
```

with s the registered sign and z the final Zj value. Both are invariant under the stated dressed gauge, and both change sign under the specified counterpart S X_M. Since Π_b persists while the original gate produces b≠z with conditional weight 1/4, the same carrier-transport rule is compatible with either bridge. **Identity persistence does not imply constancy of felt quality:** one and the same person can undergo changes of experience. Equally, B's conservation does not by itself prove that B is experienced.

## 2. An explicit, symmetry-compatible intervention

Use the existing O itself to define a later unitary, with the identity on the inactive remainder:

```text
Q = I−P+O.
```

It is unitary and commutes with P, B, G̃ and the modeled clock/work constraints. It leaves the already written M alone. On the valid sector, exchange S sends O to −O, so the intervention's **channel** is counterpart-covariant there; the minus sign is a global phase for the conjugated unitary. Q is a physical operation, not declared to be gauge. Its implementation and energetic cost are not supplied; the constraints checked are not identified with objective whole-energy.

Its exact Heisenberg action on the valid sector is

```text
Q† (B Z_M) Q = B Z_M,
Q† (Z_j Z_M) Q = B Z_j Z_M.
```

In a branch with definite b,s,z, Q therefore retains b and s but sends z to b z. It leaves the candidate sphere quality fixed, while the record quality flips for b=−. For every one of the 16 histories, the total weight of those b=− branches is 1/2. The complete B+M reduced state, conditional on a definite b,s and pre-intervention z, is unchanged by Q. This conditional statement is not a claim that all coherent superpositions of b have identical reduced states or that the whole subject is physically unchanged.

### Crucial sequential-measurement qualification

The original sign branch `(I+sO)|Ψℓ⟩/2` is already an O eigenstate. **Without an intervening j readout**, Q acts on that branch only by the phase s. If M is still coherent, those are relative phases between M branches; if M is registered/dephased, the branch density operators are unchanged. Therefore the calculation must **not** be presented as an unobserved record flipping along a pre-existing classical trajectory.

A genuine two-time comparison instead adds a fresh neutral record R, initially `Z_R=+1`, and performs a QND write of z into R *after* W and *before* Q. Its unitary is controlled by `(I−Zj)/2`; it preserves the stated gauge, B, Zj and old constraints, but does not commute with O and changes the extended coherent history. R is an extra specified apparatus and is not automatically accessible to an inhabitant. Then Q acts, leaving R and M alone. The test's z-projected branch calculation is precisely the joint probabilities for this sequential measurement, not a claim about a counterfactual hidden path.

The two-time statistics are:

| b | old z (now stored as r) | new z after Q | joint weight, summing over s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| + | + | + | 1/2 |
| − | + | − | 1/4 |
| − | − | + | 1/4 |

The first readout is essential to this interpretation. Each s has half of every displayed weight. Repeating the gate, adding an instrument, and establishing a subject/report interface are distinct physical assumptions, not free operations outside the model.

## 3. A conditional selection theorem

Consider the deliberately restricted family of sharp signed-quality candidates

```text
v = κ s f(b,z),       f(b,z) ∈ {+1,−1}.
```

Assume:

1. The quality depends only on the simultaneous commuting labels B, Zj and M in this form. In particular, s is an odd orientation reference, not bare pleasure or displeasure.
2. On the original input support, `b=z`, the quality agrees with the chosen calibration `κ s b`: hence `f(+,+)=+1`, `f(−,−)=−1`. This calibration is a phenomenal premise, not fixed by physics.
3. The quality is unchanged by Q when s is held fixed: `f(b,z)=f(b,bz)`. This is a **quality-stability law**, stronger than persistence of the carrier. It treats Q as irrelevant to intrinsic quality even though Q is a genuine physical operation and can affect other degrees of freedom.

Condition 3 forces `f(−,+)=f(−,−)=−1`. Condition 2 gives `f(+,+)=+1`. Thus

```text
f(b,z)=b  on  {(+,+), (−,+), (−,−)}.
```

These are exactly the b,z labels reached from the actual input by the original sign gate, the intermediate z readout and Q. The b=+,z=− label is not reached in this episode, and its value remains free. There are **two**, not one, full-domain binary functions satisfying these assumptions; both agree with the sphere bridge on the reachable labels. Without the Q-stability premise there are four calibrated binary functions, including the record and sphere candidates.

The selection is nontrivial because Q is compatible with the gauge and conserved carrier sector while changing the record relation in the sequential experiment. But it is conditional: the step from 'same subject' to 'same felt quality after Q' is an additional experiential assertion. Neither the gate, carrier projectors, zero nor necessary plenitude establishes it. One could instead adopt **locality to the B+M carrier algebra** as an independent quality principle; in this small class and with the same calibration it also yields v_B. That choice excludes v_j as an external-interface-dependent quality, but it does so by postulating the carrier boundary and locality, not by proving them.

## 4. Why the restricted theorem is not a full derivation

The added R record gives a concrete rival that the restricted class omits. Write `r=Z_R`, so r=+1 initially and r=z at the intermediate readout. Consider

```text
v_memory = κ s b r.
```

It has all of these properties:

- Initially, with R ready at r=+1, it agrees with the calibrated sphere assignment.
- It is gauge invariant when R is neutral under G̃, and counterpart odd when R is neutral under S X_M.
- It remains unchanged under Q, because Q preserves b, M and R. It uses the *past recorded z*, not the new post-Q z.
- On the actual sequential experiment it differs from v_B exactly when the first z readout is −, with Born weight **1/4**. The difference persists after Q.
- With the same Born-weighted incidence hypothesis as before, s is balanced at each i,b,z, so both v_B and v_memory have zero signed mean even **within each b sector**.

The memory rule does change at the *intermediate recording operation* when r changes; Q-stability alone does not prohibit this. If a proposed subject consists only of B+M, R is external and a strict local-quality rule would exclude v_memory. If a proposed subject legitimately includes R, it does not. We have not derived either boundary from the whole. Conversely, interpreting v_memory as a real feeling requires a phenomenal bridge just as v_B does. This counterexample demonstrates why requiring a conserved label and invariance under one operation is not enough to select a unique intrinsic quality among all physical observables and histories.

## 5. What mirror balance adds

Under the specified counterpart S X_M, b and z are unchanged and s reverses. The old quantum history assigns equal Born weights to the two s values at every i,b,z. If one **additionally** identifies those weights with the measure of distinct actual subject incidences and the counterpart acts on those incidences, the signed mean of each of v_B, v_j and v_memory is zero separately in each fixed b sector. R, prepared neutrally and written from z, transforms as r→r under this counterpart.

This is a more structured version of the necessary-plenitude idea: cancellation can occur without erasing either local contribution and without pairing distinct b labels. But the same mirror balance survives all three competing bridges. Further, the counterpart has not been established as a universal physical mirror or as a map between distinct actual subjects rather than descriptions, and the measure is not derived from actual incidence. Other contributions and weights can exist. Negative felt valence, if present, is not made unreal by a positive contribution elsewhere.

## 6. Consequence for the generative programme

The constructive distinction is now sharper:

- **Persistence:** Π_b is a genuine conserved sector of this episode, but calling it an experiencing carrier is a conditional selector.
- **Quality selection:** a precise stability-plus-calibration rule selects v_B on the reachable labels of a specified small algebra, but neither carrier persistence nor whole-zero forces that rule.
- **Boundary and memory:** an explicitly gauge- and counterpart-compatible memory observable survives those same conditions and produces a different signed assignment. The framework must determine which relations are intrinsic to a subject and how memory enters, rather than merely decide which sign is conserved.

The next substantive construction would derive the candidate boundary and admissible state/interaction algebra from a dynamical subject criterion, then test it under record-writing, splitting, merging and memory transport—not simply append a new preferred reference or declare the memory rival impossible. This respects the demand for a self-sufficient constitution while identifying the precise extra criterion on which this finite model still depends.
