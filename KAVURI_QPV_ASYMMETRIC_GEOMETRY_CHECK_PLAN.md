# Plan: independent asymmetric QPV geometry check

This retrospective plan follows the already-read paper, its reported ratios and the prior symmetric toy; it is not a blind or preregistered replication.

1. Use arXiv v2 supplement IV's reported verifier separation and four timing events (A/B challenge release and A/B accepted-response times) to compute the sphere radii and ellipsoid major axes, with vacuum c. Verify the mapping of cross-path times against the source's explicit region definitions. Use only rounded reported central inputs; do not use the discrepant prover arrival times or A/B prover distance as inputs.
2. Implement the article's stationary spatial projection: quantum locus is the intersection of two balls and two same-focus ellipsoids; classical locus is the union of the two specified sphere–ellipsoid intersections. By rotational symmetry independently compute axial lengths, projected areas and spatial volumes via cross-section integration. Avoid summing classical subregions without accounting for overlap, and handle disconnected axial intervals. Compute ideal classical line-length comparison separately.
3. Check numerical integration and domain boundaries, synthetic symmetric case against exact prior formulas, finite off-axis membership, and one-dimensional set unions. Compare computed central ratios against the authors' reported 4.48, 4.02, 4.53 and 2.47 but do not fit to them. Check sensitivity to the *rounding* of displayed timing and distance values, without inventing a joint measurement covariance or claiming to reproduce the authors' standard uncertainties.
4. **Post-result diagnostic:** the first calculation gave a same-latency 1D ratio of about 3.47 rather than 4.48. Before drawing conclusions, independently check the explicit axial interval union and its overlap, an upper bound from the larger ellipsoid, the effect of adding the overlapping quantum interval twice as an illustrative error, source definitions, and reasonable rounding stress tests. Label double-counting a hypothesis only, not an established explanation of the authors' number.
5. Clearly distinguish geometry from experimentally verified timestamps, Bell-test data, cryptographic soundness and genuine 4D spacetime volume; avoid projecting a physical-location estimate to human identity or experience. Document dependencies, outputs, source, limitations, focused verification and regression tests; update prior reports, refresh inventories and push the active branch.
