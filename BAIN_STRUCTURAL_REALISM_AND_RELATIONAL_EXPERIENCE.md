## The article and its conclusion

Jonathan Bain's [“There are no particles or fields, only structure”](https://iai.tv/articles/there-are-no-particles-or-fields-only-structure-auid-3243?_auid=2020) is relevant to a world described in terms of relations rather than self-contained material things. Its title is more categorical than its conclusion. Bain ultimately gives a **qualified yes** to “is particle physics about particles?”: either revise the classical particle concept, or regard classical-like particles as effective phenomena in the relevant regimes. This is not a denial that laboratory detections occur or that particle concepts have practical value.

The accessible page returned the article's full prose through its conclusion, despite displaying a subscription prompt; the discussion and linked primary technical proofs were not independently verified in full. The following assessment distinguishes the author's argument from the broader philosophical position we might adopt.

## What the physics does—and does not—say

A classical particle picture imagines persistently individuated objects, each with definite location and countable at all times. In nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, identical-particle permutation symmetry and delocalized states challenge that picture, although the appropriate metaphysical interpretation is debated. Bose and Fermi statistics have observable consequences: saying identical particles are indistinguishable does **not** mean their occupation or collective effects are inconsequential.

Bain argues that the demand for exact local and total number operators encounters obstacles in realistic relativistic quantum field theories (RQFTs), and cites Reeh–Schlieder and Haag. Those are sophisticated conditional theorems; neither should be turned into an unconditional proof that no kind of particle, observable or field exists. In particular, Haag's theorem concerns assumptions about equivalence of certain free/interacting field representations, not a simple proof that one can never count detector clicks or define useful asymptotic particle quantities. The Reeh–Schlieder theorem concerns the vacuum and local operator algebras under stated assumptions, not a demonstration of faster-than-light signalling. Exact hypotheses and the physical reading matter; see the [Stanford Encyclopedia's QFT discussion of localization](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/quantum-field-theory/#NonLocaTheo) for competing interpretations. We have read selected sections, not independently checked each theorem's proof.

Quantum fields, likewise, are not generally ordinary classical values at sharply defined points. Operator-valued distributions, smearing, states and observable algebras supply frameworks for their precise treatment. None of this straightforwardly proves that every field ontology is false. Representations, approximate models, regimes, scattering and detectors must be distinguished from one another.

Effective concepts can be physically significant without being fundamental. Bain points to David Wallace's [“Emergence of particles from bosonic quantum field theory,” arXiv:quant-ph/0112149](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0112149); **only the abstract was examined**. It specifies linear, massive bosonic field theories, not an all-purpose theorem about every QFT. Jonathan Bain's [“Against Particle/Field Duality: Asymptotic Particle States and Interpolating Fields in Interacting QFT (Or: Who's Afraid of Haag's Theorem?),” DOI 10.1023/A:1026482100470](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1026482100470) argues that the LSZ approach supports a qualified notion of localized particles; only its abstract was accessible, not its full subscription article. James D. Fraser's [“Towards a Realist View of Quantum Field Theory”](https://philsci-archive.pitt.edu/14716/) develops a renormalization-group-oriented option; **only its preprint record and abstract were examined**, not the linked full text. These examples caution against treating either particles or their absence as a settled metaphysical verdict.

## Where it fits the proposed whole

The most useful synthesis is **relational realism with real effective structures**:

1. Physical reality need not be a collection of intrinsically self-sufficient, individually labelled objects. Relations, symmetries, state, interaction and the physically appropriate observables can be central.
2. This need not make emergent particles, brains or observers unreal. A pattern can have genuine explanatory and causal importance in the regime where it is stable, even if it is not fundamental. An effective description should say which states, observables and interventions it preserves and where its approximation fails.
3. A formal structure by itself is not automatically a concrete, self-sufficient actuality. Nor does saying its intrinsic nature is experiential follow from QFT. Those are further metaphysical theses. An experiential reading of structure can be proposed without reducing experience to a second substance, but must explain the association of particular qualities and subjects with particular organizations.
4. QFT normally presupposes a spacetime framework. Its local algebras are useful constraints on a regime of the account, not a derivation of a timeless, spacetime-free ground or of emergent spacetime.
5. No step here requires a single universal subject. Genuine plurality requires a defensible constitution rule for distinct perspectives, not simply the absence of classical particles.

A relevant existing debate is [ontic versus epistemic structural realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/structural-realism/): roughly, whether reality itself is fundamentally structural, versus whether structure is what theory warrants us in knowing. Both variants face further interpretive objections. Our experiential-ground thesis adds a claim that neither follows from nor is required by structural realism. These are established debates; applying them to the project's account is not a new derivation of physics.

## A concrete test of what a relational description must retain

A useful warning against identifying the whole with a list of its parts' individual states follows from the familiar Bell states

```text
|Φ+⟩ = (|00⟩ + |11⟩)/√2
|Φ−⟩ = (|00⟩ − |11⟩)/√2.
```

Each qubit, considered alone, has the same reduced state `I/2` in both cases. Consequently every one-qubit measurement has the same distribution in the two cases; their joint `Z⊗Z` statistics also agree. Nevertheless the joint observable `X⊗X` has expectation `+1` for `|Φ+⟩` and `−1` for `|Φ−⟩`. So the two global quantum states are physically distinguishable if that joint measurement is available: their **local marginals are not their complete relational structure**.

This is a textbook-type finite-dimensional illustration, not an interacting QFT theorem and not a proof of experience or of a subject boundary. Its direct application to our framework is that any proposed subjective constitution rule must specify which joint/counterfactual relations it uses, not just attach experiences separately to reduced states. It must also say how its inputs are operationally accessible and distinguish physically redundant redescription from an actual change of state. The example is not an argument that every entangled system is conscious or that every joint observable defines one subject.

## The next obligation

A candidate account of subjecthood should give a rule that (a) respects relevant physical equivalences, (b) distinguishes organization under independently specified interventions, (c) does not infer joint experience from entanglement alone, and (d) is calibrated against independently elicited reports without presupposing that all absence of report is absence of experience. Comparing its predictions against rival boundaries and held-out data is an empirical requirement; specifying how a structural state is identical to a particular experience remains a further philosophical requirement. See [dual-aspect synthesis](DUAL_ASPECT_REALISM_AND_SOURCE_RECOVERY.md) and [subject-constitution interface](SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION_INTERFACE_RESULTS.md).

Neither Bain's argument nor this finite example establishes that the whole must be self-sufficient, that all genuine possibilities are actual within it, or that physical structure has experiential intrinsic nature. They do show why a relational proposal is a serious interpretive option and why an adequate version needs more than the slogan “there are only relations.”
