# A concrete test: extrastriate cortex, frontal consumers, and IFG stimulation

## Decision

**Do not treat the existing left-IFG cTBS experiment as a successful maintained-disclosure test.** It is useful precisely because its measured effects expose both sides of the difficulty: a frontal intervention can alter posterior readout, while task-related information remains decodable in other frontal regions. The study did not establish all three of P3, P4 and P6 in the same participants and condition. This is a study-specific assessment, not a no-go theorem for every experiment or evidence for either consciousness hypothesis. The global motivation and frozen hypotheses are in [the decision plan](GLOBAL_COHERENCE_AND_DECISION_PLAN.md) and [maintained-disclosure candidate](MAINTAINED_DISCLOSURE_CANDIDATE.md).

## 1. Concrete candidate and what was actually done

The candidate source **C** is the predeclared extrastriate face/scene representation over a specified stimulus-and-maintenance interval. Candidate consumers **D** include bilateral precentral sulcus/inferior frontal junction (IFJ), where a goal-relevance code may be available. The intervention is continuous theta-burst stimulation (cTBS) of left inferior frontal gyrus (IFG), against stimulation of left postcentral gyrus. Goal relevance is not the same variable as stimulus identity or phenomenal visual content: it is the conjunction of stimulus category with the current task set. These anatomical ROIs and variables are operational proxies, not an asserted natural boundary between experience and access.

[Lorenc, Lee, Chen and D’Esposito, 2015](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsys.2015.00169) reanalyzed data from Lee and D’Esposito (2012). In separate sessions participants received 40 seconds of cTBS immediately before scanning, then saw 600-ms face/scene presentations and performed a 1-back matching task; they gave a response on each image. Category classification used BOLD at 5–6 seconds after stimulus onset, goal relevance at 6–7 seconds. These delayed, hemodynamically blurred samples are not direct time-resolved observations of the neural or phenomenal state throughout the maintenance interval. The paper describes nine participants with TMS sessions, but its permutation-method description says eight were averaged for the condition contrast; the reason is not clear from the passages inspected. We do not substitute an assumed sample size for reanalysis. The independently acquired functional-ROI dataset comprised 24 people, nine of whom later participated in the TMS experiment: it was separate data, not a wholly non-overlapping group of people.

The 2015 analysis trained and cross-validated classifiers within TMS conditions, rather than deploying the same independently frozen classifier in both arms. The left visual functional ROI category accuracy was 63% after control versus 59% after IFG cTBS; the reported condition contrast was **p = .01 uncorrected**, not established as Bonferroni-corrected in that ROI. The right functional ROI was 63% versus 60%, contrast p = .08 uncorrected. An exploratory whole-brain searchlight also reported cluster-corrected decreases in several occipital/parietal regions. These are observations about decoding, not direct equality or inequality tests for the full neural state. They caution against assuming that a frontal manipulation leaves C intact; they do not by themselves establish a specified P3-equivalence failure.

Importantly, the study reports goal-relevance decoding above chance in **both left and right IFJ ROIs after IFG stimulation** (each p ≤ .004 corrected). Its bilateral IFJ condition contrast was p = .01 uncorrected, while separate left/right contrasts were p = .07/.05 uncorrected. Above-chance goal-relevance decoding is evidence of a remaining statistical code in both regions, not proof that C causally sent that code to them or that their codes supported experience. It does make a simple claim that the manipulation erased all goal-relevance information from both measured IFJ consumers inconsistent with the authors' reported group-level evidence. It does not prove that every relevant individual retained a code, nor establish a prespecified quantitative residual bound without reanalysis.

## 2. A sharp measurement constraint

These are direct applications of total-variation/data-processing facts, not a claim to have invented them; see, for example, [binary hypothesis testing lecture notes](https://home.ttic.edu/~madhurt/courses/infotheory2021/l8.pdf) for the standard relationship between total variation and optimal testing. We state the special case because it changes what this particular experiment can establish.

Let `X` be a binary category, `Z` be control (0) or intervention (1), and `C` a fixed measured feature vector; let `P_z^x = law(C | X=x, Z=z)`. With balanced category priors and **the same independently frozen classifier** `f` in both conditions, its population accuracies satisfy

```
|a_1(f) - a_0(f)| ≤ [TV(P_1^0,P_0^0) + TV(P_1^1,P_0^1)]/2.
```

This follows by applying the event form of TV to each class's correctness event. Thus if each conditional TV is at most ε, a reliably established accuracy difference greater than ε rules out that particular measured-distribution equivalence. It does not prove that either of the whole physical states or experiences differs. A classifier must be frozen independently of the held-out test data; for a randomized decoder, the same fixed randomization kernel must be used in both conditions. With equal class priors, Bayes-optimal accuracies also obey this bound, by the triangle inequality for TV. Estimates of separately trained classifiers are not necessarily Bayes-optimal, so their difference cannot be inserted into this inequality.

The converse also fails: equal accuracy of a frozen decoder does **not** imply that its input distribution is preserved. A separate feature may change completely while that decoder ignores it. The executable [synthetic check](verification/specific_pathway_observability.py) demonstrates this and the separate-classifier problem. Its 63%/59% example is deliberately artificial and is **not** a numerical reanalysis of the 2015 data. No 4-percentage-point TV bound is inferred from the paper.

There is a complementary consumer-side witness. For any frozen binary decoder of a measured consumer variable `Y`, with balanced binary label `X` and population accuracy `a ≥ 1/2`,

```
TV(law(Y | X=0), law(Y | X=1)) ≥ 2a - 1.
```

This is a **lower bound** on a statistical difference between conditional consumer distributions. A positive lower confidence bound for `a - 1/2` therefore supplies a lower bound on residual decodable information in that measurement. It does not identify the causal route from C to Y, establish phenomenal content, or automatically translate to the different goal-relevance and category labels in the paper. The reported significance tests alone do not provide the numerical bound required for a prespecified P4 criterion. Conversely, failing to decode is not evidence of absence without an upper confidence/equivalence bound on an appropriately specified measure.

## 3. Gate-by-gate assessment of this candidate

| Gate | What would need to be shown for this specific case | What the existing study establishes |
|---|---|---|
| P3: maintain C | Predeclared stimulus- and time-specific preservation margins for the candidate local mechanism, including feedback and measurement fidelity, during the relevant interval. Testing a fixed observed feature vector is only a restricted proxy. | Posterior decoding changed in analyses described above; P3 was not tested for equivalence and full neural preservation was not established. |
| P4: remove the specified distribution | Establish absence of the **specified** additional distribution and equivalent distributed organizations, including pertinent prior records and alternate routes. A bounded source-to-consumer causal influence is an important subtest, not the whole gate; see [boundary-cut and record audit](DISTRIBUTION_CUT_AND_RECORD_PERSISTENCE.md). | No direct causal severance estimate. Goal relevance remains decodable in bilateral IFJ after left IFG cTBS. That is evidence against total erasure of that statistical code, not a direct test of the C→IFJ channel. Right IFG and other regions also remain possible consumers. |
| P6: valid experience observation | A preregistered, intervention-valid assay for the target phenomenology, with bounded classification error and explicit hypotheses linking observations to experience, on the same intervention trials. | Matching responses measure task performance; they are not an independently validated test of preserved subjective visual experience. The study did not provide the no-report/calibration design required here. |

Even passing an observational C-feature equivalence test, residual-consumer test and calibrated experience proxy would not by itself establish every hidden physical degree of freedom, a universal consciousness or the metaphysics of experience. Their relation to H_M1/H_D1 requires the assumptions and distinctions in the [identifiability audit](DISCLOSURE_OBSERVATION_IDENTIFIABILITY.md). Rejecting this candidate experiment as a demonstration is not the same as refuting either hypothesis.

## 4. Next experiment: feasibility before a verdict

A human follow-up could, in principle, combine a randomized within-participant crossover (sham and active-site controls, counterbalanced order), independently localized extrastriate and IFJ measurements, task and gaze measures, and delayed experience reports in both stimulation arms. It must freeze the target interval, feature map, decoder, consumer set and tolerances **before** observing the test data. It must separately address cTBS effect duration and individual variability. Eye and delayed-report calibration under intervention remains a substantive assumption, not something guaranteed by adding those measures; see [calibration audit](INTERVENTION_CONSCIOUSNESS_CALIBRATION.md).

However, ordinary offline left-IFG cTBS plus fMRI cannot by itself certify selectively removing C→D influence or preserve the requisite fast local feedback. fMRI is indirect and temporally coarse, and stimulation may perturb source representations, receivers, feedback, and alternative paths. A more decisive protocol would require an independently validated temporally resolved perturbation of the source or its pathway, concurrent recordings at prespecified consumers, and a design distinguishing direct, mediated and common-input effects, along with an intervention-valid experiential assay. Intracranial approaches can provide relevant resolution but are constrained by clinical indication, coverage and safety; for an example of human intracranial face-network measurements and stimulation see [Kadipasaoglu et al.](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0188834). That study is not a ready-made three-gate experiment. Whether a feasible and ethical protocol can validate all gates in the **same participants and trial condition** is still open.

For inference, the target is not a nonsignificant intervention contrast: specify two one-sided equivalence criteria or a justified joint confidence region for restricted P3 observables, and an upper confidence bound on a predeclared causal P4 effect. Separately establish and transport P6 assay error. Preserve participant clustering, trial dependence, multiplicity across features, ROIs and gates, and any uncertainty from independent classifier training. Balance or explicitly standardize label distributions across arms; separate generalization to new participants from within-participant trial inference. Joint passage requires simultaneous coverage or an appropriate intersection-union design, not three uncorrected favorable results. A lower bound from one consumer decoder can refute a proposed *statistical-code* absence threshold, but it cannot by itself establish or rule out a directional causal-channel bound.

**Dependency status:** this yields a tractable early rejection and design filter for M4; it does not close the experience/constitution bridge, determine whether local subjects combine, or solve the user's larger physical/metaphysical endpoint. The [consumer-coverage follow-up](CONSUMER_COVERAGE_IDENTIFIABILITY.md) makes the P4 missing-channel problem exact and states a sufficient coverage bound. The separate field-instrument dependencies L3 and L4 are unchanged.
