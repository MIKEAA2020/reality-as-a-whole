## The direct answer to “what next?”

The next move is to reverse the previous argument. Instead of starting with four-dimensional relativity and finding a sphere of lightlike directions, **start with the sphere's intrinsic projective structure and ask what spacetime structure it can produce**.

There is a precise and useful construction: if the internal sphere has the structure of the complex projective line `CP¹`, it can be represented as the rays of a two-complex-dimensional vector space. From that same vector space, the real four-dimensional space of Hermitian forms carries a natural Lorentz-signature determinant. The sphere becomes its projective null cone.

This advances the idea in a definite way: **given the projective structure, a relativistic causal cone is constructed without first assuming a spacetime metric**. But the projective structure and its spinor lift are inputs. The construction is standard mathematics, not a proof that zero itself entails those inputs or that the constructed vector space is actual spacetime. Its connection to experience remains open.

## 1. The sphere as a space of relations

Let the proposed internal sphere be identified with

```text

```

A point of this sphere is a complex line, or ray, represented by a nonzero pair `λ=(λ₀,λ₁)`. Multiplying the pair by a nonzero complex number does not change the projective direction. `CP¹` is mathematically a two-sphere equipped with complex/projective structure; a bare topological sphere or a dimensionless point does not by itself specify this whole construction.

This is well suited to the user's idea of internal differentiation without a literal sphere expanding into outside space. It provides a relation-based sphere, with no preferred point or preferred scale built into its projective labels. It remains a hypothesis that the real zero whole has this structure and that its rays are actual aspects rather than mathematical descriptions.

## 2. Construct the four-dimensional causal form

Take the real vector space of Hermitian `2×2` matrices associated with the complex two-dimensional lift. Each such matrix can be written

```text
X = [ t+z    x−iy ]
    [ x+iy   t−z ],            t,x,y,z ∈ R.
```

Its determinant is

```text
det X = t² − x² − y² − z².
```

So the determinant has Lorentzian signature. The symbols `t,x,y,z` label four real components of this constructed vector space; they are not yet observed spacetime coordinates, a fundamental time, or a physical universe.

The causal cone is obtained from the same structure. For any nonzero spinor `λ`,

```text
X_λ = λ λ†,          det X_λ = 0.
```

It is positive semidefinite and nonzero. If `λ→cλ`, then `X_λ→|c|² X_λ`: changing the projective representative rescales the matrix but does not change its null ray. Conversely, every nonzero positive-semidefinite rank-one Hermitian `2×2` matrix has the form `λλ†`, with its spinor determined up to phase. Therefore

```text
internal projective sphere CP¹  ↔  future-oriented null rays of Herm(2).
```

Choosing the positive-semidefinite component fixes a cone orientation for the mathematical construction; it is not yet a derivation of a physical arrow of time. The zero matrix itself is not a projective spinor. To retain the sphere over the collapsed zero, we still need the point–sphere/blow-up representation; the plain zero matrix alone loses its directions.

The Hermitian-matrix representation and its Lorentz relation are standard; see Oblak's lecture notes. [2](https://arxiv.org/html/1508.00920)

## 3. Why no preferred external frame is required

For a complex invertible matrix `A`, the transformation

```text
X ↦ A X A†
```

preserves the Hermitian space and the causal cone, while

```text
det(A X A†) = |det A|² det X.
```

For `det A=1`, it preserves the determinant exactly and produces a proper orthochronous Lorentz transformation; `A` and `−A` give the same transformation. More generally, the projective transformations of `CP¹` correspond to the proper Lorentz group, while a change of lift by `A→cA` rescales `X` by the positive common factor `|c|²`. The conformal causal structure therefore does not select a preferred inertial frame or an absolute metric scale.

The exact group correspondence is `PSL(2,C) ≅ SO⁺(1,3)`. This is an isomorphism of mathematical symmetry groups, not a claim that an actual physical Lorentz symmetry has been derived from a dimensionless point. The complex/projective sphere, the cone orientation and the lift are substantive ingredients. Neither the physical units of scale nor an actual clock are supplied by the group identity.

## 5. A useful limitation: directions do not uniquely select a history

A positive definite Hermitian matrix can be written as a sum of two positive null matrices, but the decomposition is not generally unique. For example,

```text
I = (1,0)(1,0)† + (0,1)(0,1)†
  = (1,1)(1,1)†/2 + (1,−1)(1,−1)†/2.
```

The two pairs correspond to different projective directions but the same matrix `I`. This does not erase real differences if they are separately physically or experientially meaningful; it shows that a matrix alone does not tell us which decomposition, if any, represents actual distinct incidences. A subject cannot be identified with each ray simply because the representation contains it.

Likewise, the constructed local cone says which infinitesimal directions are null once interpreted physically. It does not specify which events are actual, how different local cones are connected, physical matter, field equations, the metric scale, quantum state or the experiential valence and unity of subjects.
