# Confined facts, richer relations and a conditional experiential-zero theorem

**Four-connection continuation:** [confinement, coherence and experience](ZERO_FOUR_CONNECTIONS_CONFINEMENT_COHERENCE_EXPERIENCE.md) extends the two endpoint states to a complete phase family, checks an actual dephased-mixture control and specifies competing hypotheses about richer experiential distinctions. No quantum subject or physical higher plane is inferred.

## What this step does

The preceding [realm-order construction](ZERO_REALM_RESTRICTION_WITHOUT_INTERACTION.md) formalized the user's correction: a game position does not need an interaction to stay fixed while a player thinks. Here we connect that distinction to the **existing physical sphere–record system**, without imposing a holding interaction or treating thinking as an unseen game move.

The result has two parts:

1. We exhibit two distinct global quantum states of the same specified five-ray system. Their entire proposed confined subject-subsystem state and their sphere/record probabilities agree; yet a richer record–current correlation has opposite sharp signs. A restricted description therefore does not determine all relations in this particular larger system. This is a structural comparison, **not a proof that actual subjects or metaphysical planes are quantum subsystems**.
2. On that same sharp-current sector, a stronger pairwise zero hypothesis has a definite consequence. If the two candidate subjects have positive **fixed** incidence weights, use the same signed response to current, and their pair balances for both opposite current orientations, then every nontrivial response must be odd and their weights must be equal. These features are *derived jointly from the stated conditions*, rather than inserted separately.

The conclusion is conditional: a whole-level zero need not imply that this pair balances in every branch, and subjecthood, felt response, incidence, outcome and the relation of this model to a real universe remain to be explained. The construction preserves these questions rather than treating the zero of a convenient ledger as the demanded no-residue account.

## 1. A restricted realm and a richer physical description

Use the state and factorization of the [sphere–record–mismatch comparison](ZERO_SUBJECT_EXPERIENCE_BRIDGE_COMMON_DOMAIN_TEST.md). It extends the earlier asymmetric five-ray system by two logical reference qubits. The stipulated subject-selection rule picks

```text
S₁={H₁,R₁},             S₂={R₂,H₂},
```

with `H₁,H₂` reference blocks and `R₁,R₂` record blocks. The selected pairs are **candidates**, not established experiencing subjects. The four-block subsystem `S=S₁∪S₂` has a full reduced density matrix `ρ_S`; its algebra of observables is one mathematically explicit restriction. The richer description contains the record blocks **and** the four memory/work blocks carrying the current. It is not a claim that the physical universe literally has a surrounding room or that a person's access is exactly the algebra of S.

Let the original five-ray state, with the two references initialized as before, be `|Ψ₊⟩`. Define a second static state

```text
|Ψ₋⟩ = Z_M₁ |Ψ₊⟩,
```

where `Z_M₁` acts on the first work-memory block, outside S. The unitary is used here to **specify and compare** two states. We need not postulate that it actually occurs between them, nor that any interaction keeps S fixed. The earlier report separately studied an optional phase pulse, including its energy limitation.

The finite verification establishes:

```text
⟨Ψ₊|Ψ₋⟩ = 0,
ρ_S(Ψ₊) = ρ_S(Ψ₋),
P₊(i,record) = P₋(i,record).
```

Thus the full vectors are orthogonal, not merely differently written global phases, while **every observable restricted to S** has the same expectation and distribution in both states. The structural Hamiltonian and its L-selection input are held fixed; the two candidate pairs remain the same. The physical spin-zero encoding, initial state, S factorization and selection rule are specified inputs, not consequences of zero.

Let `r=+1` for record `01` and `r=−1` for record `10`. In every nonzero sphere-label/record component the two original memory currents are sharp:

| State | Record `01`: `(J₁,J₂)` | Record `10`: `(J₁,J₂)` |
|---|---|---|
| `Ψ₊` | `(+1,−1)` | `(−1,+1)` |

The current pair adds to zero in every such component in **both** states. The confined subsystem, its mismatch projectors, and all of its statistics agree; the current relations reverse. This is an exact state-dependent quantum result for the encoded model. It is not a temporal claim that either quantum state occurs while the other is paused, and the two record alternatives are not successive A and B positions.

A particularly concise witness is the joint record–current observable

```text
K = Z_R₁ J₁.
```

Its value is sharp `+1` on `Ψ₊` and `−1` on `Ψ₋`. By contrast, the unconditional mean of `J₁` alone is zero in both states: detecting the distinction requires the **joint correlation**, not merely observing an average current. The complete Bob-side density matrices differ between `Ψ₊` and `Ψ₋`; hence K is a distinguishable relation in that larger fixed laboratory description. If we changed the state *and* transported every observable by the same representation change, that would not by itself be a physical distinction; here K and the apparatus identification are kept fixed.

The user's confinement point remains intact: equal restricted states do not mean that the work-sector differences are surface positions, game moves or necessarily accessible to the restricted inhabitants. Conversely, the presence of a distinguishable richer correlation does not prove it belongs to a fundamentally different plane in reality. An algebraic subsystem is an explicit **analogue** of the realm restriction; its physical and experiential interpretation needs argument.

## 3. Whole-level zero does not automatically mean pairwise zero

For the experiential proposal, the relevant question is not merely whether the physical currents oppose. Suppose, as a candidate bridge, that for two actual candidate-subject incidences the signed contribution assigned to a sharp current `j=±1` is `f(j)`. Put

```text
f₊=f(+1),     f₋=f(−1),
```

and give the two named subjects positive incidence weights `μ₁,μ₂`. Assume **the same f** applies to each and the two weights are fixed when their current signs reverse. These are substantive hypotheses, not results about the physical qubits. They do not specify that negative value is absence: if f is truly felt valence, a negative contribution represents actual negative experience.

For the two opposite orientations, the proposed pair totals are

```text
C₊ = μ₁ f₊ + μ₂ f₋,
C₋ = μ₁ f₋ + μ₂ f₊.
```

Physical current opposition alone does not say either equals zero. Nor does the user's proposed **whole-level** zero automatically imply that this one selected pair balances in each orientation: other incidences or contributions could matter. A global sum of `−1−1+2=0` illustrates the logical difference, not evidence that a particular compensating experience exists. Any actual compensation would itself need to be explained; it would not erase either negative experience.

The stronger, explicitly conditional hypothesis investigated next is:

> **Pairwise branch balance:** both C₊ and C₋ equal zero, with fixed positive weights and the same response law f.

This may be a candidate consequence of a deeper constitution, but it is not silently substituted for global zero. If it is demanded only for *actually* occurring branches, the model must also justify why both orientations are actual. Nonzero Born weight by itself does not establish that. Alternatively, one can state it as a universal candidate law required to hold for both allowed sharp orientations; that does not settle their actuality.

## 4. The conditional theorem: nontrivial zero forces oddness and equal incidence

Under the preceding assumptions, pairwise balance requires

```text
[ μ₁  μ₂ ] [ f₊ ] = [0]
[ μ₂  μ₁ ] [ f₋ ]   [0].
```

If `μ₁≠μ₂`, the determinant `μ₁²−μ₂²` is nonzero, so the only solution is `f₊=f₋=0`. If `μ₁=μ₂>0`, the equations reduce to `f₊+f₋=0`. Therefore:

> **With positive fixed incidence, a common response and pairwise balance for both orientations, either both designated contributions vanish, or the weights are equal and the response is odd on the two sharp values.**

Equivalently, requiring a **nonzero** designated response makes

```text
μ₁=μ₂,          f(−1)=−f(+1)≠0
```

necessary and sufficient for those two pair totals to vanish. This is stronger than assuming oddness first and then discovering that equal weights are needed: here the two requirements follow **together** from the common law, fixed weights, nontriviality and both balance equations.

The proof does not select the sign of `f(+1)` or its magnitude: every nonzero κ in `(f₊,f₋)=(κ,−κ)` works, as does the opposite orientation. Nor does it establish a common cardinal scale, actual experience, or equality of weights in nature. If the deeper account derives those inputs, the theorem tells it what they must satisfy; it does not supply that derivation.

### Two ways the inference can fail

1. **Average versus branch balance.** The original two record signs have equal Born weights. If `f₋=−f₊` but `μ₁≠μ₂`, then `C₊=−C₋≠0`: their weighted average is zero, though neither branch balances. An ensemble zero cannot be silently upgraded into an eventwise zero.
2. **State-dependent incidence.** Let `f₊=1`, `f₋=−2`. Use weights `(μ₁,μ₂)=(2,1)` in the first orientation and `(1,2)` in the other. Both pair totals equal zero although `f` is not odd and the within-branch weights are unequal. This explicitly violates the theorem's **fixed-weight** premise, not its logic. Whether such a sign-dependent incidence rule is justified, physically local, or capable of defining a coherent whole-level measure is a further question.

The theorem is therefore a genuine constraint on a specified candidate, not a universal theorem that zero *must* make pleasure and suffering equal or that every pair of subjects balances. A common subjective unit and a defensible domain must be established independently before any numerical experiential sum is asserted.

## 5. No-signalling, time and empirical status

For either phase state, the earlier pair and triple Alice instruments yield the same **complete Bob-side** reduced density matrix. Applying the same fixed phase convention on Bob's side preserves that equality. So neither confined measurements nor the richer K measurement can reveal Alice's instrument choice in this stipulated model. This is an operational no-signalling result, not proof of spatial locality, relativistic dynamics, or no-signalling under arbitrary experience-dependent weighting.

The static comparison presupposes no external timeline. The existing quantum preparation can be modeled with an internal clock, but the [prior clock construction](ZERO_SHARP_RECIPROCAL_CURRENTS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_RESPONSE.md) distinguishes the sharp conditioned **probe** from the full clock-Hamiltonian current, which is generally not sharp. The theorem above applies to the specified standalone sharp-current sector; extending it to full clock currents requires separate work. No preferred foliation follows from this finite model.

The earlier [pain–relief gate](ZERO_SUBJECT_EXPERIENCE_BRIDGE_COMMON_DOMAIN_TEST.md) remains the appropriate empirical challenge: independently identify the proposed physical signal and subject/episode boundary; separately measure unpleasantness, positive relief, stimulus and history; predefine the reporting model and test rivals. Existing pain–relief studies do not measure these encoded currents, establish the weights μ, or test this theorem as an experiential identity. No new human experiment or phenomenological measurement is reported here.

## 6. What has advanced—and what must be derived

The construction now connects the user's realm insight to one **actual previously specified sphere/record state** rather than merely a generic diagram. The restricted candidate-subsystem state fails to determine a real mathematical correlation in the richer quantum model; no holding mechanism is used. The pairwise-balance theorem shows exactly how one kind of nonzero positive–negative relation and equal incidence would follow if the specified zero conditions hold.

The remaining constitutive target is sharper: derive from the whole's own self-sufficient zero why there are these structures, why a pair is an actual subject pair, why the response has this character and incidence, and why pairwise rather than only whole-level balance applies. We must not assume that a finite model's distinct quantum alternatives are genuinely possible or selected actualities, or deny them merely by fiat. The user's stronger necessity/plenitude thesis remains to determine their genuine status and relation. This result neither establishes nor rules out that thesis.
