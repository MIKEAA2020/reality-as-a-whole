# Three candidate constitution rules: a constructive test and a verdict

## Executive verdict

The central problem is not a lack of examples. It is the absence of an independently justified rule connecting experiential nature, physical organization, particular qualities and individual subjects. Here are **three explicit candidate rules**, applied to exactly the same small causal system. They differ in whether joining two subsystems produces one, two or three subjects. All three can be given identical public physical dynamics. A proposed report test separates two of them **only if** a strong experiential calibration and a corresponding physical readout law are added. That extra requirement is the central result, not a solved problem.

**Follow-up correction:** the later [positive-witness analysis](FIRST_PERSON_POSITIVE_WITNESS_AND_SUBJECT_CENSUS_LIMIT.md) shows that a one-sided test of this L package need not establish a false-negative rate or complete negative calibration across subjects. It still requires a defensible positive-witness interpretation and domain coverage; M versus N remains unresolved. A further [real-case and boundary test](REAL_CASE_WITNESS_AND_MAXIMAL_RULE_BOUNDARY_TEST.md) distinguishes what DDC's actual reports support and identifies a conditional conflict between an unrestricted, all-scale version of M and the distinct-subject premise for mutually connected people. The original finite M was not a universal theory.

**Recommendation:** keep the candidates as provisional research tools, but do not elevate any of them to the project's theory. The maximal rule makes the simplest structure-only choice, but has a severe all-or-nothing sensitivity and excludes nested perspectives by fiat. The marked-local rule buys persistence by adding privileged boundaries and excludes an integrated perspective even when the subsystems exchange information. The nested rule accommodates both but adds overlapping subjects and an individuation burden. None explains why any organization is experienced or why a particular quality feels as it does. The best immediate route to substantive progress is to investigate whether an *independent, transportable* first-person-to-physical calibration can discriminate a narrowly defined integrated-experience claim; absent that, the three subject counts remain underdetermined.

This is a conceptual construction and falsification plan, not a human experiment, empirical subject census, proposed clinical assay or proof that these descriptions are genuinely possible realities. The Boolean system and the candidate rules are invented for this comparison. The construction builds on, rather than supersedes, the [plural-ground constitution analysis](PLURAL_EXPERIENTIAL_GROUND_SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION.md), the [relational facet model](RELATIONAL_WHOLE_SUBJECT_FACET_CONSTITUTION.md) and the [private nested comparison](PRIVATE_NESTED_FIELDS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTENT_COMPARISON.md).

## 1. The goal and the shared commitments

We want a rule that could eventually take independently specified relational facts and say: **which experiential subjects exist, which contents belong together for each, how their characters are constituted, and what persists across change**. We also want genuine plurality without assuming that the whole of reality is one experiencer. A first-person case gives us reason to preserve actual experience, not a general license to count other subjects from public data.

All three candidates use the same deliberately finite, discrete-time causal sector below. They share a **substantive experiential identity postulate**: when their rule selects a support, an intrinsically first-person field is constituted there. It is not a theorem that feedback, a graph or an algorithm generates experience. We also tentatively postulate a state-to-content map: if a field contains node `a`, it includes a designated left-content type when `a=1`; similarly `c=1` represents a right-content type. A field containing both with `a=c=1` includes those types **together**. A `0` here just means the designated content is absent, not that all experience ceases. Whether such a map corresponds to any actual phenomenology is precisely unestablished.

The content labels are placeholders for *genuine experiential qualities*, not an explanation of how a bit becomes a felt quality. The map is an additional bridge shared by the rivals. Subject supports are not themselves experience tokens, and physical overlap does not entail that a token is shared. The nested candidate explicitly assumes distinct, private field occurrences even if two occurrences have the same content type. There is no assumption of a universal subject; nor is its absence proven outside this small sector.

## 2. One public causal sector

Take four binary nodes `a,b,c,d`. Their simultaneous update, with binary externally supplied interventions `u_a,...,u_d`, is:

```text
a' = b XOR (g AND d) XOR u_a      b' = a XOR u_b
c' = d XOR (g AND b) XOR u_c      d' = c XOR u_d
```

The coupling switch `g` is fixed within a condition. With `g=0` each pair forms a closed directed feedback component; with `g=1` directed cross-links `d→a` and `b→c` are added. An edge means that toggling its source while holding other current-node and intervention values fixed changes the destination's next state in at least one context. The checker enumerates all binary states and inputs; it finds exactly:

```text
g=0: a↔b, c↔d
g=1: a↔b, c↔d, d→a, b→c.
```

At `g=1` all four nodes form a single strongly connected component: every node has a directed path to every other. This is **graph reachability, not an established measure of neural or phenomenal integration**. The synchronous clock, interventions and node decomposition are stipulated for this toy; they are not assumed fundamental to a timeless reality. Its bits are not neurons or colors, and the chosen mechanism is not a proposed biological model.

For the second and third rules also provide two marked cores `{a,b}` and `{c,d}`. The markings are part of their input. They are extra individuating facts unless justified by a deeper non-arbitrary rule; in particular, the local rule is **not** a structure-only rule on the unmarked graph. Relabeling nodes while transporting the marks preserves all three outputs, but relabeling invariance does not establish that the marks are physically or phenomenally meaningful.

## 3. The three proposed constitution principles

These are explicit local rules for the two displayed conditions, not complete theories for arbitrary networks:

**M — maximal feedback field.** Each maximal strongly connected component is exactly one field; no proper nested field is selected. It uses only the stipulated graph, identifies fields extensionally with supports, and makes maximality/exclusion an additional phenomenal premise. For general networks singleton or trivial components would require further eligibility conditions; no such conditions are independently established here.

**L — marked local fields.** Each marked core is exactly one field, regardless of cross-coupling; no encompassing field is selected. This makes the marks and the local-only exclusion rule constitutive. Continuing cross-boundary interaction is allowed; it is not automatically shared experience. The marks are carried through coupling changes rather than chosen from the result by a hidden tie-breaker.

**N — private nested fields.** Each marked core is a distinct field; additionally, if both cores lie in one strongly connected component, that whole component constitutes a third field. The global and local occurrences are numerically distinct even where their physical supports overlap. It does not posit a global field when the cores are separate. Its conditional nesting rule, private occurrence identity and exact subject count are all additional premises, not consequences of the graph.

For a sector in which `a=c=1`, and *under the shared, assumed content map*:

| Physical condition | M: maximal | L: marked local | N: private nested |
|---|---|---|---|
| `g=0`: separate pairs | 2 fields: `{a,b}`, `{c,d}` | same 2 | same 2 |
| `g=1`: cross-coupled | 1 field: `{a,b,c,d}` | 2 fields: `{a,b}`, `{c,d}` | 3 fields: both local and the global field |
| `g=1`: at least one field has left **and** right contents together | yes | no | yes |

The final row depends on our content map, including its strong negative claim that the local fields do not experience the other designated content. A coupled local field might in a different theory represent both sides, so the graph by itself does not establish that negative. M and N agree on the existence of a global conjunction but differ about additional local fields; L disagrees on the global conjunction in this specific formulation.

M's one field after coupling does **not** establish whether either earlier subject survives, merges or ceases; that requires a separate persistence criterion. L and N preserve the *same marked supports*, but numerical persistence of a subject over time is likewise not entailed by support identity. A changing graph alone cannot settle fission or fusion of experiencers.

## 4. A serious failure test for each candidate

1. **M's discontinuity and exclusion.** For this binary example, adding the two links changes two fields into one. If extended to any nonzero connection, even arbitrarily weak or brief links could have the same topological effect. A rule that makes subject number jump solely on path existence would need a principled coupling scale and timescale, not an ad hoc threshold selected to fit cases. Strong connectivity alone would also count trivial components without an eligibility rule. M says nested local fields disappear because maximality says so, not because their absence has been observed.
2. **L's privileged boundary.** As coupling strengthens, L still insists on exactly the marked local fields and no global field. That is a risky global non-experience claim, not just a statement that local processes persist. On the same unmarked connected graph, different core markings can yield different subject assignments; an independently justified reason for the marking is necessary. A rule tied to anatomical labels alone would also have to handle reorganization and differently realized systems. If a validated global-conjunction assay were positive under the candidate's domain, the L package would fail.
3. **N's multiplication and privacy.** N makes both local and global fields when the graph joins. It fits an intuitive wish to retain both, but that wish is not evidence. It requires a rule distinguishing multiple overlapping realizations from arbitrary subdivisions and explaining why shared physical variables yield distinct, rather than shared, experience occurrences. Any added global-only positive report can be explained by M too; N would still need a genuinely identity-sensitive discriminator for its extra local subjects.

All three face a more fundamental **quality and incidence objection**. The same physical transition structure could be paired by stipulation with different qualitative identity maps or subject boundaries. The shared palette postulate does not explain why its `left` or `right` feels as it does, nor why joint content constitutes a first-person perspective. Anchoring one's own particular experience can constrain a local map, but not automatically determine unreported contents, other systems or a complete general mapping. These failures remain even if all finite arithmetic checks pass.

No candidate here derives self-existence, plenitude, emergent spacetime, physical law or the reality of an intrinsically experiential ground. Nor does assuming that ground derive any of these three subject rules. A successful constitution principle must be compatible with the project's seven criteria **jointly**; avoiding the subject problem by denying experience, or declaring a label to be an explanation, would not qualify.

## 5. What is—and is not—an empirical prediction

### The common-sector underdetermination result

In the construction above, the physical update and any specified public readout that is solely a function of its states and interventions are **identical under M, L and N**. The rules add different experiential interpretations but do not feed back into the equation. Hence no amount of public observation of *that same closed physical model*, even under every input sequence, identifies the subject assignment. This is a result of the stated construction, not a theorem that all conceivable subject theories are empirically indistinguishable or that all three metaphysical interpretations are possible.

Calling one rule “integrated” does not make it predict a different button press. To get behavioral predictions, add a **separate, physically specified readout law**. If the physical sector is truly complete and already fixes the readout, a new law demanding contradictory responses cannot simply be overlaid on it: at least one augmented package must change its physical output dynamics or reject that mapping. This cost is often hidden when experiential hypotheses are presented as if they were already empirically distinct.

### A conditional, genuinely discriminating assay target

Consider a future assay for the proposition `Q`: at least one subject has the two designated contents together in the prepared `g=1` condition. The combined M-plus-content package and N-plus-content package say `Q=1`; L-plus-content says `Q=0`. Imagine an **additional readout model** whose binary output correctly indicates Q with false-positive and false-negative probabilities at most 0.1 in each relevant condition. Suppose 30 independent, identically prepared trials, with those error bounds valid throughout, and decide `Q=1` if at least 16 outputs are positive.

Under these demanding assumptions, the worst-case false-positive probability is at most `3.66×10⁻⁹`, and the probability of a positive decision when `Q=1` is at least `0.999999964`. These are binomial calculations, not measured error rates, sample-size advice for an actual study or claims that 30 trials provide 30 independent people. The bound persists for independent trials with unequal probabilities within the stated bounds. Without independence or with calibration drift, these numbers need not apply.

This would contrast **L against M/N**, not distinguish M from N or directly count local subjects. Moreover, calibrated `Q=0` cases are particularly hard: lack of a report does not validate absence of a global experience. A positive report may demonstrate something about the reporting subject only if the task and inference are justified; coordination, memory, post-cue integration and response bias are alternative explanations. The assumed ≤10% error rates cannot be established merely by comparing one report with another. The readout law must be physically implemented and separately specified for each complete package; it is not contained in the graph or identity postulate.

### A research program with a stop condition—not a registered protocol

Before collecting data or declaring a discriminator, prepare a preregistered study with:

1. **Domain and manipulation:** specify real systems and a defensible, independently measured candidate of within/across-subsystem coupling; avoid equating a graph threshold with consciousness. Match stimulus strength, task difficulty, timing, awareness and report channels across conditions, and record possible indirect relays. No actual intervention is proposed here for patients or healthy volunteers.
2. **Explicit competing packages:** define in advance the content maps, subject exclusions, physical reporting dynamics and predictions of M-like, L-like, and N-like rivals in that domain. Include a rival allowing one partially unified subject and a rival in which global report is generated by post-cue integration. State what each model can predict when it has hidden states or nuisance parameters; do not give only one model flexibility.
3. **Independent calibration and controls:** use first-person positive co-presence cases to anchor the relevant conjunction reports where justified; test pre-cue versus post-cue response access, stimulus encoding, memory and response biases. The illustrative two-sided assay requires defensible sensitivity **and specificity** bounds, their uncertainty and transport across conditions; the [positive-witness follow-up](FIRST_PERSON_POSITIVE_WITNESS_AND_SUBJECT_CENSUS_LIMIT.md) gives a weaker one-sided alternative. A failure to report is not a negative-experience ground truth. Include negative controls and explicit practical equivalence margins.
4. **Inference and failure:** predefine participant-level sampling, repeated-measure dependence, missingness, decision thresholds, uncertainty intervals, held-out tests and competing outcome predictions. In the actual, non-ideal study, evaluate model likelihoods with calibration uncertainty rather than using the illustrative binomial number above. Positive Q would not, by itself, distinguish M from N. A distinct-subject assay would require an independently justified identity bridge, not simply two response channels.
5. **Revised stop condition:** for a one-sided positive witness against a no-conjunction claim, a false-negative calibration is not necessary. As the [follow-up](FIRST_PERSON_POSITIVE_WITNESS_AND_SUBJECT_CENSUS_LIMIT.md) explains, a credible positive witness or defensible target-specific false-positive bound and an adequate domain mapping are still required. If these cannot be justified, the proposed comparison remains **not presently identified**. A complete absence claim or a subject census requires additional coverage and sensitivity premises. If a warranted outcome contradicts a complete candidate package, reject or revise that package; the result need not uniquely identify which premise failed.

## 6. What would count as non-incremental progress?

A major advance would be an independently motivated principle that (i) fixes a non-arbitrary realization and incidence rule, including overlap and identity, (ii) explains or genuinely constrains qualitative character rather than naming it, (iii) gives distinct physical predictions through a separately defensible observation law, and (iv) survives a preregistered cross-context test or a rigorous argument that ties it uniquely to experience. Merely selecting M, L or N by simplicity would not meet that standard. Conversely, a demonstrated impossibility under well-motivated premises, or a robust independent calibration ruling out a serious complete candidate, would also be substantial progress.

**Current verdict:** these three rules turn the main gap into explicit rival commitments and a discriminating *conditional* target. They do not close it. The immediate bottleneck is not another colored afterimage or more finite graph enumeration; it is the jointly defensible **experiential bridge plus physical realization and observation law**. Until one is supplied, this framework is a research program rather than an established account of subject constitution.

## 7. Reproduction

Run:

```sh
python verification/subject_constitution_candidate_comparison.py
python -m unittest discover -s verification -p 'subject_constitution_candidate_comparison_test.py'
```

The [receipt](verification/subject_constitution_candidate_comparison.json) records exact edge sets and stipulated fields, plus floating-point binomial bounds. Six tests check intervention-defined edges, the `2/2/2 → 1/2/3` counts, all 24 node relabelings, public update independence from the rule across every state and input for both conditions, invalid marked partitions, and the conditional binomial calculations. The public-indistinguishability test checks the construction's shared equation, not a separate observation of real systems. Test passes validate the finite code only, not experience, subjects, neuroscience or the philosophical premises.
