# What could make regard due? A comparison of three foundations

## Direct result

Of the routes examined here, **qualitative fulfillment is the most promising candidate for the intrinsic-value extension of the leading account**. It addresses why the affected being matters, rather than deriving the standard solely from the observer's project or competence as an agent.

This is a comparative philosophical judgment about explanatory fit, not a calibrated likelihood, consensus claim, or deduction of moral realism. The alternatives remain live. The comparison does not establish that normative value actualizes reality.

## 1. What must be explained

The corrected privation proposal locates wrongness in missing due practical integration of affected beings. Its foundation must explain more than:

- an interest is in fact frustrated;
- an agent dislikes that frustration;
- an institution prohibits it;
- the action is inconsistent with a rule the agent already accepts.

Those can support local evaluative claims. The stronger target is why some considerations supply reasons even where an agent has not selected them as ends.

The no-brute-facts requirement applies to that reason-giving relation itself. No route succeeds merely by installing an unexplained imperative.

## 2. Route G: chosen goals and constitutive needs

### Argument

A being has an end or constitution-dependent need. An action frustrates it. Therefore the action is bad for that end or need in the relevant respect.

### What it establishes

There can be true relational evaluations. A person's current endorsement is not necessary for every need: oxygen dependence, for example, is a matter of constitution rather than a fresh choice. Goal reversal does not make these qualified relations incoherent.

### What it does not establish

It does not yet tell us why another agent has a reason to protect the need. The existence of a condition of survival does not by itself establish that survival must always be preserved. Likewise, the success of an evolved function does not establish its supreme moral authority.

### Assessment

A strong basis for instrumental and some functional evaluation, but insufficient by itself for interpersonal normative authority. The gap is not repaired by adding “proper” to the favored function.

## 3. Route A: the value of agency

### Strong argument to examine

1. An agent pursues ends as worth achieving.
2. The agent's capacities are conditions of pursuing those ends.
3. Therefore the agent must value agency.
4. The relevant agency is not unique to that person.
5. Therefore agency wherever it occurs must receive regard.

### First inferential limit

The value in step 3 can be conditional and instrumental. Something can be valuable as a means to a chosen end without having unconditional authority. Valuing a vehicle for a journey does not establish that every vehicle must be preserved. More argument is needed to establish agency as an end rather than a condition of this agent's project.

### Second inferential limit

The generalization to step 5 requires more than recognizing a shared capacity. An agent-relative rule can consistently favor each agent's own projects. Consistent renaming does not refute such a rule. The identity of the relevant capacities does not alone establish agent-neutral reason-giving authority.

### Third limit: coverage

Even a successful account of rational agency must address beings with qualitative welfare but little or no sophisticated rational agency. It cannot silently assume that all significance is proportional to argumentative competence.

### Assessment

Agency is a plausible important good and can ground standards internal to deliberation. It has not here supplied a deduction of all interpersonal regard. Thin agency can be coherent and exploitative; morally thicker fulfillment remains a substantive hypothesis.

This does not show that every constitutivist argument is false. It identifies the steps still needed in the specific argument tested here.

## 4. Route Q: qualitative fulfillment and intrinsic significance

### Candidate identity

> The actual qualitative condition of a being constitutes at least some value; its fulfillment or deprivation supplies prima facie reasons concerning its treatment.

The proposal is not that value is whatever an observer approves, or that every pleasant experience is morally good. It is that the affected being's condition is an evaluative ground in its own right.

### Positive explanatory reasons to pursue it

**It begins with the affected being.** The reason does not have to be manufactured from a bystander's chosen project. This directly addresses the due-regard problem.

**It distinguishes welfare from project success.** A being can attain a chosen goal while undergoing a qualitative cost, and a desire can be frustrated without establishing an all-things-considered harm. These categories need not collapse into one scalar score.

**It does not require sophisticated rational agency as the gatekeeper of welfare.** A being's actual capacity for qualitative fulfillment, if present, can matter regardless of its ability to formulate a moral argument.

**It fits common constitution.** The intended explanation is that qualitative occurrence and value are aspects of one concrete nature, rather than separate substances connected by an observer's approval. This is an explanatory ambition, not a completed identity theory.

These considerations support pursuit of Q. None is a deductive refutation of instrumentalism, an error theory, or another account of reasons.

### The strongest challenge

A rival can accept qualitative experience, aversiveness, biological needs, and motivation while denying that they supply interpersonal normative reasons. The mere fact that a condition matters from within a perspective does not logically establish that it supplies a reason for every other agent.

Thus Q needs a constitutive account of the claimed identity. Repeating “intrinsic significance” is not that account. Nor does it establish equal weights, aggregation, rights, or a uniquely best possible whole.

### Assessment

Q most directly targets the desired intrinsic-value explanation. It is the preferred candidate extension to develop among these routes, but it remains a substantive conjecture. Its explanatory fit is not proof that its central claim is true.

## 5. How this bears on the user's privation proposal

Q can potentially ground what is due; the privation thesis can analyze failures of due integration. That division of labor is coherent in outline.

However, Q does not automatically establish that every negative evaluation is privative. A complete combined theory must show why the negative evaluation is constituted by missing or disordered positive fulfillment, not simply posit negative valence and rename it absence.

The improved formulation is aspect-relative:

> Actual powers and qualitative events are positive occurrences. Their badness, where present, concerns a justified shortfall of fulfillment in specified respects and circumstances.

This can acknowledge successful protective function alongside a local qualitative cost. It distinguishes ordinary absence from a fulfillment that is due. It still needs noncircular content for the positive standard.

## 6. What complete intelligibility adds—and what it does not

The objective forbids stopping at an unexplained moral command. It also forbids treating an arbitrary chosen goal, material label, or privileged observer as the ultimate standard without explanation.

It does not, by itself, entail moral realism or select Q. A nonaxiarchic account might try to explain evaluative practices, needs, and qualitative occurrence without stance-independent normative reasons. Its adequacy must be assessed, not ruled out by defining complete intelligibility as already including the desired value theory.

If Q is true under this objective, its normative relation must be necessary or otherwise fully explained through the concrete constitution. Necessity cannot merely be asserted to close the account.

## 7. A positive integrated conjecture

> One concrete nature constitutes public powers and qualitative perspectives. The fulfillment of those perspectives has intrinsic significance, not bestowed by another agent's preference. Appropriate practical organization acknowledges the reasons grounded in their condition. Wrongness can consist in deficient integration of those reasons rather than an independent negative substance.

This combines the leading monist architecture with the most promising normative route examined. The first sentence belongs to the core programme; the second is its explicit axiological extension; the third and fourth depend on the extension and the privative analysis.

The stronger conjecture that this significance is indispensable to existence remains separate. If concrete occurrence and value have a common constitutive explanation, it does not follow that value asymmetrically causes or grounds occurrence.

## 8. What would advance Q beyond a declaration

A developed argument should provide:

1. A precise account of qualitative fulfillment that is not merely the negation of a preassigned badness verdict.
2. An explanation of why it is reason-giving rather than only motivating or functionally relevant.
3. An account of interpersonal authority that does not confuse symmetry with proof of impartial morality.
4. Consequences for conflicting perspectives, actual realization, and aversive experience.
5. A contrast with a rival accepting the same descriptive facts while denying the reasons, explaining why the rival misses something rather than merely calling it callous.
6. A separate argument if value is claimed to help determine concrete actuality.

The next research target is that constitutive explanation, not additional finite models with supplied welfare scores.

## 9. Bottom line

The comparison supplies a defended research preference: **qualitative fulfillment is a better starting point for the intended intrinsic-value account than arbitrary goals or thin agency alone**. It does not eliminate all rivals or provide the missing identity law.

The privation conjecture remains serious. Its strongest present role is explaining deficient due integration once the positive standard is justified. The conjecture that Good actualizes reality is not established by that role.
