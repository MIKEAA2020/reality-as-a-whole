# Bigaj on field quanta: what counts, what exists, and what the project owes

## Result

Bigaj argues that the mathematical existence of a Fock representation and its occupation numbers does **not by itself** establish an ontology of independent particle-like objects. His strongest transferable point is a distinction between a formal way to count excitations and a justified account of what those counts refer to. He also rejects the easy converse: difficulties for particles do not automatically establish an adequate field ontology. This sharpens the project's existing relational and effective-particle work rather than proving it.

Some of the paper's specific objections need qualification. In particular, a wavepacket having nonzero amplitude over a continuum of momenta does **not** imply that all those modes are simultaneously occupied or that uncountably many particles exist. Likewise, local field operators and sharply localized one-particle position states cannot simply be identified without specifying normalization and localization. These corrections do not settle the philosophical ontology; they prevent an invalid mathematical argument from doing that work.

For the project, the immediate obligation is to map **states, operations, subsystem candidates and interventions** under genuine physical redescriptions before making a subject-incidence claim. Modes, localized algebras, detector records, field species and experiential subjects are not interchangeable units. The supplied article does not derive a subject, quality, spacetime emergence or the necessary existence of a whole.

## 1. The argument, in its own terms

**No-go background (pp. 1–3).** Bigaj reviews Malament and Hegerfeldt on particular exact localization assumptions, Reeh–Schlieder on local algebra and the vacuum, and Haag on free versus interacting representations. He allows that approximate or asymptotic particles might survive (p. 3, notes 10–11). The paper's distinctive ambition is to question the ordinary inference from quantization to particle-like objects even **before** relying on those results. Its note 9 explicitly corrects the stronger main-text formulation: Haag's theorem does not exclude every possible Fock representation for an interacting field; it concerns relations to the free representation under its hypotheses. Fock representations are not automatically interchangeable, but neither are all physically meaningful particle descriptions thereby excluded.

**One oscillator (pp. 4–5).** In the standard harmonic oscillator `H=ℏω(a†a+1/2)`, the nth level has spacing `ℏω`, with `a` and `a†` connecting adjacent levels and `a†a` having eigenvalue n. Bigaj asks why this arithmetic should compel us to add n independent entities instead of describing a single oscillator at an energy level. He regards countability as insufficient: discrete lengths or monetary units are not automatically separate objects. On his proposed criteria physical objects also admit sufficiently varied, possibly indefinite attributes that can change without loss of identity. He does not require exact localization or primitive ordering of indistinguishable particles. These criteria are *philosophical premises*, not theorems of quantum mechanics; a defender of non-individual quanta or emergent entities can contest them. His angular-momentum ladder analogy exposes a gap between operator nomenclature and ontology; it does not imply that actual transitions or emission experiments are imaginary. He acknowledges electromagnetic evidence such as photoelectric and Compton effects (note 20).

**Several oscillators and normal modes (pp. 6–7).** For independent oscillators, Bigaj considers transferring an excitation between modes and the problem of reidentifying it through multiparticle transformations. He explicitly allows that loss of reidentification need not itself defeat a quantum-object reading. For a periodic coupled chain, Fourier transformation diagonalizes the equations into N normal modes, providing a genuine calculational advantage over coupled coordinates. Bigaj grants that this strengthens the quanta vocabulary but questions whether formal simplicity alone licenses extra objects. The paper's opening paragraph on p. 7 says the reformulation forces “exactly N quanta of oscillation.” That should not be conflated with the occupation count: the standard finite N-mode bosonic system has **N modes**, whereas excitation occupation `Σ n_k` can be 0, 1, 2, and so on without a fixed upper bound. The author is right to call attention to an ambiguity between modes and excitation quanta; the two counts are not competing physical counts of the same thing. A free translation-invariant chain may have a zero-frequency mode needing separate treatment; no universal claim about its oscillator ground state is needed here.

**Fock space and individuation (pp. 7–9).** The direct sum of symmetric or antisymmetric n-fold spaces gives occupation notation and bosonic or fermionic algebra. Bigaj argues that an analogy with a Fock construction *already interpreted* as describing particles cannot by itself establish that the corresponding oscillator modes refer to possible states of an object: this inference would assume what it tries to prove. His objection to Fraser's free-field argument (pp. 8–9) is that a Poincaré-invariant ground state and relativistic dispersion can fit an alternative reading, though they are evidence a particle proponent may legitimately use in a larger argument. This is underdetermination by the cited formal feature, not a proof that no particle account is possible.

His discussion of identical fermions is more nuanced than a simple dismissal of particles: the antisymmetric two-spin singlet gives identical label-based one-factor reduced states but has one up and one down occupation in any chosen spin direction. He considers a nonstandard qualitative individuation by symmetric properties, recognizes the incompatible-direction issue, and mentions alternative carvings or a preferred spatial basis (pp. 8–9). These are interpretive proposals, not a derivation of a privileged basis or a license to ascribe all incompatible sharp properties to the same labeled electron under ordinary quantum rules.

**Field quantization and field ontology (pp. 9–13).** Bigaj follows the free massive Klein–Gordon field through a Fourier expansion, a normal-ordered Hamiltonian and the familiar dispersion `E_p²=p²+m²` in natural units. He disputes whether identifying its parameter m as a *particle's* mass is automatic rather than interpretive. A particle advocate can reply that mass-shell representations and measured scattering behavior supply more than analogy with a single mechanical oscillator; those inputs still must be specified, not just assumed from a letter in an equation. The article also considers a purported position-space creation argument (examined below), the wavefunctional representation, and the status of fields themselves. He asks what bears the quantum state when a field operator is an observable rather than a classical assignment. A quantum state or functional is not automatically one definite classical configuration; nor does taking an expectation value generally capture all state correlations. The view that states must be properties of independently identified objects is itself contestable: algebraic or structural readings can offer different accounts, but must explain their own concrete referents. Bigaj concludes neither particles nor fields have an unproblematic monopoly; he mentions events, processes and tropes without endorsing a completed third ontology (pp. 12–13).

## 2. The continuum argument needs correction

On p. 11 Bigaj suggests that a field state nonzero over a momentum interval means all the corresponding oscillators each contain at least one quantum, leading to uncountably many quanta. That inference is not generally valid. Take a normalized wavepacket `f∈L²(R³,d³p)`, nonzero on an open finite-measure set, and let

```text
|f⟩ = a†(f)|0⟩,          a†(f) = ∫ d³p f(p) a†(p),
||f||² = 1,               N = dΓ(I),           N|f⟩ = |f⟩.
```

The integral is an operator-valued-distribution shorthand; rigorously the creator is smeared with the one-particle wavefunction. Nonzero amplitudes at a continuum of momenta are alternatives in a single normalized one-particle state, not a claim of simultaneous occupation at every momentum. The total number has the sharp eigenvalue one in this representation. A continuous-mode density requires appropriate smearing or integration; the unsmeared `a†(p)a(p)` is not a collection of ordinary pointwise occupation observables on normalizable states.

Nor does the unbounded range of possible particle counts alone mean a normalized Fock state contains infinitely many particles. The bosonic Fock space is a Hilbert direct sum over finite n; an arbitrary normalized state can be a superposition over arbitrarily high n, and its number distribution has total probability one over finite integers. Its **expected** number can nevertheless diverge, in which case the state lies outside the relevant number-operator expectation domain. None of this entails that all physical states must belong to this chosen Fock representation or that finite expectations hold for all states. This addresses a specific step in Bigaj's continuity argument, not his broader interpretive challenge.

On pp. 10–11 he also suggests that combinations of one-particle momentum states can have a number other than one. A **linear superposition within the one-particle sector** remains an eigenstate with number one; combinations involving other number sectors or products of creators are different constructions. This supports the formal one-particle-sector reading without settling whether an objectively persisting individual exists in every interpretation.

## 3. Field-local preparation is not automatically sharp particle position

The paper's p. 10 treats `φ†(x)|0⟩` as a sharp position state and asserts a delta-function overlap, invoking a particle-location argument before questioning it philosophically. In the usual equal-time free real scalar convention consistent with its Eq. (5.1), `φ=φ†` and, schematically,

```text
φ(0,x)|0⟩ = ∫ d³p e^(−ip·x) a†(p)|0⟩ / [(2π)^(3/2) √(2E_p)],
⟨0|φ(0,y)φ(0,x)|0⟩ = ∫ d³p e^(ip·(y−x)) / [(2π)³ 2E_p].
```

Signs depend on Fourier convention, not the conclusion. The weight `1/(2E_p)` makes the equal-time overlap a two-point distribution, **not** the ordinary delta function under standard normalization. Point field vectors are formal distributions; use suitable test-function smearing. A Newton–Wigner-type position convention instead uses a different momentum weighting and a different localization notion. Normalization of `|x⟩` and the definition of the bra could be adjusted, so the precise source of the paper's assertion cannot be fixed without checking its cited textbook's conventions; with the displayed standard `φ` expansion the delta claim does not follow by an ordinary flat-momentum Fourier transform. Nor is the self-adjoint real field purely an annihilation or creation operator: it contains both, although its annihilation part kills the vacuum.

This caution is compatible with local measurements and with approximate particle localization; it is not a new no-go theorem. Bigaj's more general concern about whether a formal one-particle sector establishes enduring objects remains philosophical and is not decided by this normalization issue. Reeh–Schlieder's dense-set statement does not allow a deterministic superluminal preparation: normalized instruments, conditioning and classical communication matter. A local detector click is an apparatus outcome, not automatically an exact projection onto a global N-particle sector. See the [QFT source and locality audit](QFT_SOURCE_RECOVERY_LOCALITY_AND_SUBJECT_GATE.md), including its no-signalling illustration.

## 4. A representation and intervention gate for this project

There is a useful elementary example, not a proposed consciousness test. For two **degenerate** bosonic modes let `b±=(a₁±a₂)/√2`. This is a passive unitary change of one-particle basis, preserving the vacuum and total number `N=a₁†a₁+a₂†a₂=b+†b+ + b−†b−`. In the same physical state,

```text
|ψ⟩ = a₁†|0⟩ = (b+† + b−†)|0⟩/√2.
```

The `a₁` occupation is sharp one, but either `b` mode has occupation expectation one half, and the `b`-occupation basis describes a superposition of which mode is occupied. Total number remains one. If the proposal simply counts “occupied modes” as concrete objects or subjects without specifying which mode algebra, state criterion and transformation law are physically privileged, its answer is representation-sensitive. This calculation changes **coordinates**, not the physical state or the operational experiment.

The conclusion is not that every mode basis is physically interchangeable as a subsystem decomposition. The original site or spatially smeared operations can be experimentally local while their normal-mode combinations are global. In a faithful passive redescription those same operations must be transformed too; replacing local site interventions by individually manipulating `b+` and `b−` changes the intervention family. Likewise a finite-system unitary basis rotation is not a proof that unitarily inequivalent infinite-field representations are equivalent, or that a cutoff-dependent partition has a continuum limit.

A subject selector seeking to apply to these constructions should therefore provide:

1. **Domain and dictionary:** a physical state, observable algebra, dynamics, operationally admissible interventions, target windows, and a dictionary mapping each under an asserted equivalence. If the physical ontology claims a preferred decomposition, give a non-circular physical reason and its scope instead of declaring all changes gauge.
2. **Candidate transformation:** explicitly transport candidate supports, backgrounds, partitions and counterfactual operations; identify whether the comparison preserves locality, tensor factorization, apparatus and state space. If a candidate has no corresponding admissible counterpart, report an unmapped model, not automatic invariance or a failure.
3. **Incidence covariance:** on genuinely equivalent descriptions the assigned number and relations of subjects should agree up to an explicit subject correspondence, unless the account supplies additional physically meaningful distinguishing structure. This is a methodological demand on the proposed physical rule, not a theorem that no basis-dependent ontology is defensible. Exact equality of physical statistics does not settle which experiential identity law is correct.
4. **Regulator and stability:** separately establish that conclusions used at finite volume, fixed mass, finite cutoff and chosen Fock vacuum survive the particular limit or remain valid effective statements with stated errors. This is not an automatic consequence of coordinate covariance.

The [subject-redescription gate](SUBJECT_TIES_PERSISTENCE_REDESCRIPTION_GATE.md) already requires intervention-preserving maps for its finite causal selector. Bigaj supplies a different and independent motivation for that requirement: excitation labels may be features of a calculational decomposition rather than bearer-identifying facts. The two-mode example does **not** implement the project's spin selector, and its mode occupation difference is not a computed change in that selector.

## 5. Consequences for existing results

- **[Modular local net](MODULAR_LOCAL_NET.md):** its explicit Fock vacuum, Weyl operators, local interval algebras and declared CCR inputs are mathematical ingredients of a particular chiral construction. Bigaj's objection does not refute its conditional algebraic locality or recorded-instrument construction, and the report does not claim interval cyclicity. Conversely, the existence of the Fock representation or a local net does not select fundamental particle objects or phenomenally individuated subjects. A localized algebra is not automatically an isolated factor or a consciousness boundary.
- **[3+1 two-field continuum report](THREE_DIMENSIONAL_COMPLETE_INSTRUMENT_CONTINUUM.md):** its free-Fock, positive-mass, compact-volume setup, smooth bilinear coupling, common cutoff embedding and bounded-energy instrument comparison are explicitly restricted mathematical hypotheses. A change in interpretation of quanta does not by itself overturn its stated channel estimates; those internal derivations still need independent mathematical review. It does not follow that the result covers interacting QFTs in inequivalent representations, full apparatus physics, gravity, or experiential constitution. Number operators there can be legitimate model tools without carrying a claim about fundamental individuals.
- **[Bain and Wallace comparison](QFT_SOURCE_RECOVERY_LOCALITY_AND_SUBJECT_GATE.md):** their asymptotic/effective-particle arguments offer substantive rival responses rather than being refuted by Bigaj's observation about oscillator notation. The older [structural-realism note](BAIN_STRUCTURAL_REALISM_AND_RELATIONAL_EXPERIENCE.md) already treats effective structures as potentially real; Bigaj also warns that merely naming fields or structure does not answer their individuation and bearer questions. Bigaj does not establish that structural realism or the project's experiential interpretation is true.
- **[Private nested fields](PRIVATE_NESTED_FIELDS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTENT_COMPARISON.md) and [IIT comparison](IIT4_EXCLUSION_AND_PRIVATE_NESTED_FIELDS_AUDIT.md):** their “fields” are stipulated first-person occurrences, not QFT field operators. Common terminology does not supply the missing mapping to physical degrees of freedom, the count of subjects, a conjunction relation, or the q–unpleasantness law. A single algebraic model can support rival interpretations without that establishing actual multiple subjects; experiential definiteness and physical overlap remain separate issues.

Thus there is a real advance in the **requirements** on the framework, not an empirical validation or a formal solution. C3, B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open. No implication about real subjects follows from this audit, and the selected regression battery is not a philosophical pass.
