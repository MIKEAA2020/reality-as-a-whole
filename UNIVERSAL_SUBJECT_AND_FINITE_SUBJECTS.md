# One universal subject and many finite subjects

## The problem

The target is not merely an experiential totality. It includes **one genuine universal conscious subject or agent** and also **genuine finite subjects**. How can the latter be real without either reducing them to descriptions of the universal subject or treating them as independent ultimate substances?

There are established proposals for overlapping subjects and for phenomenal sharing. In particular, [Goff and Roelofs, “In Defence of Phenomenal Sharing”](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003044352-10), defend the possibility of some experience tokens being shared by distinct overlapping subjects, while explicitly not claiming to establish that sharing is actual. [Roelofs, *Combining Minds*](https://www.lukeroelofs.com/mental-combination), develops composite subjectivity. [Chalmers](https://consc.net/papers/combination.pdf) discusses the subject-combination and co-consciousness issues, and [Schwitzgebel and Nelson](https://doi.org/10.1080/09515089.2025.2520364) analyze overlapping fields and challenges to counting subjects. These are substantive precedents, not solutions already established by this project.

The following analysis adds a specific constraint on **this** target:

> If the universal subject jointly experiences all the very same experiential tokens that constitute finite subjects, then an unindexed relation saying only “some subject experiences these two tokens together” loses the differences between those subjects. It cannot by itself recover their plurality. In addition, shared-token nesting contradicts an exclusive-ownership principle.

This is a conditional result about a strong version of the target. It is not an argument that a universal subject must jointly experience everything, that experience sharing is impossible, or that merely positing subject-indexed fields constitutes subjects.

## 1. Specify exactly what is shared

For a bounded collection of qualitative **occurrence-tokens** `E`, let `I(s,e)` mean that token `e` is directly part of subject `s`'s phenomenal experience. This is a postulated relation for analysis, not a physical readout. A token is one occurrence or aspect, not merely the qualitative type “red” or a label for a record.

Suppose distinct finite subjects `s₁,s₂` exist, with tokens `a,b` respectively:

```text
I(s₁,a)       I(s₂,b)       s₁ ≠ s₂.
```

Now consider the stronger hypothesis that a universal subject `g`, distinct from those finite subjects, also experiences those **same tokens** in one jointly unified global episode:

```text
I(g,a) and I(g,b), with a and b jointly present for g.
```

All of these are substantive hypotheses. They are not inferred from the existence of a whole, communication between systems, common biology, mathematical inclusion or the assumption of a self-existent ground. They need not describe two simultaneous events in fundamental time: the hypothesis is about whatever joint phenomenal scope the proposed theory actually specifies. Whether tokens spanning distinct local times can belong to one global episode is itself unresolved.

A universal subject might instead have its own experiences *of* finite subjects, or be an agent without containing all finite tokens in one joint episode. Those are different hypotheses; neither should be quietly represented as the shared-token one.

## 2. The exclusive-ownership fork

The strong exclusivity principle says that each token belongs to at most one subject:

```text
I(s,e) and I(t,e)  ⇒  s=t.
```

That principle and the strong shared-token hypothesis conflict: `I(g,a)`, `I(s₁,a)` and `g ≠ s₁` cannot all hold. At least one must go. This is straightforward logic, not a new philosophical discovery.

The serious choices are:

1. **Sharing by overlapping subjects.** Retain distinct `g` and `s₁` and their shared token; reject strong exclusivity. This resembles the weak sharing defended by Goff and Roelofs: distinct overlapping subjects may share, even if fully discrete subjects do not. Their defense is not proof of the required actual incidence here. The theory must explain what makes each an actual subject and why the very token belongs to both.
2. **Different experiential tokens.** Keep exclusivity, the universal subject and finite subjects, but distinguish the finite subject's experience from the universal subject's awareness or representation of it. They may depend on the same ultimate reality; “different token” does not mean a separate substance, a literal simulation, or a prior act of copying. The account owes a non-circular explanation of their relation and how the universal subject's awareness relates to the finite subject's actual perspective.
3. **Deny the stronger global containment claim.** The universal subject need not have every finite token in one joint episode. Explain what its actual experiences, agency and relation to finite subjects are. Simply calling the whole experiential is not enough to make it the user's genuine universal subject.
4. **Identify the subjects.** If `g=s₁`, the particular contradiction disappears, but if all finite subjects are numerically identical to `g`, the intended plurality of genuine distinct subjects is lost. Mere plurality of viewpoints or roles is not automatically plurality of subjects.

Neither exclusive ownership nor sharing follows from self-presence alone. Exactly what counts as a token and whether “ownership” is built into its individuation are philosophically disputed. If ownership is built into a token's definition, exclusivity may become definitional; that alone does not settle whether a different conception of experiential parts can be shared.

## 3. Why a universal co-consciousness relation cannot identify the finite subjects

Define an unindexed relation:

```text
Q(e,f)  iff  at least one subject jointly experiences e and f.
```

Suppose a global subject jointly experiences *every pair* of tokens in `E`. Then `Q(e,f)` holds for every pair in `E`. This conclusion uses only the definition of `Q` and the global-unity premise.

Now compare two formal descriptions:

- **Global-only:** `g` jointly experiences the tokens in `E`.
- **Global-plus-local:** `g` has the same joint experience, and in addition two distinct finite subjects are directly related to some of those same tokens.

Both descriptions induce exactly the same complete relation `Q` on `E`. A method that receives only `E` and this unindexed `Q` therefore cannot infer which of the two subject-incidence structures is present. The observation is not that real subjects are sets or that both descriptions are genuinely possible complete wholes. It is an exact information-loss result for this specified representation.

This also sharpens a tempting proposal: **allowing nontransitive co-consciousness is not by itself enough** in the strong global-sharing case. If `Q` is defined existentially as above and `g` jointly experiences all pairs, `Q` is already universal, hence transitive, even if finer subject-relative relations are not. A subject-indexed unity relation, richer higher-order structure, different tokenization or a weaker global-unity premise may avoid the loss. Each is an additional substantive commitment, not a subject selector obtained for free.

This extends the earlier [maximal-content argument](ARTICULATED_SELF_PRESENCE.md): an encompassing field defeats individuation by maximal content inclusion. The point here is different: even **without** a maximality rule, existentially aggregating subject-relative unity erases the incidence information when global joint experience covers the domain. It does not prove that every conception of co-consciousness or every whole-first theory has this defect.

## 4. A constructive whole-first candidate

The most direct candidate retaining the user's endpoint is a **subject-indexed, whole-grounded incidence** account:

- One self-sufficient, intrinsically experiential whole is the proposed ground. Its self-sufficiency, necessity and experiential nature remain to be justified, not defined as proved.
- The universal subject is a genuine experiencing and agentive subject, not just the collection of all contents. Its experiential unity and agency require positive criteria.
- Finite subjects are genuinely distinct experiencing subjects, not merely labels for spatial or informational subsets.
- An independently justified incidence principle specifies which contents are directly present for which subjects and whether any tokens are shared. If their contents are represented rather than shared, a distinct relation specifies how; one cannot substitute representational access for shared experience by a change of words.
- The same constitution must connect incidence, qualitative character and physical organization. The `I` notation itself does not determine any of them.

This is an architecture, not a claim that these premises have been derived. In particular, granting every subset its own subject would introduce arbitrary proliferation, not an explanation. A successful principle must say why these subjects rather than other candidate subsets are real, handle temporal persistence and overlap, and respect relevant physical redescriptions. It must not choose a plurality solely because plurality is desired.

The global subject's agency also cannot be inferred from the fact that its contents form a mathematical unity. The account needs an intrinsically grounded organization supporting perception, evaluation and action or an explicitly defended alternative notion of agency. Any internal action and local temporal ordering must be described within the timeless whole, not as an external creator deciding to actualize it. None of these functional conditions alone proves phenomenal subjecthood.

## 5. Evidence and limits

Actual finite experience is direct evidence that at least one experiential occurrence exists. Reports, action and physiology can add constraints on finite subject organization. They do not directly measure whether a cosmic subject shares an exact experiential token, represents it, or is agentively unified. Conversely, failure of an existing report protocol is not evidence that an unreporting subject does not exist. See the existing [subject interface results](SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION_INTERFACE_RESULTS.md) and [split-brain source audit](SPLIT_BRAIN_DEEP_EVIDENCE_AND_TASK_AUDIT.md).

The next decisive advance is not another incidence table. It would be an independently defensible principle that fixes at least one actual subject-incidence distinction and connects it to specific physical or phenomenal evidence **without using the desired ownership assignment as an input**. A whole-level agency account then has to meet the same standard rather than extrapolating from local minds.

Finally, retaining a universal agent does not require calling it perfect. Under the user's adopted premises—if the whole were perfect our universe would be best, and our universe is not best—the whole is non-perfect in that sense. A merely non-agentive experiential ground would be a different rival, not a successful universal-consciousness account. Nothing in this analysis derives necessity, settles subject count, establishes the incidence principle or closes the frozen foundation battery.
