# Plural subjects in an experiential relational ground

## Aim and status

The [collective experience and moral-parody audit](COLLECTIVE_EXPERIENCE_SUBJECT_AND_PARODY_AUDIT.md) distinguishes a phenomenal property, its occurrence and its lived perspective, and compares fixed plural-property facts with alternative singular subject counts. Blaesi's parity challenge imposes the same explanatory standard on the preferred ground and its rivals; the subject bridge remains open.

The [full supplied Bohn paper audit](BOHN_PLURAL_COLLECTIVE_CONSCIOUSNESS_FULL_PAPER_AUDIT.md) adds an irreducibly collective-property alternative that does not require microsubjects. Its trickling-down principle and basic plural facts remain distinct from a derived E-L subject/quality rule, and its grounding assumptions need comparison with a whole-first ontology.

The [subjective-character and field-first bridge analysis](SUBJECTIVE_CHARACTER_AND_NONCIRCULAR_FIELD_BRIDGE.md) develops an explicit alternative to a subject-free base: take concrete first-personal fields as basic and identify thin local subjects with them. This avoids an added observer but assumes subject-like fields and their individuation; it does not derive the E-L subject or quality rule.

The proposed whole is self-sufficient and timeless, with experience intrinsic and fundamental relations not presumed to be embedded in spacetime. It need not be one universal subject. Actual experience is a datum, not an illusion to be explained away. The ambition is to satisfy all seven criteria together, including C3, **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, and C7, elegance and parsimony. None is declared satisfied by this note.

This note isolates what is needed for the preferred experiential-ground proposal to yield genuinely plural subjects, rather than redescribing one subject, counting qualities, or attaching a subject label to every arbitrarily chosen region. It compares alternatives and gives a symmetry constraint on one of them. It is not a derivation of subjecthood or evidence that any toy graph is conscious. A later [finite relational constitution model](RELATIONAL_WHOLE_SUBJECT_FACET_CONSTITUTION.md) shows precisely how a connected whole can represent distinct unified subjects without a universal subject, and which extra assumptions determine or leave open their count; its phenomenal premises remain stipulated.

The later [three-candidate constitution critical test](SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION_THREE_CANDIDATES_CRITICAL_TEST.md) implements a shared causal sector with three explicitly different incidence rules and states their individual failure modes and the additional assumptions needed for an empirical discriminator. It recommends none as an established constitution law.

## 1. The precise explanatory target

Distinguish four questions, none interchangeable:

1. **Instantiation:** why is anything genuinely experienced? The project accepts at least our own experience as real; treating the ground as intrinsically experiential is a proposal for its nature, not a proof that every part experiences.
2. **Incidence:** which experiential contents are together in a particular lived perspective, and which are not? A content type (such as redness), a particular occurrence, a description and an experiencer are not automatically the same entity. It is a substantive question whether a shared occurrence can belong to more than one perspective.
3. **Multiplicity:** what makes two perspectives numerically distinct rather than two descriptions, aspects or contents of one perspective? Qualitative difference is not sufficient: a single experience can be heterogeneous. Qualitative identity does not by itself establish numerical identity either.
4. **Persistence:** what, if anything, makes experiences across an experienced temporal extent or apparent succession belong to the same subject? This is a separate problem from incidence and does not require a fundamental universal time.

The [episode-incidence reformulation](EPISODE_INCIDENCE_REFORMULATION.md) already states why pair labels, physical regions and temporal continuity cannot solve these questions by definition. A subject law must earn both its positive and negative boundaries: if it says two contents occur together, why *that* togetherness; if it excludes a third, why is exclusion warranted? The absence of a report, correlation or accessible signal is not itself phenomenal exclusion.

## 2. What the ground does, and does not, buy us

Compare three experience-respecting hypotheses, all constrained to agree on accepted first-person cases and permitted physical data:

| Candidate | Basic commitment | What still must be supplied |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral ground | Fundamental facts are not themselves experiential; experiences are grounded by a further identity or constitutive principle. | Why that principle yields real experience, subject boundaries and qualities; neutral does not mean denying experience. |
| Experiential but not itself a subject | The intrinsic ground is experiential, without asserting that the whole has one encompassing point of view or that primitive parts are subjects. | A coherent account of how experiential nature is instantiated without a prior subject, and a non-circular principle for plural perspectives and their contents. Calling the ground experiential does not itself settle either. |
| Plural primitive perspectives | More than one basic subject or perspective is included in the fundament; rich experiences depend on their relational circumstances. | What individuates these perspectives, how they bear rich experience, and why this number and placement rather than others. This imports plurality rather than deriving it. |

One can also allow the ground to be a collective of experiences or perspectives without inferring that their sum has its own perspective. But describing a collection is not yet explaining how its members are individuated or how the collection is fundamental. Nor does rejecting an overarching subject prove that there is none. We favor the middle candidate as a research direction because it respects the preferred experiential ground without assuming primitive microsubjects or a universal subject. That is an explanatory preference, not a demonstrated advantage on C3 or C7: until its constitution principle is specified and motivated, it could simply have relocated the gap. An irreducibly plural basis is a serious alternative, not something our formulation invented.

## 3. Literature constraints and what they change

* Gregory Miller, [*Can Subjects Be Proper Parts of Subjects? The De-Combination Problem*](https://doi.org/10.1111/rati.12166), gives a specific subject-parthood challenge and proposes subject-relative boundaries alongside a unified subject-whole. The [full supplied-paper audit](MILLER_SUBJECT_PARTHOOD_AND_DECOMBINATION_AUDIT.md) reads all 18 PDF pages and checks key definitions visually. His account stipulates shared experience tokens and a subject-whole, neither of which follows from this project's experiential ground. His proposal is a serious alternative, not a proof that there is a cosmic subject or a completed account of constitution.
* Kadić, [*Monadic panpsychism*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-023-04464-0), proposes primitive microsubjects with relationally determined character; in one option the dominant subject changes, and in another many subjects undergo qualitatively identical human-level experiences. Its subject-summing problem is avoided by assuming subjects, not by deriving them. It also acknowledges that determining the dominant micro-entity is epistemically difficult. This is an important existing rival to a non-subject experiential ground. Read: substantive article sections 1–5 and notes, via the open HTML; bibliography was not systematically checked and cited underlying works were not thereby independently verified.
* Celayir, [*How Can Bundle Cosmopsychism Solve the Subject-Derivation Problem*](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/server/api/core/bitstreams/fdd752dc-7854-4b4c-bfcc-091f60f5e69d/content), proposes an irreducibly collective bundle of cosmic subjects, including enough to cover possible perspectives. This is closely related prior art, but it starts with subjects and its conclusions about synchronous perspectives and exclusivity are contested premises, not established facts. Read: abstract, introductory and chapter-II passages, and the opening of chapter III; the accessible extracted text returned by the fetcher stops at the start of that chapter despite indicating no more chunks. Search excerpts cover additional passages, but the remaining thesis and its complete argument were **not** systematically read. The source's own bundle is not a reason to assume a universal subject or to assert all possibilities are actual.
* Miller, [*Forming a Positive Concept of the Phenomenal Bonding Relation for Constitutive Panpsychism*](https://doi.org/10.1111/1746-8361.12207), develops co-consciousness as a proposed phenomenal bond, with analogical extension and an argument for necessitating a further subject. That is a substantive, contested account, not a neutral test of when physical relations are bonds. Read: abstract, introduction and selected later passages only; other arguments and notes unexamined.
* Mørch, [*Why Consciousness Can’t Overlap*](https://philarchive.org/archive/MRCWCC), argues against overlap on an exclusive maximal-bundle definition and argues parsimony against it under a substantive-subject view. That conclusion depends on these assumptions: physical support overlap, numerical experience-token overlap, and shared qualitative type are distinct claims. We do not assume exclusion as a law. Read: abstract, introduction and selected argument passages; remainder and figures not systematically inspected. The manuscript is marked draft.

These do not exhaust the literature. At the time of this analysis, [Chalmers, *The Combination Problem for Panpsychism*](https://consc.net/papers/combination.pdf) had been identified only through search excerpts. The [subsequent full supplied-manuscript audit](CHALMERS_COMBINATION_FULL_PAPER_AND_STRUCTURE_AUDIT.md) reads all 37 pages, including the structure argument, while noting that the published chapter was not compared. More broadly prior arguments against subject combination or cosmic decomposition do not logically refute all experiential grounds.

## 4. A boundary principle has to pass four separate tests

**Phenomenal anchoring.** Some aspect of unity or contrast should be grounded in actual first-person evidence: for example, multiple contents can be reported as jointly present in one's own experience. Reports and introspection are fallible and usually cannot directly establish another system's experience, the complete absence of unreported content, or identity of experience tokens across subjects. In particular, applying intrapersonal co-consciousness to relations *between* subjects requires additional argument, as Miller recognizes.

**Constitutive adequacy.** The proposed relation must say why its holding amounts to a lived perspective, and why its absence or different organization yields a different perspective. A physical structure, its correlations or causal interactions cannot silently be substituted for phenomenal togetherness. Defining a relation as 'whatever constitutes a subject' is a placeholder, not the missing explanation. There might be primitive subjectivity or a primitive phenomenal relation; either would be a declared premise needing independent motivation.

**Invariant individuation.** The law must respect changes of description, relabeling, and equivalent realizations to the extent the proposed theory treats them as physically equivalent. Substrate-independence is a substantive, scoped hypothesis: identify which transformations preserve the relevant organization and what operations and causal context they preserve. Arbitrary computational descriptions or mere input-output equivalence do not automatically preserve experiential constitution. If identical functional roles can be multiply realized, the account still needs to say whether they give one experience, several, or none and whether physically overlapping realizations count independently.

**Counterfactual discrimination.** Specify competing, pre-registered predictions for interventions with justified report/behavior calibration, and preserve the possibility that the theories make identical predictions. Evidence of stable behavioral dissociation, neural perturbation, or regained reporting may favor a structural account within a stated model, but does not independently validate subject number. A useful result must survive calibration alternatives and negative controls. No assumed current objective assay directly reveals phenomenal incidence in unreporting systems.

A proposed law also needs a continuity account across apparent time; it should allow undetermined or overlapping cases unless exclusion and sharpness are separately justified. It must explain particular qualities and valence too: a general statement that misalignment feels unpleasant cannot supply a calibrated or universal physical-to-experiential mapping. Plenitude, if adopted, says genuine possibilities are actual *within* the timeless whole; it does not decide which candidate laws are genuinely possible, count subjects, or supply experiential weights. No external actualization process is implied.

## 5. A precise constraint on dominant-node proposals

This section is conditional mathematics, not a general theorem of consciousness. Let a physical description be a finite graph with any stipulated node and edge attributes. Let a rule select exactly one physical node from each of two already physically marked regions. Suppose its output is **equivariant**: relabeling the description relabels the selection, and it may use no additional symmetry-breaking facts. For an automorphism of the attributed graph, the input is unchanged, so the selected set must be invariant under that automorphism. Hence a rule cannot uniquely select one node from a region when its symmetry group moves every possible node in that region.

For two disconnected, differently colored triangles, each triangle has three physically symmetric vertices. There are nine one-per-triangle selections, but none is invariant under all 36 color-preserving automorphisms. Therefore no deterministic equivariant rule based only on that attributed graph makes the stipulated selections. This illustrates a real tension for a **physical-node dominant-subject** account, not for every experiential-ground account. The two graph regions are stipulated physical regions, not identified subjects, and Kadić does not assume this graph or a deterministic rule of this particular kind.

The constraint can be avoided at a price that must be made explicit:

* **Further structure:** a distinguished node or suitable causal asymmetry can select one. Two colored three-node stars have a unique hub in each, invariant under their four color-preserving automorphisms. Yet degree is not a phenomenal bridge, and a single symmetric graph is not a model of actual brains.
* **Stochastic selection:** an invariant distribution assigning probability 1/9 to each of the nine choices exists. Its distribution is invariant, but each individual outcome breaks the input symmetry; a realized random fact or equivalent extra ontology must do the choosing. In a timeless account it must not be mistaken for a temporal selection mechanism.
* **Multiplicity or indeterminacy:** allow all candidates, an orbit, or no unique selection. This avoids choosing a privileged vertex, but neither an orbit nor a set is automatically one subject. Alternatively primitive phenomenal distinctions not visible in the physical description can select, at the cost of denying that physical facts alone fix the selection.
* **Non-node ontology:** select a whole structure, a relational pattern, or an experiential episode instead. Such a rule escapes the vertex selection constraint, but owes its own account of constitution, overlap and boundaries; selecting components presupposes that a suitable component rule applies.

The self-contained checker [plural_ground_dominance_symmetry_gate.py](verification/plural_ground_dominance_symmetry_gate.py) exhausts automorphisms and possible selections for these two graphs. It tests the concrete illustrations, not the empirical truth or novelty of the general equivariance observation. The general symmetry reasoning is elementary and no claim of an original general theorem is made.

## 6. A productive next experiment in reasoning and evidence

A subsequent [randomized pair–triple gate](TRIPLE_UNITY_RANDOMIZED_REPORT_PARTITION_GATE.md) gives a concrete conditional way to challenge exclusive incidence from common-population observations with bounded probe errors. The [shared-token versus duplication gate](SHARED_EXPERIENCE_VERSUS_DUPLICATED_TOKENS_GATE.md) separately compares numerical token identity and coordinated duplicates, including a conditional way to distinguish particular strengthened hypotheses. Neither resolves the central calibration and inaccessible-perspective obstacles.

The main progress is to expose a **choice of extra premise**, not to pretend one has been derived:

1. Explicitly formulate rival constitution maps on a common physical description: (A) a subject-bearing relation applied to structures, (B) primitive perspective loci with a context-dependent quality law, and (C) an experience-respecting neutral-ground identity/constitution law. Each must say what counts as a realization, support overlap and persistence; each can allow indeterminacy. None should equate reportability with experience.
2. For each map, list what is primitive, which relabelings preserve it, and which first-person and third-person observations it predicts. Where all observable predictions coincide, record **underdetermination**, not a fabricated empirical victory. Count the extra selection facts and laws when comparing parsimony; fewer words are not automatically fewer assumptions.
3. Seek first-person protocols for positive co-presence and discriminable transitions, with blinded, preregistered controls for reporting and task access. Test candidate *physical* boundary proxies by interventions and cross-context transport, retaining uncertainty and the possibility of no validated experiential mapping. First-person anchoring supports what is experienced by the reporting subject; it does not by itself expose others' internal fields.
4. Look for a proposed experiential constitution law that gives distinctive, risky predictions across multiple calibrated conditions, including split and recombination cases, without defining the answer using those conditions. If none can do this yet, retain the experiential ground as a philosophical research candidate, not a solved account. An independently justified phenomenal bridge, not another clustering threshold, is the blocking premise.
5. Only after a boundary law is defensible revisit global plenitude, persistence and the full physical reconstruction. Winning one comparison would not establish self-existence or solve emergent spacetime, physical dynamics or experiential intelligibility.

**Bottom line:** experiential fundamentality may make experience less surprising than a neutral description, but it does not automatically make plurality intelligible. A realistic path requires a principled experiential incidence law, non-arbitrary individuation, and evidence strong enough to constrain alternatives. These are still open; the whole is not assumed to be a universal subject.
