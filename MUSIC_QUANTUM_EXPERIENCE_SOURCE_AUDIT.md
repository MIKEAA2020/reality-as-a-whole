# Does musical structure help explain quantum physics and experience?

## Verdict

**Yes as a source of structural ideas and a useful empirical testing ground; no as a derivation of quantum mechanics, an experiential ground, or a self-existent whole.**

[Bridget Queenan's article, “The musical structure behind quantum physics”](https://iai.tv/articles/the-musical-structure-behind-quantum-physics-auid-3393), is directly relevant to the user's proposed relation between vibration, harmony/dissonance and experience. Its positive point—that relative frequency relationships and wave patterns can organize phenomena across different media—is worth retaining. But an analogy between musical scales and quantum energy spectra does not itself establish that the two are the same physical phenomenon or that a mathematical relation feels pleasant or unpleasant.

The [alternative IAI page](https://iai.tv/articles/the-musical-structure-behind-quantum-physics-auid-3393?_auid=2020) presented the complete article text, including its tables and conclusion. Some formulas appear only as images and are not fully represented by text extraction; the numerical check below uses the table values and the SI definitions independently, not unverified image transcription. The original URL sometimes served only the introductory portion. The article is a conceptual essay, not a controlled consciousness experiment or a quantum-gravity derivation.

## 1. What can be retained

- **Relational structure is physically useful.** Ratios, phases, oscillation modes and interactions can matter more than names assigned to isolated objects. Music gives accessible examples of contextual organization; such relations are legitimate candidates for an experiential theory.
- **Multiple materials can realize analogous patterns.** Air, strings and electronic audio equipment can produce similar acoustic structures. This is suggestive for material substrate-independence, but similar audible outputs are not themselves proof of equivalent experiences or of the same complete causal organization.
- **The mathematics of waves is genuinely shared.** A fixed-end classical string has normal-mode frequencies `f_n=n v/(2L)`. A quantum harmonic oscillator has energy levels `E_n=ℏω(n+1/2)`. These share useful mathematical structure, but the former is a set of normal-mode *frequencies* in a classical wave problem, whereas the latter is a set of allowed quantum *energy measurement outcomes* for one oscillator. A classical mode can have continuously variable amplitude and energy. See the [standing-wave demonstration](https://www-personal.umd.umich.edu/~jameshet/IntroLabs/IntroLabDocuments/150-12%20Waves/Waves%209.0.pdf) and the [MIT oscillator notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/5-61-physical-chemistry-fall-2017/f5910cca963785bb17c7c0808f1bb665_N4vMgwWT-80.pdf).
- **A real quantum-acoustic connection exists.** [Quantum control of surface-acoustic-wave phonons](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0719-5) reports preparing and measuring nonclassical mechanical states in a controlled resonator coupled to a qubit. This is actual quantum acoustics, not evidence that an ordinary chord is entangled or that a resonator experiences sound.

A musical scale selecting discrete frequencies from a continuous range is a tuning/selection convention. It is not the same claim as a quantum system having discrete energy eigenvalues. Likewise, neither all sound frequencies nor all quantum energy spectra are limited to a single equally spaced musical ladder.

## 2. Particular claims that need correction or qualification

**Sound is not the foundation of the electromagnetic spectrum.** The article describes audible waves as providing that foundation. Ordinary sound is a mechanical disturbance in a material medium; light is electromagnetic and can propagate through vacuum. Both admit wave descriptions, but one is not simply a low-frequency segment of the other. [NASA's overview](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/02_anatomy/) distinguishes the two. Quantum phonons are collective excitations in suitable materials, not the same entity as photons or fundamental strings.

**The numerical coincidence around A=440 is close, but not exact.** The article's table compares `1 eV/h` with `880 × 2^38 Hz`. Using the exact modern SI definitions ([BIPM](https://www.bipm.org/en/si-base-units/kilogram); [BIPM's SI summary for the electronvolt](https://www.bipm.org/documents/20126/41483022/SI-Brochure-9-concise-EN.pdf/2fda4656-e236-0fcb-3867-36ca74eea4e3); [NIST's conversion factor](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Convert?exp=0&num=13.6&From=ev&To=hz&Action=Convert+value+and+show+factor)):

```
1 eV / h           ≈ 241,798,924,208,492 Hz
880 Hz × 2^38       = 241,892,558,110,720 Hz
relative excess     ≈ 0.03872%.
```

Dividing the first frequency by `2^39` gives approximately `439.8297 Hz`, about **0.67 cents below** 440 Hz. This is a genuinely close numerical mnemonic, not exact equality or a derivation of why 440 Hz is used. An energy divided by `h` is an energy-equivalent frequency; it is not by that fact an audible sound. The numerical closeness depends on the selected 1-eV reference and tuning convention. For example, folding the frequency of **1 meV**, a different physical energy, into the same octave gives approximately `450.386 Hz`, about 40.39 cents above 440 Hz. Changing a unit does not change a fixed physical energy; the point is that choosing “one unit” of a different size chooses a *different comparison energy*. The [exact-arithmetic receipt](verification/music_quantum_units_audit.json) makes these choices reproducible.

The article calls 432 Hz the official A and calls 440 Hz suspiciously sharp. Scientific pitch and 432-Hz conventions have been proposed, but they are not a uniquely mandated natural reference. The current [ISO 16 standard](https://www.iso.org/standard/3601.html) specifies 440 Hz for the reference A. Setting a different reference pitch can preserve the same intervals, so the choice of 440 versus 432 Hz does not itself establish which relative tuning is correct.

**Rationality and units:** the article calls `h` irrational. Planck's constant has units of action, so rationality of its *numerical value* is not a unit-independent property of nature. Its exact numerical value in today's SI is `6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J s`, a terminating decimal by definition. Also `E=h f=ℏω` uses cyclic frequency `f` and angular frequency `ω=2πf` consistently; substituting a value in hertz directly for angular frequency while switching `h` to `ℏ` introduces a factor-of-`2π` error.

The Pythagorean comma `3^12/2^19` is a genuine dimensionless tuning ratio, about `1.0136433`, or 23.46 cents. That mathematical fact does not itself establish a link between music, fundamental constants and experiential valence. The article offers such links as suggestive; they remain hypotheses rather than demonstrated mechanisms.

## 3. The valuable empirical link: structure is not the same as valence

There is direct, relevant research on sound organization and experience:

1. [McDermott et al. (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature18635) found that, in the tested stimuli, Tsimane’ listeners rated conventionally consonant and dissonant harmonies similarly despite showing discrimination and aversion to acoustic roughness, while comparison groups showed consonance preferences. This is evidence of cultural variation, **not** that auditory biology plays no role or that every Tsimane’ person has identical preferences.
2. [McPherson et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-16448-6) found greater perceptual fusion of some simple-ratio note pairs in both tested US and Tsimane’ groups. Yet fusion did not predict aesthetic judgments in those groups. This is particularly useful: **perceptual unity and pleasantness can come apart**. Here fusion means perceived auditory grouping of tones within listeners, not fusion of separate subjects.
3. [Marjieh et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-45812-z) reports five behavioral studies totaling **235,440 judgments**, not 235,440 participants, from US and South Korean populations. Varying timbre could reshape consonance preferences, including preference for some inharmonic intervals. It supports the importance of context and spectrum beyond a frequency-ratio-only rule; it does not prove that every aesthetic preference is cultural or settle all auditory neuroscience.

Together, these results motivate separating **physical interval, spectral/timbral organization, perceptual grouping, pleasantness, and subject incidence**. They are related but cannot be treated as synonyms. The experimental reports are evidence about human hearing under studied conditions, not direct measurements of fundamental reality's intrinsic nature. We consulted the linked published descriptions and did not download or independently reanalyze their raw data.

## 4. What this does to the current hypothesis

The user's proposal that certain kinds of misalignment may be intrinsically unpleasant remains worth investigating. The literature above challenges the overly simple version:

> “A bare numerical departure from small integer frequency ratios universally determines unpleasant experience.”

That does **not** refute a more specific hypothesis about a subject's physiological or relational conflict, coupling preferences, timescale, or context. The earlier [vibrational-dissonance test](VIBRATIONAL_DISSONANCE_EXPERIENCE_TEST.md) already distinguishes structural frustration, state-dependent mismatch and relaxation; a fixed phase pattern or low synchrony does not by itself establish frustration. We should extend that work, not re-label musical dissonance as a physical explanation of pain.

A concrete empirical discriminator would compare **the same fundamental interval with different independently measured timbres**, and separately measure perceptual fusion and pleasantness. A theory whose only input is that interval predicts no timbre-dependent difference; a spectrum-and-context account can predict one, if specified before testing. The 2024 study supplies evidence against interval-only invariance in its tested domain; a novel framework experiment would need its own prespecified models, controls, independent calibration and held-out comparison. Musical preference and painful suffering are distinct targets; extrapolation requires additional evidence.

This also clarifies substrate-independence. A structural account can be independent of material without ignoring relevant organization: two instruments playing the same nominal notes may differ in timbre and in what a listener hears. Nor does identical auditory input alone guarantee identical subjects or qualities. The open problem is the constitution of **the listener's actual experience**, not whether two oscillators have the same name.

## 5. Effect on the whole-account

Keep the mathematical and empirical constraints; do not promote the musical analogy into an answer to why reality is self-existent, which relational organizations constitute plural subjects, or how local quantum physics and time arise from a timeless experiential whole. The results also do not establish that all genuinely possible configurations are actual, prescribe a subject measure, or imply that the whole is a universal consciousness or agent. The framework's incompleteness is unchanged; see the [frozen battery](CURRENT_FRAMEWORK_BATTERY_DOSSIER.md).

The most promising addition is the **empirically grounded separation between relational grouping and felt valence**, which connects the user's idea to an existing research literature while making it more specific and falsifiable. It is evidence about aspects of experience, not proof of an ultimate experiential ontology.
