# Leibniz: continuous creation, distinct substances, and perspective

## Verdict

The proposed interpretation preserves something important—continuous dependence, active finite unities, and perspectival expression—but its concluding identification of monads as **modes of one divine subject** is not supported by the supplied *Monadology*. It turns a possible nondual reconstruction into an attribution to Leibniz.

**Textually safer conclusion:** Leibniz affirms one necessary God and many genuinely distinct, dependent created monads, with internal principles of perception and activity, coordinated through pre-established harmony. His language of continual derivation does not by itself erase the creator–creature distinction or make creatures states of God's consciousness.

Source for this audit: [the supplied Robert Latta translation of the Monadology](sources/uploads/leibniz-monadology-latta.pdf). Sections 1–17, 38–48, 51–60, and 78–86 were checked directly for this assessment. This is not a survey of Leibniz's complete corpus. The quotations about “emanation” and producing creatures “as we produce thoughts” in the proposed evaluation have not been independently located in an original text here; their attribution and context remain unverified. Even genuine derivation language would not alone establish substance monism.

## 1. Creation is explicit; independence is not

In §6 a monad “can come into being only by creation” and end “only by annihilation.” Sections 10–11 call the monad a created being and locate natural change in its internal principle.

Section 47 calls God the original simple substance and speaks of “all created or derivative Monads” as products of “continual fulgurations of the Divinity from moment to moment,” limited by the created being's receptivity.

These passages support **continuous ontological dependence**, not a deity's one-time construction of self-sufficient creatures. But the inference

    continuously dependent -> not a distinct created substance

is invalid. Something can be distinct from its source while depending on that source for its entire existence. Continuous conservation/dependence is not by itself opposed to classical creation doctrine; a picture of creation followed by divine abandonment is not a neutral definition of classical theism.

Likewise, “not independent of God” and “a mode of God” are different claims. Section 40's wording—nothing outside God being independent of God—does not say nothing distinct from God exists.

## 2. Windowlessness does not abolish substantial plurality

Section 1 defines a monad as a simple substance; §9 says each monad differs from every other through intrinsic difference. Section 7 denies the entrance of substances or accidents into a monad from another created thing. Section 51 describes inter-monadic influence as ideal and mediated through God.

The absence of direct created-to-created causal influx therefore explains why Leibniz invokes coordination. It does not imply that the monads are one subject or that no distinct substances exist. Pre-established harmony is a harmony **between** the substances (§78), not a statement that they are a single divine episode under different labels.

## 3. One represented universe is not one representing subject

Sections 56–57 describe monads as living mirrors and compare their differing perspectives with different views of the same town. Section 60 locates their limitations in their ways of knowing the whole.

This is a powerful resource for a perspectival account. But it distinguishes:

- the one universe expressed;
- the multiple monads expressing it;
- God as their necessary source and perfect knower.

It does not identify these three. In §60 Leibniz says that otherwise each monad would be a deity. The perspective analogy cannot independently establish that all finite perspectives are states of God's own first-person awareness.

## 4. Substance and its perceptions are not interchangeable

Section 14 distinguishes a monad's passing perceptual state from the monad itself, and distinguishes perception from apperception/consciousness. Sections 13–16 permit multiplicity in a simple unity.

Consequently, saying that a monad is active and perceptive does not make it a passing perception or mode of another subject. The proposed claim of formal but not real distinction from God requires a separate argument and textual support. It is not supplied by calling monads thoughts, mirrors, or emanations.

The label **emanationist perspectival monism** therefore overstates what has been established. **Theistic monadological pluralism with continuous dependence and perspectival expression** is a more accurate label for the position evidenced here. “Monadological idealism” can be useful with interpretive qualifications; detailed body/aggregate ontology and its development across Leibniz's writings require further sources.

## 5. Appearance is not synonymous with illusion

The useful opposition is not creation versus a Berkeleyan dream. Berkeley's immaterialism is not adequately characterized as denying the reality of the experienced world or denying real spirits. Its differences from Leibniz require more precise treatment than “Berkeleyan illusionism.” No primary Berkeley text was assessed in this update.

For present purposes, distinguish the status of a monad, its perceptual state, the represented body/world, and their ultimate ground. The supplied passages do not warrant treating monads themselves as mere appearances in God's mind. Nor does rejecting that interpretation require saying bodily appearances are deceptive or that substances are independent of God.

## 6. What our framework may adopt—and where it departs

**Retain:** sufficient reason, one necessary perfect source, simplicity with differentiated content, degrees of disclosure, finite active organization, and unity in variety.

**Do not attribute as established Leibnizian doctrine:** the absolute is the only substance; monads are modes of one divine subject; the creator–creature distinction disappears; every perspective is uncreated; creation is only appearance.

Our [necessary articulated unity](NECESSARY_ARTICULATED_UNITY.md) proposes finite disclosure within one absolute without a separate production ontology. That is a constructive departure from the monadological plurality and creation language in the supplied text, not merely a restatement of it.

**Constitutive consequence:** continuous dependence alone does not explain finite incidence or establish ownership within one awareness. Our whole/local relation must be justified in its own right. Leibniz offers a comparison and conceptual resources, not an already completed decombination law for the proposed absolute.
