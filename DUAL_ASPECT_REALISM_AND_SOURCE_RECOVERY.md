# Relational matter and experience: one reality, two aspects

## A substantive middle position

The proposal is that reality is not first made of mindless matter with experience added later, nor a merely subjective appearance manufactured by a universal mind. There is one real, self-sufficient, relational whole. Its physically describable structures and their relations are real, and experience is the intrinsic nature of appropriately constituted reality. The physical and experiential descriptions refer to the same concrete processes, not two substances communicating across a gap. Matter is not a hallucination or an optional approximation merely because its intrinsic nature is experiential. Individual subjects and perspectives can be genuinely plural; no cosmic person or universal agent is assumed.

This proposal takes the successes of physics and neuroscience seriously: interventions on neural systems change behavior and reported experience, and physical science gives reproducible models of processes that need not depend on human observation. It takes the reality of felt experience seriously too: descriptions of relations and behavioral capacities do not automatically convey what a sensation is like. It **offers a strategy for addressing the hard problem** by denying that an intrinsically non-experiential world has to produce experience out of nothing. Calling it solved would go beyond the argument: a candidate must still say which concrete organizations constitute which subjects and experiences, and justify the identity rather than merely label a structure as feeling.

This is related to established [Russellian monism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/) and dual-aspect monism; it is not a new invention. The Stanford overview's opening and accounts of core theses were consulted, not all literature on these views. The user's experiential-ground version is not automatically the usual **neutral** monism, whose ultimate nature is neither mental nor material. Depending on definitions, other authors may classify a view of experiential intrinsic nature as a form of idealism, Russellian panpsychism, or nonstandard physicalism. Labels matter less than precise commitments; no interpretation is refuted merely by calling it an extreme. Some idealists grant a real, law-governed physical world, and many physicalists explicitly accept consciousness.

Nor does a midpoint follow logically just because two camps have strengths and weaknesses. The reason to investigate this position is that it aims to preserve independently motivated constraints **without** postulating independent interacting mind and matter. Whether it succeeds depends on its specific constitution and physical law, not on balancing labels.

## Meaning of “equally fundamental”

There are two possible claims that must not be conflated:

1. **No denial or replacement:** the physical and experiential are both indispensable, real aspects of the same concrete reality. This fits the proposal and does not need two independent substances.
2. **Equal ontological priority:** basic physical and experiential features are equally basic, neither grounded in the other. This is stronger than the thesis that experience is the *intrinsic nature* and physical structure the relational description of that nature. Under an intrinsic-ground reading, experiential character may be ontologically basic while physical structure is no less real and is indispensable to individuating it. Alternatively one can posit co-fundamental aspects with no asymmetric grounding. The two alternatives should be explicitly compared, not quietly interchanged.

Matter in ordinary physics consists of fields, particles, interactions and their effective material organizations; it is not established that everyday matter is itself fundamental, and the timeless whole is not assumed to contain fundamental spacetime or mass. “The physical aspect is basic” means at minimum that the whole has genuine, lawful relational structure capable of yielding physical domains; it does not assume an external arena or universal clock. It is a framework commitment still requiring a derivation of quantum physics, spacetime, local time and matter—not a substitute for them.

If intrinsic nature is experiential throughout, that need not mean that a rock or an electron is a unified subject, or that the whole is one subject. Alternatives include irreducible experiential relations, local subjects only under specific constitution conditions, or a distinction between experiential nature and individually bounded episodes. Each needs explicit semantics to avoid a merely verbal “experience without anyone experiencing.” Neither literal micro-subjects nor a collective macro-subject should be added without argument.

## Operational interface and remaining work

For a candidate domain, specify (i) a realized structure `X` and admissible interventions, (ii) a physical description `P(X)` with measurable records and error bounds, (iii) subject incidence `S(X)`, and (iv) experienced content/valence `Q(X,s)` when `s` exists. The proposed identity is **not** `P(X)=Q(X,s)` as formulas or labels; `P` and `Q` are different descriptions of one occurrence `X`. A candidate must state necessary/sufficient conditions and identify the concrete occurrence to which both descriptions apply.

Three research gates prevent an unfalsifiable relabeling:

- **Preservation:** show why the ordinary laws, scientific predictions, neural interventions and causal-explanatory roles are retained. An experience is not a ghost that pushes neurons alongside physics; on the identity hypothesis its physical causal description and its felt nature belong to the same event. But identity alone does not prove that its specific phenomenal character explains behavior rather than remaining explanatorily idle.
- **Discrimination:** compare independently specified, rival subject and content mappings, including variations that leave the same operational physical statistics but posit different subject counts or qualities. Without independent phenomenal anchors such rivals may be observationally equivalent; direct experience and carefully calibrated reports constrain relevant cases but do not automatically distinguish every mapping. This is underdetermination, not an argument that identity is false.
- **Constitution:** specify boundaries, multiplicity, persistence, qualitative contrasts and affective valence. In particular, a mathematical mismatch, dissonance or complexity score is not unpleasantness merely because it correlates with a report. Compare the existing [integrated candidate](INTEGRATED_EXPERIENTIAL_MISALIGNMENT_CANDIDATE.md) and [grounding rival battery](EXPERIENTIAL_GROUNDING_RIVAL_BATTERY_GATE.md).

The preferred whole remains timeless and self-sufficient. Strong possibility=actuality does not require a temporal mechanism bringing possibilities into being. It does not by itself establish why the whole is self-sufficient, which possibilities are genuine, how many subjects exist, their qualities, measure, or the physical laws. Parsimony applies after empirical adequacy and logical coherence; adding unspecified phenomenal or material components is not automatically elegant.

## Newly accessible sources

### The PNAS appendix supplied by the user

The published [main article](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2613021123) and its [supplementary PDF](https://www.pnas.org/doi/suppl/10.1073/pnas.2613021123/suppl_file/pnas.2613021123.sapp.pdf) were retrieved as text. All five chunks of PDF text were inspected, including appendices A–F and references. PDF extraction garbles some equations; not all displayed formulas or figures were visually verified, and this is not an independent complete proof audit or full line-by-line version comparison.

The published main text makes explicit that its disagreement example is **protocol-agnostic**: Bob updates the common prior with his own outcome, without an explicit record of whether Alice's measurement occurred. When a classical register is included, assessments about the recorded state under the authors' assumptions satisfy their agreement result. This reinforces the earlier analysis and rules out the slogan “quantum theory proves different observers can have arbitrary inconsistent facts.”

Appendix D specifies `q_A=1/3`, `q_B=(1+t)/3`, with common-certainty weight one; Appendix E gives the authors' no-signaling/Specker-triangle counterexample and a signed phase-space representation. A new [exact checker](verification/quantum_agreement_supplement_gate.py) verifies their three pairwise tables, shared one-site marginals and signed weights; it also confirms that any nonnegative joint model would force `P(a=1)=0`, whereas the observed pair table says `1/4`. This is an independently checked instance of the **authors' existing contextual example**, not a new theorem and not an experiment or evidence about consciousness. Appendix F's inequalities have not been fully audited.

### Earlier agreement supplement

The publisher's [supplement](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41467-021-27134-6/MediaObjects/41467_2021_27134_MOESM1_ESM.pdf) is now accessible through PDF-to-text. We inspected the classical proof, the two-input/two-output common-certainty proof (Supplementary Note 4), and the reduction/quantum-void passages. Extraction errors and figures were not visually verified; we did not audit every proof step. Note 4 confirms the use of Tsirelson's vector representation noted by Liu, Chung and Ramanathan. Their [later treatment](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.06304v4#S4) supplies a different proof and explains a concern about marginals; this is not evidence that the original theorem is false. The later PNAS setting concerns a different joint property and remains compatible with the earlier result.

### OKN primary article

The earlier missing primary paper, [Aleshin et al., *Perceptual reversals in binocular rivalry: Improved detection from OKN*](https://jov.arvojournals.org/article.aspx?articleid=2728678), is accessible. We inspected selected main methods, results and discussion, not every section or all figures. It compares pursuit with voluntary reports and physical replay in eight trained observers and uses broader cohorts to assess robustness. The authors distinguish precision of *oculomotor transition detection* from how faithfully the transitions track subjective perception; their discussion explicitly says the latter remains to be determined. This qualifies but does not negate the earlier demo-only warning: the separate public demo lacks independent report labels, even though the full paper does present report/replay comparisons. We have not rerun its data or code.
