## 1. The object to identify

For a concretely realized physical process P, seek a law

    F(P) = (Gamma(P), chi_P),
    Gamma(P) = (E, I, L).

E denotes actual phenomenal episode occurrences, if any. I specifies their qualitative and physical incidence and temporal extent. L specifies continuation or lineage without automatically identifying it with numerical identity. chi specifies qualitative character and organization for each episode.

A law must say which domains it covers and what counts as sufficient physical specification. It cannot merely accept an already supplied episode, call an output state a quality, and claim to have derived both.

The full explanatory task additionally requires an identity/constitution argument for F and an account of the foundation. An empirically successful local F would be real progress, but not yet a completed no-brute-facts whole-account.

A measurement protocol is a way to investigate the process. It is not automatically part of the process's phenomenal constitution. If an apparatus changes the process, that intervention must be represented physically; if it merely records or discards a feedforward output, it cannot acquire the right to redefine the same intrinsic episode.

## 3. First result: measurement completeness does not fix intrinsic geometry

The recent [geometry construction](research/constitutive_geometry/REPORT.md) used the two-response family with tensor, at x=1/4 and y=1/3,

    g_direct = diag(16/3, 9/2).

Now leave the source process unchanged and record its first output through a known bit-flip channel with error 1/4, while retaining the second output. The observed first-response probability becomes

    z = 1/4 + x/2.

The new tensor in the **same source coordinates** is

    g_noisy = diag(16/15, 9/2).

The noisy experiment still identifies both source parameters in principle: x = 2(z-1/4), and y is unchanged. Thus merely requiring parameter-identifying or informationally complete observations does not select a unique Fisher tensor.

These two tensors are not even proportional: one coordinate's information shrinks by a factor of five, while the other's does not. No single positive scale restores equality. This channel is invertible as a map for identifying source parameters, not reversibly invertible as a physical stochastic operation; its algebraic inverse would require negative transition weights.

Consequently, if one fixed intrinsic phenomenal tensor h and one fixed identification chi were required to equal each experiment's Fisher tensor up to a scalar, the two requirements would conflict. In the second coordinate they fix the relative scalar, while the first coordinate contradicts it.

This is a conditional obstruction to a **protocol-independent generalization** of the metric hypothesis. It does not refute the earlier hypothesis restricted to one declared protocol, nor prove that no intrinsic metric can be defined by a stronger physical construction.
