# Split-brain evidence: public data, anatomical sparing, and the limits of unity claims

Research update, 7 November 2026. This is an empirical update, not a completed theory of consciousness or a revision of the frozen framework dossier.

## Bottom line

1. Recent behavioral and imaging results strengthen the case that callosal anatomy matters for interhemispheric function. A patient retaining a small posterior splenial segment performed well on several crossed bedside tasks and had broadly bilateral resting-state connectivity; patients with complete sections often showed marked dissociations. But the evidence concerns rare, heterogeneous cases, not a universal anatomical threshold or a direct measure of the number of subjects.
2. An independent inspection of the behavioral study's **public aggregate table** finds a discrepancy: the CSV's topognosis chance-level fields for two patients disagree with the paper's methods. Recalculating one patient's intra-manual result using the CSV value rather than the paper's value changes the nominal two-sided 0.05 threshold decision. **The paper's reported p-value matches its stated methods**, so this is not evidence that the paper itself used the CSV's inconsistent value. The clinical and other behavioral evidence is not thereby refuted.
3. Strong performance and no significant difference in a few trials do not establish equivalence, and resting-state correlation does not by itself demonstrate directional information transfer or phenomenal unity. An improved design must distinguish these separately.

## 1. Behavioral and imaging studies: do not double-count patients

Bekir et al. describe four adult epilepsy patients: three complete callosotomies (NR, TJ, LJ) and one partial case (BT) retaining approximately 1 cm of posterior splenium. Five bedside tests sampled vision, tactile localization and naming, visuospatial performance, and language-related hand gestures. BT scored 4/4 in each visual-field finger-perimetry condition, 9/10 in bilateral same/different judgments, and 10/10 on both same- and opposite-hand tactile localization. Complete patients showed several striking crossed/uncrossed dissociations, with task- and person-specific exceptions. The authors report no preregistration, uneven trial numbers, and sometimes stopped testing early because of fatigue or distress. The tests use clinical lateralization assumptions and are not a universal measure of every possible integrative ability.

Santander et al. studied six adult callosotomy patients: four complete (HY, DT, TJ, LJ) and two partial (EM, BT), compared with 100 healthy HCP reference participants. Their resting-state network measures were generally more lateralized and their left/right network dynamics less synchronized after complete section; the two partial cases fell within healthy reference distributions on some summary measures. BT's modules, however, had a distinctive topology rather than simply reproducing every normal network feature. **BT, TJ and LJ occur in both reports:** the behavioral and imaging results give complementary observations but are not independent patient replications. The 100 HCP participants are controls, not additional callosotomy patients.

The plausible posterior-hub/reorganization account is not established merely by measuring a remnant. Neither study randomly allocated resection extent; surgical decisions, epilepsy, medication, time since surgery, individual anatomy and selection can matter. The authors also discuss earlier splenium-only cases with selective disconnections: differences in sparing, timing and assessment are possible explanations, not established resolutions. BT's time since surgery makes reorganization plausible, but no matched preoperative-to-postoperative trajectory was shown for that claim.

## 2. What the public data reveal

The paper specifies the chance probabilities in topognosis as **25% for NR and TJ** and **20% for BT and LJ**, depending on which finger-response procedure each performed. The released CSV agrees for BT and NR, but lists **20% for both TJ rows and 25% for both LJ rows**. The other counts in this check are aggregate counts, not recovered observations.

| Illustrative row | Data | Paper methods chance | CSV field | Exact two-sided p, paper / CSV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| TJ, inter-manual | 4/30 | 0.25 | 0.20 | 0.203598 / 0.494443 |
| LJ, intra-manual | 3/4 | 0.20 | 0.25 | 0.0272 / 0.05078125 |

The two-sided exact binomial test here sums null probabilities of outcomes no more probable than the observed outcome. The manuscript's TJ value `p = 0.204` and LJ value `p = 0.027` agree with **its stated methods**, not the contradictory CSV fields. For LJ, using the CSV value in an automated replication would cross the nominal 0.05 cutoff for that row; this does not establish which procedure was actually administered or invalidate the other observations. These are unadjusted, model-dependent p-values, and a result at a threshold is not a sharp biological boundary. The paper and release need author clarification; we have not requested it.

The notebook's binomial example uses TJ's 4/30 with the paper's 0.25 null and reproduces `0.2035977`. A separate Monte Carlo illustration uses the same 4/30 with a **0.50 null**, returning zero exceedances in 10,000 simulations. For that different null, the exact two-sided value is approximately `0.00005948`; a simulation returning zero is possible and does not imply an exact p-value of zero. This example does **not** prove the full study simulations used 0.50: the public notebook is illustrative, and its examples do not reconstruct all published tests.

One further reporting ambiguity merits clarification: Table 2 and the CSV list NR's verbal-command hand-gesture results as 6/6 right and 5/6 left; the results prose reports 5/5 and 4/5. We cannot resolve whether this is a transcription, counting, or other difference without the underlying records. It does not change the above topognosis audit.

## 3. Why ‘no detected difference’ is not an equivalence result

BT's 10/10 crossed and 10/10 uncrossed localization counts are impressive descriptive observations. Under an illustrative fixed-sample, independent-identically-distributed binomial model, however, a one-sided 95% exact lower confidence bound for accuracy in **one** 10/10 condition is only `0.05^(1/10) ≈ 0.741`. For example, accuracy 0.75 would yield 10/10 with probability about 0.056. This is **not** a confidence interval for the difference between conditions, and those modeling assumptions may fail with clinical trials, task-specific items or adaptive stopping. The point is limited: a few successes do not establish near-perfect general ability. Similarly, a nonsignificant test against chance does not prove performance equals chance; an absence of a significant hand difference is not an equivalence test.

The paper defines a raw disconnection score as `accuracy_intra − accuracy_inter`. For perfect intra-condition performance and crossed accuracy equal to chance, that formula gives 0.80 if chance is 0.20, or 0.75 if chance is 0.25, **not 1** as the Fig. 4 caption's verbal endpoint suggests. The raw score can also be negative. This arithmetic does not negate the observed deficits, but the scale's interpretation and between-task comparability should be stated carefully. A chance-normalized score such as `(intra − inter)/(1 − chance)` answers a different question and is not supplied as a validated replacement; it also inherits uncertainty in the chance model and may fall outside [0, 1].

## 4. Longitudinal and countervailing evidence

[Roland et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5740665/) examined pre- and approximately one-day postoperative resting-state fMRI in 22 pediatric patients (16 complete and 6 partial callosotomies), with a much smaller three-person follow-up 2–7 years later; two of those later scans were sedated. Complete surgery was associated with marked interhemispheric correlation loss, while some primary sensory correlations remained. The three-person follow-up did not show convincing recovery. A before/after design strengthens the inference that the operation affects connectivity, but concurrent surgery-related changes, sedation and the tiny follow-up constrain interpretation. A clinical section is not an experimentally isolated intervention on a single functional link. It does not rule out recovery in all patients or at later times.

[Johnston et al. 2008](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/28/25/6453) observed a similar pre/post reduction in one child; the degree to which older samples overlap should be checked before treating them as independent replications. [Marcantoni et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11133720/) reported residual bilateral and thalamocortical correlations in six long-term patients. Santander et al. explicitly discuss synchronized external input as a possible contributor to residual visual correlations, and other intact pathways may contribute. Therefore a large reduction after section and some residual synchrony are **compatible**, not a contradiction. Nor is an fMRI correlation, or its square, an estimate of communication capacity, directional transfer, or experiential integration.

As a complementary developmental contrast, the abstract of [Barnby et al. 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-78475-z) reports lower confidence-related metacognitive measures across three cohorts with congenital callosal dysgenesis, despite generally normal perceptual accuracy. This is relevant to dissociating accuracy and confidence, but congenital development and potential reorganization are not equivalent to adult surgical section, and the abstract does not establish subject count. Only the abstract was accessible in this search.

## 5. A discriminating next study, not a solved assay

**Operational objective:** measure whether independently randomized information from both visual fields changes a response beyond the best measured one-field and cue-leakage strategies; separately test a prespecified tolerance for near-equivalence across anatomically defined cases. Neither endpoint alone is subject count.

1. Recruit partial and complete cases where clinically and ethically feasible, with preregistered eligibility, individual anatomy, time since surgery, seizure/medication and longitudinal scan schedule. Never perform surgery or invasive perturbations for this experiment; use only clinically approved opportunities and stop on participant request or distress. Separate independent cohorts from overlapping participants, and report exclusions and stopping.
2. On each trial independently randomize left and right stimuli and require a non-degenerate joint function (for example XOR), with blinded trial generation, matched one-field controls, fixation and gaze logging, masking and trial-level timing. Provide response methods calibrated for motor and linguistic asymmetries. Include simultaneous and delayed conditions. Independently measure potential sound, movement, sequential/cross-cueing and other shared signals, including cues that carry information **jointly conditional on one stimulus**, not just marginal correlations. Define an admissible nonintegrative strategy class and its finite-sample performance bound before looking at results.
3. Preregister participant-level uncertainty, multiple-testing controls, and an equivalence margin justified in advance, with adequate trial planning and safety limits. Model missingness, item difficulty, sequential dependence, repeated visits and informative stopping; do not declare equality from a nonsignificant difference. Given the tiny population, report individual trajectories, uncertainty and feasibility rather than relying on an underpowered group-only test. Share de-identified trial data and analysis where consent permits.
4. Add simultaneous or temporally matched measures of anatomy and activity, and where clinically indicated, appropriate task-dependent stimulation or recordings with sham/controls and prespecified causal estimands. Compare spared-pathway, subcortical/common-input and external-cue models by predictions under explicitly stated assumptions. An association, pathway disruption, or single decoder is not a conclusive route identification by itself.
5. Treat phenomenology as a **separate, currently unvalidated bridge**: independently calibrate report error, attentional failures and signal access, rather than treating task integration or consensus as proof of a single experiencer. If rival subject-constitution rules predict the same complete intervention/report histories, these data alone cannot distinguish them.

Existing design work and its bounds are in [the joint-access gate](JOINT_ACCESS_COMMON_SOURCE_DECISION_GATE.md) and [the deeper task audit](SPLIT_BRAIN_DEEP_EVIDENCE_AND_TASK_AUDIT.md). This proposal adapts established split-brain task controls; it is not a claim of a new validated experiment. The relevant gain for the framework is a tighter empirical constraint on any subject-boundary proposal, **not** a derivation of subjects, a settled universal-consciousness claim, or proof of the self-existence and experience assumptions.
