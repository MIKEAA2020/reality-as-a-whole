# A specific orientation pathway: V4 to frontal eye field

## Decision

The next candidate is **not** a global claim that a particular brain area is consciousness. It is a restricted, operational comparison for one finite visual-orientation target:

- Candidate maintaining basis `C`: a prespecified, retinotopically matched V1–V4 visual population and its relevant recurrent dynamics during a declared short visual interval.
- Candidate additional consumer `D`: orientation-related information available to the **frontal eye field (FEF)** in that interval, including information arriving indirectly and relevant prior records.
- Target `E`: the visual character of an independently anchored horizontal-versus-vertical experience, with a distinguishable no-orientation/blur report—not the stimulus label or forced-choice accuracy alone.

This is a **prospective specialization** of the existing [H_M1/H_D1 contrast](MAINTAINED_DISCLOSURE_CANDIDATE.md), not a result derived from it. Call the new pair `H_M2` and `H_F2`: M2 says the specified preserved C is sufficient for the anchored target without the nominated FEF distribution; F2 says that distribution is necessary even with C preserved. Neither assertion is known to be true; a candidate whose actual local basis includes FEF or different visual regions is not ruled out. FEF is not equivalent to a complete global workspace, and these are not blanket versions of GNWT, IIT, physicalism or experiential monism.

The **best current decision is feasibility failure / not yet tested**. A known V4–FEF pathway supplies a concrete target, but the examined studies do not demonstrate the jointly required source preservation, removal of the nominated consumer content, and intervention-valid experience measurement. They involve different tasks, organisms and procedures; they are not one pooled experiment.

## 1. Why this is a real pathway rather than an invented cartoon

[Noudoost, Clark and Moore (2021)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.64814) recorded from FEF in two macaques performing a **spatial memory-guided saccade task** and used electrical stimulation of retinotopically corresponding V4 sites. Of 311 recorded FEF neurons, stimulation reliably elicited spikes in 115; 96 had short latencies, reported as consistent with a monosynaptic input, and were the authors' focus. They distinguished antidromic from orthodromic stimulation using a collision procedure. The paper's receptive-field mapping used bars at 0, 45, 90 and 135 degrees, but its principal experimental task and result concern remembered **location**, not an orientation-experience intervention.

The authors found that V4-evoked FEF responses depended on the remembered location. This is evidence that a V4-to-FEF influence can be measured and depends on context. It does not show that stimulation establishes all natural pathways carrying orientation, that FEF is necessary for phenomenal vision, or that an evoked response is a calibrated measure of orientation communication during our nominated task. Nonsignificant activation of a given FEF unit does not rule out inputs from other V4 sites; the paper's peer review makes this coverage issue explicit. The published raw data are listed in [G-Node](https://doi.org/10.12751/g-node.b0lth8); the listed archive is 193 GiB and was **not** downloaded or reanalyzed here.

Existing evidence also makes a one-way simplification unsafe: FEF activity can influence visual cortex, and the 2021 paper discusses FEF projections back to V4. Thus `V4 → FEF` and `FEF → V4` are useful labels for nominated influences, not proof of independent channels or a complete circuit. The minimal schematic, with omitted routes explicitly left open, is:

```text
orientation stimulus → V1 ↔ V4 ──→ FEF → eye movement / potential reports
                          ↑         │
                          └─────────┘   feedback
             other routes / prior downstream records: not shown
```

A whole-FEF manipulation can affect the return arrow, an output mechanism, and routes other than the forward arrow. A diagram is not an experimentally established edge intervention or an exhaustive brain map.

## 2. Direct evidence that FEF inactivation changes the proposed V4 source

[Noudoost, Clark and Moore (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3824-13.2014) provide a closer match to the candidate than the 2019 study below: they reversibly inactivated a retinotopically mapped **FEF site**, recorded V4 neurons before and after, and used oriented bars during a macaque visually guided saccade task. Across 27 orientation-selective V4 neurons from two monkeys, the paper reports mean orientation-discrimination AROC `0.68 ± 0.02` before and `0.64 ± 0.02` after inactivation, with paired change `−0.04 ± 0.01`, `p < .001`. The paper reports uncertainty as SEM; neurons are not independent human participants. Stimuli at four orientations were tested for a subset of eight neurons, not necessarily the fixed pair from our proposed experiment.

The same paper reports no significant change in the **average tonic V4 visual response** for the 33-neuron sample (`p=.809`), while orientation discriminability decreased. This is a concrete counterexample to using average response alone as proof that orientation-related visual processing was preserved. No-significance is not an equivalence test, and these are different population summaries. The study does not establish that FEF orientation information was absent everywhere, nor report the animal's experienced visual character. The behavioral task, inactivation, and time windows differ from our hypothetical human near-threshold orientation task. Thus it undermines the **specific shortcut** of treating regional FEF inactivation plus stable mean V4 activity as an established source-preserving separation; it does not prove P3 fails for every intervention or every physical basis.

[Bichot et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-13761-7) provide a related network control: they reversibly inactivated part of the **ventral prearcuate region (VPA)** in two macaques during a free-viewing color-and-shape search and recorded in V4. VPA is not the same as FEF, and direct VPA–V4 connectivity was not established by this result; the observed effect could involve direct or indirect routes. This is a *network feasibility warning*, not the proposed FEF/line-orientation experiment.

The authors report that inactivation impaired search in the affected hemifield and substantially reduced feature-attention modulation of V4 activity. For population contrast ratios before the first saccade, reported medians were:

| Monkey | Control | VPA inactivation |
|---|---:|---:|
| F | .063 | .027 |
| J | .030 | .007 |

They also analyzed a simpler single-stimulus detection task: the early-average V4 response comparison was not statistically significant in either monkey, while a later detection-window response was significantly smaller in monkey F. A nonsignificant difference does **not** establish preservation within a given equivalence margin. Neural samples came from separate interleaved sessions, units were not necessarily the same, and the feature-selection neural analysis used correct trials. The paper does not provide an orientation-target P3 failure rate or an upper bound on remaining orientation information in FEF.

**Practical implication:** suppressing a prefrontal node cannot simply be called a clean cut of outgoing visual information with its visual basis untouched. The intervention can alter visual cortical dynamics through feedback or network reconfiguration. If the declared C includes the particular V4 modulation that changes, that evidence would count against exact preservation for that measured task. It does not prove that every possible V4-to-FEF manipulation must alter C, or that the 2019 manipulation violates P3 in the different orientation experiment.

## 3. Freeze the separation, including what counts as a failed gate

The following are operational requirements for a *future design*, not achieved measurements:

1. **Define C before testing.** Identify stimulus retinotopy, relevant V1/V4 sites and recording limitations, the visual interval, orientation-specific activity, and the feedback/dynamic features claimed to matter. A proposed 150–300 ms analysis window is a **design hypothesis**, not a demonstrated experience interval or a transplant of the 2019 search timings. Define the relevant counterfactual powers and perturbation family. Matching one classifier or mean firing rate is at most a restricted proxy; equal proxy values do not establish the whole C.
2. **Define D narrowly.** Nominate a jointly measured FEF population and relevant memory/side information, and distinguish the **direct anatomical projection** from all indirect routes by which the orientation signal reaches that FEF population. A localized result for FEF does not establish absence from all prefrontal or other consumers. Past information may survive a cut as a record.
3. **Require independently warranted channel removal.** For a specified pair of physically implementable source/preparation interventions and a declared common background, define an upper bound on the induced difference in the joint FEF consumer state, including relevant earlier records. If a direct-axon-specific claim is made, an ordinary node-level intervention must additionally distinguish direct, indirect, and off-target pathways; electrical pulses or coherence alone do not do that. Any probe that alters C must be transported to the valid separating trials with explicit justification. A null statistical test is not the required upper confidence bound.
4. **Require independently warranted preservation.** State in advance which empirical signatures and causal features of C are to be preserved and what tolerances are acceptable; use simultaneous uncertainty. Full H_M2/H_F2 requires the actual constitutive basis to be preserved, not merely the sampled signatures. If the latter are incomplete, any bound covers only the measured proxy, and P3 remains open.
5. **Require an intervention-valid experiential assay.** Obtain and analyze participant descriptions of the target separately from stimulus labels, guessing, confidence and no-report proxies, and justify error bounds on the subset where the physical gates hold. As the [orientation study audit](ORIENTATION_EXPERIENCE_CONSTITUTION_TEST.md) shows, “no orientation seen” and “no stimulus seen” are not equivalent. Multiple proxy measures are not automatically independent or gold standards.

The [complete-boundary certificate](DISTRIBUTION_CUT_AND_RECORD_PERSISTENCE.md) shows how prior records and an entire boundary transcript might provide a sufficient bound on a specified downstream effect **if** the common-kernel factorization and all bounds are separately warranted. In the [robust contrast](ROBUST_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTRAST.md), let `G` denote all valid-target, source-preservation and distribution-removal prerequisites; M2 predicts preserved anchored target `E=1` on G and F2 predicts `E=0`. With independently warranted gate-failure bounds `η_M, η_F` and assay false-negative/false-positive bounds `β, α` **on the intervention's G-valid subset**, their predicted positive-assay probabilities are guaranteed disjoint only if

```text
(1 − η_M)(1 − β) > α + η_F(1 − α).
```

These are bounds on the particular binary target assay, not a definition of consciousness. No data here supply the required empirical values; a statistical confidence failure probability is also not itself a trial-level gate-failure rate.

## 4. What can and cannot be tested in humans

A conceptually appropriate human study would independently localize candidate visual and FEF regions, use a near-threshold orientation task with graded reports and catch trials, and compare control and carefully characterized perturbation conditions. The proposed analysis must include source-preservation and destination-removal equivalence/upper-bound tests, feedback, prior information and calibrated phenomenology in the **same experimental condition**.

Noninvasive TMS may perturb a region and induce network-wide changes, but it has not thereby selectively blocked only V4→FEF information. EEG and fMRI have different coverage, source-localization, and temporal limits; an absence of detected FEF information is not a whole-consumer absence certificate. Human intracranial recordings, where clinically indicated, may improve access but coverage and ethical controls are limited. Rodent or macaque pathway-selective interventions are not automatically suitable for identifying a human's subjective orientation experience. Whether any **safe, ethical and physically selective** intervention can satisfy P3/P4/P6 simultaneously is genuinely unresolved; do not suggest an invasive intervention outside independent clinical indication or claim such a protocol is ready.

A clean result for the narrow FEF consumer would still not settle wider-distribution theories, other percepts, the hard problem of qualitative character, or the metaphysical existence of a universal subject. If the physical gates are not established, no positive or negative result decides M2 versus F2. If their shared gates are established and an independently calibrated assay gives a decisively opposite target outcome, the relevant scoped hypothesis can be challenged; both can fail if their predictions are not exhaustive.

## 5. Next decision, with a stopping rule

**Do not start a new abstract circuit or claim an experiment is possible just because a pathway has a name.** A targeted literature search found the close 2014 macaque FEF-inactivation/oriented-bar study discussed above, but it does not supply a human first-person assay, an FEF orientation-removal bound or demonstrated preservation of the proposed visual basis. A [human FEF TMS–EEG study](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-21979-7) found occipital oscillatory changes and, in a separate experiment, altered motion discrimination; it does not supply the joint orientation-specific gates either. This search is not a systematic review and does not establish that no better study exists.

On the evidence examined, **stop: the FEF candidate is not empirically decidable**. The next useful step is to identify either a more accessible narrow comparison or a published dataset with (i) orientation-specific source and FEF consumer measurements, (ii) a perturbation with independently characterized feedback, and (iii) a credible target-experience measure under that condition. Predeclare which data could actually yield quantitative source-preservation and consumer-effect confidence bounds; a negative search is not proof of impossibility. No empirical threshold, new subject, or V4-to-experience identity has been inferred here.

The project-wide status remains unchanged: this is a bounded step in the local physical-to-phenomenal bridge, not a resolution of structural or experiential intelligibility, fundamental time, or the probability of a whole-reality framework.
