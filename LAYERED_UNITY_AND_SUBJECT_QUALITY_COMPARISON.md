# Layered unity and the subject–quality bridge: a common-domain comparison

## Verdict

**Private-token follow-up:** [Private local and global fields](PRIVATE_NESTED_FIELDS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTENT_COMPARISON.md) adds a stipulated three-subject nested candidate. Under newly defined content-type targets it matches the C-layered local/global conjunction pattern while differing in subject incidence; the follow-up does not assume the earlier occurrence-specific targets are numerically identical across candidates.

**Primary-evidence follow-up:** the [attention-capacity audit](ATTENTION_CAPACITY_VERSUS_LIVED_UNITY_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md) checks accessible abstracts and excerpts of three studies underlying the attention argument, distinguishes their tasks and patient groups, and identifies where the inference to experienced centrality remains open. Full primary methods and results were not available in that follow-up.

The integrated candidate's two-subject L rule and the one-subject C rival do **not** by themselves predict everything needed for a test of lived unity. In particular, a coarse aspect of two sides might be experienced together while detailed aspects are not. A. Lopez's layered account develops this as a serious, experience-respecting possibility, not as a new patient measurement. Taking it seriously exposes an extra assumption in our earlier binary test: a positive **global** conjunction contradicts the earlier *exclusive two-field package* only if that package also rules out an encompassing field or a global conjunction within an existing field. Conversely, evidence of global conjunction does not by itself establish conjunction of the detailed local aspects.

The research advance is a sharper **conditional decision table**, not a newly discovered subject or a physical-to-phenomenal law. Existing split-brain findings and first-person testimony are relevant but do not implement this table on the four-spin sector. The `q`-to-unpleasantness identity remains an independent postulate, and neither a boundary result nor a report prediction settles whether experience is the intrinsic ground of the whole.

The [analysis plan](LAYERED_UNITY_COMMON_DOMAIN_FIXED_PLAN.md) was written after reading the article's search excerpts and after the earlier model and clinical results were known, but before reading its full accessible text. This is retrospective conceptual analysis, not a preregistered human study.

## 1. Lopez's actual proposal and its support

Azenet Lopez, [*A layered unity model of split-brain consciousness*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02339-3), *Philosophical Studies* 182, 2159–2190 (2025), argues that an individual can have **global** conjoint phenomenology across right and left while some **local** aspects are not experienced together [1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-025-02339-3). Global here concerns properties of groups or configurations—such as visual scene gist or occupied locations—not necessarily a universal subject, a large brain region, or a summation of local intensities. Local concerns features of individual objects such as shape or color. These are experiential distinctions; a computation of an average is not by itself an experienced global aspect.

Lopez regards cross-hemifield behavioral abilities, including some coarse spatial and temporal tasks, as motivation for global conjunction and interprets cross-matching difficulty as suggesting local disunity. The proposed **conjoint phenomenology thesis** is deliberately weaker than the full layered model: some aspects are felt together, without settling which aspects. The layered account specifies global unity and local disunity. Lopez allows that local conjoint experience might instead exist without access and explicitly discusses **duplicate representations** as a rival interpretation; whether duplication is ruled out requires empirical work. Failure on a matching task is not logically equivalent to absence of the relevant experience.

The paper's attention argument is substantive, not merely “attention equals consciousness”: drawing on Watzl and Wiese, Lopez considers (i) a single experienced centrality structure and (ii) voluntary attentional agency as proposed sufficient unity-making relations. She argues that alertness and some top-down/executive capacities remain sufficiently unified, while exogenous reorienting shows larger breakdowns; the relation from those measured capacities to an experienced centrality structure remains an inference. She acknowledges uncertainty and does **not** give a settled one-to-one mapping from each attention capacity to each experiential layer. Phenomenal centrality is not retinal central vision: a visually peripheral event may be at the center of attention. The individual attention studies are reported as described by Lopez, not independently reanalyzed here.

An important pressure point is **nontransitivity**: local left may be unified with global left, global left with global right, and global right with local right, without local left being unified with local right. Lopez recognizes the objection that this is partial rather than full unity and defends its intelligibility; whether one and the same subject can have that structure is contested, not solved by drawing the relations. She also considers two independently duplicated global representations, and notes that both cases could preserve different notions of unity. Her proposed application beyond vision, including affect, is explicitly unfinished. The project's L rule is **not IIT**, and her conditional discussion of IIT does not constitute a test of L.

**Reading scope:** we read all 22 retrieved chunks of the open published HTML, including main text, notes and references and the text captions, but did not visually inspect the figures or audit every underlying primary study. The earlier [Pinto comparison](SPLIT_BRAIN_FIRST_PERSON_CONSTITUTION_COMPARISON.md) separately read its main article, not its supplement or trial-level data. No new patient data, experimental results or phenomenological interviews were inspected here. The prior [deep evidence audit](SPLIT_BRAIN_DEEP_EVIDENCE_AND_TASK_AUDIT.md) had already cited Lopez as a layered alternative; this is a deeper source reading, not discovery of that proposal.

## 2. One physical sector, three explicitly completed packages

Use the [integrated candidate's](INTEGRATED_EXPERIENTIAL_MISALIGNMENT_CANDIDATE.md) unchanged four-spin chain and original one-update influence query at coupling parameter `t=1/4`. L selects `{0,1}` and `{2,3}`; C selects `{0,1,2,3}`. The physical law and all specified physical records are the same for all packages. There is no established mapping from a human brain or a split-brain operation to these four spins; `t` is a coupling parameter, not time.

For a single defined hypothetical episode, **add** four designated experiential aspects: local left `lL`, local right `lR`, and global-side aspects `gL`, `gR`. The names are placeholders for distinct, specified phenomenological aspects, not physical spins or proof that actual experiences decompose into independent tokens. In all three packages below, stipulate that the four aspects occur as specified, each local aspect is linked to its same-side global aspect, and the eligible reporting channel and window are identified. These realization and reporting links are not derived from the spin law. For simplicity we consider the designated field population only; additional inaccessible fields are not ruled out in reality.

| Package | Incidence and ownership added to the same physics | Local conjunction `Jlocal` | Global conjunction `Jglobal` | Quality and explanatory cost |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| **L-exclusive** | The L-selected pairs are two distinct fields; each owns its own side's local and global aspects. No designated field contains aspects from both sides; no encompassing or cross-side field. | 0 | 0 | The original pair `q=1`→designated unpleasant contrast and `q=0`→neutral is postulated, not derived. Incidence, exclusivity and localization are further assumptions. |
| **C-full** | The C-selected component is one designated field containing all four aspects; each named pair of aspects is experienced conjointly. | 1 | 1 | C alone gives no actual quality law. The full pairwise-conjunction and incidence claims are added; q need not apply to this component. |
| **C-layered** | One designated field for the C-selected component contains all four aspects. Stipulate `lL–gL`, `gL–gR` and `gR–lR` unity but not `lL–lR` unity. | 0 | 1 | A **project-constructed** Lopez-inspired completion, not Lopez's spin model or a derivation from C. It needs a defensible same-subject partial-unity relation and a separate quality law. |

`Jlocal=1` means some designated reporting subject experiences the two **specified local aspects** together in that window; `Jglobal=1` is the same claim for the specified global aspects. Zero is the negation *within the declared designated population*, not a statement about every subject in reality. A subject experiencing each aspect separately does not automatically satisfy the corresponding conjunction. Here the global pair is a named pair of coarse aspects, not a literal average; specifying what would make them jointly experienced is part of the proposed phenomenal mapping.

The C-layered description changes the **experiential completion** of the original C rule; C by itself asserts neither all-pairs phenomenal unity nor nontransitive unity. Lopez herself is not claiming that the brain has four spins or endorsing our incidence selector, and her paper does not supply our postulated global-side aspect tokens. Treating local/global nontransitivity as a coherent same-subject structure is itself a substantive position. Under an alternative definition in which phenomenal unity means *if and only if same owner*, it cannot hold: sameness of owner is transitive. One must reject that definition, revise ownership/aspect individuation, or reject this completion. This conditional fork is a conceptual constraint, not proof that partial unity exists.

The comparisons are formal research descriptions with the same stipulated physical kernel, **not** proof that each is a genuinely possible complete reality under the project's strong modal thesis. The fact that L and C select different physical sets is derived from the specified rule and input; which sets correspond to fields, their content and cross-aspect unity are not. At the tie setting `t=1/2`, L is unresolved; it must not be reinterpreted as an empty population or lack of experience.

## 3. What evidence would discriminate the packages?

The following is a **logic table**, not a claim that such evidence has been obtained or that the proposed targets are easy to observe. The two truth values concern the same predefined episode, aspect mapping and designated population. A positive means a justified, target-specific first-person occurrence; a negative means a justified absence claim with adequate coverage—not merely an absent response, failed match, lack of statistical significance or a patient saying nothing. If the subject is unknown, the reporter cannot be linked, the aspect was not present, or no defensible negative test exists, label it `unknown`.

| Evidence about `(Jlocal, Jglobal)` | L-exclusive | C-full | C-layered |
|---|---|---|---|
| `(1,1)` | Challenges its no-cross-side conjunction | Consistent | Challenges its stipulated local disunity |
| `(0,1)` | Challenges its no-global-conjunction restriction | Challenges full local conjunction | Consistent |
| `(0,0)` | Consistent | Challenges both conjunction commitments | Challenges its global-conjunction commitment |
| `(1,0)` | Challenges its no-local-conjunction restriction | Challenges its global conjunction | Challenges both its local-disunity and global-unity commitments |
| Either target unknown | No complete discrimination from this table alone | Same | Same |

A supported positive on either target can in principle challenge **L-exclusive** if its subject, target and physical mapping are secured; a positive local conjunction alone also challenges the particular C-layered completion. A supported negative is much harder: inability to report may reflect access, attention, inference, memory, motor channels or response demands. To challenge an existential positive claim with a negative requires adequate access to the **entire designated field population**, not just lack of a report from one accessible channel. A first-person positive report concerns the reporting perspective; it does not automatically count every other possible subject. Some credible evidence might support one target while leaving the other unknown, so partial conclusions are possible even though the full two-bit row is unavailable.

The `(1,0)` row is deliberately adversarial: it challenges **all three** completed packages. It does not validate a fourth package or prove the evidence right. Any conflict can also implicate the assumed physical mapping, content definitions, reporter identity, observation model, or boundary rule; it does not uniquely locate the failing premise. Prespecified uncertainty ranges and a common observation model—not a report kernel selected after seeing the data—are needed for probabilistic decisions. This is a refinement of the earlier [same-window conjunction comparison](SPLIT_BRAIN_FIRST_PERSON_CONSTITUTION_COMPARISON.md), not a retraction of its conditional logic: that comparison meant designated aspects with an explicit no-encompassing-field assumption, not all possible meanings of “global.”

### What the existing clinical evidence does and does not say

Pinto et al. report that both patients described seeing their whole visual field and unchanged felt unity; this is real, relevant first-person evidence, not something to dismiss as an illusion. Their cross-field detection and localization performances also challenge a rigid response-hand model. In the two-stimulus matching test, only one participant was assessed; difficulty there is meaningful but is not proof that local conjoint experience was absent. Their general reports are not established as item-specific, layer-specific judgments in the same 120 ms trial. The uninspected supplement could contain relevant context. Neither the known reports nor the behaviors identify the four-spin L/C input, establish the designated four aspects, or classify either target above with the coverage needed for the table. Lopez's attentional interpretation offers one reason to pursue the distinction, not an independently calibrated test of it.

## 4. Quality is a separate question

E-L's stated pair-level quality hypothesis is `q=(1−s_i s_j)/2`, for an outer pair in the chosen encoding, with `q=1` postulated to have a designated unpleasant rather than neutral character. This is **not** a visual-local layer, an attention score, pain intensity, an observed neural statistic, or proof of real unpleasantness. Nor is the q of a pair automatically the q or valence of the C-selected whole. The integrated C rival has no specified whole-field quality law, and the C-layered completion above supplies none. Their lack of a quality law is an explicit explanatory debt, not a reason to assign either E-L's quality map without argument.

A useful quality comparison already exists: the [current-versus-history audit](CURRENT_MISMATCH_VERSUS_HISTORY_EXPERIENTIAL_GATE.md) holds L's selected pair and physical histories fixed while contrasting current q with a different onset-only *stipulated* unpleasantness assignment. The two assignments make conditional differences, but neither has an independently calibrated real-person q or a proven report link. The practical test requires separate current unpleasantness reports and intensity, independently specified preferences and physical measurement, a fixed episode and context, and uncertainty for history-dependent access and report. If the physical preference or mapping cannot be established, stop rather than relabel a measurable proxy as q.

Even if a future test robustly constrains a q-to-report relationship, that prediction would not alone explain **why** the contrast is unpleasant, establish its subject's boundary, or identify the metaphysical grounding direction. Pain intensity, unpleasantness, expectation, fear, anxiety, mood and valence are distinct constructs.

## 5. What would make the comparison informative?

A credible common-domain test would first state, for a realistic and ethically appropriate observational setting:

1. **Physical domain and alternatives:** independently define the subsystem, inputs, time scale, influence/selection procedure and uncertainty, including residual pathways and alternative factorizations. Do not assume two hemispheres are the two toy pairs. No intervention or clinical procedure is justified merely by this conceptual exercise.
2. **Content and targets:** specify in advance what counts as local and global aspects, whether each actually occurs, their time window, and the relevant conjunction. Control fixation, leakage, attention, timing, memory and potential cueing; measure what the apparatus can rule out rather than assuming zero unmeasured pathways.
3. **Perspective and evidence:** identify the reporting perspective and record first-person descriptions, uncertainty and nonresponse, alongside separate task/behavioral controls. Develop condition-valid evidence for a conjunction that does not simply assume C or L. For a negative existential judgment, justify coverage of every designated relevant field; if not possible, leave it unknown.
4. **Prediction and calibration:** freeze the competing complete incidence/content/report packages, clinically appropriate contrasts, error/uncertainty assumptions and decision criteria before relevant outcomes. A common report model must be justified under the actual context; if unknown or ranges overlap, retain an inconclusive result. Handle correlated trials and individual differences rather than treating repetitions as independent subjects.
5. **Ground and quality:** test boundaries and q-quality separately. Even an ideal justified conjunction result could distinguish these specified incidence packages while leaving the matched experiential-base and neutral-base L descriptions observationally identical by construction, as in the [subject/record interface](SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION_INTERFACE_RESULTS.md). Other, fully specified grounding theories could still yield distinct predictions; no universal indistinguishability theorem follows.

At present these prerequisites are not met, and the access requirement for ruling out unreported perspectives may be intrinsically difficult or infeasible. The reasonable decision is **do not apply the table as a clinical verdict**. The work identifies what to ask of a bridge rather than pretending the missing bridge has been supplied.

## 6. Framework consequences

Experience and first-person evidence remain genuine starting points. The whole being intrinsically experiential does not itself settle which local fields exist, whether unity is total or layered, how qualities arise, or whether the whole is itself a universal subject. Nor does a neutral-ground rival derive these facts simply by matching outputs. The source encourages a less coarse target for evaluation but does not decide L, C or any actual subject count.

C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, not just structural consistency; B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open. This source reading is not a q calibration, a new experiment, a full reading of Klinge's book, or a substantive battery pass.
