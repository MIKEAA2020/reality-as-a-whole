# Why have we not fully solved the problem of time?

## The diagnosis

**Concrete problem:** the repository can construct internal change in stationary constrained models and can compare some clocks, yet it cannot demonstrate that its account supplies one physically consistent description of the same clocked events across the relevant quantum-gravitational regimes. An earlier document declared the problem closed, but its own [evidence audit](TIME_SOLUTION_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md) retracts that conclusion.

**Deepest supported diagnosis:** we do not yet know, from the constitution and laws of the whole, how to obtain and consistently compare the physically meaningful relations that function as clocks, events and records—without treating a chosen time variable, factorization, ordering rule, or classical spacetime as an unexplained external input. This is a *diagnosis of the present explanatory gap*, not a theorem that such a construction is impossible or that there is one universally agreed metaphysical root of every time problem.

There are two levels to keep distinct:

1. **Physical root:** the usual external time of ordinary quantum theory is not an automatically available physical clock in generally covariant gravity. Internal clocks are themselves dynamical quantum systems. Their domains, measurements, backreaction and clock changes must be treated within the theory; different reductions or quantizations can be inequivalent. This is a technical and conceptual problem, not merely a verbal paradox. Anderson surveys several distinct facets [7](https://nyaspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nyas.12510).
2. **Deeper root for the user's programme:** the desired zero/point–sphere whole must itself explain why there are determinate physical relations, actual locally ordered events and experiences, and applicable clock laws—rather than merely represent them in a model whose ingredients are selected from outside the explanation. That explanatory task is not discharged by a vanishing scalar constraint. Nor has the formal work proved that the user's self-sufficiency proposal cannot do it.

A universal external clock is **not** required by this diagnosis. A successful answer could use a clock-neutral relational structure, local clock patches and actual records, or a different formulation; no single global monotonic time need exist. And a timeless whole need not be imagined as later being made or animated from outside: internal order can be part of its constitution. What has not been established is why the particular physical and experiential order obtains, and how its applicable descriptions fit together.

## 1. Why the apparent freeze occurs—and why it is not the whole problem

In a compact, boundaryless canonical model, a Hamiltonian constraint can take the form

```text
C |Ψ⟩ = 0.
```

A change in the redundant label generated by C does not create a new physical state. In GR the full situation involves local Hamiltonian and momentum constraints, their domain/algebra, and possible boundary charges; it is **not** generally captured by one global matrix or the claim that every notion of energy vanishes.

**Why does that look like no change?** Ordinary quantum mechanics usually presupposes an external t against which a system state changes. If the corresponding label is gauge, that comparison is not directly available. But the inference “C annihilates the whole, therefore nothing internally differs” is invalid: one can ask how one physical quantity is correlated with another.

The project's [stationary sphere-echo result](ZERO_PAGE_WOOTTERS_1983_RELATIONAL_SPHERE.md) makes the distinction concrete. The stipulated 20-step clock/echo history is stationary under its constructed cyclic shift, yet two relational J-sign questions at ticks 7 and 10 have disagreement weight 1/2 in the specified model. The tests check the actual original echo histories and the separately constructed reduced clock model. This shows a *possible mechanism* for internal change without an external whole-clock. It does not show that the actual universe uses that clock, that the selected order follows from zero, or that a subject experiences it.

Even a simpler fixed-constraint model makes the same point. With

```text
C = −|1⟩⟨1|_C⊗I + I⊗|1⟩⟨1|_S,
|Ψ±⟩ = (|00⟩ ± |11⟩)/√2,
```

both states obey `C|Ψ±⟩=0`, and both separate subsystem densities are `I/2`. Nevertheless the *joint*, constraint-commuting observable `R=|00⟩⟨11|+|11⟩⟨00|` has expectation +1 or −1. Discarding the relation loses a physical distinction that the joint observable retains. The repository's [intrinsic-clock correlation test](INTRINSIC_TIME_CLOCK_REMOVAL_AND_CORRELATION_GATE.md) works through this example and a clock POVM. It does not decide whether every intrinsic-time formulation loses that information.

**First root:** the problem was never simply “a zero whole cannot contain change.” It is how to specify and justify the *right internal relational observables and their physical interpretation*, rather than smuggling an external time back in or discarding the correlations.

## 2. Why naming an internal clock does not finish the job

A clock must be a suitable physical variable or instrument on a specified domain, with a usable relation to the events being described. In the project's classical compact FLRW model, `φ` can serve as a local/global-on-the-selected-branch clock when its conserved momentum is nonzero:

```text
a(φ) = a₀ exp[−σ √(κ/6) (φ−φ₀)].
```

The same solution has zero Hamiltonian constraint and positive local scalar density. This is a real mathematical counterexample to “constraint zero means no change,” but the model assumes the action, branch, scalar clock and state; it is not a full quantum-gravity or whole-energy theorem. See [the derivation](ZERO_TOTALITY_FLRW_CONSTRAINT_AND_ENERGY.md).

**Why not just choose a clock once and for all?** A chosen clock can turn, recur, interact, fluctuate, have limited resolution or fail on part of the state space. In the explicitly solved [turning-clock atlas](TURNING_CLOCK_ATLAS.md), `q=cos θ` takes the same reading on the two sides of a turn. Retaining orientation or using another chart distinguishes those branches; q alone need not. In that model, discarding orientation at `q=0` makes the orthogonal system states `|+x⟩` and `|−x⟩` yield the same reduced output. No deterministic processing of that *same reduced output* can reconstruct both inputs. Keeping a record, restricting the input family, or using a different instrument can avoid that particular loss; the example is not a no-go for all clock switches.

The project also constructs exact changes of two clocks and transports the **same** specified sequential instruments in a finite model; see [clock-choice equivalence](CLOCK_CHOICE_EQUIVALENCE.md). This is genuine positive progress, but it assumes one fixed physical constraint and favorable clock evaluations. It does not prove that all independent quantum-gravity reductions are equivalent. Höhn and Vanrietvelde similarly construct switches through a clock-neutral Dirac description in a toy model; that is important prior work, not a general resolution by our example [8](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04153).

A further obstruction is not merely lack of ingenuity. In our particular interacting geometry-clock model, normalizable bound eigenstates occur. A normalized real-valued POVM covariant under the specified scalar-parameter evolution on **the entire** interacting sector would give an eigenstate a translation-invariant probability measure on the real line. Such a probability measure cannot exist: equal masses for all unit intervals would either have infinite sum or all be zero. Hence that specific global clock cannot exist on the full sector. This does **not** forbid clocks on restricted sectors, local or periodic clocks, an additional no-reading outcome, or a different generator. The analytic result and its assumptions are in [the interacting spectral analysis](ZERO_SPHERE_INTERACTING_CLOCK_SPECTRUM_AND_BOUND_STATES.md).

**Second root:** a clock reading is a physical, state- and domain-dependent relation, not a universally invertible coordinate supplied for free. A successful theory must state what is retained and what is lost, not force every clock to be global and unitary.

## 3. Why multiple clock choices can change physics rather than just notation

If two clock descriptions come from the **same** physical theory and represent the same preparations, conditioning events, instruments and records, their overlapping predictions should agree when those objects are transported correctly. But two constructions can instead quantize after different reductions, impose different boundary conditions or select different domains. Merely calling each choice a “coordinate” does not prove that the resulting quantum theories and their predictions are equivalent.

A concrete external example is the flat-FLRW cosmology of Gielen and Menéndez-Pidal: for the particular quantizations and unitarity choices studied, different clock choices lead to different conclusions about singularity behavior, and their later analysis finds a further recollapse option [2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.05357), [10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.02660). This does not prove every choice of internal clock is inequivalent or that their model is the actual universe. It shows why clock-dependent quantization, domains and boundary conditions are not a cosmetic concern.

**Why do we not have the required common account?** Our positive switch and instrument results hold in specific finite or symmetry-reduced systems. Correction: the later [full continuum geometry-slice result](ZERO_GEOMETRY_CLOCK_FULL_CONTINUUM_SLICE.md) proves full-state reconstruction on almost every slice for the selected single-window model, beyond the earlier cyclic-subspace result. The subsequent [autonomous pointer–memory model](ZERO_AUTONOMOUS_SPHERE_GEOMETRY_POINTER_MEMORY.md) incorporates a physical record. The distinct noncommuting [two-window model](ZERO_AUTONOMOUS_TWO_GEOMETRY_WINDOW_RECORDS.md) also has a [full-state continuum reconstruction](ZERO_TWO_WINDOW_FULL_SLICE_CLOCK.md) using almost every 13-tuple of slices; one slice suffices for its scattering sector, but full-state single-slice recovery is not established there. An [integrated extension](ZERO_INTEGRATED_SPHERE_AUTONOMOUS_INCIDENCE.md) gives a 26-slice sufficient bound for its changed constraint. These are ideal complete-complex-signal results, not operational finite-data geometry clocks or equivalence of independently quantized reductions. The [finite-record continuation](ZERO_TWO_WINDOW_FINITE_RECORD_CLOCK_COMPARISON.md) demonstrates a concrete limitation and transports one instrument in a regulated coordinate change. The [inhomogeneous evolution](ZERO_TOTALITY_NONLINEAR_GRAVITY_CLOCK_EVOLUTION.md) checks a classical planar reduction and a constant-lapse redescription; it is not the full quantum gravitational constraint algebra or a proof of arbitrary refoliation invariance.

**Third root:** we have not derived a physically and mathematically controlled map from the candidate whole to its various clock-relative descriptions, including their domains, interventions, records and overlapping probabilities. Depending on the theory, the relevant reductions may be equivalent only on a sector, may describe different operations, or may genuinely be inequivalent. That distinction has to be proved, not settled in advance.

## 4. Why this remains an issue even if the whole is timeless

A mathematical state or set of correlations can encode an ordered family. That is an important positive result: it avoids assuming that the whole must move through an external time. But a proposed solution must still address, as applicable:

* **Physical:** what establishes the state, constraint/algebra, clock and event observables, and the comparison of different internal descriptions?
* **Operational:** what makes a clock reading and a record available to a physical subsystem; what happens with backreaction, repeated crossings and erased information?
* **Spacetime:** how are local causal structure, clock rates and the classical relativistic regime recovered, without treating a preferred foliation as fundamental by accident?
* **Arrow:** what physical conditions and coarse-graining explain the observed direction of durable records and entropy? A timeless constraint, an interaction or a bounce alone does not establish an arrow.
* **Experience:** what accounts for the actual subject and its experienced succession? This is an additional issue for the user's larger physical–experiential aim, not a standard prerequisite for every technical formulation of quantum gravity. A correlation or report bit is not automatically an experience; equally, genuine first-person evidence is not negated by that observation.

These questions interact, but they are not identical. A successful local relational-clock theorem could resolve a precise physical facet without deriving consciousness. Conversely, identifying experience with time by definition would not establish the physical constraint algebra or a clock instrument.

## 5. Keep asking why: what is the meta-level issue?

1. **Why is the ordinary parameter unavailable as a global physical clock?** In generally covariant formulations, coordinate labels can be redundant and the system includes whatever could serve as a clock.
2. **Why is selecting an internal variable not enough?** It can have turning points, quantum uncertainty, interaction, domain and measurement limitations; multiple choices need not lead to the same quantum construction.
3. **Why do we not know which account is right?** We lack, for the candidate whole, an independently justified physical state/algebra and complete clock-neutral or otherwise equivalent prescription that recovers the *same physical questions* across the relevant domains and produces the observed classical limit.
4. **Why has the zero framework not yet supplied that prescription?** Its explicit clock, coupling, state, clock labels, transport and record rules are currently model inputs. A constraint set to zero allows internal change, but zero as a number does not by itself determine which nontrivial constraint, state or event algebra is actual.
5. **What is still deeper?** The user's objective is for zero's own constitution to necessitate—not merely be compatible with—the physical/experiential organization and its internal ordering. We do not yet have a demonstrated constitutive derivation or a justified terminal explanatory principle for that. This is the current meta-level research problem, **not** a proof that “bare zero” is the correct or incorrect ontology, or that the user’s no-residue demand is unattainable.

It would be a mistake to declare a logical vicious circle merely because an ordering relation is internally specified: a self-contained structure can have internal order without being externally assembled in time. It would also be a mistake to say “the order is internal” and thereby treat its physical, experiential and explanatory grounding as already demonstrated. Which of these becomes an ultimate bedrock depends on the as-yet-undetermined constitution and on what counts as a sufficient explanation.

## 6. Why our earlier work overstated completion

The failure was partly methodological. The historical [“Problem of Time: Closed”](PROBLEM_OF_TIME_CLOSED.md) aggregated passing checks for different models and labeled broad interpretive facets as solved. Its warning and the [evidence audit](TIME_SOLUTION_EVIDENCE_AUDIT.md) correctly supersede those conclusions. A finite Page–Wootters example does not establish full gravity; a modular-flow cocycle does not by itself identify unique physical time; a modeled bounce does not derive a thermodynamic arrow; and a record or workspace model does not establish phenomenal presentness.

This does not erase the actual progress. The stationary echo, branchwise FLRW evolution, nontrivial interacting spectral obstruction, clock charts and instrument transport all answer **bounded** questions. The mistake would be to add their labels together as if they proved that their assumptions, domains and physical meanings form a single derivation.

## 7. Most feasible next discriminating step

Work in topological order, starting with an already specified interacting clock model rather than attempting a universal conclusion:

1. **One physical experiment:** start from the existing autonomous two-window pointer–memory constraint and its already specified finite-resolution records, preparation and physical state/inner product. Determine whether they suffice for a geometry-clock instrument, including no-reading/multiple-crossing cases and backreaction; do not claim that this closed interaction itself is new.
2. **Same question in two clocks:** on a stated common domain containing the relevant interacting sectors, transport the *same* instrument, not a newly selected measurement. Compare complete joint outcome probabilities and normalization, including sectors that the detector mixes. If a clock cannot describe a sector, expose the failure or preserve the additional outcome rather than silently discard it.
3. **Prove or bound the continuum step:** build on the single-window full-state and two-window 13-slice full-state continuum results; analyze operational finite-record information, stability, and the still-open full-state single-slice question. Bound and scattering sectors must be retained. A finite-grid success is not a continuum proof.
4. **Then gravity:** extend or compare the result with a concrete inhomogeneous constrained model, checking the actual constraints and refoliation as applicable. An arbitrary foliation, autonomous detector, and quantum physical inner product cannot be presumed from the homogeneous example.
5. **Then the larger constitution:** ask whether the same proposed principle derives those physical relations and the actual subject/quality/incidence bridge. Neither formal time order nor a recorded event, by itself, establishes subjective passage or an objective total energy.

A counterexample at any step would diagnose precisely which assumption fails; success would be a bounded advance, not automatic solution of the entire problem of time. The existing [time research roadmap](TIME_RESEARCH_ROADMAP.md) records the closest prior-art comparisons and a similar operational programme.
