# Visual stimulation: what same-current reports do and do not identify

## Decision

The pinned [visual-stimulation dataset](https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds005169.v1.0.0) is useful as a **selected-case report-metadata stress test**, not a decisive test of experiential misalignment, subject unity, persistence or boundaries. Its annotations vary even within a patient at the same recorded current. That rules out an exceptionally simple patient-plus-current lookup for these observed annotations, but it does not rule out any specified experiential-misalignment theory or distinguish it from experience-respecting rivals that also use site and brain state.

We wrote the [fixed plan](STIMULATION_VISUAL_REPORT_MATCHED_INTENSITY_FIXED_PLAN.md) after reading the paper and two event files, but before the full metadata scan. Its strict three-row eligibility missed 105 of 112 files. The proposed held-out benchmark is therefore **not reported**. The explicit [post-inspection amendment](STIMULATION_VISUAL_REPORT_METADATA_AMENDMENT.md) permits only a descriptive all-file scan and an optimistic in-sample oracle. Neither is prospective or independent validation.

## What was actually checked

From the [pinned mirror](https://github.com/OpenNeuroDatasets/ds005169/tree/ff529027bef934f384e7385f1b83d7190ef307d0), the complete 258,150-byte compressed metadata archive was read without extracting links, executable code or annexed signals. The Git commit's root tree was checked and 112 event TSVs and 112 matching event sidecars were verified against the tree's Git blob IDs. The tree is nontruncated. The [receipt](verification/visual_stimulation_report_benchmark.json) records the archive SHA-256, counts and checks; the [verifier](verification/visual_stimulation_report_benchmark.py) and [tests](verification/visual_stimulation_report_benchmark_test.py) reproduce the calculation after refetching the exact pinned inputs. No raw report text or participant identifiers are retained here.

Each of 112 event files has one row with a recorded current, contact field, a nonempty label and binary values for all symptom columns; these rows span 20 subject directories. All 112 stimulation markers have zero event duration: this does **not** mean the train lasted zero seconds. The paper describes trains of up to five seconds. There are 105 four-row files and seven three-row files; one lacks an exact `poststim` label. We have not established the semantics of all extra rows or independently audited the recording timeline. For the current-bearing rows, all have at least one symptom flag, and none has the word `sham` in the label. Neither observation shows that the authors failed to collect sham or negative controls; it only describes these particular rows. All 112 matching EEG, marker and header paths are Git symlinks; their linked signal content was not read.

Across all 112 event sidecars, `sym_color` is described as “Color (black and white) hallucinations”, while `sym_non_color` says “Non-color (black and white) hallucinations”. The paper defines color as the rest of the spectrum and non-color as black and white. This is a sidecar wording inconsistency, not proof that the flags are wrong. We did not treat the two descriptions as equivalent labels or silently repair them.

## Matched-current descriptive constraint

Define a group by subject directory and exact listed current, without requiring the same contact pair, session or state. Allow an oracle to see all annotations and choose its own binary output for each group; a group contributes the minority-label count as its minimum possible in-sample mistakes. There are 24 such groups with at least two rows. For the four initially chosen flags:

| Annotation | Ones among 112 | Patients with at least one | Mixed repeated groups | Oracle minimum errors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Static | 71 | 17 | 11 | 17/112 |
| Dynamic | 25 | 10 | 10 | 12/112 |
| Continuous | 68 | 16 | 10 | 14/112 |
| Intermittent | 28 | 9 | 10 | 15/112 |

These are annotation counts, not counts of statistically independent people or independent events; zeros are not a validated claim of experiential absence. The oracle uses labels from the same rows and is **optimistically in-sample**. The 17/112 static constraint, for example, says only that even a separate best constant static-flag guess for each patient/current group must mislabel 17 of these rows. It does not say current cannot affect experience, or that identical neural states have different experiences: different sites, stimulation histories and recordings remain possible. The post-inspection all-file criterion makes the descriptive calculation exploratory.

## Why this cannot constitute subjects or felt quality

Stimulation at different sites can elicit distinct reported visual characteristics at the same recorded current; this is a constraint on dose-only shortcuts. A candidate mapping based on relational dynamics, a rival mapping based on stimulated site and context, and a report-oriented phenomenological mapping may all accommodate it. No q/preferred-coupling measure was calculated, and phase locking to a common stimulation reference is not itself the physical frustration or preferred interaction identified in [the phase gate](OBSERVED_PHASE_LAGS_VERSUS_PHYSICAL_FRUSTRATION_GATE.md). Spatial network reach is not a demonstrated boundary of a single subject; repeating reports at different clinical sessions does not determine identity through time. Visual-content flags contain no independently calibrated unpleasantness scale. A model that merely attaches an experience label to a network remains a postulate, not a bridge to why it is felt.

A defensible next test would first locate the linked **paired lower-intensity and sham recordings**, their report timing and clinical selection logs, then independently validate stimulation-artifact handling and define in advance competing, experience-respecting mappings for the same people and anatomical coverage. Even a successful predictive comparison would require a separately defended constitution bridge, rather than identifying prediction with experience. This gate recommends no new invasive stimulation.

## Source coverage and reconciliation

[Mihai et al., *Multi-modal connectivity of the brain underlying visual hallucinations evoked by high-frequency intracranial stimulations*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brs.2025.05.138): main paper text, methods, results, table and figure captions were read; figure images, six supplementary files, custom analysis code and underlying EEG were **not** inspected. The paper describes case-by-case sham, response replication and typically half-intensity negative trials; it reports a difference in evoked response magnitude between positive and negative stimulations, but intensity and report status differ together. These are real controls and should not be erased; they do not by themselves identify the effect of report status holding stimulation constant. Alternating-polarity stimulation and comb filtering are the authors' method for separating artifacts under stated assumptions, not proof that every derived signal is artifact-free. Their paper also notes sparse clinical sampling and variability.

The paper reports 130 stimulation sites producing visual symptoms in 22 patients, excludes two patients with modified anatomy for network analysis, and its figure caption specifies 115 sites in 20 patients; the 112 event files in 20 subject directories need not be identical to 115 sites. The two-patient exclusion clarifies the patient-count difference, but the three-site/file discrepancy and complete site-to-file correspondence remain unverified; later analyses have different artifact-curation eligibility. The dataset README gives 115 stimulations and 22 patients: its statement is not reconciled with the 20 directories. We did not inspect the supplementary table or reconcile individual sites and do not infer why the counts differ.

[Barborica et al., *Imaging the effective networks associated with cortical function through intracranial high-frequency stimulation*](https://doi.org/10.1002/hbm.25749): search excerpts only; full methods, figures, proofs of artifact assumptions, and data not inspected. The preceding [dataset record](https://doi.org/10.18112/openneuro.ds005169.v1.0.0) and its pinned README, all event TSVs and event sidecars were examined; other metadata and all linked EEG/MRI contents were not systematically reviewed. No source supplies a completed bridge from these measurements to a plural-subject ontology. C3 remains **full structural and experiential intelligibility**; B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open, as do the other previously open battery items.
