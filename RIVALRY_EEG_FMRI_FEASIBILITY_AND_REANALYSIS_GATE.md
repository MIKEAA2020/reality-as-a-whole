# Can an EEG–fMRI rivalry dataset test the experience bridge?

## Decision

The Roy–Jamison dataset is a useful **retrospective test of whether a frequency-tagged EEG decoder tracks a participant's continuous reports**, and potentially of EEG-informed fMRI contrasts. It is **not** the prospective random-prompt, motion/eye-tracking experiment in [the existing audit protocol](RIVALRY_RANDOM_AUDIT_EXPERIENCE_BRIDGE.md). It cannot by itself establish an error rate against experience, isolate phenomenal constitution, distinguish the C/M subject rules, or test their intervention gate.

This is a documentation and accessibility audit plus an explicitly **synthetic** design witness. No participant archive, EEG, fMRI, event file or group-map image has been obtained or analyzed in this environment. Published findings below are not new results. A source-specific and actionable advance is the identification of several unresolved reconstruction choices and the specification of a held-out agreement estimand that can be calculated once the data are accessible. This is not a solution of the experience bridge.

## 1. Existing experiment and what was already found

[Roy et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2017.02.041) studied 20 healthy participants with simultaneous EEG and fMRI during rivalry and replay. Green and red stimuli were frequency-tagged near 6.67 and 8.57 Hz. In rivalry, different stimuli reached the two eyes; smooth replay delivered physically changing input to both eyes, using a one-second expanding wedge with durations derived from that participant's earlier rivalry reports. Participants reported throughout both conditions. Instantaneous replay was also included. See also the published [methods in full](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5531216/) and the prior SSVEP methods of [Jamison et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jneumeth.2015.01.024).

Roy et al. report that SSVEP dominance crossings preceded green and red dominance button presses by averages of **1,142 and 912 ms**, respectively, in rivalry, versus **312 and 381 ms** in smooth replay. These are their estimates from button-aligned averages; they are not individually verified trial errors and not direct experience onsets. For their interaction `[rivalry (reported transition − SSVEP cross)] − [smooth replay (reported transition − SSVEP cross)]`, they report a 50-voxel left mid-cingulate cluster at MNI `(0, −31, 46)`, `t = −5.37`, cluster `qFDR = .011`. Their peak-versus-cross interaction did not survive FDR correction ([Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5531216/table/T1/)). These are *published* results, not an independent replication. A contrast found in the same data is not independent confirmation if its selected peak is reused as an ROI.

The contrast contains a meaningful electrophysiological comparison, but ongoing report, attention, eye and hand movements, different physical stimuli, possible signal artifacts and timing differences remain possible explanations. It does not isolate a causal conscious substrate. Smooth replay approximates rivalry, not a random intervention that holds all nonphenomenal variables fixed. Neither condition gives an independent ground-truth label for felt experience.

## 2. Exactly what is accessible and what is not

The [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/5035511) lists 20 participant ZIP files totaling 16.8 GB, an electrode-location file and a [dataset README](https://zenodo.org/records/5035511/files/rivalry-eeg-fmri-upload-readme-20170225.pdf?download=1). Its README describes per-scan preprocessed EEG MATLAB files, button, visual-trial and TR latencies; 64 channels, 250 Hz, and preprocessed fMRI as `.hdr`/`.img` pairs. This is a description, **not inspection of the actual fields or event contents**. Raw MR and cardioballistic artifact removal cannot be replayed from these released preprocessed EEG alone without the corresponding raw recordings and methods.

The metadata lists `S09.zip` as 606.6 MB with MD5 `e842f75d4a09166eaf4e46371f391c9a`. The browser-accessible ZIP preview responded “Too many files inside the ZIP file.” Direct `curl` requests from this execution environment to both Zenodo and NeuroVault failed at TLS connection; the literature-reading tool can fetch their text but did not deliver participant binaries for analysis. The [NeuroVault collection API](https://neurovault.org/api/collections/2180/images/?limit=100) lists nine group T maps, including unthresholded maps, but its metadata is not the image data, first-level design, or independent confirmation. An OpenNeuro rivalry MEG dataset exists but is a different experiment and cannot fill the Roy-file gap without a separate audit.

The README states research, noncommercial use and requests citation of both Roy and Jamison; check the applicable rights and permissions before redistribution. Do not automatically copy participant data into this public repository. No raw-byte checksum has been verified here.

## 3. Reconstruction hazards to resolve from files or authors

1. **Time length.** The README describes 129 MRI volumes per scan at TR 2.2 s and says skip five, which would leave 124. Roy describes a *typical* design with 1,452 volumes across 12 runs, 121 per run. These are incompatible as a simple accounting identity, but may describe different processing stages or selections; the reason is unknown. Count actual files, timestamps, dropped volumes and retained scan indices. Do not silently delete three more volumes or assume one source is wrong.
2. **EEG filtering.** The README describes released EEG bandpass 1–35 Hz; Roy's methods describe 0.5–30 Hz. The processing stage difference or other reason is unverified. Treat released data as 1–35 Hz *as described*, not raw or automatically identical to the authors' analyzed series. Exact replay requires provenance or author clarification; extra filtering cannot generally undo a prior filter.
3. **Design matrix.** Roy describes three event regressors *per run*, a block-diagonal run-specific design plus motion regressors, but also describes nine condition-level contrast weights. A hand-built nine-column design need not be the published analysis. Obtain actual SPM design, run/session contrasts and scaling if possible. The [supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/instance/5531216/bin/NIHMS878886-supplement.pdf) includes an example design image, not a machine-readable design matrix. Check durations, motion files, high-pass filtering, serial-correlation assumptions, and contrast weights.
4. **Event semantics.** Roy's fMRI transition events are *midpoints of reported mixed periods*, not green/red dominance button times. Its GLM EEG peak/cross events are extrema of `abs(f1−f2)`, whereas other described peak-aligned analyses use individual envelope peaks. Do not conflate these event definitions. Verify units, sample indexing, timestamp origins, trial boundaries, multi-button behavior, overlaps and missing periods against the `.mat` structure. PDF text extraction appears to shuffle some scan-type lists; verify each condition against original layout and archive or study records rather than copying an uncertain extracted list.
5. **Tag assignment across papers.** Roy and the release README specify green `6.67` and red `8.57` Hz. [Jamison's earlier methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359627/) say red `6.67` and green `8.54` Hz. The papers describe samples of 20 and 23, respectively. This is a documentation difference, not proof that either paper or a particular subject file is wrong. Verify color, frequency, stimulus timing and any subject-specific counterbalancing from original records; do not silently use the other paper's mapping. The 0.03 Hz difference is not a normal rounding of 8.57 to two decimals and merits checking; the actual stimulus frequency and analysis frequency may differ.
6. **Published minor sign inconsistency.** In Table 1F the manuscript prints a `+4.07` t-stat under a “Negative Cluster” heading for the left mid orbital gyrus; its text calls this deactivation and reports the cluster nonsignificant after FDR. This is not the significant mid-cingulate result in 1E. Without inspecting the actual map or source files, do not correct the sign or infer an analysis error.

These are reproducibility gates, not claims that the published finding is false. Artifact removal, SSVEP extraction and cluster inference have substantial established literature; follow the cited methods rather than presenting their reimplementation as a new discovery.

## 4. Why distinguishable event times may still be difficult to separate in BOLD

The existing paper uses an HRF-convolved event GLM. As a **toy diagnostic**, [the executable witness](verification/rivalry_hrf_timing_witness.py) constructs two identical periodic event trains shifted by 1.142 s, samples a double-gamma-shaped HRF at 2.2 s, and includes an intercept. The two centered columns correlate **0.9078**. Under ordinary least squares with spherical equal-variance errors and only these columns, including both rather than just the same single regressor inflates its coefficient variance by **5.685×**. The script and [JSON output](verification/rivalry_hrf_timing_witness.json) specify every onset and assumption.

This is a *possibility demonstration*, not the real Roy design or evidence of its collinearity. In particular, the 1.142 s is the published average cross-to-green-**dominance-button** estimate, while the fMRI transition regressor is the **mixed-period midpoint**; they cannot be substituted. Real event timing, excluded volumes, filtering, additional regressors, colored noise and HRF variation change the answer. Once real files and the first-level model are available, inspect residualized column correlations, singular values, estimability of the declared contrasts, variance and sensitivity to HRF and timing uncertainty by run. Do not infer that an insignificant contrast proves equal neural processes.

## 5. A concrete retrospective EEG–report test

**Question:** in the recorded continuous-report context, how well can a decoder trained on other data predict held-out green versus red reports from frequency-tagged EEG, with uncertainty and mixed periods accounted for? This is a different content, modality and sampling regime from the existing OKN/random-prompt proposal. It is exploratory with respect to this already published dataset, not an independent preregistered experiment or a fresh discovery merely because our analysis is frozen before reading the archives.

1. **Inventory and alignment first.** Privately hash files, inspect actual variables and shapes, compare scan IDs and conditions with the source, and confirm timing and color mapping. Establish which files contain usable EEG and events; count missing/ambiguous scans, time spans and exclusions by person. If labels or units cannot be established, stop; do not infer them from model performance.
2. **Define the target and denominator before testing.** Use a fixed time grid within visual stimulus trials; each eligible point has its documented contemporaneous report state, including green, red, mixed and no/uncertain/missing state. Denote decoder output `Y` as green/red/unresolved and report `R` as green/red/unresolved (mixed is unresolved for this binary target). For the conservative full-coverage outcome, `d_full` is the fraction of eligible points with `Y ≠ R` **or either output unresolved**, including points for which the report itself is mixed or missing. This is a deliberately stringent binary-coverage measure, not a statement that mixed perception is erroneous. Separately show agreement and class-specific errors on documented pure-report points, their proportion of all eligible time, and confusion/coverage summaries; this conditional outcome is selected by report and must not be presented as all-time performance. Trial definitions, grid spacing and eligible periods must be frozen after inspecting file schema but before analyzing outcome associations.
3. **Keep training and test apart.** Assign whole runs or participants to development versus test; prioritize participant-held-out generalization if enough data exist, and show within-person held-out-run results separately if needed. Fit all preprocessing that learns parameters, spatial weights, normalization, thresholds, color mapping, delay and decoder using development data only. Group temporally adjacent windows in the same split; when filtering across a split is unavoidable, insert a guard longer than the filter's effective impulse response and largest lag, or process runs separately. No test-set label-based sign flip, threshold tuning, channel or ROI selection. Report the lag convention; a frozen lag predicts a time-shifted report, not necessarily instantaneous experience.
4. **Evaluate and quantify uncertainty.** Compute per-participant full-coverage `d_full` then the equally participant-weighted mean; report its distribution, green/red conditional accuracy and unresolved rates, plus the majority-class baseline fixed on training data. Use a prespecified participant-level bootstrap interval as a descriptive uncertainty estimate, retaining entire participants (and all their runs) per resample; report the small `n`, and that this relies on participants being an appropriate independent sample. One participant cannot provide a population confidence interval. Publish denominators, missingness, split assignment and all choices, not only favorable windows.
5. **Stop and interpretation.** A high disagreement challenges the particular frozen decoder/report correspondence in this context; a low value supports only that correspondence. Replay is a useful control, not a direct label of subjective rivalry or proof of transport across tasks. A failed SSVEP extraction is an assay failure, not evidence of absent experience. A successful report decoder does not measure what the person experienced independently of reporting.

The 10% threshold in the prospective protocol is an illustrative proposal for a different motion and eye-movement assay, not an a priori success threshold for this dataset. A genuinely prospective test needs a new protocol and frozen analysis, a suitable data collection and independent data.

## 6. Relation to the framework's actual decision gate

For the adapted binary content target, let `E` be the experiential state and `r` a separately justified, target-condition bound on report-to-experience failure. If it were available, the existing union bound gives `P(Y wrong or unresolved about E) ≤ min(1, d_full + r)` for the specified coding and denominator. The value of `d_full` has **not** been computed here; no numerical `r` is established. Matching reports and EEG, even perfectly, does not by itself produce `r`; the two channels may share error or reflect access/report rather than the episode's qualitative character. Physical replay, higher confidence, repeated questioning or multiple proxies may be informative but do not automatically solve this latent-identification problem.

The stronger [robust intervention contrast](ROBUST_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTRAST.md) additionally requires a defined intervention and independently justified assay-error and physical-gate failure bounds satisfying `(1−η_M)(1−β) > α+η_D(1−α)`. No manipulation in Roy validates the required maintenance-preservation/distribution-removal gate, and no bounds are provided. The dataset therefore cannot distinguish those hypotheses or the subject-constitution rules in [the locality gate](COMPETING_SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION_AND_LOCALITY_GATE.md). The remaining bridge is not repaired by relabeling neural signatures as experience.

## 7. Immediate route to real analysis

Supply **the released `S09.zip`**, or, if that is inconvenient, all its preprocessed EEG `.mat` scan files plus any accompanying scan-condition/timing documentation. The full archive allows checksum comparison with the Zenodo-listed MD5; an extracted subset needs its own SHA-256 hashes and provenance and cannot independently verify that archive checksum. Other participants' corresponding files would be needed for population analysis and proper held-out-person testing. Do not include personal identifiers or upload private data without authorization; the requested files are from a public research release, subject to its stated terms.

With an accessible archive the next action is schema/quality and timing inspection, followed by a transparent **single-person feasibility pilot only**. Freeze the cross-person plan before analyzing held-out participants; if essential run labels, signal quality, time units or split independence cannot be validated, stop and report the missing item. MRI images, motion files and actual SPM design or author clarification are additionally needed for a faithful fMRI reanalysis. Do not claim a replication unless its scope and deviations are documented and actual outputs obtained.
