# Collective causal response is not necessarily collective mechanism

## Result

The previous [higher-order gate](HIGHER_ORDER_CAUSALITY_AND_SUBJECT_SELECTION_GATE.md) showed that a three-bit input can change a collective three-bit output distribution while every one- and two-output marginal remains unchanged. That establishes a collective **operational response** in the defined model. It does **not** establish that the three subsystems interact during the response, or that they form one subject.

Here is a stronger check: the entire previous channel—not merely its pairwise marginals—has both:

1. a **centralized** implementation that receives all three inputs, and
2. a **distributed** implementation where each site receives only its own input and a pre-shared random variable.

The two implementations have exactly the same joint output distribution for every input, with no postselection and no setting-dependent source distribution. With fresh independent shared seeds each trial, even an adaptive sequence of observations of inputs and outputs cannot distinguish them. An idealized intervention on the shared seed *can* distinguish the specified mechanisms. These are mathematical models, not observed devices, subjects or brains.

The constructive gain is a more demanding interface for a proposed subject rule: **capturing higher-order operational dependence is necessary to avoid one blind spot, but is not by itself sufficient to identify the physical mechanism or a subject boundary**. We do not claim a new Bell theorem, a new result about shared randomness, or a literature-wide novelty. The project-specific contribution is the explicit factorization and mechanism comparison applied to the previous gate.

## 1. Prior art and scope of reading

Brunner, Cavalcanti, Pironio, Scarani and Wehner, [*Bell nonlocality*](https://arxiv.org/html/1303.2849v3), discuss local response functions and shared randomness. The abstract, introduction and selected searched passages were inspected, not the complete 60-chunk review, figures or proofs. Bell-local correlations are an established idea: no-signalling, classical locality and quantum correlations are not interchangeable.

The prior gate cites [Kořenek, Sanda and Hlinka](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08295v2), whose complete five-chunk preprint text was read (figures, code and published-version comparison not audited), and [Yang, Wang and Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.03267), of which the abstract, introduction and selected passages were read, not the full paper. Neither paper was replayed. Higher-order synergistic information and causal hypergraphs are established work. The construction here does not calculate their proposed measures.

## 2. One channel, two implementations

For `x,y∈{0,1}³`, let `p(x)=x₁⊕x₂⊕x₃`. The previous channel is

```text
Kκ(y|do(x)) = [1+κ(−1)^(p(x)⊕p(y))]/8,    −1≤κ≤1.
```

The main example remains `κ=1/5`. Let `U,V` be independent fair bits and let `N` be independent of them, with

```text
P(N=0)=(1+κ)/2,    P(N=1)=(1−κ)/2.
```

Draw `(U,V,N)` independently of the chosen input, fresh for each trial. Consider these two physically **different stipulated architectures**:

```text
C, centralized:  Y = ( U,        V,        U⊕V⊕p(x)⊕N )
D, distributed:  Y = ( x₁⊕U,    x₂⊕V,    x₃⊕U⊕V⊕N )
```

C's third output uses all three input values at its central device. In D, site 1 receives `x₁,U`; site 2 receives `x₂,V`; site 3 receives `x₃` and the already shared values needed to form `U⊕V⊕N`. There is **no communication between the sites after input delivery** in D. The shared seeds can have a common preparation in the past; the sites are not assumed to be independently prepared or causally unrelated throughout their history.

For either architecture, fixing `x,y` fixes the noise value `N=p(x)⊕p(y)` and one pair of seed bits; summing over the seed gives precisely `Kκ(y|do(x))`. In D this is a standard local hidden-variable factorization:

```text
Kκ(y|do(x)) = ∑λ P(λ) ∏ᵢ 1[yᵢ = fᵢ(xᵢ,λ)],
λ=(U,V,N),  P(λ) independent of x.
```

So the prior joint response can be generated by shared classical randomness and local responses. It is not a Bell-inequality violation, a quantum derivation, a detection-loophole explanation, or a challenge to actual Bell experiments. For `κ=1/5`, both have the same nonzero **input-parity to output-parity** dependence and the same full transition mutual information `≈0.02905` bits under uniform input interventions. That dependence is real for the specified channel, but alone does not establish a direct interaction or a single processor among the three sites.

This also sharpens interpretation of the earlier gate's phrase “collective dependence”: it denotes a dependence of **the joint response distribution**, not proof of an irreducible physical interaction or subject. At fixed hidden seed, the distributed outputs do depend individually on their local inputs; averaging over the seed hides those dependencies from their single-output marginals. Higher-order statistical or intervention-response structure and the internal mechanism must not be conflated.

## 3. What unlimited external tests can and cannot identify

Assume fresh independent seeds in both architectures on each trial, and restrict the investigator to choosing `x` and observing `y`. Since `Kκ` is identical for every input, any adaptive rule that chooses its next input from past input–output records has the same conditional next-output law under C and D. Induction gives identical transcript distributions for **any finite number** of trials, including randomized adaptive strategies whose randomization is independent of the devices. With equal prior weights, the best discrimination probability is exactly `1/2` under this restricted data interface, however many trials are collected.

This is not a universal impossibility result. It depends on the stipulated input–output interface and fresh-seed condition. If seeds persist, a device leaks extra records, initial conditions differ, or other physical interventions are possible, the indistinguishability need not hold. The verifier explicitly checks all 512 possible three-round output transcripts under one adaptive policy; the all-strategies statement is the induction above, not an extrapolation from that finite test.

A candidate constitution rule that uses only `Kκ` must make the same assignment in these two models. If another candidate explicitly uses the physical routing, it can give them different assignments—but input–output data alone cannot decide which architecture is present. Even once routing is known, there is no demonstrated rule saying that C is one subject or D is three; those would be additional rival phenomenal postulates. Nothing here assumes a universal subject.

## 4. A separating physical intervention, and its limits

Suppose the apparatus explicitly exposes and can safely set the three seed inputs without changing the response wiring. Set `U=V=N=0` and `x=(1,0,0)`:

```text
C returns y=(0,0,1),    D returns y=(1,0,0).
```

Thus an observer who can independently control those seed inputs can, in the specified model, distinguish the architectures in one ideal trial. Merely relabeling a variable does not make such an intervention physically available; it must exist in each implementation and be justified as setting the same declared seed variables. Alternatively, observing the seed reliably with a well-defined joint law could give additional information; conditioning on it is not automatically equivalent to physically intervening on it.

For an illustrative robustness budget, define `T=1` if the recorded output is `(0,0,1)`, and `T=0` otherwise, with missing output conservatively included in the independently bounded readout error. Suppose **separately justified** trial-level bounds, in the intervention condition and under either model, are:

- probability the intended input/seed setting or response wiring fails: at most `η=1/20`;
- probability that the recorded binary `T` differs from the ideal binary value for the actual physical output, including missing: at most `r=3/100`.

Without assuming the failures independent, the union bound gives

```text
C: P(T=1) ≥ 23/25 = 0.92;     D: P(T=1) ≤ 2/25 = 0.08.
```

The bounds are stipulated, not measured. With independent trials and the respective uniform mean bounds, an `n=11` threshold test at sample mean `1/2` has Hoeffding upper bound below `0.025` for each model. The bound assumes the models and `η,r` were fixed in advance; it excludes the cost of estimating/calibrating them, participant and device dependence, drift, and multiple comparisons. No real device, neural intervention or experiential assay is thereby validated. If the observed rate conflicts with both predicted ranges, examine the controls or reject both simplified models rather than forcing a winner.

## 5. An explicit candidate subject rule—and its cost

We can now make one **conditional** candidate concrete, reusing the project's earlier positive-connectivity rule rather than presenting it as an established consciousness theory. Declare the three input/output locations to be the eligible sites and the shared-seed source to be outside those sites. Draw an undirected edge between two distinct sites if, for some fixed seed and fixed other inputs, toggling the input of either site changes the other site's output. This is a property of the specified intervention-accessible mechanism, not a property of `Kκ` alone. Define candidate rule `C*`: **stipulate one subject for every connected component**, including isolated singletons, in that graph.

The finite checker gives:

```text
C architecture: edges {1—3, 2—3}; one connected component → one C* subject.
D architecture: no cross-site edges; three singleton components → three C* subjects.
```

These are **predictions of C*** given the chosen sites, fixed-seed interventions, external-source boundary, and an additional identity between graph components and subjects. They are not actual subject counts. In particular, identifying three tiny classical sites as three subjects is an unvalidated and potentially implausible consequence of the rule, not a claim that bits feel anything. A rival rule could select no subjects or apply different eligibility conditions to the same graph. No physical data in this exercise independently justify choosing C* or its singleton clause.

The checker transports sites, their inputs, outputs and interventions through all six site permutations and independent bit-label reversals on inputs and outputs: all **384** transformations preserve the transported graph and its component count. This is a positive, narrow redescription check, not invariance under arbitrary factorization or coarse-graining. In particular, changing the chosen physical boundary to include the shared seed source changes the graph's candidate domain and requires a new calculation. Conditional on the natural symmetry and unchanged background, disconnected extension is stable for this simple component rule, but previously identified weak-link and persistence difficulties remain. The rule has not passed a phenomenal assay, the full subject battery, or the previous tie and factorization constraints.

To make it empirically discriminating, an independent subject-appropriate anchor would have to operationalize the difference between one joint experience and three separate experiences without making C* true by definition. The seed-control response in §4 can test the **physical** architecture; it cannot test that phenomenal postulate. A report bridge also needs condition-valid error and missingness bounds, as in the [joint-access decision gate](JOINT_ACCESS_COMMON_SOURCE_DECISION_GATE.md). No such calibrated bridge is supplied here.

## 6. Consequences for the experiential account

A constructive route now has three separate layers:

1. **Operational:** estimate suitable joint response laws under declared, physically possible interventions, including uncertainty and background conditions. Pairwise tests may miss relevant dependence.
2. **Mechanistic:** identify relevant routing, internal interventions and common sources; do not infer integrated physical organization just from a joint response law. Higher-order statistics do not automatically rule out local shared-source mechanisms.
3. **Experiential:** specify and independently constrain a rule connecting the identified mechanism to subject incidence, experiential content and persistence. Neither operational nor mechanistic integration automatically proves one subject, plurality, qualitative experience or a measure over subjects.

Strong possibility = actuality does not select one of the above models by itself. They are mathematical models of local occurrences, not different global realities brought into existence. Their inputs/outputs represent local ordered records, not an external universal time. They do not establish why an experiential whole is self-sufficient or how spacetime and physics emerge.

The immediate research dependency is to compare `C*` with explicit rival **mechanism-to-subject rules**, including eligibility, weak links, persistence and equivalent descriptions, and derive a first-person or report prediction not assumed in the definition. If the required phenomenal bridge is missing, the outcome should remain a physical-mechanism result—not a subject-count result.
