# Localization, transport and records in one modular construction

## 1. Constructive result and scope

Starting with the **same** Fock space, vacuum, half-line algebra M and recovered translation group from [modular clock selection](MODULAR_CLOCK_SELECTION.md), define

    M_a = U(a) M U(a)^*,  U(a)=Gamma(T(a)),  a in R,
    A((a,b)) = M_a intersect M_b',  a<b.

All commutants here are taken on the original Fock Hilbert space. These bounded-interval algebras form a nontrivial, local, isotonic, positive-energy, affine-covariant observable net. Every interval contains explicit noncommuting Weyl observables. The vacuum is invariant and globally cyclic, and is the unique translation-invariant vector up to scalar.

A localized instrument in this net detects a preparation in an overlapping interval; any complete instrument in a disjoint interval leaves its unconditioned statistics unchanged. Its unitary record completion uses the earlier construction, with entries now in the same interval algebra.

This closes a specific bridge: the positive translations recovered from modular logarithms also transport localization regions and their recorded experiments. It does **not** independently select the initial modular pair, establish a complete conformal net, or derive four-dimensional spacetime.

Verification: [modular_local_net.py](verification/modular_local_net.py), [exact output](verification/modular_local_net.json). Polynomial calculations test the witnesses and instruments. Infinite-space existence, locality and covariance are proved below, not inferred from a finite matrix approximation.

## 3. Explicit bounded-interval observables

For real compact smears f define

    f_hat(p)=(1/sqrt(2*pi)) integral exp(ipx) f(x) dx,
    F_f(theta)=i*p*f_hat(p).

Take first f smooth with compact support strictly inside (0,infinity). Then F_f belongs to K. Here is a direct domain check, rather than an assumption about what the half-line algebra means.

For z in the upper half-plane, integration by parts twice gives a bound

    |f_hat(z)| <= min(C_0,C_2/|z|^2).

The exponential has modulus at most one on positive support. Consequently, for z=r exp(iy), 0<y<pi,

    integral |i*r*exp(iy)*f_hat(r*exp(iy))|^2 dr/r
        <= integral r*min(C_0,C_2/r^2)^2 dr < infinity,

uniformly in y. Thus the strip Hardy condition holds. Reality of f gives the required upper boundary value: -i*p*f_hat(-p)=conjugate(i*p*f_hat(p)). The argument also works for compact C1, piecewise-polynomial smears whose function and first derivative vanish at the support endpoints; their second weak derivatives are integrable.

Reflection gives negative-half-line membership. For f supported negatively, let h(x)=f(-x), supported positively. Then

    F_f=-J F_h in JK=K'.

Translations satisfy F_(f(.-c))=T(c)F_f. Hence any real sufficiently regular f compactly supported inside (a,b) obeys

    F_f in T(a)K intersect T(b)K',
    W(F_f) in M_a intersect M_b'=A((a,b)).

This proves membership in the actual algebra intersection, without assuming that every intersection element has a particular smearing representation. No equality between an algebra intersection and a real-subspace intersection functor is needed.

## 4. Every interval contains noncommuting observables

Let

    f(x)=x^2(1-x)^2 on [0,1], zero elsewhere,
    g(x)=x*f(x).

Both are C1 with vanishing endpoint value and derivative. For these conventions,

    sigma(f,g)=2 Im<F_f,F_g>=integral f(x)g'(x) dx.

This follows from Plancherel and the reality condition on the Fourier transforms. Integration by parts gives

    sigma(f,g)=(1/2) integral f(x)^2 dx = 1/1260.

The Weyl law is

    W(f)W(g)=exp[-i*sigma(f,g)/2] W(f+g).

Thus W(f)W(g)=exp[-i/1260]W(g)W(f), so these are genuinely noncommuting, nonscalar bounded observables.

Place their support strictly inside any interval by the affine substitution f(x)->f((x-c)/r), with [c,c+r] inside that interval, and likewise for g. The symplectic integral is unchanged. This proves nonabelianity of **every** A((a,b)), not merely one selected region.

## 5. Net axioms proved from the nesting

**Isotony.** If (a,b) is contained in (c,d), then c<=a<b<=d. Thus M_a is contained in M_c and M_b' is contained in M_d', so A((a,b)) is contained in A((c,d)).

**Locality.** If b<=c, then

    A((a,b)) subset M_b',
    A((c,d)) subset M_c subset M_b.

The two interval algebras commute. This is locality for disjoint intervals of a chiral line, not a statement that successive timelike events in a four-dimensional laboratory commute.

**Translation covariance.** Unitary conjugation preserves intersections and commutants, giving

    U(c) A((a,b)) U(c)^* = A((a+c,b+c)).

**Dilation covariance.** Let V(s)=Gamma(D(s)). The previously proved affine relation gives

    V(s) M_a V(-s)=M_(exp(s)*a),
    V(s) A((a,b)) V(-s)=A((exp(s)*a,exp(s)*b)).

This same V(-2*pi*t) is the modular group of the original half-line algebra. The modular flow and the recovered translations are distinct, affinely related groups; neither distinction nor their relation is suppressed by calling both a clock.

**Positive energy and vacuum.** U(c)=exp(ic P_total), with P_total=dGamma(p)>=0 recovered from the modular pair. Both U and V fix Omega. Since p>0 almost everywhere, no nonzero n-particle sector with n>=1 has a zero eigenvector of the sum of its momenta. Therefore ker(P_total)=C Omega. These groups are strongly continuous.

**Global cyclicity.** The complex span of F_f for real smooth compact smears on the whole line is dense in H. To see this, start with a smooth F compactly supported in theta. Set f_hat(p)=F(log p)/(ip) on p>0 and zero on p<=0. This is smooth compactly supported away from p=0; its inverse Fourier transform is Schwartz, possibly complex. Smooth compact truncations converge in H1, hence in the homogeneous half-Sobolev norm that controls ||F_f||. Splitting these truncations into real and imaginary parts proves the stated density. Such compact smears each fit inside a bounded interval. The standard exponential-vector totality result then makes Omega cyclic for the algebra generated by all interval algebras.

Omega is also separating for each interval algebra, because that algebra is contained in M_a and Omega is separating for M_a. We do **not** infer bounded-interval cyclicity from global cyclicity. No Reeh-Schlieder theorem for each interval, Haag duality, strong additivity or conformal completion is claimed here.

## 6. A recorded experiment entirely using local field operators

Fix an interval I containing the smears above, and abbreviate W_f=W(F_f). Define a two-outcome instrument

    K_+=(I+W_f)/2,  K_-=(I-W_f)/2.

Both Kraus operators are in A(I), and direct expansion proves

    K_+^*K_+ + K_-^*K_- = I.

Let c_f=exp[-||F_f||^2/2], strictly between zero and one. The vacuum Weyl expectation is c_f under the convention in section 4. The recorded plus probability is

    p_+ = omega(K_+^*K_+) = (1+c_f)/2.

Prepare instead the state W_g Omega, where W_g is another local Weyl operator. CCR conjugation gives

    W_g^* W_f W_g = exp[-i sigma(f,g)] W_f,
    p_+ after preparation = (1+c_f*cos(sigma(f,g)))/2.

For g above the phase is 1/1260. Replacing g by 1260*g gives phase exactly 1 and hence a manifestly nonzero probability change. No numerical evaluation of the Sobolev norm is needed to prove it.

This is not just an ordering identity: it is a normalized local instrument with distinct recorded probabilities for two explicitly specified preparations in the same Fock representation.

### Unitarily writing the record

Use the [previous instrument completion](RELATIONAL_INTERVENTIONS_AND_RECORDS.md). The column C=(K_+,K_-) is an isometry. On H_Fock direct-sum (H_Fock tensor C2),

    U_C = [[0,C^*],[C,I-CC^*]]

is a self-adjoint unitary. Identifying this space with H_Fock tensor C3, its entries lie in A(I). Applied to a blank pointer, it produces K_+ psi tensor |+> plus K_- psi tensor |->. Reading the pointer gives exactly the probabilities above.

The pointer is an explicitly supplied finite apparatus, not a finite subfactor or phenomenal subject claimed to have been extracted from A(I). The coupling is localized through its field entries. This apparatus extension does not redefine the original net or its modular input. Persistent records follow when later operations preserve its outcome projectors, as in the earlier construction.

## 7. Translation dependence and locality in the same experiment

Take the preparation smear to be f_a(x)=f(x-a), transported by the **recovered** U(a). Its relative phase with the fixed readout is

    s(a)=integral f(x)f_a'(x) dx.

Exact compact-polynomial integration gives

| a | s(a) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 0 |
| 1/4 | 15201/4587520 |
| 1/2 | 41/17920 |
| a>=1 | 0 |

Moreover s(-a)=-s(a). Thus the recorded probability (1+c_f*cos(s(a)))/2 changes with recovered translation while the smears overlap, and returns to its unperturbed value when they are disjoint. The even cosine readout does not distinguish translation orientation; no globally injective clock reading is claimed.

There is a stronger, state-independent locality statement. For A in A(I) and a complete finite instrument {L_j} in A(J), with I,J disjoint,

    sum_j L_j^* A L_j = A sum_j L_j^*L_j = A.

Consequently every state's unconditioned statistics in I are unchanged by that intervention in J. Conditional statistics may differ through correlations; this is not an exception to the unconditioned no-signaling result. For instruments in overlapping regions, no such invariance is generally implied, as section 6 explicitly demonstrates.

## 8. Geometric content recovered versus imported

The algebraic formula A((a,b))=M_a intersect M_b' uses only the modularly recovered translation orbit, its ordered nesting and commutation. Once those are fixed, no separate local Hamiltonian table or independent assignment of interval observables is added. Nontriviality, locality, transport covariance and the record formulas all hold in one concrete model.

What remains input is substantial: the original standard real subspace and inclusion embedding already encode a chosen half-line/strip localization structure, a bosonic CCR theory and a vacuum. The real translation parameter gives an affine line, not a metric with a physically calibrated length unit. The Fourier variable x used to prove nontriviality is dual to the supplied positive p; it is not an independently derived spacetime coordinate. The example does not establish a unique actual net.

There is no derivation here of a second light-ray direction, a Lorentzian metric in higher dimension, realistic interacting matter, a gravitational constraint algebra, finite phenomenal ownership or necessary actuality. The local net is nevertheless a genuine additional construction: the same modular data now support bounded nonabelian localization, positive transport, causal commutation and intervention records, rather than separate models for each.

This continues M0 within W1-W6. It strengthens the physical/mathematical bridge without substituting that bridge for the experiential, constitutive or no-brute-facts completion requirements.
