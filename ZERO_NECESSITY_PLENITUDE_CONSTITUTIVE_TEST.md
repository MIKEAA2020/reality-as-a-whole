# One necessary whole: the constitutive test

## The direct continuation

The user's proposal is that full intelligibility is **structural and experiential**, that the strongest whole is one, and that every genuine possibility is actual because nothing may be left to an unexplained selector. The task is to derive this from zero's own self-subsistent nature, including the physical organization and the character of actual experience.

There is a useful logical bridge: **if a single necessarily actual constitution determines the physical–experiential whole up to equivalence that preserves actual subjects and felt qualities, then any internal feature genuinely realizable under that constitution occurs in that whole.** One does not need a further process that chooses or actualizes each feature.

This bridge is conditional. It does not establish the required zero constitution, its necessary instantiation, or the identity of physical organization and feeling. It does clarify what would constitute a non-circular success—and why unique structure plus zero balance is not enough.

## 1. From a determining constitution to plenitude within one whole

Let `T` denote a candidate account of the complete physical–experiential constitution, and let `W` be the actual whole. Suppose:

1. `W` actually realizes `T`, and `T` is necessarily instantiated in the relevant sense.
2. Any two admissible **complete physical–experiential realizations** of `T` are equivalent by a mapping preserving the relevant physical relations, real subject incidence and felt qualitative facts. If a probability or whole total is being compared, the mapping must also preserve the relevant measure and quantity.
3. An internal feature `F` is *T-admissible* if some admissible complete realization of `T` contains an occurrence with feature `F`.

If `F` is T-admissible, there is an occurrence of `F` in some realization `R`. By 2, an equivalence maps `R` to `W` and preserves `F`. Hence `W` contains an occurrence of `F`. Conversely, any qualifying feature in actual `W` is T-admissible, since `W` realizes `T`.

```text
T-admissible internal feature  ↔  feature occurs in the actual W.
```

If 1 and 2 hold across the intended modal scope, that internal occurrence is also necessary. Different features may occur at different subjects or relations; an occurrence need not have incompatible features simultaneously. No external creator, simulator, alternative ultimate whole or event of “turning possibility into actuality” is introduced.

The real work is in the assumptions. This becomes non-circular only if `T` has an independently motivated content, its actual self-subsistent instantiation is justified, and T-admissibility can be determined without defining it as “whatever already occurs in W.” A purely abstract model's satisfying formal sentences is not, by itself, an actual experience or proof of concrete realizability. Most importantly, equivalence of mathematical structure does not automatically preserve felt quality.

This is a **logical sufficient route**, not a claim that uniqueness is the only route to plenitude, nor that the requested constitution has been found. It sharpens the user's principle: one determining physical–experiential whole could make local plenitude and necessity consequences of the same constitution rather than separate selections.

## 2. The decisive test: structural uniqueness versus experiential identity

Consider a **formal control**, not a proposed world or model of conscious beings. Fix one six-position ring with one physically distinguished root. Suppose a theory also requires each position to carry one of the signs `+1` or `−1`, neighboring signs to alternate, and the unweighted sum to be zero.

There are exactly two sign assignments:

```text
root:   + − + − + −       total 0
root:   − + − + − +       total 0.
```

The physical graph and the distinguished root are the same. Each assignment contains both signs, with the same counts, and both have zero total. Yet if the root is a real, independently fixed physical role and sign labels denote real felt distinctions, an equivalence must preserve both the root and those distinctions. No such equivalence maps one assignment to the other: their root signs differ.

This demonstrates a precise insufficiency: **even an otherwise uniquely fixed physical structure, the presence of both qualitative types and a zero signed sum do not by themselves determine which type belongs to a given physical role.** The example does not show that both assignments are genuinely possible in the user's metaphysics, that ring positions are subjects, or that real valence is binary. If the strong necessity thesis is true, incompatible complete alternatives cannot both be genuinely possible. The point is that the stated structural conditions alone have not determined which formal interpretation, if either, corresponds to actuality.

The point also holds for measures: giving the root a different incidence weight makes the weighted total nonzero in either formal assignment, despite its unweighted sum being zero. Actual subject incidence cannot be inferred merely by counting positions.

## 3. First-person evidence is not an external selector

Suppose an actual subject in an independently identified physical role reports a negative experience. That first-person fact can help us **discover** which physical–experiential identity is correct. Treating experience as evidence does not insert an external observer or make felt character unreal.

But observing that one particular role is unpleasant is not, on its own, a constitutive explanation of why the same physical organization has that character, nor a general law assigning every other subject and history. A successful identity could be informed by observation and have further independently checkable implications. It must not assume those implications merely by calling a physical variable “valence.”

Equally, full experiential intelligibility need not mean that a third-person structural formula propositionally reproduces what suffering feels like. The claim to be justified is that the actual occurrence and its physical description are **the same constituted reality**, with its qualitative character preserved, rather than two disconnected additions.

## 4. Necessity is not moral or causal impotence

Under the [possibility–actuality thesis](ZERO_PLENITUDE_NECESSITY_AND_TWO_SENSES.md), if some suffering actually occurs, its occurrence somewhere in the whole is necessary at the stated global scope. This does **not** imply that every individual must suffer, that relief is futile, or that suffering is justified by a balancing sum.

If an intervention relieves a particular subject, both the intervention and the relief are themselves internal events of the necessary whole. Necessity does not turn an effective cause into an ineffective one. Conditional predictions about treatment can remain empirically meaningful; how to interpret their alternatives, and physical probabilities, within the proposed necessary whole is a further question. Nothing here assumes an external creator, literal simulations or a fundamental preferred time slicing.

## 5. What zero still has to determine

The logical bridge in section 1 identifies a clear target `T`, but does not construct it. For the user's zero proposal, the account must show, from its own non-circular nature:

* why a concrete, internally differentiated physical–experiential whole exists necessarily;
* why its physical laws, actual organization and relational time have their specific form;
* why its actual subjects have their particular felt characters, including real negative valence;
* why the actual incidence measure and the relevant objective whole-level signed quantity are what they are; and
* why that quantity is zero, if the proposed experiential or energetic zero is to be the whole's self-sufficient character.

The strong modal thesis can make actual facts necessary **if justified**; it does not by itself show which facts obtain, prove a physical–experiential identity, or assign a value to `Q`. The user's reason for favoring a whole-level zero is retained; the settled case against the relevant infinity candidate is not reopened.

The previous sphere and null-cone constructions may be useful constraints on `T`, but assuming them cannot establish that they follow from zero. The finite control here is an explanatory test, not new evidence for a final theory. We have not established the requested self-subsistent constitution, nor proved that it is impossible.
