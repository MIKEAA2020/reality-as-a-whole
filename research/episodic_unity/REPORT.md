# Constructive step 3: joint presentation is not joint prediction

## The result

The preceding rounds developed contrast covariance and a necessary test of contrast-complete explanatory scope. Neither establishes why predicted contrasts belong to one experience.

This round isolates that missing question.

> **Joint readout, complete pairwise compatibility, and a transitive closure of pairwise witnesses do not by themselves establish a single joint episode.**

A positive conditional result also emerges:

> If candidate supports refer to genuine content-occurrences, cover the relevant domain, and satisfy an overlap-amalgamation law, then their maximal supports form a unique partition of that domain.

The derivation is exact. The law's phenomenal authority and the identification of content-occurrences are not derived. Applying the same law to shared causal hardware or repeated content types can create false unities.

**24 new tests pass.** With the previous two rounds, the research branch has **78 passing tests**. These are finite constructions and consistency checks, not empirical evidence that any modeled system experiences anything.

---

## 1. Three notions that need different evidence

Consider three candidate contents, provisionally called \(a,b,c\).

1. **Joint prediction:** a physical model predicts a tuple of outputs.
2. **Pairwise witness:** for each pair, an episode is proposed or reported in which that pair occurs together.
3. **Joint episode:** one episode contains all three actual content-occurrences together.

The first does not establish the second, and the second does not logically establish the third without additional conditions.

A reporter can combine signals arriving from different processes or different times. A common controller can coordinate distinct processes. Multiple reports about similar contents can concern numerically different occurrences.

The key issue is not the availability of a larger tuple. It is the identity and organization of the occurrence that presents it.

## 2. A representation that does not assume a partition

Use a finite vertex set \(V\) of **supplied candidate content-occurrences**, not merely names of content types. A family \(\mathcal H\) of nonempty supports describes candidate episode memberships.

A support \(e\in\mathcal H\) says that the selected candidate occurrences belong together in a proposed episode. This is a hypothesis in the model, not a consequence of placing them in a set.

Starting with a hypergraph rather than a partition matters. A partition would already require each occurrence to belong to exactly one block and would build a nonoverlap answer into the formalism.

The hypergraph allows the project to test whether partitionhood can be justified. It does not assert that overlapping phenomenal episodes really exist. A theory that excludes them must state and defend the exclusion.

An additional scope qualification is important: these supports concern a declared episode window. Continuing subjects, temporally extended contents, and momentary episodes are not automatically the same domain.

## 3. The pairwise projection loses higher-order jointness

Define the arity-\(k\) joint-support profile as

\[
J_k(\mathcal H)=\{S\subseteq V:1\leq |S|\leq k,
\ \exists e\in\mathcal H\;S\subseteq e\}.
\]

This records that a support exists for a selected set. It does not record how many witnesses exist or identify their actuality.

Compare

\[
\mathcal H_1=\{\{a,b,c\}\},
\]

with

\[
\mathcal H_2=\{\{a,b\},\{a,c\},\{b,c\}\}.
\]

Then

\[
J_2(\mathcal H_1)=J_2(\mathcal H_2),
\qquad
J_3(\mathcal H_1)\ne J_3(\mathcal H_2).
\]

Every pair is supported in both descriptions. Only the first has a triple support. Both families already consist entirely of maximal supports: choosing maximal elements does not resolve the ambiguity.

The exhaustive three-vertex enumeration confirms:

- 109 covering families of nonempty supports;
- under the complete-pair profile and the maximal-support/antichain restriction, exactly the two families above remain;
- supplying the triple profile selects the one-triple maximal support in that restricted class.

These are support families, not simultaneous observer populations. The counts do not imply probabilities of one-half or counts of one versus three actual subjects.

### What the counterexample does not establish

If a defended theory requires disjoint maximal phenomenal episodes, \(\mathcal H_2\) is inadmissible as an assignment of these same phenomenal occurrence-tokens. The example then exposes the **extra nonoverlap premise** rather than refuting that theory.

The formal alternative is not a proof of a metaphysically possible world with overlapping experience. Its role is to show that pairwise data alone do not contain the premise excluding it.

## 4. Pairwise co-presence is not automatically an equivalence relation

Suppose a relation is defined by

\[
aRb\quad\text{iff some proposed episode contains both }a,b.
\]

For supports \(\{a,b\}\) and \(\{b,c\}\), \(aRb\) and \(bRc\) hold while \(aRc\) does not. The existentially witnessed relation need not be transitive.

Taking its transitive closure is mathematically available. It is not a derivation of phenomenal co-presence. Likewise, completing a pairwise clique into an episode adds a higher-order support not supplied by the original family.

This sharpens an earlier suggestion in the broader inquiry. If co-consciousness is independently established as an equivalence relation, its classes do give a partition. But **equivalence must not be obtained by defining away the case being investigated**.

Reflexivity, symmetry, transitivity, common timing, and token identity each require the appropriate domain. Reports at different times or judgments about recurring content types do not automatically establish those properties for individual phenomenal occurrences.

## 5. The exact additional law that produces a partition

Consider the following candidate:

### Overlap-amalgamation

For every \(e,f\in\mathcal H\) with \(e\cap f\ne\varnothing\), there is \(g\in\mathcal H\) such that

\[
e\cup f\subseteq g.
\]

Informally: if two supports genuinely share a relevant occurrence, they are included together within a common episode support.

### Conditional theorem

If \(V\) and \(\mathcal H\) are finite, \(\mathcal H\) covers \(V\), and overlap-amalgamation holds, then the inclusion-maximal supports form a partition of \(V\).

**Proof.** Finiteness puts every support inside a maximal one, so maximal supports cover \(V\). If distinct maximal supports \(e,f\) overlapped, amalgamation would supply \(g\supseteq e\cup f\). Maximality forces \(e=g=f\), a contradiction. Therefore the maximal supports are disjoint and covering. ∎

No numerical threshold or integration score is needed. But the closure law is substantive.

In fact, for this finite covering setting, partitionhood of maximal supports also ensures the amalgamation condition for all supports: any two intersecting supports must lie in the same maximal block. Thus the condition makes explicit a partition-like commitment; it is not an independent proof that actual experience must obey it.

The code checks the theorem across all covering families on three and four vertices. The proof, not the finite enumeration, establishes the stated finite result generally.

### Status of the principle

Overlap-amalgamation is a **candidate constitutive law to defend**, not an independently established law of consciousness. Naming supports “episodes” does not establish it. Nor does a desire for one answer justify adding it.

This round therefore supplies a precise sufficient condition for a partition, rather than claiming to derive a partition from mere presentation or causal structure.

## 6. Why the law cannot be applied to causal supports indiscriminately

Return to two processes \(x,y\) sharing a controller \(c\). Their causal supports may be

\[
\{x,c\},\qquad\{y,c\}.
\]

Imposing overlap-amalgamation produces the union \(\{x,y,c\}\). That calculation does not show that the three constitute one experience.

The shared controller could be enabling machinery. Even if it contributes to the constitution of both processes, shared physical contribution need not mean the *same phenomenal content-occurrence* appears in both episodes.

The missing bridge is now more exact:

> What turns a concrete causal contribution into an experiential content-occurrence, and under what conditions does its shared occurrence justify common phenomenal unity?

The theorem cannot answer that by operating on hardware nodes. It requires the appropriate phenomenal-occurrence domain before its intended application.

This prevents a circular shortcut: derive a causal overlap, call it shared presentation, then cite amalgamation as proof of a single subject.

## 7. Content types can manufacture both overlap and missing multiplicity

A second construction avoids assuming that actual episodes overlap at all.

Consider three disjoint candidate episodes:

\[
\{a_1,b_1\},\qquad\{b_2,c_1\},\qquad\{a_2,c_2\}.
\]

These have six distinct occurrence-tokens. If the subscripts are discarded as though only content types matter, their supports become

\[
\{a,b\},\qquad\{b,c\},\qquad\{a,c\}.
\]

The type-level projection has manufactured apparent overlap that was absent at the occurrence level. Amalgamation of that projection then manufactures an apparent triple.

Similarly, two genuinely distinct \(abc\) episodes can collapse to one support when projected to content types. Even complete type-level jointness then loses numerical multiplicity.

The same error arises across time. Contents \(b@0\) and \(b@1\) may share a type without being one occurrence. Removing their temporal provenance can create an overlap and a supposed unity that the time-indexed description never contained.

These constructions reinforce the earlier q/τ distinction. Neither a type graph nor a state quotient provides episode occurrences. Temporal and causal provenance cannot be discarded merely because a comparison task ignores them.

## 8. What richer joint data can positively identify

For a finite support family, the complete joint-support profile is its downward closure. Its maximal elements are exactly the original maximal supports:

\[
\operatorname{Max}(J_{|V|}(\mathcal H))
=\operatorname{Max}(\mathcal H).
\]

**Reason.** Every member of the profile lies inside an original support; every original support belongs to the profile. Their inclusion-maximal elements therefore coincide.

This gives a useful constructive result:

> If the genuine joint-presentation profile and occurrence domain were independently established, maximal episode supports could be recovered without an arbitrary scalar score.

But it has three limits:

1. The data would need to establish actual joint presentation, not merely joint reporting or pairwise judgments.
2. The result recovers maximal supports, not automatically continuing subjects or every nested episode.
3. The support-existence profile discards repeated witnesses and cannot alone determine their numerical population.

An episode with support \(abc\) and an additional support \(ab\) have the same full joint-support profile as \(abc\) alone. Deciding whether the \(ab\) entry is a real nested episode, a subdescription of the same episode, or inadmissible requires more structure.

Thus higher-order evidence improves identification, but even full support data do not automatically solve occurrence counting.

## 9. How this improves the constitutive candidate

The previous rounds required a sufficient description to preserve experiential contrasts across context and time. It now also needs to distinguish:

- differences in the contents;
- differences in how those content-occurrences belong together;
- differences in the number of episodes realizing the same content types.

The strengthened necessary requirement is:

> **Contrast-complete constitution must account for joint presentation at the appropriate occurrence and temporal scope—not merely component contrasts or a joint output tuple.**

That follows from the target. If unity is something the theory claims to explain, an unexplained choice of episode incidence cannot be treated as an irrelevant relabeling.

One may formalize the missing relation as

\[
\eta\subseteq\Omega_{\rm content\ occurrences}
\times\Omega_{\rm episodes}.
\]

This is an explanatory obligation, not an additional mechanism declared solved by its symbol. The task is to derive or defend the occurrence domain and membership relation from concrete constitution, or justify a different characterization of unity.

The best constructive lead is now **token-sensitive joint presentation**, not a bigger integration number. The candidate overlap-amalgamation law tells us one exact route to partitionhood and exactly which premise would need an independent phenomenal or constitutive justification.

## 10. How to seek that justification without assuming it

The next inquiry should ask whether sharing a *genuine phenomenal occurrence*, within the same episode scope, entails the relevant common episode—or whether the proposed tokenization already assumes the answer.

Three approaches remain legitimate:

1. **Phenomenological constraint:** investigate higher-order co-presence rather than infer it from a series of pairwise judgments. Comparative first-person evidence is relevant but fallible; a later report can itself be a new unified occurrence reporting earlier separate events.
2. **Constitutive argument:** specify what a content-occurrence is independently of a partition into subjects. Then test whether overlap-amalgamation follows from that nature rather than from a definition that already fixes membership.
3. **Principled limitation:** if the available evidence and constitutive premises do not distinguish the candidate organizations, retain the identification family. Do not uniformly weight it or count its members as observers.

None is established by an external observer collecting several channels into one database. Nor does a common physical cause or a nonlinear joint response prove first-person co-presence.

### A bounded empirical design

A prospective test would distinguish direct simultaneous multi-content presentation, sequential pairwise judgments, and later aggregate reports. It should record report delay, memory dependence, attentional changes, and independently supported content-token provenance.

An independently warranted simultaneous triple presentation could rule out a model containing only pairwise episode supports for those very occurrences. A report of a triple is evidence to assess, not an infallible membership oracle.

No new measurements have been collected here. The finite constructions identify what evidence would discriminate the hypotheses, not a completed experiment.

## Final verdict

> Joint prediction and pairwise witnesses are insufficient grounds for joint experience. Higher-order presentation structure matters, and it must be attached to actual occurrence-tokens rather than recurring labels or shared hardware. A defended overlap-amalgamation law would yield a finite partition of maximal episode supports, but its authority is precisely what remains to establish. Full joint-presentation data could identify maximal supports conditionally; it would still not by itself explain their actuality or count every episode. The constitutive programme has acquired a sharper unity obligation and a concrete sufficient-law candidate, not a prematurely declared subject criterion.
