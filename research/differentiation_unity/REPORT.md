# Constructive step 8: differentiation is not disunity

## Consolidated result

The interventional-core proposal survives one important objection: **independently variable contents do not require dynamically independent realizers**. A finite process can admit all combinations of two independently prepared content coordinates while having no decomposition into independent coordinate dynamics.

A further positive result narrows the calibration problem: adding temporal structure reduces eight content-geometry matches to one in the worked example.

But even that unique content match does not determine which content occurrences belong to a common episode. The remaining bridge is specifically from concrete process organization to **episode incidence**, not merely from physical states to differentiated content.

**24 new tests pass; 198 tests pass across the eight constructive rounds.** No actual phenomenal measurements are supplied.

## 1. Four different meanings of independence

The candidate must distinguish:

| Question | Mathematical object | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Can either content vary while the other stays fixed? | Available state combinations and preparation operations | Independent dynamics or divided experience |
| Are their sampled values statistically independent? | A distribution over states | Causal separation |
| Do their future processes factor? | Transition/intervention structure | Phenomenal separation without a bridge |
| Do the contents occur in one presentation? | Episode incidence over content occurrences | A physical mechanism by itself |

Confusing these questions would make any eventual constitutive principle unstable. An experience can be richly differentiated without the theory being entitled to describe it as several experiences.

This round establishes mathematical non-equivalences. It does not claim that every formal episode description used below is phenomenally possible.

## 2. Independent preparation with nonfactorable dynamics

Retain the previous physical source:

\[
A(x,y)=(y,x\oplus y),\qquad(x,y)\in\{0,1\}^2.
\]

All four states are available. Define local preparations:

\[
R_x^a(x,y)=(a,y),\qquad R_y^b(x,y)=(x,b).
\]

These operations commute:

\[
R_x^aR_y^b=R_y^bR_x^a.
\]

They prepare any requested pair (a,b) from every starting state. Each changes only its selected current coordinate.

Nevertheless, A is not a product

\[
A(x,y)=(f(x),g(y)).
\]

The previous round also proved that no static bijection of its four states produces two independent binary transition factors: the transition permutation has order three, whereas a product of two binary permutations has order at most two.

Therefore:

\[
\text{independent current preparation}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{independent dynamical factors}.
\]

If x and y are proposed content coordinates, their independent controllability does not contradict a physical realization in a common dynamically irreducible process. This removes a potential inconsistency in the candidate. It does not establish that this process is conscious.

## 3. Statistical independence is not dynamical independence

A is a permutation of the four states. Consequently it preserves the uniform distribution:

\[
p(x,y)=\tfrac14=p(x)p(y).
\]

Thus the two coordinates can be statistically independent at every observation time despite the nonfactorable update rule.

Conversely, two identity processes

\[
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y)
\]

can preserve a correlated initial distribution concentrated on 00 and 11.

Therefore neither instantaneous statistical independence nor correlation identifies causal separation or integration.

This matters for a prospective empirical bridge: low or zero observed correlation need not refute interaction, and high correlation need not establish a common constitutive process. Interventions and the stipulated time domain matter.

These probabilities concern sampled abstract states, not observer weights or self-location probabilities.

## 4. Follow the preparation operations through time

Because A is invertible, transport a current preparation into next-step coordinates:

\[
\widetilde R=A R A^{-1}.
\]

Write next-step coordinates as (p,q), so

\[
A^{-1}(p,q)=(p\oplus q,p).
\]

Then:

\[
\widetilde R_x^a(p,q)=(p,a\oplus p),
\]

\[
\widetilde R_y^b(p,q)=(b,p\oplus q\oplus b).
\]

None of these four operations equals a native local reset of p or q.

The effect of an independently prepared current contrast has become a context-dependent future transformation. That is a concrete consequence of the coupling, not a verbal declaration of integration.

Yet transported x- and y-preparations still commute, because conjugation preserves commutativity:

\[
(A R_x A^{-1})(A R_y A^{-1})
=A R_xR_y A^{-1}
=A R_yR_x A^{-1}.
\]

So even commuting preparations do not establish independent dynamics. Noncommutativity is not a universal necessary signature of the kind of coupling tested here.

## 5. Temporal contrast structure can identify a content map

Take the independently prepared four content combinations as a square, with one-coordinate changes defining its edges. There are eight distance-preserving maps between two such squares: two axis orders and four choices of axis flips.

A static square match therefore leaves eight candidate correspondences.

Now suppose a separately characterized target contrast process has transition B and require:

\[
h\circ A=B\circ h.
\]

For the worked case B=A, exhaustive testing of the eight square maps leaves **exactly one**, the identity map.

The transition fixes 00 and orients a three-cycle through the remaining states. That extra structure breaks the static square's symmetries. No separately imposed point anchor is needed in this mathematical example.

Controls:

- identity dynamics on both squares leave all eight maps;
- source A versus target identity dynamics leave no match.

### Scope of this identification

B=A is a synthetic target specification here. No actual phenomenal transition law B has been independently measured. Selecting B because it is copied from A would not constitute empirical validation.

The genuine result is conditional: **independently established temporal contrast structure can resolve correspondence ambiguity that static geometry leaves open**.

It identifies a map between specified contrast structures. It does not explain why the target contrasts are qualitative, why either process occurs, or whether they form a unified episode.

## 6. Unique content mapping still leaves incidence undecided

For each content state (x,y), consider two formal descriptions:

### Joint-description fixture

Two content occurrences have one common episode support:

\[
H_J=\{\{c_x,c_y\}\}.
\]

### Split-description fixture

Each has a separate support:

\[
H_S=\{\{c_x\},\{c_y\}\}.
\]

These descriptions have the same content values and can be assigned the same time-indexed content transitions. They differ in co-presentation incidence.

They are deliberately stipulated descriptions, not demonstrated conscious systems. In particular, writing H_S alongside coupled content dynamics does not establish that an actual unified subject could split in that way.

The limited conclusion is exact: a data representation containing only content values and transitions does not encode which of H_J or H_S holds. The unique content isomorphism from section 5 does not recover information absent from that representation.

The construction therefore isolates, rather than solves, the unity obligation.

## 7. A two-sorted explanatory target

A sufficiently comprehensive local episode model needs at least two distinct structures:

1. **Contrast structure:** differentiated contents and their transformations.
2. **Incidence structure:** which content occurrences are jointly presented, with temporal and occurrence provenance.

An adequate bridge must preserve the relevant parts of both. A perfect contrast match can remain incomplete about unity. Conversely, declaring an undifferentiated whole does not explain the independently variable contents within it.

This is a necessary adequacy condition relative to an independently warranted target—not a sufficient psychophysical law. If incidence is simply supplied as an input, the code has represented unity rather than derived its constitution.

A useful way to state the burden is:

> Explain how differentiated contrasts belong to one presentation without identifying differentiation with physical disconnection, or identifying a common physical transition system with phenomenal unity by definition.

## 8. The sharpened interventional-core conjecture

The previous structural candidate can now be made more explicit for the limited two-contrast setting:

> **ICU candidate:** At an independently justified temporal grain, two matched content contrasts are co-present exactly when their constitutive realization belongs to the same inclusion-minimal autonomous, dynamically nonfactorable core.

Here the physical core must be independently characterized, the content mapping independently warranted, and the episode incidence independently available as evidence. The conjecture must not define the latter two from the first.

This is a **substantive psychophysical proposal**, not an independently derived law. Its appeal is that it distinguishes implementation from passive copying while allowing independent content preparation within a core.

Within the toy example it predicts joint incidence for the source pair. For two independent identity registers it predicts separate incidence. Neither prediction is an observed phenomenal fact.

### What would count against it

Subject to a justified intervention and observation domain:

- warranted co-presentation persisting across genuinely separate minimal constitutive cores;
- warranted divided presentation despite the candidate's identifying a single constitutive core;
- a necessary target dependence lying outside the proposed autonomous support;
- failure of its core assignment to remain stable under physically appropriate changes of description;
- inability to justify the temporal grain except by tuning it to the desired incidence result.

If such a contrast is established, the candidate must be revised or rejected—not protected by redefining the episode or the intervention domain after the fact.

A highly differentiated experience is not by itself an objection. A shared report is not by itself confirmation.

## 9. Why the candidate is not yet independently justified

Two achievements must not be merged:

- **Independently justified:** a complete account must track warranted contrasts and incidence; observational copies need not share interventional organization; independent preparation need not mean independent dynamics.
- **Still postulated:** minimal autonomous dynamical cores coincide with unified phenomenal presentations.

The second does not follow from the first. No test in this repository establishes the necessity of autonomy for experience, the sufficiency of nonfactorization, or a one-core/one-episode law.

The phrase “dynamically nonfactorable” also requires a supplied factor class. A four-state cycle can be described as one register, and changing the temporal grain can make its sampled dynamics factor. These earlier limitations remain fully active.

Thus the standing request for an independently justified constitutive principle has been narrowed but not completed. The programme now has a clearer falsifiable candidate and a cleaner statement of its unpaid bridge, rather than a certified explanation of consciousness.

## 10. Token discipline

Content occurrences carry a run identifier, time, feature identity, and value. Recurring content values do not identify their occurrences across times or runs.

The joint and split fixtures share the same supplied content occurrences when comparing incidence. This is controlled bookkeeping, not proof that an actual episode can be regrouped while all phenomenal facts remain fixed.

The fixtures are also not an assumption that all real episodes form a partition. They are two particular covering hypergraphs used to expose lost incidence information. The data structure permits more general covering support families.

No number of square maps, fixture supports, protocol conditions, or simulation runs is an observer count. No phenomenal actuality follows from a successful Python execution.

## 11. Constructive direction from here

The most valuable next work is not another static analogy. It is to investigate **boundary-change contrasts** under the ICU candidate:

- keep differentiated content possibilities available;
- vary the physical coupling that creates or removes the proposed core boundary;
- distinguish changed content, lost access, altered memory, and changed co-presentation;
- test whether any independently warranted unity evidence tracks the predicted boundary change.

A useful formal preparation is to test whether arbitrarily weak coupling can merge the candidate cores. If every nonzero coupling forces the same unity verdict, the candidate needs either an independently justified discontinuity or a principled robustness criterion. An arbitrary threshold would merely relocate the bridge.

This provides a concrete next adversarial test without pretending that the current formal results are empirical phenomenal evidence.

### Final verdict

The candidate can accommodate differentiated contents without requiring divided dynamics. Temporal contrast structure can uniquely determine a content correspondence in the finite example. But content correspondence, even when unique, leaves episode incidence unproved. The proposed core-to-unity bridge is now explicit and testable in principle, with weak coupling and boundary robustness as its next critical obligations.
