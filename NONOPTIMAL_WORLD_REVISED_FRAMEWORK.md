# Revising the whole-account for a non-optimal world

> **Historical revision, not the current complete framework.** Later user clarifications provisionally replace the universal-subject target with plural subjects and explicitly adopt the bridge from a perfect whole to a best universe, making the whole non-perfect under the user's premises. They also reject literal simulations as the point of the Bostrom adaptation. See the [current self-existent-plenitude candidate](SELF_EXISTENT_PLENITUDE_PLURALITY_CORRECTION.md) and its [foundational gap analysis](PLENITUDE_FOUNDATION_GAPS_AND_SYMMETRY.md). Neither is a completed zero-residue account.

## Governing change

The user's stance that our world is not best or optimal is a substantive constraint, not a difficulty to be explained away. The prior formulations that made a **perfectly good, unsurpassable absolute** a governing requirement are withdrawn as such. Completeness of explanation, self-sufficiency, universal consciousness, and moral optimality are distinct claims. We retain the first three as goals, not established facts, but do not build in the last.

In particular, sections of [the old absolute framework](ABSOLUTE_CONSTITUTION_FRAMEWORK.md), [the earlier whole-account conjecture](CONJECTURAL_WHOLE_ACCOUNT.md), and [the perfection–privation test](COMPLETE_AWARENESS_FINITE_DISCLOSURE.md) that treat moral perfection or an unsurpassable whole as mandatory are historical proposals, not the current governing thesis. Some logical distinctions and stress tests in those files remain useful. This correction does **not** establish that ultimate reality is morally imperfect: the answer depends on what is being compared and how value applies to the absolute.

## 1. What to retain, reject, and leave open

| Topic | Revision |
|---|---|
| Fundamental nature | Retain the user's working target: one self-sufficient, timeless absolute with no fundamental spacetime or mass, whose intrinsic nature is experience, and one universal consciousness. These are hypotheses/goals, not derived results. |
| Experience | Retain first-person experience as a serious datum and differentiate acquaintance from third-person structural description. Neither establishes by itself the identity or scope of the universal subject. |
| Non-optimality | Do not assume that our universe is morally optimal, that all suffering is necessary for a greater good, or that reality selects the best. The user has clarified that the non-best premise concerns our universe; its precise comparator and standard remain to be specified. See [whole implications](NONOPTIMAL_WORLD_WHOLE_IMPLICATIONS.md). |
| Complete intelligibility | Retain the demand to explain every structural and existential fact and make experiential nature intelligible in the appropriate experiential sense. A complete account of a harmful fact does not morally justify it. This aspiration is not a proof of closure. |
| Good and evil | Retain due-good privation as a candidate account of some harms, not an implication of finitude and not a dismissal of positive experienced pain. Account for concrete suffering, agency, and the effects on each perspective. |
| Axiarchism | Drop any working principle that concrete existence must select a uniquely best world. A weaker hypothesis that value is intrinsic to experiential life, or even plays a different explanatory role, remains open but must be argued rather than inferred from existence. |
| Modal claims | Retain possibility = actuality and essence = existence as goals; do not assume a proof, or silently interpret possibility = actuality as either global modal collapse or plenitude. |
| Physics and simulation | Continue requiring empirical recovery and risky predictions, including emergent spacetime and actual clock comparisons; do not equate a self-differentiated whole with Bostrom's simulation or infer simulated observers without an implementation and measure. |

The earlier **P0** (“no real privation anywhere”) is incompatible with real due shortfalls if those are granted. The earlier **P1** (“an unsurpassable, non-gratuitous whole”) is incompatible with a strictly better possible totality. Non-optimality of a constituent alone does not establish such a total comparison, but the user's additional bridge from whole perfection to a best local universe entails that the whole is not perfect in that sense. No perfection claim is retained as a governing requirement.

## 2. Define the non-optimality claim without weakening it

A strong reading is:

> **C2:** There is a genuinely possible world W+ that is strictly better than the actual world W in an appropriate all-things-considered comparison.

This is stronger than “there is actual suffering,” and stronger than “some future improvement in this world's history is possible.” Local harm alone does not prove a globally better alternative; identifying W+, what remains the same, and the comparative standard is a real task. The user can take C2 as a premise without proving it here. The relation may be partial, not an assumed numerical value scale; merely denying a *greatest* element in a partial order does not always imply the existence of a strictly better alternative, since incomparable worlds can exist. We do not use that technicality to dismiss the user's strong premise.

The decisive ambiguity is **world**:

- **Local-universe reading:** W is our universe or a finite/perspectival world within an absolute. Another possible W+ may be strictly better even if both are realized inside one actual whole. Whether W+ actually exists requires a separate realization argument.
- **Total-actuality reading:** W is the entire absolute, including all of its sectors. If only one total actuality is possible, then no distinct better total actuality is possible. Strong C2 and that global-collapse claim cannot both hold.

The user has clarified that the premise concerns **our universe**, and has adopted the bridge from a perfect whole to a best universe. The two premises entail that the whole is not perfect in that bridge's sense. A **better possible totality** is a further comparative claim, not automatically entailed; see [whole implications](NONOPTIMAL_WORLD_WHOLE_IMPLICATIONS.md). An indexed plenitude model is a candidate consistent with the earlier goals, not a result. If “not optimal” means merely rejection of a universal best-world theorem or bad local facts, that is a weaker commitment than the strong C2 displayed above.

## 3. A concrete compatibility model, not a metaphysical derivation

For the local-universe reading only, consider an abstract fixed whole containing two internally separate candidate world-histories, W0 and W1, with the same two idealized welfare-bearing roles. Stipulate their role fulfillment profiles as `(0,0)` and `(1,0)`, respectively. Using strict Pareto comparison, W1 is better than W0: one role is better off and the other no worse off. Both local candidates are included in the one whole. In a one-element space of allowed total configurations that whole is the only possible total configuration.

This thin model demonstrates **logical compatibility of the indexed statements under stipulated meanings**: one fixed whole; every candidate in the declared two-element local domain actual; and a non-best local world. It does not show that actual universes or duplicate subjects exist, that these welfare profiles are metaphysically possible, that two-role Pareto comparison captures ethics, or that the whole is an experiential subject. Any universal subject is a separate hypothesis, not a consequence of the set representation. Nor does this establish that *all* coherent descriptions are compossible, concrete, or actual, or that different physical laws emerge from an absolute. The example is not evidence that the entire fixed whole is non-optimal under a global comparison.

The indispensable bridges are therefore: what qualifies as a locally possible world, why its realization is necessary, how its physical/experiential realization follows from the actual constitution, and what independent standard warrants the comparison. Infinite collections would add serious measure and value-aggregation problems; see [Bostrom, *Infinite Ethics*](https://nickbostrom.com/ethics/infinite).

## 4. Separate existence, explanation, and value

A self-sufficient necessary existence would answer “what does the absolute depend on?” if independently established; it would not by itself answer “is it morally unsurpassable?” A fully intelligible theory can in principle explain why a harmful occurrence exists without declaring it right or unavoidable for the best. Conversely, asserting that an outcome is bad does not by itself explain why the absolute exists or why a better alternative is realized.

No-go criterion: if a candidate says “the actual complete world is necessarily the best among all possible complete worlds,” it conflicts with strong total-world C2. If it says “this universe is part of an absolutely best whole,” it is not *logically* excluded by local-world C2, but it is an additional unestablished claim, not something to import merely to accommodate suffering. [Leibniz's account](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2019/entries/leibniz-evil/) illustrates the best-world inference and the distinction between local evils and whole-world comparisons.

Optimization in an unrelated technical sense is not forbidden: a mathematical variational principle or a candidate consciousness-boundary rule is not automatically a claim of moral optimality. But a technical extremum must be justified on its own; the existence of such a principle does not justify actual suffering, prove a phenomenological law, or pick a welfare standard.

## 5. Concrete next steps

1. Take C2 as concerning **our universe** as clarified by the user; specify a strictly better possible comparison universe, its value standard, and whether a whole containing it can be compared with ours.
2. Write the modal domain and realization map: distinguish necessary total actuality, possible local alternatives, and actual local sectors. Do not infer their identity from vocabulary.
3. State a value comparison that respects real suffering and agency, specifies corresponding or different subjects, and deals with incomparability and infinities without assumed numerical utility or a hidden optimum.
4. Continue the existing physical and phenomenal bridges independently. A moral or metaphysical preference cannot establish Einstein dynamics, a universal subject, a real experimental intervention, or experience in a simulated organism.

The most coherent *provisional* research route that preserves the original listed endpoint is an **intrinsically experiential, self-sufficient whole with non-optimal local worlds**, conditional on the local-world reading and without assuming that every possible local world exists. This is a proposed architecture, not an established solution. If C2 is global instead, the route must be revised rather than calling global non-optimality merely local.
