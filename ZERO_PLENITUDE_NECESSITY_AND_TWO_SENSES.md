# Plenitude, two kinds of intelligibility, and necessity of the whole

**Continuation:** [the necessary-whole constitutive test](ZERO_NECESSITY_PLENITUDE_CONSTITUTIVE_TEST.md) shows conditionally how a unique physical–experiential constitution entails internal plenitude, and tests why unique physical structure plus zero balance does not alone determine the felt character of a distinguished role.

## The correction

The user clarifies that complete intelligibility has **structural and experiential** senses; that there is only one whole in the strongest sense; and that every fact happens out of necessity, because a merely possible but unactualized genuine alternative would require an unexplained selection.

My preceding response framed a “uniquely determined whole” as if it were an alternative to the user's possibility–actuality thesis. That was misleading. **If the unique determining constitution makes all other complete alternatives genuinely impossible, it is compatible with—and can be a way of articulating—the user's thesis.** The real distinction is between proving such determination from the nature of the whole and merely defining “possible” to mean “actual.” The latter is a restatement, not an independent explanation.

The view proposed here is one self-subsistent whole, not an external creator, cosmic subject, simulator or independent collection of ultimate wholes. The question of multiple physical domains or universes is secondary and does not settle the issue by itself.

## 1. The user's argument in its strongest form

The proposal is:

1. The exhaustive whole cannot depend on an external selector.
2. Full intelligibility leaves no independent unexplained residue, either in structural organization or in real experiential character. Experiences are not merely structural labels; their actually felt qualities must be included.
3. If a genuinely possible complete alternative were left unactualized for no reason intrinsic to the whole, actuality rather than that alternative would amount to an unexplained selection.
4. The whole's own constitution must therefore account for what is genuinely possible and for the complete physical–experiential actuality, without such a selection.
5. On the user's strong thesis, **genuine possibility and actuality coincide**, and the facts of the whole could not have been otherwise.

This is a coherent and powerful *candidate argument*, not the weaker assertion that the present author happens not to know why some alternatives are excluded. The user's no-residue standard does not permit us to say merely “a law selects this world” while leaving the law, its actual instantiation or its experiential significance unexplained.

The key substantive step is that the two senses of full intelligibility, together with the no-unexplained-selection demand, must **necessitate this modal conclusion**. That does not follow from the bare definitions of structural knowledge and experiential occurrence alone: it depends on the further principle that no complete qualitative or structural determination may be contingent on an ungrounded alternative, and on showing why the whole's constitution genuinely satisfies that principle. This preserves the strength of the user's claim without pretending that merely saying “self-subsistent” has already proved its detailed constitution.

The most promising formulation is not that an external agent surveys and actualizes possibilities. It is that possibility and actuality are two aspects of **one necessary internal constitution**. “Actualizes” need not describe a process in time.

## 2. What follows if genuine possibility equals actuality?

For complete-whole propositions `p`, let `◇p` mean genuinely metaphysically possible and `□p` mean necessary. The user's strong claim is

```text
◇p ↔ p.
```

Assume the usual classical modal duality `□p ↔ ¬◇¬p`, actuality implies possibility, and the identity applies also to `¬p`. Then

```text
◇¬p ↔ ¬p
¬◇¬p ↔ p
□p ↔ p.
```

Therefore, **every actual proposition in the stated scope is necessary**, and every genuinely possible proposition in that scope is actual. This is often called *necessitarianism* or, in a propositional setting, modal collapse. The terminology describes the consequence; it is not a proof of the premise. A philosophical discussion of strong necessitarian readings, distinct from ordinary temporal determinism, appears in the Stanford Encyclopedia's Spinoza article. [2](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2021/entries/spinoza-modal/)

We do not have to posit a collection of independently existing complete worlds to use the notation. Nor does the conclusion mean that distinct subjects, experiences or physical regions cannot exist **within** the one whole.

The logical scope matters. If the identity is restricted to a subset of propositions, only those propositions follow as necessary; unmentioned facts may remain undetermined by that restricted claim. A single abstract model in which a proposition holds is not independent evidence that the actual whole necessarily has that feature.

## 3. Plenitude within one whole

Let `F(s)` be a qualitative or physical feature of an internal occurrence `s`. If the unrestricted thesis applies to the complete-whole proposition `∃s F(s)`, then

```text
◇(∃s F(s))  →  ∃s F(s)  →  □(∃s F(s)).
```

Thus every **genuinely possible internal feature**, understood as something that can occur somewhere in the one complete whole, occurs there necessarily. It need not occur everywhere, at the same time, or in the same subject as an incompatible feature. Suffering and pleasure can be distinct local experiences within one necessary whole.

This is plenitude without multiple ultimate wholes or a cosmic act of selection. But a merely conceivable description or consistent mathematical model is not thereby established as a genuinely possible concrete feature. If the proposition “there is no suffering anywhere in the entire whole” is contrary to the actually experienced existence of suffering, the strong modal thesis makes that global absence not genuinely possible. That does not say a particular individual must suffer, or that suffering is good, justified, unreal, or erased by positive experience.

The relevant distinction is **not** an arbitrary loophole to exclude unwanted cases. A successful constitution must explain non-circularly why its genuinely possible internal features are exactly those it entails. It cannot first stipulate the observed facts, call every other case impossible and present the definition as a discovery.

## 4. Does this mean determinism?

It means **global metaphysical necessity**: the actual whole, including the facts in scope, could not have been otherwise. This is closely related to, but distinct from, temporal determinism.

Temporal determinism ordinarily says that a complete state at one time and the laws fix the state at later times. That leaves two separate questions:

* Even deterministic laws may have multiple allowable initial states. Unless the whole's constitution determines the initial state too, temporal determinism alone does not show that the totality is necessary.
* A necessary complete whole need not be fundamentally described as a state evolving from an earlier state. Time may be relational or emergent. A single necessary relational structure may contain multiple internal continuations or histories; whether actual physics does so is a separate substantive question.

Thus “everything is necessary” is a better label for the user's claim than the narrower “the future is determined by the past.” Quantum probabilities and local uncertainty require a further account under the proposed whole: necessity of the complete totality neither derives the Born rule nor decides, by itself, whether probability represents ignorance, branching or another physical structure. No literal simulations are assumed.

## 5. Where the two senses must enter a non-circular derivation

Let `T` be a candidate characterization of the zero whole, and let `p` describe a physical or an experiential fact. A clear sufficient route is

```text
T is necessarily instantiated;
every realization of T agrees on p, preserving actual physical and experiential distinctions;
therefore p is necessary.
```

The second line is a substantive determining condition, not merely “T has a unique abstract graph.” It must preserve real subject unity, qualitative character and valence as well as physical organization. A formal permutation that changes suffering into pleasure cannot be dismissed as a harmless relabelling without justification.

To satisfy the user's demand, the account needs to explain together:

1. Why the zero constitution `T` is self-subsistent and necessarily instantiated, rather than assuming the existence of a specially named abstract model.
2. Why its structural relations determine the actual physical organization and laws, with time possibly internal or emergent.
3. Why and how the **same constitution is actually experienced**, including subject plurality and genuine felt positive and negative character, rather than appending phenomenal labels to a mathematical object.
4. Why those determinations are exhaustive: no genuinely possible unactualized physical–experiential alternative, and no unexplained choice of subject, sign, intensity or incidence measure.
5. If a well-defined objective whole-level quantity `Q` is the relevant zero, why `T` determines `Q=0`. Modal necessity of `Q` alone does not determine its value. A zero total does not erase local nonzero experience; if negative felt valence is part of `Q`, its physical meaning and compensating contributions require explanation.

The zero/infinity argument about a whole-level quantity is part of the user's motivation; this analysis does not reopen its settled exclusion of the relevant infinity candidate. Nor does it demand that fundamental reality be merely a quantity.

## 6. What is established and what is not

**Established conditional on the user's thesis:** at the stated global scope, possibility–actuality identity makes actual facts necessary; any genuinely possible internal occurrence is actual and necessary somewhere in the one whole. Actual experience implies that global absence of experience is not genuinely possible on this reading. This result was already developed in [Self-presence and the impossibility of nothingness](SELF_PRESENCE_AND_NO_NOTHINGNESS_UNIFICATION.md); it is not presented as a new discovery.

**The substantive proposal:** structural and experiential intelligibility together demand a single self-subsistent, no-residue physical–experiential constitution, with no external selection and no genuinely possible unactualized alternative.

**The unresolved derivation:** why that demand follows from zero's own nature, why it determines the actual laws and felt characters, and why any proposed global experiential or energetic quantity equals zero. No conclusion here says the user's demand is impossible, or promises that the derivation will automatically succeed.
