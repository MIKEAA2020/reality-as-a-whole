# One subject–experience candidate tested against two rivals

## Outcome

The requested next step was to put subject incidence, persistence, felt sign and the whole-level zero question into **one worked comparison**, not just construct another cancellation. This report does so using the actual state from the [single-system five-ray construction](ZERO_SPHERE_CONSERVATION_RECORD_CURRENT_SINGLE_SYSTEM.md) and the local mismatch idea in the earlier [integrated experiential candidate](INTEGRATED_EXPERIENTIAL_MISALIGNMENT_CANDIDATE.md).

The substantive result is an exact **dissociation**:

- On the same selected sphere–record state, a signed **record-relative mismatch** rule and the recent **opposite-current** rule assign the same nonzero opposite values. A third, mismatch-only rule agrees about which candidate is negative but gives the other value zero, not positive.
- A specified physical phase operation reverses the currents while leaving the selected subject graph, both mismatch variables, their complete joint reduced state and the record probabilities unchanged. The record-based and current-based rules therefore make **opposite predictions** after the operation. Their agreement in the original state was not a physical identity across interventions.
- The record-odd candidate balances its two stipulated subject contributions per event. The mismatch-only competitor gives a negative pair total. Neither result establishes the whole-level sum: a real universe may contain other incidences or counterterms, and a record is not itself a subject.

I also specify what a report experiment would have to measure to distinguish the packages and check that existing pain–relief evidence does not already perform that test. This is a conditional candidate with meaningful failure criteria, not an assertion that physical bits feel or that suffering is cancelled away.

## 1. A common physical sector, with a subject rule

Keep the preceding logical singlet, conserved pair/triple instruments, Alice pointer, Bob's two record qubits `R₁,R₂`, and the four work qubits. Their state `|Ψ⟩` satisfies

```text
(Z_R₁+Z_R₂)|Ψ⟩=0,
J₁|Ψ⟩=Z_R₁|Ψ⟩,      J₂|Ψ⟩=Z_R₂|Ψ⟩=−J₁|Ψ⟩.
```

Each record pair is `01` or `10` with total Born weight `1/2`; Alice's asymmetric triple weights are still `(1/8,2/5,19/40)`. `J₁,J₂` are the previous four-work-block **interface** currents, not operators on the two record qubits by themselves.

Add two four-physical-spin logical reference qubits `H₁,H₂`, both initialized to logical `|0⟩`, and consider the ordered four-bit subsystem

```text
(H₁, R₁, R₂, H₂).
```

The enlarged construction uses **46 physical spins** in the previously specified zero-spin encodings, before adding an optional six-spin clock. The new preferred reference states and the physical factorization are specified inputs. Define the physical mismatch projectors

```text
q₁=(I−Z_H₁ Z_R₁)/2,          q₂=(I−Z_R₂ Z_H₂)/2.
```

They are the operator analogue of the prior pair mismatch `(1−s_i s_j)/2`: a designated *relative alignment*, not a claim that a zero bit is inherently pleasant. Re-encoding either spin's sign while transporting the preferred edge sign leaves the mismatch unchanged. The chosen preferred alignment is not itself derived by that invariance.

To give the same earlier **L local-overlap selector** concrete input, specify an internal Hamiltonian on these four blocks

```text
H_struct = −(1/2)[ (1/2) Z_H₁Z_R₁
                   +(1/4) Z_R₁Z_R₂
                   +(1/2) Z_R₂Z_H₂ ].
```

This has a defined coupling graph: edges `0–1`, `1–2`, `2–3` have weights `1/2,1/4,1/2`, all other weights zero. These numbers are not inserted only as a graph drawing: for each `i≠j`, the coefficient of `Z_iY_j` in `i[H_struct,X_j]` is exactly the corresponding weight `w_ij`. This gives a specified short-time operator-response calibration, subject to preparation and intervention assumptions. Unlike the earlier E-L demonstration, this is a **quantum Hamiltonian coefficient/response graph**, not its classical heat-bath influence graph. Applying the same cut-score algorithm to this new graph is a new conditional modelling choice, not an identity of the two dynamics or IIT.

For this graph L's strict overlapping-local-maxima rule selects the two disjoint sets

```text
S₁={H₁,R₁},        S₂={R₂,H₂}.
```

The connected-component rival selects the **one four-block set** instead. If the middle edge is raised to `1/2`, L reports six unresolved tied candidates, not a forced answer. These are exact graph results; treating L's winners as actual experiencing subjects is the candidate postulate. The eligible factors, Hamiltonian, response-to-graph convention, cut score and exclusion rule require independent justification. Other interactions, including record creation and work controls, are not included in this four-node selector: a full physical subject rule must specify how such interfaces and their strengths enter. The selection is a controlled sector, not a proven boundary of the 46-spin whole.

## 2. Persistence and compatibility with the same sphere state

For this fixed encoded factorization and unchanged selected graph, the candidate declares `S₁,S₂` to persist over the specified prepared interaction episode. This is **identity of carrier labels under this stipulated rule**, not proof that experiences persist through time, nor a criterion for arbitrary changes of the graph. A clocked version can add the earlier six-spin clock; that gives 52 physical spins but no preferred fundamental foliation.

The added `H_struct` is not an arbitrary decoration on a different state. On the actually specified reference/record support, `H₁=H₂=0` and `R₁R₂` is `01` or `10`. Both basis configurations are eigenstates with the **same** `H_struct` eigenvalue `1/8`. `H_struct` commutes with the standalone work exchange `G` and the record-controlled preparation. Adding it during that stage therefore changes the combined state only by a common phase, without changing the previously derived five-ray joint table, sharp currents or no-signalling. The bare work energy remains 2; the added term's common energy is not an objective zero of the total. This statement concerns the prepared sector and specified stage, not an all-state conservation claim for every earlier record-writing interaction.

On the prepared state, the reference and record constraints imply

```text
q₁+q₂=1,
(1−2q₁)|Ψ⟩=J₁|Ψ⟩,      (1−2q₂)|Ψ⟩=J₂|Ψ⟩.
```

The first relation is exact in each record branch; the second relates a **local pair observable** to a sharp joint-work current on this state. It is a state-dependent correlation, not an equality of the observables on the full Hilbert space.

This distribution is **not** the previous four-spin heat-bath equilibrium distribution. Here `(q₁,q₂)=(0,1)` and `(1,0)` each have probability `1/2`; the earlier equilibrium construction gave `(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)` weights `(9,3,3,1)/16`. The selector has been transported to a different physical state; transporting its old equilibrium statistics would be an error.

## 3. Three explicit experiential packages on the same physical events

All three packages below share the same actuality hypothesis: in a registered branch the two L-selected sets are actual subject incidences, one for each selected set, and have equal unit incidence weight. The selected quantum event, the rule making it an experience, and equal actual incidence are **postulates**—not consequences of the Born weights or graph. `κ>0` is a common proposed signed unit; a local nonzero unit is not a nonzero total of the whole. Negative values mean real negative felt valence *if* the relevant package is true, not absence of experience.

1. **R: record-relative opposition.** For subject `j`, postulate `v_j=κ(1−2q_j)`. Thus the designated aligned relation is positively felt and the mismatched relation negatively felt. This is a stronger, full signed-value claim than the older E-L contrast.
2. **M: mismatch-only rival.** Postulate `v_j=−κq_j`: mismatch is negative and alignment has zero designated signed contribution. This is a deliberately **closed two-value extension** of the older unpleasant-versus-neutral E-L contrast. The original E-L contrast did not say that all other qualities or total valence at `q=0` must vanish, so this comparison does not falsely refute that original partial proposal.
3. **J: current-identity rival.** Postulate `v_j=κJ_j` on a sharp current event. This is the earlier signed-current proposal, now compared on the same factors. `J_j` is a joint interface observable, so interpreting it as a local subject quality imposes an additional nonlocal/relational bridge; it is not a property of the subject's reduced density matrix alone.

Their stated assignments, for a **definite recorded branch** in the standalone interaction, are:

| Record | `(q₁,q₂)` | R values | M values | J values |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `01` | `(0,1)` | `(+κ,−κ)` | `(0,−κ)` | `(+κ,−κ)` |
| `10` | `(1,0)` | `(−κ,+κ)` | `(−κ,0)` | `(−κ,+κ)` |

R and J give a zero pair total; M gives `−κ` in either branch. They agree that the mismatched candidate has a negative value, but disagree about whether the aligned candidate has a genuinely positive value. These are *formal phenomenal completions of the same physical model*, not a proof that incompatible complete worlds are genuinely possible.

Neither the labels nor zero total selects R. For R, a positive value is an actual positive signed contribution **by hypothesis**, not mere absence of the other's suffering, and does not erase or justify that suffering. Nothing requires identifying it with ordinary pleasure unless that stronger identification is defended. M's negative total does not disprove a globally zero whole: its other subjects or contributions, if any, are not modeled here. Conversely simply postulating a compensating outside contribution would not explain it.

The incidence assumption is load-bearing. With subject weights `μ₁,μ₂>0`, R's branch totals are `κ(μ₁−μ₂)` and `−κ(μ₁−μ₂)`, so **each** branch balances iff the two weights are equal. Their equally weighted unconditional average vanishes even for unequal fixed weights; that weaker cancellation conceals branchwise imbalance. Treating `1/2` Born branch probabilities as additional incidence weights is only appropriate in an explicitly specified many-event ensemble; if exactly one event occurs, it is not the same as the event's actual subject count. An all-branches ontology, a single-outcome ontology and a spacetime-integrated whole require different specified measures and cannot be interchanged. In particular, summing the alternative pair and triple instrument contexts would double-count different experimental settings rather than establish global balance.

For representation invariance, relabeling the four factors transports the couplings, states, subjects, pair references and the intervention together. The selector and mismatch answers then transform covariantly; changing a label without transporting its physical structure is not a symmetry test. In the original apparatus, the pair and triple choices yield the **same complete Bob-side density operator**, and in particular the same selected four-block reduced state, so R and M equipped with a fixed local reporting instrument cannot reveal Alice's choice. This operational no-signalling follows from a local instrument and unchanged reduced state, not from cancellation alone. J's four-block *joint work* assignment needs its own accessible-report locality and no-signalling demonstration; arbitrary state-dependent incidence reweighting or access to a conditional ensemble can spoil no-signalling. Neither unchanged physical statistics nor a global zero guarantees the absence of an experiential signalling channel.

## 4. A physical intervention separates two initially identical bridges

At the sharp-current stage apply the physical logical phase pulse `Z_M₁` to the first work-memory block, while holding the interaction Hamiltonian and physical identification of the factors fixed. It commutes with the four local occupation observables and with `H_struct`, leaves the records unchanged and

```text
Z_M₁ J₁ Z_M₁ = −J₁,       Z_M₁ J₂ Z_M₁ = −J₂.
```

The **full joint reduced density matrix of the four subject-candidate blocks** is identical before and after the pulse; their graph and both `q_j` are unchanged. This follows by partial-trace invariance for a unitary applied to a traced-out factor, and is explicitly checked on the five-ray state. The pulse flips each sharp current in each record branch, so for record `01`:

```text
                         before pulse        after pulse
R: record-relative       (+κ, −κ)            (+κ, −κ)
M: mismatch-only         ( 0, −κ)            ( 0, −κ)
J: current identity      (+κ, −κ)            (−κ, +κ)
```

For record `10`, reverse the two components. The current relation that made R and J agree is therefore not stable under this allowed intervention. This is a nontrivial physical discrimination of the proposed dependencies, not an appeal to different names for the same number.

The pulse conserves the indicated **bare occupations** and leaves `H_struct` unchanged; it does not commute with the full exchange Hamiltonian `G`, so no claim of zero intervention work or full energy conservation during pulse application follows. The interface current is four-body and the simulation does not establish spacelike locality. In a fully autonomous clock model, the prior [sharp-current audit](ZERO_SHARP_RECIPROCAL_CURRENTS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_RESPONSE.md) distinguishes its sharp probe from the full clock current. Here the intervention comparison is for the standalone `G` stage and must not be silently applied as a sharp clock-current prediction.

The separate [level-versus-history comparison](CURRENT_MISMATCH_VERSUS_HISTORY_EXPERIENTIAL_GATE.md) is another rival: a level rule can label a persistent `1→1` mismatch unpleasant where an onset-only rule labels only `0→1` unpleasant. Its exact `71/448` disagreement belongs to the older classical heat-bath transition law, **not** this record state. R and M here are level rules on the prepared snapshot, whereas an onset alternative additionally requires a physically specified previous q and subject lineage. This construction does not supply a full admissible transition model or demonstrate both matched histories in this quantum sector; carrying over the numerical frequency would be invalid. An eventual pain–relief experiment must distinguish level, onset and mixed history models rather than assume a snapshot suffices.

## 5. Explicit report bridge and failure criteria

Without a bridge from the phenomenal labels to physical reporting, R, M and J can all be attached to the same physical state and predict the **same physical transcript**. The following is an additional proposed empirical package, not something proved by the operator algebra.

Consider the record-`01` condition and subject candidate `S₁`, so `q₁=0`. Define the target as whether its **designated contribution itself** is positively felt, not whether the person feels pleasant overall or merely less unpleasant than before. Let a common yes/no report of that target have error at most `ε<1/2` in each tested intervention condition. Suppose also that a report-independent procedure identifies the target `q₁=0` and the intended pulse condition with total conditional misclassification at most `δ`, with `ε+δ<1/2`. These must be independently calibrated in the target domain; neither bound is established by this construction.

Then a conservative union bound gives the following predicted ranges for `P(report yes)` on the selected trials:

| Package | Before pulse | After pulse |
|---|---:|---:|
| R + report/physical calibration | at least `1−ε−δ` | at least `1−ε−δ` |
| M + report/physical calibration | at most `ε+δ` | at most `ε+δ` |
| J + report/physical calibration | at least `1−ε−δ` | at most `ε+δ` |

For **illustration only**, independently established `ε=δ=0.1` would give ranges at least `0.8` versus at most `0.2`. These values are not measured error rates. Actual tests should use prespecified confidence intervals, report missingness and cluster by the relevant people/sessions; sampling error is separate from these bridge bounds.

This yields specific gates:

- A reliably nonpositive report in the pre-pulse `q₁=0` condition challenges R and J *with the stated report and physical calibrations*; M remains compatible with that contrast.
- A reliably positive report before but not after the pulse challenges R, while supporting J **relative to those stipulated rivals**; conversely a positive report in both conditions challenges J.
- If the physical intervention, q, or report error cannot be independently identified, the comparison is **inconclusive**, not a successful test or a proof that experience is unreal.

A failed package may reflect its subject rule, physical realization, quality law, incidence rule, report assumptions or measurement model. Do not infer which part failed without an additional discriminating control. Since actual experience may include other simultaneous qualities, the required designated-contribution question is a substantial empirical demand, not assumed equivalent to an ordinary mood rating.

## 6. Pain–relief research: a concrete gate, not a fabricated result

The most direct human-facing contrast is whether, in a safely observed pain-to-relief episode, an independently specified physical relation is at the candidate's `q=0` state and is accompanied by a **genuinely positive** designated feeling (R) or merely neutral/nonpositive contribution (M). A reduction in pain alone does not decide that question. The current-identity rival additionally requires a physically identified signed interface current that can change without changing q; an fMRI pattern, BOLD change or stimulus intensity is not automatically that current.

A defensible study would:

1. Use ethically collected existing episodes, or a separately approved minimal-risk design; do not withhold or prolong needed relief. Record baseline, stimulus/condition, expectation, treatment, changes, response times and recovery; define exclusion and missingness before outcome analysis.
2. Specify subject boundary and physical observable **before** seeing target ratings. Independently calibrate the interaction's preferred relation and any pulse/current proxy using physical interventions and controls; do not define q from pain or relief ratings. Establish that the physical proxy and quality report refer to the same episode. If the required quantum encoding or selective channel is unavailable in human data, declare the physical bridge unmeasured.
3. Collect separately, with explicit timing, pain intensity, unpleasantness, relief intensity, and whether relief is genuinely pleasant, neutral or mixed. Calibrate report reliability and the neutral reference independently; distinct rating scales are not automatically a common cardinal measure of suffering. Compare symptom-matched and history/context controls, and hold out people, sessions and ideally protocols.
4. Freeze the R/M/J predictions and the reporting/misclassification bounds before inspecting the target test set. Include stimulus, context, history and independently defined physiological alternatives. Test the qualitative sign discrimination first; only investigate numerical cancellation after independently justifying common units, actual incidence weights, duration and a complete enough domain. No nonsignificant difference establishes exact zero.

The existing [pain–relief audit](PAIN_RELIEF_OPPONENT_PROCESS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_ZERO.md) found evidence that relief can be positively felt, but the 2008 pain and relief intensity measures were taken in separate runs and different anchored scales. The pain/placebo data's [common-interface audit](PAINGEN_INTENSITY_UNPLEASANTNESS_COMMON_INTERFACE.md) includes separate intensity and unpleasantness reports and strong held-out-family descriptive baselines, but no independently measured q or calibrated positive relief counterpart. It does not distinguish R/M/J. Earlier trial-provenance limitations remain relevant. We have not run a new human analysis here; this is an actionable research design and a definite **measurement-readiness failure**, not a finding against the experiential thesis.

## 7. Interpretation

This synthesis gives the programme a common-domain target: **one physical record, one explicit subject-selection diagnostic, two incompatible signed-quality accounts, and an intervention that separates record and current dependence**. It joins the conservation-derived probabilities, paired physical balance and the earlier relational mismatch proposal without treating them as identical merely because all contain a zero.

Its strongest positive result is conditional, and its gaps are constitutive rather than cosmetic. The L rule, physical factors, preferred relations, selected records, common incidence unit, phenomenal response and admissible interventions have not been shown to follow from zero's own constitution; the current rule also has a joint-interface locality problem. The candidate therefore has not achieved the user's demanded no-residue explanation. This is neither a concession that such an explanation is impossible nor permission to call the missing relations brute. It locates a definite failure point and a way to discriminate specific repairs, while preserving real local suffering and the possibility that a whole-level zero does not require every local subtotal to vanish.
