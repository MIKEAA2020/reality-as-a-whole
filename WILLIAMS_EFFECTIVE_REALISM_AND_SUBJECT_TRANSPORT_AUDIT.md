# Effective realism, scale and the experiential candidate

## Source and result

Porter Williams, [*Scientific Realism Made Effective*](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axx043), argues that effective quantum field theories (EFTs) can support realist commitments without being exact descriptions of nature at every scale. The supplied [38-page PDF](sources/uploads/williams2019.pdf) was read in full as extracted text, including notes and references; its pagination is cited below. Pages 19, 23 and 29 were also visually inspected. The supplied copy has SHA-256 `da007a63d521c899dfa1006cde355ac2d0a09d1516eb48dfdf14aeece6094ed3`. It is a manuscript-form copy; page numbers here are PDF pages, not the journal pagination. Its cited theorems, calculations, technical sources and empirical results were not independently replayed. The copyright notice is included in the supplied PDF; its presence here follows the user's explicit direction to include supplied source files in the public repository, not a claim that all redistribution rights have been independently established.

**Consequential lesson for this project:** the reality of a scale-dependent pattern need not imply that it is a fundamental object or that its modeling cutoff is physical. But a robust effective physical pattern still does not, by itself, determine a subject or what an experience is like. Effective realism is compatible with the user's plural experiential ground; it does not derive it or remove the demand for **full structural and experiential intelligibility**.

## Williams's argument

1. **Interpret empirically applicable theories as effective.** Williams contrasts a stylized 'standard interpretation' that takes a theory as exact, exhaustive and isolated, with interpretation informed by its actual range of validity and scientific applications (pp. 2–12). His 'standard account' is a deliberately constructed composite, not a claim that every named philosopher accepts every listed ingredient (p. 4).
2. **Use qualified robustness rather than treating every formula as ontology.** Following Wimsatt's robustness and selective realism, Williams stresses empirically important elements accessible in multiple independent ways, and use of renormalization-group methods to examine dependence on high-energy descriptions and regulators (pp. 13–24). Stability is evidence for a commitment, not a proof of unique metaphysics. Regulator changes require appropriate matching and convergence; two numerically different descriptions cannot be declared equivalent just because both have cutoffs.
3. **Allow higher-level entities to be real.** His QCD example argues that hadronic degrees of freedom matter for explanation even though they are not the putative most fundamental description (pp. 25–31). He explicitly distinguishes physics-practice derivations from a fully rigorous derivation of confinement (p. 29, note 40). This supports the legitimacy of examining real effective subjects, not an identification of subjects with hadrons or proof that every effective pattern has experience.
4. **Lattice example is conditional.** His scalar illustration uses Euclidean lattice symmetries, a specific model and an RG argument for suppression of certain symmetry-breaking terms; it identifies analytic continuation to Lorentzian spacetime as an additional step (pp. 22–23, note 31). It is not a general theorem that all discrete or non-spatiotemporal microscopic models automatically recover relativistic physics. The manuscript's printed exponent on p. 23 uses `dim(O_i)+4`; its dimensional convention is not clear from this page. We visually verified that transcription but have not resolved the convention or reproduced its RG calculation. Do not use this displayed power as a checked formula.

Williams cites [Collins et al., *Lorentz invariance and quantum gravity: an additional fine-tuning problem?*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.93.191301) (p. 21, note 28). Its [abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15600824/) warns that in the authors' preferred-frame setting loop effects may produce unsuppressed low-energy violations unless parameters are tuned. Only that abstract was read, not its proof, figure or responses. This is a reason to specify symmetries and radiative stability, **not** a claim that every lattice regularization is a physical preferred frame or that Williams's example has been disproved. Regulator dependence and hypothetical actual microphysics are different issues.

## A common-domain transport obligation for E–L

Apply Williams's realism guidance to the **existing** [integrated candidate](INTEGRATED_EXPERIENTIAL_MISALIGNMENT_CANDIDATE.md) and its [same-kernel horizon audit](INTEGRATED_SUBJECT_HORIZON_AND_VALENCE_AUDIT.md), rather than introducing a new subject selector. This section is a project-specific conditional requirement, not a claim made by Williams.

Consider a proposed physical realization with two descriptions `d₁,d₂`. Before computing subject or valence outputs, specify:

- an operational domain `D`: preparations, local interventions, observables, time windows, error tolerances and resources; and an independently justified matching procedure relating descriptions on `D`;
- whether the difference is **mere representation** (e.g. regulator or permitted relabeling) or a **different physical question** (e.g. changing intervention horizon). For a gauge/redescription, a physical output must respect the equivalence; for distinct horizons, invariance is not automatic;
- subject output `S(d)`, numerical mismatch `q(d)`, a cross-description map identifying or relating subjects, and the purported phenomenal output. Specify whether the map is one-to-one, multigrain, approximate, or unavailable; a subject count alone is not a correspondence;
- which output is asserted as a physical estimator and which is a postulated experiential bridge, with independently anchored report/condition controls and an experience-respecting rival on the **same** matched domain.

For a precise diagnostic, let `σ_D(d)` denote a vector of the selected physical operational predictions on `D` and let `q(d)` be the candidate's mismatch. If the physical data in `σ_D` alone are asserted to identify `q` exactly, there must be a function `f` such that `q(d)=f(σ_D(d))` throughout the declared admissible class. Otherwise two admissible descriptions `d₁,d₂` with identical `σ_D` but different `q` invalidate that **particular identification claim**: no single `f` can output both values. If `|q(d₁)−q(d₂)|=Δ`, any single estimator based only on their common signature errs by at least `Δ/2` for at least one of them, by the triangle inequality. This elementary observation does not establish that such a pair exists for a real neural system; it says what a claimed identification would need to exclude. A richer signature or additional justified structure can remove the ambiguity.

For approximate matching, require a declared metric and demonstrate an explicit modulus `|q(d₁)−q(d₂)| ≤ ω(ε)` whenever `dist(σ_D(d₁),σ_D(d₂))≤ε` within the specified admissible class. Such a bound is a **requirement on a proposed inference**, not something established by this paper, a generic consequence of EFT, or a proof that a particular finite set of interventions is complete. Cutoff extrapolation, measurement noise and the subject-correspondence map need their own error accounting. A discontinuous argmax selector can change its winning boundary even when inputs move very little near a tie; score stability alone therefore does not guarantee subject-label stability.

This physical-description transport requirement complements the existing [experiential calibration transport gate](EXPERIENTIAL_MISMATCH_CALIBRATION_TRANSPORT_GATE.md), which studies a different problem: transporting an independently anchored source error bound between populations under condition-stability and overlap assumptions. Passing either requirement does not establish the other. The factorization diagnostic and triangle bound above are elementary identifiability reasoning, not a new theorem.

The existing four-spin result illustrates a different issue: one- and nine-update horizons give different L winners on the same kernel. Those are **different operational queries**, not yet proved to be alternative regulators of the same query. A contradiction arises only if both are independently claimed to represent the very same horizon-independent subject decomposition. Otherwise the candidate needs a defended local horizon, a cross-grain account, or another justified rule. One-update was already specified; the observed crossing does not invalidate that stipulation, but does not make it experientially privileged either. Local dynamical scales need not be an external universal clock or fundamental spacetime.

Even a successful transport test for `q` and `S` would constrain only the proposed physical maps. To meet C3, the framework still needs a defensible constitutive account of **why that physical relation is this experienced quality for this subject**, not merely a reliable correlate or assignment by definition. First-person experience remains an accepted datum, not an artifact to be explained away. Robust neural patterns need not be fundamental, and their possible effective status does not require a universal subject.

## Battery and next decisive test

B4–B5 require a justified subject correspondence and experiential constitution; B6–B8 require actual recovery and control of physical domains, including emergent spacetime and gravity; B10 requires independently anchored predictions and interventions. This paper improves the criteria for these tasks but **passes none of them**. To advance rather than repeat an abstract toy, choose an authorized common dataset or experimental platform where interventions, sensor processing and relevant time windows can be independently checked, predeclare matching and tolerances, and compare the existing candidate against context/expectation and other neural predictors. Independently test whether the proposed physical interaction measure survives admissible changes of reference and resolution. Distinguish failure of that particular proxy from failure of experiential ground or all relational-misalignment proposals.
