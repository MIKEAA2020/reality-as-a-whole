# Experiential ground and the choice of relational description

## The remaining issue

The hair example distinguishes an ordinary object's lack of a separately evidenced experience from the question whether reality's fundamental nature is experiential. The defensible working view is **selective subjecthood with the status of a fundamental experiential ground unresolved**, not a claim that the whole has one universal subject. See [hair and intrinsic nature](HAIR_EXPERIENCE_INTRINSIC_NATURE_AND_STRUCTURE.md). The question now is what a proposal must specify before “experience is intrinsic to relations” becomes more than a way of redescribing the same predictions.

Wainwright and Jordan's [*Graphical Models, Exponential Families, and Variational Inference*](https://doi.org/10.1561/2200000001) appendix E.3 gives an established auxiliary-variable transformation. Their monograph's extracted text has now been read across all 305 pages, including appendices and references; this is not a complete figure, equation or independent proof audit, and the recovered [mirror](https://github.com/alphabetakappa/Probabilistic-Graphical-Models-Materials/blob/e97f3fe56c394b437f1826e1b57ec97df97c9fbb/2008%20Graphical%20Models%2C%20Exponential%20Families%2C%20and%20Variational%20Inference%20%28M.%20Jordan%29.pdf) has not been byte-compared to the authors' PDF. We apply their *known* construction to a precise representation-invariance check below; we do not claim the construction as new. The broader invariance requirement and intervention-preserving bijections were already developed in [subject constitution and factorization](SUBJECT_CONSTITUTION_FACTORIZATION_GATE.md); this example extends that earlier check to a redundant, non-bijective augmentation rather than discovering the general principle.

## One probability law, two graphs

Take three binary variables `X=(x1,x2,x3)` with a three-way parity factor: give even-parity configurations weight `2` and odd-parity configurations weight `1`. Normalization gives total weight `12`. The corresponding factor graph has three variable nodes and a genuinely three-variable potential.

Introduce an auxiliary variable `A=(a1,a2,a3)` with eight possible values. Give `A` the same parity weight and connect it to each original bit using the pairwise factor `1[ai=xi]`. Summing over `A` recovers the **same normalized joint law of X** exactly. The auxiliary is a deterministic copy in this mathematical representation; it is not thereby an independently existing neuron, physical object or subject.

[`verification/auxiliary_representation_subject_gate.py`](verification/auxiliary_representation_subject_gate.py) checks the full original and augmented probability tables, all **27** patterns of conditioning on the original variables (including no condition), and the equality of the original and augmented joint entropies. The new graph has four variable nodes rather than three, but the same accessible original law; its fourth variable is eight-valued, not another binary variable. The [receipt](verification/auxiliary_representation_subject_gate.json) records the results.

A rule that naively assigns one subject to every variable node gives three subjects in the first description and four in the second. That difference is an artifact of the representation. This finite example therefore rejects **that specific rule**, if both graphs are descriptions of the same physical realization. It does not reject a rule based on independently identified actual components or processes: a real auxiliary device is not automatically equivalent to a redundant mathematical variable merely because both can be drawn as a node.

## What this does—and does not—resolve

- **Representation invariance is necessary.** A constitution rule cannot change its subject count or qualities under a merely redundant encoding of the same declared physical system. It needs a map between descriptions and a specification of which features are physically represented rather than just bookkeeping.
- **A probability law is not the entire causal organization.** The check covers conditioning on the original variables, not interventions or dynamics. Giving the artificial auxiliary its own intervention can change the model and need not correspond to any physical operation. Equivalent observational laws can have different mechanisms, as the prior [causal-limit example](RETINA_CONDITIONAL_SCORE_DECOMPOSITION_AND_CAUSAL_LIMIT.md) illustrates.
- **Relations may be genuine without every chosen relation being a subject.** No inference from graph vertices or factors to experiential occurrences is justified by the probability calculus itself. Conversely, a relational-experiential account can add an explicit constitution rule; this example alone cannot disprove it.
- **An experiential aspect requires a coherent specification.** If a proposal says that every fundamental occurrence is experiential while denying a separate subject per occurrence, it must say what “experiential” means without quietly reintroducing a subject for each mathematical node. If the proposal instead says certain organizations alone have experience, it must identify and justify them. Neither condition is satisfied merely by relabeling statistical factors.
- **The metaphysical alternatives remain open.** A selective organization-based account can be combined with several ontologies; an experiential ground and a neutral/structural ground may agree on all measurements so far. Differences in prior explanatory cost are philosophical judgments, not measured Bayes factors. The example does not decide which account is true, and is not evidence that hair or any element of this toy model is conscious.

A serious future discriminator must specify a physical domain and its admissible redescription and intervention maps, derive different observable or independently accessible experiential consequences from rival constitution rules, and include appropriate negative controls. Nothing here assumes a universal agent, proves necessary plenitude, or derives local time from a timeless absolute.
