# A worked argument for due regard: entitlement, consistency, and privation

## 1. The target

The question is no longer whether an absence can be described. It is whether we can identify something **due**, rather than simply call a favored fulfillment good.

The user's money-and-power example gives a tractable case. Some acquisitions are embedded in commitments and entitlement rules. Within a specified rule, what is due can be derived without inspecting an agent's feelings of empathy. This supplies a local conditional argument, not the ultimate moral foundation of every entitlement rule.

## 2. A concrete conditional example

Suppose an employer and worker have a valid agreement under which completion of specified work entitles the worker to a specified payment. Suppose the work is completed, the obligation has not been discharged, and no relevant exception or defeating condition applies.

Then, under that rule:

1. The worker meets the conditions for payment.
2. Payment is due.
3. Deliberately withholding it fails to discharge something due.

The employer's agency, planning, production, and possession of money are actual. The relevant defect is not that money or acquisition is a negative substance. It is the failure to realize the due relation between completed work, the commitment, and payment.

This does not prove that every contract is morally valid. Coercive or exploitative arrangements may be objectionable despite formal legality. Validity and the absence of defeating conditions are explicit assumptions, not conclusions smuggled into the example.

## 3. The exact logical structure

Let Q(a,c) mean that agent a meets the qualifying conditions in circumstances c. Let E(a,c) mean that the entitlement applies. Adopt the specified rule:

    for every a and c: Q(a,c) -> E(a,c).

Given Q(w,c), E(w,c) follows by universal instantiation and modus ponens. Denying E(w,c) while retaining the rule, the qualifying facts, and the absence of exceptions is inconsistent.

The result does not create a normative rule from logic. It establishes what follows **within** the rule. An agent can instead dispute Q, identify a genuine exception, reject the rule's authority, or admit violation. Those alternatives are not all logically contradictory, though some may be factually false or normatively unjustified.

If the agent invokes the same general rule to protect their own entitlement while denying another's qualifying entitlement, an exception must be supported by a distinction allowed by that rule. “It is mine” is not automatically a valid exception. Agent-relative roles may sometimes be relevant, but their relevance must be stated rather than invented to preserve a desired verdict.

## 4. What the privative analysis gains

The proposed structure of wrongness is:

    actual powers and activities
    + an applicable due relation
    + failure to realize the required integration.

This is more precise than “destructive direction” or “missing empathy.” It locates the proposed deficiency in the relation between action and a justified standard.

The withheld payment and its consequences can have entirely actual causes. A privation analysis does not deny intentional action, interference, or harm. It identifies the respect in which the action fails a standard, not a complete causal theory of the deed.

Nor is every absence a failure to pay: no payment is due where the relevant qualifying conditions do not obtain. The standard distinguishes ordinary absence from the relevant deprivation.

## 5. Empathy is neither necessary nor sufficient for compliance

Within the example, an agent can feel little empathy yet discharge the obligation. Another can understand and feel another's distress yet withhold what is due.

The relevant proposed privation is therefore **missing due practical integration**, not necessarily a missing psychological faculty. To take a claim into account merely as information is insufficient: the action must treat it with the force the applicable standard gives it.

This clarifies the user's correction without converting it into a universal diagnosis of wrongdoers.

## 6. Where the local argument stops

A coherent adversary can reject all entitlement rules and recognize only power. Logic alone has not shown that adversary inconsistent. An agent can also accept a coherent but unjust institution. Institutional consistency is not identical with moral legitimacy.

The analysis therefore separates three questions:

- **Application:** what follows from this standard and these facts?
- **Justification:** why is this standard authoritative rather than merely imposed?
- **Constitution:** what in reality grounds that authority without an unexplained moral addition?

We have supplied an exact conditional application argument. We have not completed the justification or ultimate constitution argument.

This is not evidence that the latter are impossible. It prevents a successful local inference from being reported as a universal derivation.

## 7. A more specific candidate justification

The qualitative-value hypothesis can propose that agreements and claims are justified insofar as they organize relations among real beings with needs, agency, vulnerability, and forms of fulfillment. Their authority would not come from the bare existence of a convention, but from how that convention acknowledges those beings and their legitimate relations.

This is more concrete than saying that all norms are arbitrary goals. But it remains a proposed normative grounding. We must still justify why these features supply reasons and how they constrain conflicting claims. Mutual benefit alone cannot justify every arrangement, and consent or legality cannot simply be assumed sufficient in every circumstance.

On the proposed whole-account, the reasons would belong to the same concrete constitution as the beings and their relationships. They would not require another substance. An explanation of that identity remains to be supplied.

## 8. What is now established and what is conjectural

### Established conditionally

An applicable general entitlement rule plus qualifying facts entails the corresponding entitlement. An arbitrary exception incompatible with the retained rule cannot consistently be added. An unmet entitlement can be analyzed as a failure of due integration without denying the actuality of the action or its consequences.

The last claim is a coherent analysis of this specified case, not a proof that every wrong is privative.

### Better supported as a formulation

The user's example should be formulated in terms of **due regard and fulfillment of legitimate claims**, rather than the presence or absence of empathic feeling. This makes its dependence on a standard explicit and avoids false psychological generalization.

### Still conjectural

That every legitimate normative standard is grounded in qualitative fulfillment; that every wrong-making feature is privative; that intrinsic Good determines the ultimate constitution; and that Good explains concrete existence.

## 9. Consequence for the leading account

This provides a worked local instance of the architecture:

> Actual beings and powers enter relations whose fulfillment can be due; a wrong can consist in failing that due integration, rather than introducing a negative substance.

The next philosophical burden is now precisely located in the authority of the standards, not in the ability to describe missing integration. Under the complete-intelligibility objective, merely accepting an entitlement rule as a useful convention cannot be the final stopping point.
