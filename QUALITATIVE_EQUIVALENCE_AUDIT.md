# Is the remaining qualitative ambiguity substantive or representational?

## Question

The last finite audit left two candidate assignments after partial calibration. Its report correctly qualified that result: exchanging unanchored symbols is not automatically a different experience. Conversely, calling a permutation “gauge” cannot erase a difference whose qualitative reference is independently fixed.

The next constitutive question is therefore:

> **What makes two proposed qualitative organizations the same, rather than merely structurally similar?**

An answer could remove some apparent explanatory residue. It could also conceal the missing identity claim if adopted without argument.

## 1. Distinguish three cases

### A. Pure change of notation

Suppose c and d are only unused names. Exchanging them everywhere, including every relation and reference convention, changes the presentation but not its target. Counting both presentations as different phenomenal worlds would manufacture an unexplained difference.

No controversial consciousness theory is needed to reject duplication by notation. This is the realization/presentation discipline already adopted in the inquiry.

### B. Independently fixed qualitative reference

Suppose c and d refer to genuinely distinguished experiential contrasts, identified independently of the physical assignment under test. Exchanging which physical states constitute c and d is then a different proposed identification, not merely renaming the contrasts.

Whether those references are sufficiently warranted is an evidence question. The labels alone do not establish it. But if it is warranted, quotienting away their exchange would discard the very phenomenon the theory must explain.

### C. Same specified qualitative relations, disputed further difference

Two models may agree on all supplied similarity, discrimination, temporal, affective, and co-presentation relations while a proponent claims a further intrinsic difference. This is neither automatically a real difference nor automatically notation.

It is the substantive point of disagreement between a relational-exhaustion hypothesis and a view that qualitative character is not exhausted by those relations. Finite agreement on measured relations does not establish either position about the full qualitative nature.

## 2. A precise quotient calculation—and its limitation

Let S be a set of candidate maps f from physical states to a qualitative target structure Q. Let G be the group of target transformations declared to be mere re-presentations, preserving the independently fixed anchors. If g is an admissible presentation change, it acts by f↦g∘f. Then the intended identification problem concerns S/G, not merely the raw cardinality of S.

In the four-state example, take the two survivors

\[
f_A=(a,b,c,d),\qquad f_B=(a,b,d,c).
\]

If the exchange g=(c\ d) is an admissible change of unanchored notation, they belong to the same orbit. If c and d have independently fixed distinct reference and the admissible presentation group fixes that reference, g is not permitted and the two remain distinct candidates.

The mathematics computes the consequence of the chosen equivalence relation. It does not establish which transformations preserve actual qualitative identity. Selecting the larger group solely to obtain uniqueness would encode the answer.

An independently specified qualitative relation can also constrain the maps without identifying them by convention. If the target relation distinguishes c from d relative to an anchor and the candidate physical correspondence predicts that relation, one candidate may fail. That is substantive matching information, conditional on the correspondence hypothesis. It is not a license to derive experience from an arbitrary physical distance metric.

## 3. The stronger candidate principle

A possible additional premise is **qualitative-relational exhaustion**:

> A qualitative episode has no further qualitative difference beyond its complete actual qualitative-relational organization. A transformation preserving that entire organization and its occurrence relations does not leave an additional inverted qualitative fact behind.

This is a genuine identity hypothesis, not just a mathematical invariance convention. It rejects the claim that two genuinely identical complete qualitative organizations could nevertheless differ in an additional unrepresented qualitative respect.

Its strength depends entirely on what “qualitative-relational organization” independently specifies:

- If it includes the full qualitative identity of each constituent by definition, the principle is true but does not explain those identities.
- If it means only public behavior or discrimination statistics, its sufficiency is a strong, unestablished psychophysical claim.
- If it specifies intrinsic experiential relations independently and exhaustively, it is a substantive phenomenal structuralism whose truth and adequacy need argument.

“Complete” must not mean “whatever additional features make the principle true.” The candidate must declare its basis rather than change it whenever a proposed difference is raised.

## 4. What the principle could accomplish if justified

It could show that some proposed qualitative inversions are representational alternatives rather than additional facts needing a physical selector. That would be a genuine reduction of explanatory residue, rather than a new law arbitrarily pairing physical states with hidden quality labels.

It could also support a framework-neutral identity criterion: different mathematical descriptions could concern the same episode when they preserve the specified actual organization. This is compatible with the architecture of mutually constraining presentations.

But this is not yet an identification of physical organization with qualitative organization. There are two separate claims:

1. qualitative identity is exhausted by a specified qualitative-relational basis;
2. a specified realized physical process constitutes that basis.

The first, even if true, does not establish the second. The physical and phenomenal structures cannot be made identical merely by giving them matching mathematical forms.

Nor does either claim alone establish which constituents share one episode. Co-presentation cannot be put into the input and then reported as a derived composition law.

## 5. Can the extra premise be independently justified here?

Not yet. Several tempting arguments fall short:

### “An undetectable difference is not a difference”

This conflates public detectability, first-person distinction, and identity. A theory needs a defended identity criterion; operational indistinguishability in a restricted domain is not sufficient by itself.

### “All qualitative contrasts are relational”

Contrasting qualities involves relations, but it does not follow that the intrinsic character of the relata is fully explained by those relations. That additional inference requires an argument.

### “The same abstract structure must be the same experience”

An abstract structure can have multiple concrete realizations. A structural correspondence does not establish actual occurrence, phenomenal sufficiency, or numerical identity of tokens.

### “The remaining inversion is inconceivable, so it is impossible”

Limits on imagination are not a constitutive proof. Conversely, a merely asserted conceivable inversion does not prove metaphysical possibility. The theory must identify what is claimed to differ and whether its specified basis really preserves it.

### “The principle removes the unexplained residue, so it must be true”

The whole-account objective motivates seeking a successful dissolution, but does not justify one just because it would complete the explanation. The dissolution must be independently defended.

The repository therefore cannot promote qualitative-relational exhaustion to an established constitutive principle.

## 6. A sharper explanatory test

A candidate identity theory must now answer two explicit questions:

**Q1. Which distinctions are genuine target facts?**

State the independently warranted qualitative contrasts and relations, and identify the transformations that merely change their presentation. Do not require a physical law to select among names that have no independently fixed difference.

**Q2. Why does this actual process constitute those facts?**

Give the identity or realization argument and its evidence. Do not remove genuine target facts by enlarging the presentation group until the physical description has a unique match.

This prevents two opposite errors: inventing residual qualities by symbol permutation and eliminating real qualitative differences by mathematical quotienting.

Only after both questions are addressed can uniqueness of the mathematical match contribute to a constitutive explanation. Even then, token actuality, episode incidence, and the foundation of the proposed nature remain separate obligations.

## 7. Current result

The most defensible refinement is **reference-sensitive constitutive identity**:

> Preserve independently warranted qualitative differences; identify mere changes of presentation; and require a substantive realization argument for the remaining physical–phenomenal identification.

This is a discipline for building the missing law, not that law itself. The stronger qualitative-relational-exhaustion premise is a clearly stated candidate for dissolving some apparent residue, but remains unestablished. Our earlier relational matching work already supplied constraints on maps; the new issue here is the semantic and constitutive warrant for treating two maps as the same identification.

No new executable or empirical result is claimed. The existing 1,060 tests do not establish the qualitative equivalence relation or the truth of phenomenal structuralism.
