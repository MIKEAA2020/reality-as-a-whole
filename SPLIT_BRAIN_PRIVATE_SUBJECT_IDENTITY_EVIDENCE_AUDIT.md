# Private nested subjects versus a layered subject: the evidence gap

## Decision

The most informative source checked here is Pinto et al.'s [2023 DDC study](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2023.1278025), especially its **simultaneous** matching task with a direct confidence judgment and an anecdotal first-person remark. DDC reported finding cross-field displays easier, and was often highly confident even though cross-field same/different performance did not exceed chance. This is real evidence about his reporting perspective and task experience, not a report of a specific simultaneous local detail and global conjunction. It is a warning that confidence in a comparison must not be treated as a validated assay of its phenomenal contents. The later successful query task was **sequential**, not a simultaneous dual-content report.

Together with the two patients' broader reports in Pinto et al. 2017, the evidence gives positive weight to ordinary felt unity and constrains simple behavioral disconnection stories. It does **not** establish simultaneous P-style private local and global subject-occurrences, or rule them out by establishing complete access to all possible perspectives. Nor does it establish the one partially unified subject of C-layered. Both packages make the same coarse and detailed conjunction predictions, but disagree on numerical subject identity. A second hand, response, neural process, or similar content is not by itself a second experiencer. An independently warranted subject-incidence bridge is the decisive missing ingredient.

This is a retrospective focused source audit, not a new clinical study or a proof that empirical discrimination is impossible in principle. The [bounded reading plan](SPLIT_BRAIN_SUBJECT_INCIDENCE_EVIDENCE_READING_PLAN.md) was written after the existing project audits and initial search snippets were seen, and before detailed reading of the 2023 source for this question.

## 2. What the participants actually contributed

### 2017: ordinary experience and cross-response

In the 2017 article, **DDC and DDV both** reported seeing their entire visual field, feeling and controlling their whole body, and unchanged felt unity since surgery; the article glosses the latter as no other conscious agent seeming to be present. This is evidence about their experiences as communicated, and should not be dismissed because it is first-person. It is also not a complete transcript or a same-trial assertion about two specified briefly flashed items. A reporter's lack of apparent awareness of another agent does not establish absence of a private non-agentive subject, but neither does it constitute positive evidence that one exists.

Both took part in the single-circle detection/localization experiment; the circle was shown for 120 ms, with verbal and left/right-hand responses in separate blocks. The paper reports DDV hits/false alarms 100%/0%, DDC 97.5%/7.7%, and successful localization across fields and response types. DDC alone completed the further matching and confidence experiments. In simultaneous two-item matching, the article reports a mean 55.4% correct across fields and 90.4% within fields, with across-field performance not significantly above chance. The report modalities across blocks are not simultaneous, independent first-person testimonies from distinct subjects. The positive detection results contradict a rigid claim that each field can only be reported through its same-side hand; the nonsignificant cross-match result does not establish zero information or no conjunction.

The 2017 authors report full callosal removal for both; DDC also had most of the anterior commissure cut, and was selected for follow-up because his disconnection was reported as more severe. These are descriptions of distinct patients with histories and other possible routes, not a measurement that all relevant information channels have been eliminated. The authors describe fixation and cross-cue controls and discuss testing long after surgery. The participant histories cannot be pooled as though identical, and no one-to-one identification of hemispheres with model subjects is provided.

### 2023: confidence *during* a simultaneous task

The 2023 methods identify **the same DDC** as in the 2017 research and describe complete callosal and most anterior-commissure removal. The study used an eye tracker, fixation and exclusion rules, a chin rest and an experimenter unable to see the stimulus screen; these are meaningful controls, not proof of zero leakage or all relevant pathways. A saccade threshold and missing eye positions triggered exclusions in stated windows. We have not inspected trial-level exclusions, remaining distributions or confidence intervals.

In Experiment I, two shapes appeared **together for 120 ms**, both within one visual field or on opposite sides of fixation. The conditions were randomly intermixed, with three blocks of 48 trials; DDC responded verbally whether they were the same or different, then rated confidence from 1 to 3. Across-field performance was not significantly above chance (`p=0.5` in the authors' permutation test); within-field performance was above chance (`p=0.001`). He was highly confident on 52% of across-field trials versus 21% of within-field trials, a reported difference `p<0.001`; his confidence predicted accuracy within fields but not across them. The authors report his **anecdotal remark** that the experiment was much easier when stimuli appeared around fixation.

This is a useful matched-window subjective datum: the remark and trial confidence are about the task as performed, not merely an unrelated general impression. But the rating is confidence in a *same/different judgment*, not a request for whether the two individual items were experienced together, let alone whether a local subject and a separate global subject coexisted. The positions also differ across conditions: cross-field shapes were more central than at least one shape in the within-field displays, a possible contributor the authors note. Confident errors do not show that he saw nothing, that he had no unified coarse visual experience, or that his felt ease was insincere. Conversely they do not prove that a coarse bilateral experiential conjunction occurred. The authors discuss both a possible introspective deficit and their favored two-route explanation; neither is directly established by the confidence result.

In Experiment II, a 120-ms shape was followed by a fixation interval whose duration depended on an experimenter's refixation check, then a 120-ms question mark or X in the same or opposite field. An X instructed an X response; a question mark instructed reporting the earlier shape. DDC responded verbally. The paper reports 80.8% overall across-field accuracy and 68.7% on cross-field question-mark trials, and proposes a slower, deliberate route. Neither the two visual displays nor necessarily the underlying experiences were concurrent. The authors explicitly acknowledge delay and cross-cueing as alternatives and compare a different prior delayed-matching study; that comparison does not experimentally isolate delay from task and participant differences. Their article does not resolve whether a transferred representation is visual, conceptual, or verbal, or establish a specific neural path. Its nonsignificant across/within differences are not equivalence demonstrations. The [earlier query-task audit](SPLIT_BRAIN_DEEP_EVIDENCE_AND_TASK_AUDIT.md) explains why the selected question-mark result and the reported overall number do not automatically defeat every one-input strategy.

## 3. Mapping to the completed comparison

The fixed [private-nested comparison](PRIVATE_NESTED_FIELDS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTENT_COMPARISON.md) concerns the same stipulated four-spin episode and a designated population. It is **not** a patient model. For one declared window, detailed left/right content types and coarse occupied-location types are introduced by additional phenomenal stipulation:

| Completed package | Detailed conjunction `D` | Coarse conjunction `G` | Distinct local-detail and global-conjunction subjects `N` | Composition |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| L-exclusive | 0 | 0 | 0 | Two local fields; no designated global field. |
| C-full | 1 | 1 | 0 | One designated whole field with both detailed aspects. |
| C-layered | 0 | 1 | 0 | One designated subject has local and global aspects; detailed cross-side conjunction is absent. |
| P | 0 | 1 | 1 | Two local subjects plus a distinct global subject; each corresponding coarse occurrence is private to its own field. |

`N` requires at least one **local detailed** experiencer and a **different** global coarse-conjunction experiencer in the same window, within the designated population. P has exactly three designated fields in this example, not merely two channels. Shared type does not mean a shared token; physical overlap does not itself imply any subject or phenomenal overlap. C-layered's same-subject but nonconjoint detail is a contested incidence/unity premise, not a clinically established fact or a claim that Lopez endorses P. `D`, `G`, `N` are existential about this specified population: a positive can be evidenced by an accessible instance; a negative requires adequate coverage or another justified exclusion argument. Unknown is not zero.

To transfer a clinical observation to this table one would need: a prespecified common physical episode and target window; justified identification of physical supports with the stipulated sector, not just naming hemispheres as spins; independently anchored detailed/coarse **experiential** content types; a subject-individuation rule and access model; and the appropriate population coverage. No paper examined supplies these bridges. The 2023 120-ms display gives stimulus simultaneity, not directly the onset, duration or overlap of experiences. A 2017 general unity report, or 2023 task-confidence rating, does not fill in these missing mappings. The project selector L is not IIT, and no IIT value has been computed for these patients.

## 4. What observations would challenge the packages?

All rows below are **conditional** on the same target domain, content mapping, window, valid access and independently justified uncertainty bounds. An observation can instead expose failure of the bridge, the task or the package; it is not automatically a verdict on an isolated mathematical selection rule.

| Warranted target observation | Conditional implication | What is still missing |
|---|---|---|
| A particular subject genuinely experiences the two designated coarse side aspects **together** (`G=1`). | Contradicts L-exclusive's no-global-field package; fits C-full, C-layered and P. | Establishing joint phenomenal content, not just two input-dependent responses or a whole-field impression. A positive does not count hidden subjects. |
| A subject genuinely experiences the two specified detailed aspects together (`D=1`). | Contradicts both P and C-layered as completed for this window; fits C-full. | Correct detail, simultaneity and conjoint phenomenology; different content, a later composite memory, or different context is not the target. |
| A local-detail experience and the global coarse-conjoint experience occur concurrently, with independently warranted **distinct owners** (`N=1`). | Contradicts the closed, one-subject C-layered and C-full packages; compatible with P but also other plural accounts. | Distinctness and simultaneity cannot be assumed from differing reports, preferences, anatomical supports or same content type. Even `N=1` does not by itself establish P's exact three subjects, private tokens, q law, or physical constitution. |
| An independently warranted **complete** designated-population finding `N=0`, in the specified P episode. | Contradicts P's `N=1`; compatible with C-layered if its other stipulated contents hold. | Requires exclusion or adequate coverage of P's predicted private local/global experiencers. One apparently unified reporter, failed second response, or an unvalidated subject counter cannot establish this. |
| Adequately warranted `G=0` in the specified population. | Challenges P and both C completions, all of which stipulate `G=1`; fits L-exclusive at this target. | A negative is especially demanding: no report, chance performance or failure to reject chance is not such a finding. |

On the available records, none of these target-level findings has been secured. In particular, the evidence does **not** favor P over C-layered simply because a participant can give a global-sounding report while different processing occurs locally. Nor is the 2017 report that no second *agent* was apparent a direct prediction failure of P, which allows local subjects without separate agents or report access. Similarly, if a future result challenges both P and C-layered, that need not select C-full: other models and bridge failure remain live.

## 5. Why matched reports do not settle `N`

Consider one fixed physical record and a reporting protocol whose transcript contains a coarse bilateral description plus separate side-specific responses. Suppose, **as an explicit illustration rather than an empirical causal model**, both completions are assigned that same transcript distribution. In C-layered, the transcript is generated by access to aspects of one postulated subject. In P, it may be generated through a global report with local processing or local experiences that are not separately readable; its private local and global coarse occurrences are distinct even when they share a content type. If the stipulated observable distributions are identical, the transcript alone cannot distinguish the completions; likelihood ratios on that transcript are one. This is a conditional identification example, not evidence that all physically possible or actual observations must be identical, nor a license to alter P's report model post hoc.

Conversely, reliably different left and right manual answers can have a common controller, cross-cueing, separate task processors without experience, or a single subject with partially disunified aspects. A genuine second first-person report, if accessible, deserves positive weight; but calling a response first-person because it is produced by a different hand builds the conclusion into the observation. Conflicting preferences or self-descriptions can also occur within a single subject or across time. The **same-window** condition excludes merely alternating perspectives; it is still not sufficient for distinct subjects without an incidence principle. Same reportable content is compatible with separate private tokens; same content is not numerical token identity.

An ideal subject census would settle the closed packages by definition. It would not be an independently available empirical method merely because we name it. Likewise, independently observing P's required three incidences would challenge C-layered, but specifying how that judgment could be warranted is the hard problem—not a separate simple counting operation.

## 6. A realistic evidential contract, not a promised assay

A future ethically reviewed, consent-based observational protocol in appropriate existing clinical participants could narrow the gap without imposing a research surgery or assuming that one exists:

1. **Predefine patient-specific hypotheses.** Record lesion, preserved pathways and prior history from available imaging/records, timing since surgery, sensory and language abilities, report capacities and fatigue. Fix window, local-detail versus coarse-conjunction questions, field population, allowed alternative routes, and each rival's predicted joint report distributions and missingness *before* outcomes. If no defensible mapping from a patient's neural domain to the four-spin supports exists, test only a clinical analogue, not the toy package.
2. **Anchor the first-person content.** In consultation with the participant, elicit open descriptions and separately ask whether each specified detail and the two coarse locations were experienced together during the designated display, rather than merely whether the images matched. Include uncertainty, no-experience and inability-to-answer options, nonleading wording, comprehension checks, no-stimulus and within-field controls. Reports concern an experience reconstructed after the stimulus; probing may alter attention or the episode. Confidence and stimulus correctness alone cannot calibrate true phenomenal conjunction.
3. **Time and channels.** Randomize simultaneously presented, independently selected input features and compare matched sequential controls; log stimulus, gaze, response onset and order, and provide appropriate nonverbal as well as verbal channels without treating channels as owners. Do not assume simultaneous stimuli produce simultaneous experience, or that delayed reports refer to stable original tokens. Avoid excessive trials and fatigue; track nonresponse and all exclusions.
4. **Test causal alternatives.** Control and quantify gaze shifts, light spillover, apparatus, tactile/acoustic/motor cues and feedback, operator knowledge, sequential memory and response interference. Analyze the full joint input distribution and possible joint-function leakage rather than only one-input correlations. Account for unknown subcortical and spared routes; resting connectivity is not content transfer. Use patient-level inference, uncertainty intervals and dependence-aware analyses, rather than pooling repeated trials as independent patients. A negative cue test bounds only detectable monitored channels.
5. **Individuation rather than relabeling.** Seek converging, independently motivated, potentially defeasible signs of concurrent subject-specific perspectives: for example, separately elicited structured first-person content with time-locked, independently varied inputs, repeatable incompatible self-locations or access restrictions, and responses robust to known communication and switching alternatives. Specify *before* data why the total pattern would be less expected from one partially unified subject, one subject plus nonconscious processors, delayed reconstruction or a shared response system, and what observations would undermine the proposed signs. Content accuracy and noncircular evidence that each report actually expresses a lived perspective are separate requirements. Even striking dissociations may remain compatible with C-layered unless a justified subject criterion and error bounds make the competing predictions differ; no such validated criterion is supplied here.
6. **Protect negative conclusions.** To challenge P via `N=0`, justify coverage or a separately defensible exclusion principle for every P-predicted subject in the actual window, including noncommunicating ones. No practical method for this coverage has been demonstrated here. An independently warranted `N=1` need not require a census, but still requires reliable occurrence and distinct-owner evidence. If either bridge is unavailable, report `N` as unknown, not zero; do not invent accuracy or false-negative rates.

An authorized, explicitly post-hoc analysis of existing trial data could first reconstruct actual exclusions, timing and conditional performance and compare fixed operational alternatives. It cannot recover unrecorded phenomenological reports, certify unmonitored channels or retroactively make a task a subject census. Further data access and participant contact have not been undertaken.

## 7. Consequences for the project

The substantive progress is a sharper **asymmetric** evidence target: positive conjunction can constrain an exclusive no-global package; positive distinct-owner evidence can challenge a closed one-subject package; a negative plural-subject finding needs far stronger coverage. DDC's confident, phenomenologically meaningful same-task response makes it particularly unsafe either to read poor matching as absence of experience or to treat confidence as proof of simultaneous conjunction. Both P and C-layered remain live stipulations, not demonstrated experiential constitutions. Their overlapping support and private versus partially unified incidence still need independently motivated rules; their assigned qualities, including the illustrative global OR law, are not explained or measured by these studies.
