The two times are different kinds of quantities:

1. Real/elapsed time (clock time)

Defined by: a clock or physical duration — seconds, minutes, hours between events.
Intrinsic qualities: continuous, linear, uniform, irreversible, event-independent. It flows whether or not anything happens in the game.
In chess: it is extrinsic to the position and move number. It matters only if the game is timed; then it becomes a resource and can affect the result, e.g., by flag fall.

2. Game time / notation time (move time)

Defined by: the rules of chess notation — move number or ply count. A ply is one player’s move; a move number usually advances after Black replies.
Intrinsic qualities: discrete, ordinal, event-driven, non-durational. It does not measure how long anything took; it measures how far the formal game has progressed.
In chess: it changes only when a move is completed. During deliberation, it remains fixed.

The difference is: elapsed time answers “how long?” and flows independently; game time answers “how far?” and advances only on moves. A player can spend thirty minutes of real time with zero change in game time. In a timed game, real time can constrain or end the game, but it still does not by itself alter the position or move number.

deepseek response

They have not been fully reconciled. The apparent conflict is really between nonrelativistic quantum mechanics, which inherits Newton’s absolute time, and general relativity, in which time is local, dynamical, and part of spacetime.

A useful way to see it:

In ordinary QM, $t$ is an external parameter in
$$
i\hbar \frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t} = H\psi .
$$
It is not an observable, and all observers are tacitly assumed to share the same $t$. This is a Galilean/Newtonian approximation.


In GR, there is no universal $t$. A clock measures proper time along its worldline:
$$
d\tau^2 = -g_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu .
$$
Different observers on different trajectories generally accumulate different proper times. Coordinate time is arbitrary; only clock/proper time is physical.

So the conflict is less “QM vs. GR” than “Newtonian absolute time vs. relativistic local time.” Once QM is made relativistic, even special relativity already removes absolute simultaneity: $t$ becomes frame-dependent, and one uses quantum field theory.

A partial reconciliation exists in quantum field theory on curved spacetime. There, quantum fields propagate on a fixed classical curved metric. Time is not universal, but for any observer or foliation one can define evolution between spacelike surfaces, or use the Tomonaga–Schwinger equation for arbitrary foliations. This works well for Hawking radiation, inflationary cosmology, and similar settings. But it is not a full quantum theory of gravity: the metric is still classical, and backreaction is treated only semiclassically, e.g.
$$
G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G \langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle .
$$

The full reconciliation is the problem of time in quantum gravity. If the metric itself is quantized, time may no longer be a classical background parameter. In canonical quantum gravity, the Hamiltonian constraint gives the Wheeler–DeWitt equation
$$
\hat H \Psi = 0,
$$
which contains no external time at all. Time must then emerge from something internal: correlations with a clock, relational variables, entanglement, or thermodynamic/statistical structure.

Leading proposals include:

Relational time / Page–Wootters: the global quantum state is static, but a subsystem behaves as if it evolves when conditioned on a clock subsystem.
Emergent time: time arises from entanglement or thermodynamic properties, as in the thermal time hypothesis.
String theory / holography: time may be emergent from a more fundamental boundary description, as in AdS/CFT.
Loop quantum gravity, causal sets, causal dynamical triangulations: time is recovered from causal order or relational dynamics, not assumed as a background.

None of these is established. The most defensible statement today is:

Ordinary QM’s absolute time is an effective, low-energy approximation. GR’s local dynamical time is more fundamental in the classical gravitational regime. A full reconciliation requires a quantum theory of gravity, in which time is likely emergent or relational and reduces to both limits appropriately. Until then, the two notions are reconciled only in semiclassical approximations, not fundamentally.

I'll approach this rigorously. The core issue is that the word "time" is being asked to do two incompatible jobs: act as an external parameter (QM) and be a dynamical, relational feature of geometry (GR). Bridging them requires not a new definition of time, but a precise mathematical meta-framework in which both are derivable. I'll propose a concrete research program borrowing from six fields, each solving a specific piece.

1. The Target Formal Structure: A Fibration of Temporal Theories

The goal is a mathematical object $ \mathcal{T} $ such that:

A "global section" of $ \mathcal{T} $ recovers the absolute parameter $ t $ of non-relativistic QM.
The "base category" of $ \mathcal{T} $ encodes local, relational, or causal structure from which GR's proper time $ \tau $ emerges as a connection or metric.

Category theory provides the language. The proposal from recent work on knots and temporality is to view time not as a coordinate but as a functor between a category of "physical configurations" and a category of "temporal indices"[reference:0]. In this view, QM and GR are two different fibers over a common base of relational data (events, causal order). The "problem of time" becomes the problem of choosing a cleavage (a consistent way to split the fibration), which is exactly what a gauge choice is in GR.

2. A Timeless Base: Order Theory and Causal Sets

The base category must be pre-temporal. The rigorous tool here is order theory, specifically locally finite partial orders. Causal set theory (CST) is the canonical physical application: the fundamental structure is a discrete set with a causal order $ \prec $, and time is not an input but an emergent property[reference:1].

A recent axiomatic result is sharp: for a Lorentz-invariant discrete order, the causal relation already carries time with sign and structure, while space carries neither[reference:2]. This means the base category is not a blank slate; it has a temporal orientation built in as a structural feature. The "time" that emerges is not a fundamental flow but a grading of the order.

3. Lifting to Quantum and Geometric Fibers: Topos Theory and Sheaves

Given the base causal order, we need a way to attach quantum and geometric data. Topos theory, developed for quantum gravity by Isham, Butterfield, and Döring, is designed exactly for this. The motivation is that the smooth manifold model of spacetime is too restrictive for quantum gravity; a topos provides a generalized space where the continuum is not fundamental[reference:3].

Concretely, one constructs a presheaf over the causal order. Each "stage" of the order carries a local algebraic structure (a C*-algebra or von Neumann algebra). The sheaf cohomology of this presheaf encodes the obstruction to gluing local descriptions into a global one[reference:4]. This is the mathematical analogue of the problem of time: a global time parameter exists only if a certain cohomology class vanishes. The Chrono-Structural Invariant (CSI) proposed in recent work is a candidate for this class[reference:5].

4. From Timeless Constraint to Schrödinger Evolution: Page–Wootters and Operator Algebras

The Page–Wootters mechanism shows how a global constraint $ (\hat{H}_C + \hat{H}_S)|\Psi\rangle = 0 $ yields an effective Schrödinger equation for the system $ S $ when conditioned on a clock $ C $. The rigorous formulation uses von Neumann algebras. In the thermodynamic limit, the clock's algebra becomes a type III₁ factor, and modular flow (Tomita–Takesaki theory) generates an emergent time parameter[reference:6]. This is not an analogy; it is a theorem: in a thermal state, the modular automorphism group provides a one-parameter family of automorphisms that can be interpreted as time evolution[reference:7].

The key insight is that time is the modular flow of a state. This makes time state-dependent and observer-relative, which is exactly what GR demands, while still reproducing the Schrödinger equation in the appropriate limit.

5. Operational Semantics: Time as a Measurement Protocol

To make the bridge to QM rigorous, borrow from operational semantics in computer science. The Overtime interpretation of QM proposes that a quantum system has no definite temporal location prior to measurement; time is established only through a synchronization protocol between the system and a reference clock[reference:8]. This is a direct formalization of Einstein's operational definition of simultaneity, extended to the quantum domain[reference:9].

Formally, one defines a time reference frame as a physical system whose algebra of observables includes a clock variable. A transformation into a different time reference frame is a unitary transformation on the joint Hilbert space[reference:10]. The QM parameter $ t $ is then the classical limit of this operational synchronization, valid when the clock is effectively classical.

6. Information-Theoretic Emergence: From Correlations to Temporal Order

Finally, information theory provides a quantitative bridge. The Emergent Time from Informational Mechanics (ETIM) framework posits that temporal order arises from the causal and informational organization of discrete events[reference:11]. It introduces a time intensity field and an information update map, showing that the Wheeler–DeWitt equation appears as a coarse-grained description of informational dynamics[reference:12]. A complementary result shows that entropy production defines an emergent time parameter $ d\tau = \lambda(C) dS_{\text{obs}} $[reference:13].

This connects to the thermal time hypothesis: time is the flow generated by the modular Hamiltonian, which is the information-theoretic conjugate of entropy.

Synthesis: The Bridge as a Research Program

The six tools assemble into a single program:

Base: A locally finite, Lorentz-invariant partial order (causal set).
Sheaf: A presheaf of operator algebras over this order (topos quantum theory).
Clock: A distinguished subalgebra (the clock) whose modular flow defines a local time parameter (operator algebras).
Operational: The physical interpretation of this parameter is given by a synchronization protocol (operational semantics).
Information: The modular flow is the information-theoretic flow of a state (ETIM, thermal time).
Emergence: The QM parameter $ t $ is the global section of the fibration when the clock is classical and the cohomology class vanishes. GR's proper time $ \tau $ is the connection on the base order induced by the sheaf of algebras.

The "problem of time" in this framework is not solved by a new definition, but by demonstrating that the QM fibration and the GR fibration are two cleavages of the same underlying presheaf. The rigorous work ahead is to prove the existence and uniqueness of such a presheaf for a physically relevant class of states, and to show that the CSI vanishes in the semiclassical limit. This is a concrete, falsifiable, and mathematically precise research program.

1. Adjudication of contradictory points

1.1 Cleavage vs. gauge choice

Grok writes that “different choices of cleavage typically correspond to different gauges or different internal clocks.” Sonnet correctly objects.

In a Grothendieck fibration $p:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{B}$, a cleavage is a choice of cartesian lift for each morphism in $\mathcal{B}$. It is extra structure on the fibration, but it does not change the fibration itself. Two cleavages of the same fibration are not gauge choices; they are equivalent up to natural isomorphism in a suitable sense. A gauge choice is better modeled as a section of the fibration or a functor out of the total category $\int F$.

Adjudication: Sonnet is technically correct. The original phrase should be replaced by: “two readings (functors out of the total category) agree on a suitable subclass of states.” Grok’s underlying point—that the choice of clock is ambiguous—is valid, but it is an ambiguity of sections, not cleavages.

1.2 Presheaf of algebras vs. presheaf of states

Grok says one can imagine a “presheaf of von Neumann or C*-algebras over a causal set.” Sonnet corrects this.

In algebraic QFT, the net $O\mapsto \mathcal{A}(O)$ is a covariant functor from the poset of regions (ordered by inclusion) to C*-algebras: if $O_1\subset O_2$, there is an inclusion $\mathcal{A}(O_1)\hookrightarrow \mathcal{A}(O_2)$. It is a precosheaf (or copresheaf), not a presheaf. States, however, restrict: if $\omega$ is a state on $\mathcal{A}(O_2)$, it restricts to a state on $\mathcal{A}(O_1)$. Thus
$$
\mathcal{S}(O)={\text{states on }\mathcal{A}(O)}
$$
is a presheaf on the opposite poset of regions.

Adjudication: Sonnet is correct. The natural object is a presheaf of states, or equivalently a copresheaf of algebras. The formal framework should be built on $\mathcal{S}$, not on $\mathcal{A}$ as a presheaf.

1.3 CSI definition

Grok says “CSI” is not defined. Sonnet provides a concrete candidate definition using Connes cocycles.

Given a cover ${O_i}$ with faithful states $\omega_i$, the Connes cocycles
$$
(D\omega_i:D\omega_j)_t
$$
are unitaries in $\mathcal{A}(O_i\cap O_j)$. They measure the mismatch of local modular flows. Connes’ theorem says the modular flows of two faithful states differ by inner automorphisms, so the class in $\mathrm{Out}(\mathcal{A})$ is canonical and state-independent. Sonnet defines the Chrono-Structural Invariant (CSI) as the class of these cocycles in nonabelian Čech cohomology
$$
\check{H}^1(\mathcal{C};\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A}))
$$
or in its image in $\mathrm{Out}(\mathcal{A})$.

Adjudication: Sonnet advances the program by giving a precise definition. Grok’s caution that this object has not been shown to be the correct obstruction is warranted, but the definition itself is well-posed and worth adopting as a working hypothesis.

The corrected, well-posed version of the original idea is:

On a class $\mathfrak{S}$ of states with geometric modular action, the QM time (modular flow or Page–Wootters conditional evolution) and the GR time (proper time along causal curves) agree up to proportionality. More precisely:

Base category $\mathcal{C}$: finite causal sets or causally convex regions of a globally hyperbolic spacetime, ordered by inclusion.
Net of algebras: a covariant functor $\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{C}\to \mathrm{C^*Alg}$.
Presheaf of states: $\mathcal{S}:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to \mathrm{Set}$, $\mathcal{S}(O)={\text{states on }\mathcal{A}(O)}$.

The Chess CSI: A Formal Toy Model

1. The Category $\mathbf{Ch}$

Let $\mathcal{P}$ be the set of legal chess positions. A position is a tuple

$$
P = (B, \sigma, c, e, h, n)
$$

where $B$ is the board configuration, $\sigma \in {W,B}$ is the side to move, $c$ encodes castling rights, $e$ the en passant square, $h$ the halfmove clock, and $n$ the fullmove number.

Let $R \subseteq \mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{P}$ be the move relation: $(P,Q) \in R$ iff there is a legal move from $P$ to $Q$. Define $\mathbf{Ch}$ as the free category on the graph $(\mathcal{P}, R)$. Its morphisms are finite move sequences. The initial position $P_0$ is the standard starting position.

The game tree $\mathcal{T}$ is the universal cover of $\mathbf{Ch}$ from $P_0$. Its nodes are games (paths from $P_0$); its edges are moves. Two games that transpose to the same position are distinct nodes of $\mathcal{T}$ but map to the same object of $\mathbf{Ch}$.

This distinction is the whole point. It is the chess analogue of the distinction between a spacetime (where worldlines are distinct even if they meet at the same event) and a configuration space (where only the state matters). Notation time lives on $\mathbf{Ch}$; clock time lives on $\mathcal{T}$.


2. Time Functors

A time functor is a functor $T : \mathbf{Ch} \to \mathbf{T}$, where $\mathbf{T}$ is a monoid or ordered set.

Functor Target Definition Descends to $\mathbf{Ch}$?
$T_{\text{ply}}$ $(\mathbb{N},+)$ $+1$ per move Yes
$T_{\text{full}}$ $(\mathbb{N},+)$ $+1$ per Black move Yes
$T_{\text{half}}$ reset monoid $+1$ unless capture/pawn move, then $0$ Yes
$T_{\text{clock}}^W$ $(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},+)$ $+\delta_W$ on White's moves, $0$ otherwise No
$T_{\text{clock}}^B$ $(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},+)$ $+\delta_B$ on Black's moves, $0$ otherwise No

The crucial column is the last. $T_{\text{ply}}$, $T_{\text{full}}$, and $T_{\text{half}}$ are determined by the position: they are functions $\mathcal{P} \to \mathbb{N}$. They factor through $\mathbf{Ch}$. Clock times do not. Two paths to the same position can have different clock readings. This is the chess analogue of proper time being path-dependent in GR.

The halfmove clock is the chess analogue of the thermodynamic arrow: it counts moves since the last irreversible event (capture or pawn move), and it resets. It is a discrete, state-dependent, non-monotone time. The 50-move rule turns it into a physical constraint.


3. The Two-Clock System and the Čech CSI

3.1 Local clock data

A segment $S$ of a game is a contiguous sequence of plies. Its clock content is the pair

$$
(w(S), b(S)) \in \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^2
$$

giving the time spent by White and Black during $S$. Clock content is additive: if $S = S_1 \cup S_2$ with $S_1 \cap S_2 = \emptyset$, then $w(S) = w(S_1) + w(S_2)$, and similarly for $b$.

A cover of a game $g$ is a family ${S_i}$ of segments whose union is $g$. On each $S_i$, we have the local clock pair $(w_i, b_i)$. On an overlap $S_i \cap S_j$, the pairs must agree if the clocks are globally consistent.

3.2 The cocycle

Define the Čech 1-cochain

$$
g_{ij} = (w_i - w_j, ; b_i - b_j) \quad \text{on } S_i \cap S_j.
$$

This is a cocycle: on triple overlaps, $g_{ij} + g_{jk} + g_{ki} = 0$. It is a coboundary if there exist local shifts $h_i$ with $g_{ij} = h_i - h_j$.

The chess CSI is the class

$$
\mathrm{CSI}{\text{chess}} = [g{ij}] \in \check{H}^1({S_i}; \mathbb{R}^2).
$$

3.3 Standard chess: trivial CSI

In a standard tournament game, there is an arbiter's clock: a single wall-clock time $T_{\text{arb}}$ that runs continuously through the whole game. Every segment's clock content is a restriction of this global clock:

$$
w(S_i) = T_{\text{arb}}(S_i) - \text{(Black's moves in } S_i), \qquad b(S_i) = \text{(Black's moves in } S_i).
$$

More precisely, the arbiter's clock gives a global section of the sheaf of clock data. The cocycle $g_{ij}$ is therefore a coboundary: $g_{ij} = h_i - h_j$ with $h_i = (w_i, b_i) - (w(g), b(g))$ or similar. The CSI vanishes.

This is the QM limit. There is a global external time. The two clocks are not independent; they are slaved to one arbiter. The modular flows of the two "local algebras" (White's clock and Black's clock) are inner-equivalent because they share a global reference.

3.4 Arbiter-free chess: nontrivial CSI

Now remove the arbiter. Two players, two independent clocks, no global wall-clock. The clock content of a segment is now determined only by the players' own clocks. On an overlap $S_i \cap S_j$, the two players' readings can disagree in a way that is not resolvable by a global shift.

Concretely: suppose White's clock runs slow and Black's runs fast. Then on the overlap, the cocycle $g_{ij} = (w_i - w_j, b_i - b_j)$ is nonzero. There is no 0-cochain $h_i$ that makes it a coboundary, because there is no global time to absorb the discrepancy.

The CSI is nontrivial. It measures the obstruction to synchronizing the two players' clocks. This is the chess analogue of the absence of a global time in GR.

The two clocks are like two local proper times along different worldlines. The cocycle is the chess analogue of the Connes cocycle. The CSI is the chess analogue of the Chrono-Structural Invariant.

3.5 Relativity of simultaneity

The chess analogue of simultaneity is the pairing of White's move $n$ with Black's move $n$ to form fullmove $n$. This pairing is a foliation of the game into "simultaneity surfaces" (fullmoves). It is canonical in standard chess: White and Black alternate.

But if the players could move independently — say, in a variant where both players move whenever they like, with independent clocks — then there is no canonical pairing. Different observers (different ways of slicing the game into fullmoves) would disagree on which White move is simultaneous with which Black move. This is exactly the relativity of simultaneity, and it is the source of the nontrivial CSI.

4.1 Superposition of games

$$
\hat{C}_W |g\rangle = C_W(g) |g\rangle, \qquad \hat{C}_B |g\rangle = C_B(g) |g\rangle,
$$

where $C_W(g)$ is White's total clock reading at the end of game $g$, and similarly for $C_B$.

4.2 The commutator

The two clock operators generally do not commute:

$$
[\hat{C}_W, \hat{C}_B] \neq 0.
$$

They fail to commute because White's clock reading and Black's clock reading are correlated in a way that depends on the game. If the game is a superposition of games with different clock profiles, the two clocks cannot be simultaneously diagonalized.

The commutator is the chess CSI. It measures the obstruction to a joint eigenbasis, i.e., to a global time in which both clocks are simultaneously definite. This is precisely the chess analogue of the Connes cocycle: the cocycle measures the failure of two modular flows to be inner-equivalent, and the commutator measures the failure of two clock operators to be simultaneously diagonalizable.

4.3 Page–Wootters chess

Impose a constraint

$$
(\hat{C}_W + \hat{C}B) |\Psi\rangle = \hat{T}{\text{total}} |\Psi\rangle,
$$

where $\hat{T}_{\text{total}}$ is the total time. Solutions are

$$
|\Psi\rangle = \int dt , |t\rangle_W \otimes |\psi(t)\rangle_B,
$$

and conditioning on White's clock gives a Schrödinger-like evolution for Black's clock:

$$
i \partial_t |\psi(t)\rangle_B = \hat{H}_B |\psi(t)\rangle_B.
$$

If $[\hat{C}_W, \hat{C}_B] = 0$, this is exact. If not, there are $O(\hbar)$ corrections — the chess analogue of the Kiefer–Singh corrections. The chess CSI is the leading term in this discrepancy.

6. What This Achieves and What It Does Not

Achieves

Makes the CSI concrete. The chess CSI is a finite Čech cohomology class. It can be computed for any game or tournament. It vanishes iff there is a global clock.
Separates gauge from physical time. Notation time is gauge; clock time is physical. The chess model shows how both can coexist.
Shows the thermodynamic arrow is a resetting clock. The halfmove clock is a clean discrete model of a non-monotone, state-dependent arrow.
Makes the type I/type III distinction tangible. Standard chess is type I; arbiter-free chess is the finite analogue of type III. The obstruction is visible in both.
Provides a Page–Wootters toy model. Quantum chess with a constraint gives conditional evolution. The commutator is the CSI.

Does not achieve

It is type I, not type III. Chess algebras are finite-dimensional. The modular flow is inner. The CSI is trivial in the strict algebraic sense. The nontrivial CSI in arbiter-free chess is a cohomological obstruction, not an outer modular class.
No diffeomorphism invariance. Chess has a preferred frame (the board). GR does not.
No backreaction. The board does not curve in response to the pieces.
No continuum limit. Chess is discrete, but the discreteness is not the same as causal-set discreteness. There is no Lorentz invariance.

These limitations are real, but they do not invalidate the toy model. The Ising model is not a ferromagnet either, but it captures the essential structure. The chess CSI captures the essential structure of the obstruction to global time.
