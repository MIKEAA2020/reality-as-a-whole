what is it like to be a strand of hair, there's no feeling. is the grown tip dead?


deepseek response

There is no “what it is like” to be a strand of hair, because a hair strand has no nervous system, no sensory organs, and no subjective experience. It doesn’t feel anything—not pain, touch, or its own existence. When you feel a hair being pulled, the sensation comes from nerve endings in the skin and hair follicle, not from the hair shaft.

Biologically, the visible hair shaft is mostly dead, keratinized material. The living part is the hair follicle below the skin: cells there divide, produce keratin, and push older cells upward. As those cells move up and harden, they lose their nuclei and metabolic activity and become the hair shaft. So the grown-out tip is indeed dead keratin—but more precisely, it is a nonliving structural product made by a living follicle, not a dead organism.

So: no feeling, no experience; the shaft is nonliving/dead keratin; the tip is the oldest exposed part and can be damaged or split, but it was never a living, feeling tip.
