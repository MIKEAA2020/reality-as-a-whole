**A Zero-Residue Simulation Metaphysics**

**1. Why Bostrom's version fails**

Bostrom's trilemma is a claim about our location within reality, not an account of reality. Taken literally, it leaves at least these residues:

1. **Regress:** who simulates the simulators?
2. **Base reality:** why does a bottom level exist, and why does it have these physics?
3. **Motive:** why would anyone simulate anything?
4. **Particularity:** why this simulation and not another?
5. **Mind:** why does computation produce experience?
6. **Ground:** why is there computation, or anything, at all?

A zero-residue account must remove all six. The strategy is to stop treating simulation as an event in a contingent world and treat it as a structural feature of a necessary one.

**2. The construction**

**Step 1: Reject any contingent base.**
Any base level that merely happens to exist, with particular laws, is a brute fact. So the ground must be something that could not have failed to exist and could not have been otherwise.

**Step 2: The ground is the space of all computations.**
Whether a given program halts, or what a given machine outputs at step *n*, is true or false independent of any physical machine running it. These are arithmetical facts, and they are fixed by logic, not by anyone's decision. So computation does not need a computer. Every computation is already "run" in the only sense that matters: its states and their relations obtain necessarily.

**Step 3: The regress ends because nobody is simulating.**
Nested simulations (A runs B, B runs C) are real structures within this space. But the space itself isn't run by anything. "Who simulates the simulators?" has the same answer as "what causes 2+2=4?": the question has a false presupposition. Simulation becomes a relation of containment inside a necessary structure, not a causal act inside a contingent one.

**Step 4: Plenitude removes selection.**
Why this computation and not another? Any restriction (only these programs, only these laws) is an arbitrary cutoff, so a brute fact. The only restriction-free option is no restriction: every computation, or more broadly every consistent structure, is instantiated. The theory is not "everything exists" added as an extra posit. It is what remains once every arbitrary selection principle is refused.

**Step 5: Particularity becomes indexical.**
Why am I in this world? Every observer-containing structure is real, and each contains observers asking the same question. "This one" is an indexical, like "now" or "here." It indicates a location and doesn't describe a contingent fact about reality as a whole. Nothing needs to select it.

**Step 6: Orderliness comes from measure and self-selection.**
Why is our world lawful rather than chaotic? Observers require stable, information-bearing structure, so observers find themselves where such structure exists. Simple, compressible programs also dominate the measure over computations (a Solomonoff-style weighting). Lawfulness is the typical condition of observers, not luck.

**Step 7: Motive becomes optional.**
Bostrom-style ancestor simulations, built for purposes, are included as a subclass of the structures in Step 4. Purpose is no longer needed to explain existence. Some simulations are purposive, and the foundation doesn't depend on them.

**Step 8: Mind is identified with structure, not linked to it by law.**
If consciousness were a further fact added to computation by contingent psychophysical laws, that would be a brute fact. So the account requires a structural identity thesis: experience is what certain informational-causal organizations are from the inside. There is no extra ingredient, so there is no bridge law to explain.

**Step 9: Ground the explanatory principle.**
Why accept that everything must be explained? Because the alternative, positing a brute fact, arbitrarily privileges one way reality could be. The principle of sufficient reason is a norm against arbitrary privileging. Plenitude together with necessary mathematical structure is the unique position that violates it nowhere.

**3. The resulting picture**

- **Reality** = the totality of all structures, with computations as their dynamical aspect.
- **Existence** = necessary; there is no "nothing" alternative to explain.
- **Simulation** = containment of one structure's dynamics within another's.
- **Our world** = one observer-supporting structure, picked out only indexically.
- **Bostrom's trilemma** = a consequence: since simulated observers vastly outnumber unsimulated ones across the totality, we should expect to be in a nested structure.

**4. Honest ledger: where residue might remain**

I can't claim to have shown zero residue, only to have moved the candidates to the deepest level.

1. **Which logic or mathematics?** "All consistent structures" presupposes a notion of consistency. Arguably no alternative is even statable without it, but that is an argument, not a proof. Indefinite extensibility (no set of all structures) also threatens "the totality."
2. **Measure.** "Simple programs dominate" needs a measure over an infinite space. The choice of reference machine looks arbitrary, and nothing yet shows that no privileged measure exists.
3. **Identity thesis.** Step 8 removes bridge laws by fiat. Critics will say that the *identity* itself is the brute fact, since it is not derived from anything.
4. **Necessity of the ground.** Claiming mathematical truths are necessary and self-subsisting is a substantive metaphysical commitment (Platonism-like), not a derivation.
5. **PSR and modal collapse.** A strong PSR tends toward necessitarianism. Plenitude makes this harmless, since all possibilities are actual, but the cost is that the theory is nearly unfalsifiable and its predictive content depends on the unresolved measure problem.

**5. Bottom line**

The most defensible zero-residue version of the simulation hypothesis is no longer "someone built a computer." It is: **reality is the necessary, unrestricted totality of computational structure, and what we call simulation is nesting within it.** That eliminates the regress, the motive problem, and contingent selection. It leaves, at most, a thin set of foundational commitments about logic, measure, and mind, each arguably forced by refusing arbitrariness but none proven.
