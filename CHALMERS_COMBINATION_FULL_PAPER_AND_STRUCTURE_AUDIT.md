# The combination problem: full supplied paper and the relational candidate

## Source and scope

The supplied [*The Combination Problem for Panpsychism*](sources/uploads/combination.pdf) by David J. Chalmers is a 37-page author manuscript. All pages, including the previously truncated sections 8–9 and references, were read from its extracted text; all pages contain text, and the PDF reports no embedded raster images. The source file is retained without modification, SHA-256 `e7948184f9db70613d5dd8048436d640cd0ab1059018e979bd0d55772e40f8ee`. The [published chapter DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780199359943.003.0008) identifies the work, but the published version has **not** been compared with this supplied manuscript; page numbers below refer to the manuscript. Other works cited in it have not been independently verified merely by reading its references.

The paper makes no claim to have solved or proved insoluble the combination problem (p. 3). Its distinction between subject, quality and structure—plus unity, boundary, awareness and grain—strengthens the requirements of the project. C3 is **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with the other criteria; experience is accepted as a datum. The author's panpsychist micro-subject framing is not simply identical to our current relational, experiential-ground candidate.

## 1. What the complete argument adds

**The position under test.** Constitutive Russellian panpsychism treats microphenomenal facts as the categorical bases of microphysical roles and holds that they, possibly with physical relations, constitute macroexperience (pp. 3–6). The paper does not restrict the constitutive base to a bare list of subjects; it explicitly permits relevant causal and other physical relations (p. 6). E-L hypothesizes an intrinsically experiential whole and genuine plural subjects but does **not** claim that each fundamental physical particle is a subject, that microscopic subjects sum, or that a single cosmic subject divides. Avoiding those particular commitments removes some premises of Chalmers's subject-summing and cosmic-decomposition formulations, not the need to explain individual subject incidence and quality.

**The subject-summing challenge.** Given certain subjects and all specified physical relations, why must there be a further subject? Chalmers offers a conceivability-to-possibility argument, explicitly dependent on contestable premises (pp. 8–10). It is not a proof that all forms of experiential grounding fail. Equally, the user's strong thesis that every genuinely possible reality is actual does not settle whether this counterdescription is genuinely possible. We cannot dismiss it simply by pointing to a set of mathematical models or affirming plenitude.

**Phenomenal bonding and the boundary problem.** A genuinely phenomenal relation may have a physical relational role, but naming it is not yet showing how a distinct unified subject arises. Chalmers asks how a bonding relation avoids global fusion through pervasive relations and, at the other extreme, fragmentation (pp. 23–25). A simple physical-connectivity closure would give the weakly coupled four-spin graph one component, whereas the existing L rule picks two local pairs. Neither output is an independently identified subject; the comparison shows why the choice matters, not which is correct. The whole is not assumed to be a universal consciousness.

**The palette and awareness.** A few primitives may in principle support many qualities, but the paper stresses that distinct subjects' separately instantiated qualities do not automatically produce a further jointly experienced quality (pp. 28–30). Merely adding a tuple of mismatch bits, as in [the cross-grain facet gate](CROSS_GRAIN_SUBJECT_FACET_AND_QUALITY_GATE.md), addresses an informational capacity constraint under its conditional transport premise; it does not produce the felt character of any quality or demonstrate joint awareness. Rejecting a separately primitive ego is not the same as denying that real subjects and felt experiences exist; wholesale eliminativism is not an eligible resolution of this project.

**The previously unread structural argument.** On pp. 30–34, Chalmers reconstructs an argument from correspondence of microphenomenal and microphysical structure to an alleged mismatch between macrophenomenal and macrophysical structures. He explores whether “macrophysical” equivocates: narrowly, ordinary physical-space and physical-property structure; broadly, any higher-level structure constituted by microphysics, including informational or computational structure. He considers an informational route promising but argues it still owes an account of why such a higher-level phenomenal structure is constituted rather than associated by a brute principle, and how it relates to physical macroquiddities without causal-exclusion problems. His conclusion offers a combination of possible approaches, not an achieved synthesis (pp. 34–35). This philosophical **structural mismatch** is not the candidate's proposed physical misalignment or frustration as unpleasantness.

**Textual care.** In the supplied manuscript on p. 33, the discussion of the narrow/broad distinction says “premise (2) is true only of narrowly macrophysical structure,” but premise (2) on p. 30 says microphenomenal structure constitutes macrophenomenal structure; it contains no macrophysical comparison. In context, the author appears to mean the mismatch claim—premise (5) in the six-premise version, or (4) in the earlier tetrad. This is a likely manuscript cross-reference error, not a demonstration of a substantive logical mistake; the published chapter has not been checked. Likewise, p. 15 calls the “third” class combinatorial although the list identifies the **fourth** as combinatorial. The surrounding taxonomy makes the intended contrast clear.

## 2. A constructive test of the narrow/broad distinction

Use the existing four-spin `t=1/4` heat-bath kernel, not a brain model, and the [fixed calculation plan](CHALMERS_STRUCTURAL_ALTERNATIVES_FIXED_PLAN.md). For each of four physical descriptions, draw an unweighted edge when a positive-probability one-update transition takes one represented state to a **different** represented state. Suppress self-loops. The exact [verifier](verification/chalmers_structural_alternatives.py), [tests](verification/chalmers_structural_alternatives_test.py), and [receipt](verification/chalmers_structural_alternatives.json) give:

| Physical description of the *same* kernel | Support graph | Vertices / edges | Max projected-row TV within a current-state fiber |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Four individual spin signs | 4-dimensional cube | 16 / 32 | 0 |
| All three adjacent mismatch bits | complete bipartite `K4,4` | 8 / 16 | 0 |
| Outer two mismatch bits | 4-cycle | 4 / 4 | `2/21` |
| Any-mismatch bit | one edge | 2 / 1 | `5/24` |

All four graphs have different vertex counts and are therefore nonisomorphic as simple graphs. The first graph is the full spin-flip support, not the interaction graph of the four sites. The all-edge representation identifies only global spin-flip partners. Its exact stochastic quotient exists because the kernel respects that symmetry; it preserves the three-bit trajectory law, not absolute spin signs. The outer-bit and any-mismatch support graphs nevertheless exist as images, but their positive TV values show no common first-order transition kernel reproduces all fine-state initializations. In particular, a coarse graph's apparent simplicity can hide environmental context and memory. A physically open subject is not thereby ruled out.

This example is deliberately stronger than just listing different graphs: it distinguishes the exact dynamical quotient from merely projected transition support. The `K4,4` graph follows because a flip of an endpoint toggles one edge bit while a flip of an interior spin toggles two adjacent bits; the resulting eight-state graph has all 16 links across the parity classes `qL=qR` and `qL≠qR`. Left/right reflection transports the two outer bit roles and leaves the any-mismatch bit unchanged. These are exact statements about a finite constructed system, not a claim that these representations are biologically appropriate, phenomenally meaningful, or fundamental.

The prior [relational dynamics gate](INTEGRATED_SUBJECT_CONTEXT_AND_RELATIONAL_DYNAMICS_GATE.md) already established strong lumpability of all three bits and the `2/21` outer-bit discrepancy. We cite and reuse that derivation; the new part is the side-by-side support-graph comparison and `5/24` any-mismatch discrepancy in the same kernel. The block-criterion itself is established Markov aggregation theory, not a new theorem.

## 3. What this does and does not answer

**What the example answers:** a single physical system can have several explicitly defined broad physical structures that are not isomorphic to its fine state-transition support graph. Therefore a comparison of an experiential field with just one narrow graph would not, by itself, rule out the availability of a different physical structure. This is an illustration of a distinction already made by Chalmers, not a novel refutation of him.

**What remains unanswered:**

1. **Physical selection:** why would a specific broad physical description, intervention set and environmental scope be the right one for an actual subject? The any-mismatch bit erases both local facets; the outer-bit projection loses predictive context. The exact three-bit quotient retains model dynamics, but predictive closure neither identifies nor is a necessary condition of subjecthood.
2. **Phenomenal selection:** which empirically anchored experiential distinctions and relations correspond to the physical distinctions? A diagram's isomorphism or a graph's ability to encode data does not show its intrinsic nature is feeling. The user's misalignment idea remains a hypothesis about felt unpleasantness, not a measure validated by this calculation.
3. **Subject and ownership:** why are the contents one lived perspective here, two perspectives there, or something overlapping? Neither a connected component nor the stipulated L criterion supplies an independently warranted subject bridge. The cross-grain two-to-one comparison is between different queries, not an observed fusion.
4. **Causal relevance and explanatory necessity:** a law that simply pairs a broad physical structure with a stipulated qualitative label leaves the question of why this experience has this character. A successful report predictor could constrain that pairing but would not alone establish a non-brute constitutive relation.
5. **Persistence and measure:** neither the model's equilibrium distribution nor actualization of genuine possibilities supplies identity across local records or a reference-class measure. No external universal clock or temporal actualization process follows.

The two experiential-ground views disagree about incidence; N-L is matched to E-L by construction. A physical support-graph comparison cannot choose the winner. These are **research descriptions**, not a claim that incompatible complete accounts are all genuinely possible. Experience-respecting rivals remain eligible; none denies the datum of experience. A candidate with different empirically constrained report or intervention laws might be distinguishable, but none is specified well enough here to claim that it has been tested.

The most direct path forward is to develop one physical/experiential interface on an independently anchored common domain: predefine actual subject-indexed contrasts, clinically appropriate perturbations or naturally occurring dissociations, intervention-preserving descriptions, non-report controls and rival predictions; separately defend why the selected physical nature is intrinsically the relevant lived contrast. No new invasive stimulation is recommended.

The paper sharpens the governing objection but does not supply the missing bridge. B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open; no physics reconstruction, time-problem closure, substrate-independent constitution, experiential necessity or full battery pass is claimed.
