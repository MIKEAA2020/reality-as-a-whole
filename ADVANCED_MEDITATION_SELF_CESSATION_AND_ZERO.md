# Advanced meditation: self, cessation and the zero framework

Follow-up: [From reported cessation to a discriminating experiment](ZERO_MEDITATION_ACCESS_MEMORY_IDENTIFIABILITY_AND_PROTOCOL.md) gives an exact access-versus-memory identifiability example, tests the effect of a disruptive report interface, and develops a preregistrable protocol.

## The article in context

Matthew D. Sacchet and Judson A. Brewer's [Scientific American article](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/advanced-meditation-alters-consciousness-and-our-basic-sense-of-self/) (25 June 2024) surveys research on advanced meditation and the reported transformation of self-experience. It is a perspective by researchers in the field, not a new controlled trial. Its central relevance here is that **self-related narrative, the felt boundary of the self, consciousness, and pleasant or unpleasant valence can vary separately**. They should not be collapsed into a single quantity called “awareness,” nor should an experience of oneness be dismissed merely because it is not a proof of metaphysical oneness.

This complements the separate [2026 non-duality and neural-timescales analysis](NONDUAL_MEDITATION_NEURAL_TIMESCALES_AND_ONE_REALITY.md). The populations, practices, measures and studies differ; they are not independent replications of one another's detailed findings.

## What the article actually draws on

- **Absorption and the self.** The referenced [Yang et al. 7T fMRI study](https://academic.oup.com/cercor/article-abstract/34/1/bhad408/7369445?login=true&redirectedFrom=fulltext) studied one highly experienced meditator in 27 repeated jhāna runs over five days. The study analyzed brain activity and later ratings of attention and experiential qualities. Twenty-seven runs are **not 27 independent participants**. The work is exploratory and supports within-person investigation, not a universal brain map of consciousness.
- **Nondual and compassionate states.** [Schoenberg et al.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810017303069) collected EEG from 30 advanced meditators, with 29 usable datasets, during baseline and instructed Indo-Tibetan practice. They reported estimated current-density changes across regions and frequencies. This is not evidence of uniformly reduced neural activity, nor a direct measurement of compassion or a definitive localization of selfhood. The stages were practiced in a sequence; state, time, instructions and other factors may be intertwined.
- **Reported cessation.** The [2024 network analysis](https://researchinformation.umcutrecht.nl/en/publications/neurophenomenological-investigation-of-mindfulness-meditation-ces/) analyzed 37 reported events from **one** experienced meditator across 29 sessions. It reported decreasing large-scale alpha-band functional interactions from approximately **21 seconds before** the events and recovery across the subsequent 40-second window; it did not find modulation of its network-integration measure. The article groups alpha power and connectivity together as starting to decline about 40 seconds before cessation: the 2024 network paper's abstract specifies 21 seconds for connectivity. Estimated EEG functional connectivity is not a direct observation of all brain connections.

The 2023 spectral, 2024 connectivity and [2026 criticality](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667242126001120) reports analyze the **same 37-event individual dataset**. Their different signal analyses are informative, but should not be counted as independent participant replications. Event timing relied on an associated eye blink and subsequent reporting; the blink is an artifact and an imperfect proxy for the start and duration of subjective experience. The 2026 paper reports additional exploratory signal results and discusses the artifact and single-person limitations.

## The crucial distinction: a gap in report is not necessarily a gap in experience

The article presents cessation as a full loss of consciousness. That is an important interpretation of a report and of a contemplative tradition. But the data do not directly establish that *all* experience was absent throughout the interval: a later report of a discontinuity is also compatible, in principle, with experience that was not encoded, recalled, or available to the reporting system. These are alternative hypotheses, not an assertion that one has been established. Loss of ordinary self-narrative is likewise different from absence of every kind of experience. Reports of continuity, discontinuity, formlessness and well-being deserve to be studied in their own terms.

Nor does the neural result require interpreting experience as manufactured by something wholly non-experiential. On the proposed intrinsic/extrinsic view, first-person variation and brain variation are potentially two descriptions of related processes in one reality. The same correlations are also compatible with other mind–brain theories: they do not by themselves select that ontology, show access to a whole beyond the individual, or prove the self is unreal in every sense. They do challenge a simplistic identification of an enduring narrative ego with all possible consciousness.

## A constructive next step: distinguish four dimensions, not one

A testable bridge would represent a state by at least four separately assessed dimensions:

1. **Self-model:** intensity of narrative and self–world boundary.
2. **Access and memory:** responsiveness to minimally disruptive probes and subsequent recognition or recall.
3. **Phenomenal characterization:** first-person descriptions, including whether content, formlessness or a reported discontinuity is present.
4. **Valence and suffering:** pleasantness/unpleasantness and distress, separately from depth or unity.

Neural dynamics would be modeled alongside these dimensions, rather than equating one EEG metric with one dimension. The existing [conserved sphere-sign model](ZERO_CONSERVED_SPHERE_SIGN_AT_MAXIMAL_ORIENTATION.md) illustrates mathematically that a report interface and a signed observable can be distinguished while underlying constraints remain fixed. **It is only a structural analogy:** no sphere pointer is identified with a brain region, subject, meditation state, or felt valence.

One risk-bearing study would recruit multiple experienced practitioners and matched comparison participants, use repeated and counterbalanced meditation/control periods, and obtain brief structured phenomenological reports near each period. When ethically and practically compatible with the practice, randomly timed, low-intensity sensory stimuli and subsequent recognition tests could quantify *access or memory* around reported transitions. Stimuli themselves may disrupt the states; the protocol must compare probed and unprobed runs. Predefine event detection, artifact control, physiological monitoring, held-out person/session validation and analyses separating self-boundary ratings from valence and vigilance.

A specific hypothesis could be: **a preregistered multivariate neural-and-behavioral model predicts within-person changes in self-boundary ratings out of sample beyond attention, arousal and valence alone**. Failure to improve held-out predictions would count against that particular operational bridge. Even success would not establish that neural measures exhaust experience, settle continuity during an unreportable interval, or prove that one consciousness contains every subject.

For cessation specifically, reliable stimulus recognition would challenge a claim of complete inaccessibility for that stimulus and interval; failed recognition would not prove phenomenal absence, since perception, encoding, memory and reporting can all fail separately. Multimodal markers and replication can improve the evidence while leaving this inference problem explicit.

## What this contributes to zero

The framework proposes one self-sufficient reality whose intrinsic nature is experiential and whose extrinsic appearance includes matter, with a zero whole that can have nonzero differentiated contributions. These meditation results make a **real phenomenological contrast** relevant to that program: a person's sense of separateness can change without requiring the disappearance of all differentiated physical processes or all positive and negative feeling. They do not measure a globally additive experiential quantity, demonstrate cancellation between experiences, establish zero whole-energy, or determine why any sign should have a particular felt quality. Felt suffering is not canceled by declaring that another state is positive.

The constructive challenge is to explain, within the same constitution, why such distinct first-person organizations and their physical correlates arise and why a particular organization has its particular felt character. The article gives concrete research constraints and an empirical design target; it neither proves nor refutes the necessity or self-sufficiency of the proposed zero whole.
