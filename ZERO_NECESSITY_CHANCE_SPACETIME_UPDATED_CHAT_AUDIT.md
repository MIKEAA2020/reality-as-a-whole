The important logical distinction is:

```text
A particular outcome is necessitated    ≠    a probability rule is necessitated.
```

A necessary rule assigning `1/2` to `A` does not, *by itself*, determine whether `A` occurs. ## 1. Emergent spacetime and parsimony

The user's correction is important: **derivational difficulty is not itself an extra fundamental entity or law**. An account can be ontologically parsimonious even if extracting its consequences requires long and difficult mathematics. It is reasonable to find a genuinely self-sufficient, non-spatiotemporal constitution more elegant if it recovers spacetime without introducing an independent preferred foliation.

But we cannot rank every emergent-spacetime theory ahead of every alternative simply by counting the labels “pregeometry” and “spacetime.” We must compare their complete **independent** primitives, principles, parameters and realization conditions, not how many symbols appear in their presentations. An emergence map that follows from the fundamental constitution is not a new fundamental posit; a separately stipulated bridge or unexplained selection would be. Conversely, a preferred-frame proposal can itself have different ontological formulations. Fewer names on a list does not prove fewer independent assumptions or no residue.

The programme therefore adopts emergent/relational time and no fundamental preferred foliation as the user's constructive direction. It still owes an explicit recovery of causal structure, Lorentzian geometry, gravitational dynamics, physical clocks, experience and empirical predictions. Emergence is an ontological and explanatory relation, not necessarily an event that happens *at a time* before time exists. For the philosophical and physical recovery problem, see Wüthrich. ## 2. A necessary probability law versus a necessary outcome

The updated chat's final answer says that if probabilities are derived from a necessary structure, the outcome is therefore fixed. That inference needs an additional premise.

As a control on the **form of inference**, imagine two formal histories both specified by the same `P(A)=1/2` rule: `A` is recorded in one, `B` in the other. The rule is fixed in both descriptions, while the recorded outcomes differ. This does not demonstrate that both descriptions are genuinely possible complete realities; it shows only that the sentence “the rule has probability one-half” does not logically entail a particular record.

Under the user's stronger thesis, the **complete actual outcome**, not only the rule, is necessary. Then the actual `A` rather than `B` is not an irreducibly undetermined global selection. A candidate account must explain that outcome from the whole's nature; adding a necessary probability law while leaving a selected outcome brute would not satisfy the user's no-residue demand.

The core argument is therefore: (i) complete intelligibility requires a sufficient explanation of each actual particular outcome, not just of the distribution; (ii) irreducibly open chance, in the stipulated strong sense, leaves which particular outcome occurs unfixed by the complete constitution and without such an explanation; (iii) hence the two are incompatible. Premise (i) is the user's substantive no-residue principle of sufficient explanation, not a theorem of probability. Someone using a weaker conception of intelligibility could dispute it; that dispute does not undermine the conditional argument.

A deterministic theory can likewise leave its initial state, boundary conditions, measure or laws unexplained. Determinism alone is not the desired self-sufficiency.

## 3. What “probability” can mean under necessity

Use the user's strict sense of **irreducible objective chance**: a genuinely open fundamental alternative where the complete physical constitution does not fix which outcome occurs. A fully necessitated particular outcome excludes that kind of openness by definition.

It does not eliminate all uses of probability:

* **Epistemic:** a subject lacks access to the full necessary state and assigns credences given partial evidence.
* **Frequency or coarse-grained statistics:** a fixed history can contain many outcomes in a definite distribution.
* **Structural/self-location weight:** a necessary whole may contain multiple actual subject-incidences with nonnegative weights; a probability may be a normalized ratio if the measure is justified.

The last two can be objective in the sense of being physical or mathematically defined features of the whole, without being irreducible open chance. Philosophers also use “objective chance” in broader, contested ways that can include deterministic theories; for that reason a blanket claim that **no objective probability in any sense** can coexist with determinism is terminologically too strong. This does not require the user to adopt that broader usage. [2](https://sites.rutgers.edu/barry-loewer/wp-content/uploads/sites/195/2019/06/Loewer-Determinism-and-Chance.pdf)

“One inevitable outcome out of many” fits an individual definite-record description. It need not mean that the whole contains only one recorded outcome: a necessarily actual whole could contain distinct outcomes and subjects, if a justified physical theory establishes them. Nor do branch names or mathematical descriptions automatically constitute different subjects. No literal simulations or external assigning agent are assumed.

Most importantly, **a fixed or necessary measure is not automatically a justified experiential incidence measure**. Born weights, subject identity, actual self-location and the relevant signed experiential total need separate derivation. The occurrence of all relevant types alone does not fix their weights.

## 4. What quantum physics does—and does not—settle

Quantum theory supplies successful probability predictions. Standard presentations contain deterministic unitary evolution and a measurement postulate with probabilistic outcome predictions; whether the fundamental ontology includes an irreducible collapse, a deterministic configuration, multiple relative outcomes or something else depends on the interpretation. Observed statistical regularities alone do not select metaphysical necessitarianism or irreducible chance.

Relational quantum mechanics and QBism should not be listed as automatically deterministic merely because they offer relational or epistemic interpretations of the quantum state. Everettian accounts involve multiple relative outcomes and need an account of how branch weights connect with experienced probabilities; they do not simply select one inevitable global outcome. [5](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1405.7907)

Nonrelativistic Bohmian mechanics is an explicit example in which the wavefunction and complete particle configuration obey deterministic equations; recovering the standard outcome statistics uses the quantum-equilibrium/typicality structure and its assumptions. It is nonlocal and is **not** a demonstrated zero-based, foliation-free, no-residue experiential theory. [4](https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/fall2016/entries/qm-bohm/)

Conversely, calling ordinary quantum predictions “indeterministic” can be accurate when discussing a specified stochastic collapse model; it is not automatically a universal claim that all successful quantum interpretations posit irreducible chance. Nor is a necessary global quantum state enough by itself to settle the physical interpretation of observed records or derive the Born rule.

### Bell's theorem: the important qualification

The source's final remark treats “indeterminism” as a loose shorthand for Bell's exclusion of local hidden variables. That is too imprecise. Bell inequalities test a conjunction including a locality/factorizability condition and supplementary assumptions such as measurement independence; standard versions apply to **stochastic as well as deterministic** local models. Reproducing Bell-violating correlations requires rejecting or revising at least one relevant assumption, not rejecting determinism as such. [1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/bell-theorem/)

For deterministic local outcomes `A₀,A₁,B₀,B₁∈{−1,+1}` on each shared state,

```text
S = A₀B₀ + A₀B₁ + A₁B₀ − A₁B₁ = ±2.
```

A common, setting-independent ensemble has `|⟨S⟩|≤2`; the singlet prediction at suitable settings gives `2√2`. Loophole-free experiments observe a violation under their stated assumptions: Hensen and colleagues report `S=2.42±0.20` over 245 trials. [6](https://discovery.researcher.life/article/loophole-free-bell-inequality-violation-using-electron-spins-separated-by-1-3-kilometres/5cd7f40640d63025aefcd11c056a7a85)

A further distinction matters for this project: **deterministic or necessary settings do not automatically imply measurement dependence**. In a deterministic statistical model, the settings-generating variables and the relevant source variables can still have an independent distribution over the analyzed trials. A model that deliberately makes their distributions setting-dependent changes a Bell premise and must justify that choice; no such mechanism follows merely from the user's no-residue thesis. The included tests illustrate both points algebraically, not as alternative empirical results.
