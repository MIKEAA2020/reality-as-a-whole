# Can intrinsic symmetry determine the differentiated organization?

## The question

The [previous candidate](ZERO_TOTALITY_INTERNAL_DIFFERENTIATION_CANDIDATE.md) obtained nonzero zero-sum local responses from a single assumed relational functional, without fixing unequal references. But in its differentiated regime, the minima form a continuous family. Is the particular internal organization another unexplained selection?

A constructive possibility is that **an additional intrinsic relation distinguishes the organizations without naming a preferred location**. In the finite example below, a permutation-symmetric term reduces the entire family of minima to one permutation orbit. It yields positive and negative nonzero responses and a zero total at every minimum. No externally selected index is built into the term.

This is a conditional structural result, not a solution of the foundational problem. The added relation and its sign are assumptions, not consequences of zero; the model's variables have not been shown to be actual experiences. Moreover, one orbit is one physical organization only if the relevant permutations genuinely preserve the **complete** physical and experiential structure, including subject incidence. Symmetry of the simplified functional alone does not prove that.

## 1. A symmetric intrinsic discriminator

Use the previous finite functional `F(x,y)` and notation

```text
xbar = sum_i x_i/3,   z_i = x_i-xbar,   r²=sum_i z_i²,
q_i = x_i-y_i.
```

Assume the same `lambda>0`, `mu>15/4`, and define

```text
Phi(x,y) = F(x,y) + kappa (z_1 z_2 z_3)²,    kappa>0.
```

The added term is invariant under every permutation of the three indices, simultaneous sign reversal of the deviations, and the joint common shift of x and y. It does not privilege a named index or a positive rather than negative direction. It is nonetheless an additional specified structural principle, not a theorem of permutation symmetry or an established law of nature.

Let

```text
r0² = (mu-15/4)/lambda > 0,
y*_i = xbar+z_i/4,
d_i = y_i-y*_i.
```

Completing the squares yields the exact identity

```text
Phi(x,y) + (lambda/4) r0⁴
  = (lambda/4)(r²-r0²)²
  + kappa (z_1 z_2 z_3)²
  + 1/2 sum_i d_i²
  + 1/2 sum_(i<j) (d_i-d_j)².
```

All terms on the right are nonnegative. There are configurations with all of them zero. Therefore, every global minimum has `y=y*`, `r²=r0²` and at least one `z_i=0`. Because `sum_i z_i=0` and `r0>0`, the other two entries are equal and opposite. Consequently, with a joint shift left arbitrary,

```text
z is a permutation of (r0/sqrt(2), -r0/sqrt(2), 0),
q = 3z/4,                  sum_i q_i=0.
```

There are exactly six labelled orientations, all related by permutations; in the formal quotient by permutations and joint shifts there is one minimum orbit. This is a proof for the stated finite functional, not an empirical claim or a derivation of that functional from zero.

## 2. A worked comparison: zero and symmetry did not select the orbit

Choose `lambda=1`, `mu=23/4`, `kappa=1`. Then `r0²=2` and one minimum is

```text
z=(1,-1,0),  y*=z/4,  q=(3/4,-3/4,0),  Phi=-1.
```

The other five labelled minima are permutations of the same values. The signed zero does not make the nonzero responses absent. The q=0 component need not represent either a neutral experience or no experience: a physical–experiential mapping is still needed.

Now compare

```text
z'=(5/7,3/7,-8/7),   sum z'=0,   sum (z'_i)²=2.
```

With `y'=z'/4`, this is also a global minimum of the **previous** functional F with the same parameters; it has `q'=3z'/4`, zero total and the same squared response magnitude as q. Yet it is not a permutation of `(1,-1,0)`, nor can global sign reversal turn it into one. Its product `z'_1 z'_2 z'_3=-120/343` is nonzero, so it is not a minimum of Phi.

The important point is not that the new arrangement is more real. **Both functionals have the same permutation symmetry, and both can produce zero-total differentiated responses, but their minimum organizations differ.** Symmetry and zero alone do not select the added term. A claim that the term follows from zero's own nature would need a positive argument, not merely the observation that it is elegant or gives a desired orbit.

## 3. One mathematical orbit versus one actual whole

Several claims must be kept separate:

- **Relabelling:** If indices carry no intrinsic identities, consistently renaming all aspects and all relations gives a different description of the same structure. The six displayed coordinate representations are then one orbit.
- **Physical exchange:** If indices refer to distinguished physical situations or subjects, changing which situation has which response can be a different actual arrangement. The functional's permutation symmetry alone cannot erase that difference.
- **Full experiential equivalence:** To justify a quotient, the mapping must preserve everything relevant: qualitative character, subject boundaries, memories, physical relations and relational histories. A permutation of a bare q-list is not enough.

For example, if the first index has an independently meaningful fixed role, only permutations preserving that role can be treated as mere relabellings. The six minima then fall into three orbits according to whether that role has positive, negative or zero q. Which of those descriptions is physically appropriate is not decided by this toy functional. In particular, one must not identify an individual's suffering with another individual's pleasure by silently erasing their subject incidence. A real negative experience remains real even if the signed total vanishes.

The actual zero-sum theorem itself holds for **each** stated minimum, whether or not orientations are physically identified. The stronger claim that the whole is uniquely determined up to a legitimate equivalence requires the full equivalence to be independently justified.

## 4. Could possibility–actuality help?

The user's possibility–actuality proposal supplies another potential route: if distinct oriented organizations are genuinely possible and the whole realizes all such possibilities at their proper scope, an external selector need not choose just one to exist. But a minimum of this formal functional is not automatically a genuinely possible physical–experiential configuration, and local possibilities are not automatically distinct complete global possibilities.

Further, the model does not say whether multiple orientations are realized, whether they overlap, how their subjects are counted, or what measure they have. If separate actual sectors each independently satisfy a zero-sum relation, a finite sum of their totals is zero; this conditional arithmetic does not establish their existence, disjointness, weights, or a well-defined infinite sum. The [global modal argument](ZERO_TOTALITY_MODAL_SYMMETRY_BRIDGE.md) depends on a stronger same-whole interpretation than merely realizing locally different possibilities.

Thus there are two live options worth investigating—not a choice settled here: legitimate physical equivalence could remove apparent multiplicity, or a properly scoped plenitude principle could make multiple internal realizations actual without an external selection. Either must preserve real qualitative differences and explain its own application.

## 5. The experiential and foundational burden

The candidate might be developed as one self-subsistent physical–experiential constitution, whose intrinsic relations give both internal differentiation and a zero total. But the present model still assumes:

1. the variables and their relations, the minimum principle and the differentiating parameter regime;
2. the extra product-squared relation with `kappa>0` and the common scale;
3. which configurations and subjects are actually present, and the incidence weights;
4. why q is felt positive or negative character rather than only a mathematical response;
5. which full transformations are physical equivalences rather than changes of actual experience;
6. any connection to a global energy constraint and to relational time.

A proposed answer must establish how these are unified by zero's nature rather than treated as independent unexplained rules. Adding a symmetric term shows that an external orientation **need not be stipulated in this finite model**; it does not yet show why this particular internal term is necessary or why the structural model constitutes experience. Equally, the failure of this example to establish the full result does not show the no-residue demand is impossible, or rule out non-additive versions of zero-totality.
