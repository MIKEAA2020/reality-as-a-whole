# Full continuum geometry-slice map for the interacting model

## Result

For the specific interacting FLRW sector developed in the previous reports, the open embedded-eigenvalue problem can be settled by a direct estimate:

* **There are no eigenvalues embedded in the scattering band**, including its endpoints.
* Each pointer channel has precisely the one simple exterior bound eigenvalue proved in the [preceding report](ZERO_GEOMETRY_CLOCK_MOURRE_AND_BOUND_SPECTRUM.md), and no singular-continuous spectrum.
* Combining this with the [almost-everywhere cyclicity argument](ZERO_GEOMETRY_CLOCK_CONTINUUM_CYCLICITY.md), **almost every geometry slice determines the entire physical state** from its complete complex scalar-clock signal. The same set of good slices works simultaneously for the two logical levels and the two pointer channels.
* Between any two good slices there is an exact unitary change of solution-data representation, including the bound state and the scattering continuum. We give its spectral-measure formula below.

This is a substantive solution of the **full-state slice-reconstruction problem in this selected model**, not a solution of the full problem of time. The slice data are the entire complex covariant solution as a function of φ, not outcomes of an autonomous finite clock. “Almost every” does not identify α=0 as good for the scattering sector. No physical state, action, clock, subject, global energy or experience has been derived from the zero point.

The analytic estimate is below; [verification/zero_geometry_clock_no_embedded_eigen_test.py](verification/zero_geometry_clock_no_embedded_eigen_test.py) checks its constants and controlled finite-grid analogues. Passing a finite-grid test does not prove the continuum statement.

## 1. Model and a derivative bound

In each channel, write

```text
H = T − 2h − c d E,          T(p)=p²/6,    I=(−7,−6),
h∈{0,1}, d∈{+1,−1},       c=4/5,
Ω=√H.
```

E is the positive band-compressed Gaussian-smeared geometry-window effect, with

```text
K(x)=exp(−x²/2) sin(2x)/(πx)
    =(1/2π) ∫_ℝ g(y) exp(ixy)dy,
g(y)=∫_(−2)^2 exp[−(y−r)²/2]/√(2π) dr.
```

The window is even and its second moment is `∫y²g(y)dy=28/3`. Using the vanishing first moment and `|exp(ixy)−1|≤|xy|`,

```text
|K′(x)| = |(i/2π) ∫ y g(y)[exp(ixy)−1]dy|
         ≤ [14/(3π)] |x|.
```

For any `f∈L²(I)`, `G=Ef` is continuously differentiable, and Cauchy–Schwarz together with `∫_I |p−q|²dq ≤ 1/3` gives

```text
sup_(p∈I) |G′(p)| ≤ M ||f||₂,     M=14/(3π√3).
```

These bounds are uniform up to the endpoints; the smooth finite-band kernel justifies differentiating under the integral.

## 2. No embedded or endpoint eigenvalues

Suppose `H f=λf` for nonzero f, with λ in the closed free band `[6−2h,49/6−2h]`. There is a unique `p₀∈[−7,−6]` with `λ=T(p₀)−2h`. The eigenvalue equation reads

```text
[T(p)−T(p₀)] f(p) = c d G(p),       G=Ef.
```

Because G is continuous, `G(p₀)` must vanish: otherwise f behaves as a nonzero constant divided by `p−p₀` and is not square integrable, including if p₀ is an endpoint. For a.e. p≠p₀, the mean-value estimate and `|p+p₀|≥12` imply

```text
|f(p)| = c |G(p)−G(p₀)| / |T(p)−T(p₀)|
       ≤ (c/2) sup_I |G′|.
```

The interval has length 1, so taking the L² norm yields

```text
||f||₂ ≤ (c M/2) ||f||₂
       = [28/(15π√3)] ||f||₂
       ≈ 0.3430 ||f||₂.
```

This forces f=0, contradicting the assumption. Thus **no eigenvalue exists anywhere in the closed band**. The proof is independent of d and h and does not use finite matrices. More generally this particular estimate works when `0<c<3π√3/7`; the present c is comfortably inside that range. Other model assumptions, including positive-root existence and the exterior-state estimate, must still be checked if c is changed.

## 3. Exact continuum spectrum and the slice consequence

The previous report proved, for c=4/5, no singular-continuous component and exactly one simple eigenvalue outside the band in each d channel. Kato–Rosenblum identifies the a.c. sector with the multiplicity-one free band. Together with the no-embedded result,

```text
H_phys_(h,d) = H_ac_(h,d) ⊕ span{b_d},
```

where b_d is the unique exterior bound state, the same vector for both h levels. There are **no other point states**. This direct sum is a statement about the selected physical spectral sector, not a global energy decomposition.

For `a_α(p)=exp(−ipα)/√(2π)`, the cyclic subspace generated by a_α under Ω is the part visible in the full complex slice signal. The preceding analytic argument established that for almost every α it contains the entire multiplicity-one a.c. sector. It also showed b_d is visible for every `|α|<π`, and for almost every α overall. Since there are now no additional spectral sectors, K_α equals the **full channel Hilbert space for almost every α**. Taking the intersection of the four channel-wise good sets preserves full measure.

Consequently, for any two states ψ and χ in this physical sector, equality of all four complex functions `Ψ_(h,d)(α,φ)` for all real φ implies ψ=χ on almost every α. This is an injectivity statement for mathematical wavefunctions, not an assertion that a single observer can measure a wavefunction's complex amplitudes. Unknown global phase is physically immaterial; the statement keeps a fixed mathematical phase convention.

## 4. The continuum change of slice

For each channel let P(ds) be Ω's spectral measure. For a good α define

```text
μ_α(B) = ⟨a_α,P(B)a_α⟩,
ν_(ψ,α)(B) = ⟨a_α,P(B)(2Ω)^(−1/2)ψ⟩,
g_(ψ,α) = dν_(ψ,α)/dμ_α.
```

Then

```text
Ψ_ψ(α,φ) = ∫ exp(isφ) g_(ψ,α)(s) μ_α(ds),
||ψ||² = ∫ 2s |g_(ψ,α)(s)|² μ_α(ds).
```

The measure contains both its a.c. part and the single bound-state atom. Because a_α is cyclic, this weighted L² space is a unitary representation of the complete channel Hilbert space. The complex measure is finite, so its Fourier transform for all φ uniquely determines it.

For another good β, set

```text
ζ_(β,α)(s) = d⟨a_β,P(ds)a_α⟩ / dμ_α(ds).
```

The two scalar spectral measures are equivalent, and

```text
μ_β(ds) = |ζ_(β,α)(s)|² μ_α(ds),
g_(ψ,β)(s) = g_(ψ,α)(s) / conjugate[ζ_(β,α)(s)]
```

almost everywhere, with the appropriate scalar spectral representatives and channel labels. Cyclicity makes ζ nonzero almost everywhere. Thus this transformation is **unitary between the induced weighted physical spaces** and includes the bound atom; it is not generally unitary in an unweighted L² norm. Composition is inherited from the common physical Hilbert space.

Any bounded physical effect F or instrument Kraus operator B, including the complete first-reading construction from the earlier report, transports by this unitary. Its expectation and joint probabilities are therefore identical in the two representations. Detector z mixes the d channels, so its transport must include their inter-channel overlaps; the scalar formulas above apply channel by channel, not by identifying unrelated channel spectral labels. This equality follows from the common physical representation, not from an independent quantization in geometry time.

## 5. What this does and does not resolve

This result replaces the previous tentative discussion of singular-continuous or degenerate embedded sectors **for this particular model**. It provides a full, continuum, almost-everywhere geometry-slice *solution-data* representation with an explicit physical norm and a consistent way to transport the same detector instrument.

It does not show that a finite, localized device can extract these slice data, that every α is good, or that the geometry coordinate is monotone for every state. The bound state still rules out a normalized real-valued POVM covariant on the whole interacting sector under scalar evolution, as shown earlier; full slice reconstructibility does not evade that no-go. It also does not prove an α-local evolution law: the band-compressed interaction is nonlocal in α. Scalar checkpoints and instrument couplings were stipulated, and the model does not implement the inhomogeneous GR constraint algebra.

The next meaningful physical task is to replace ideal all-φ solution data and externally scheduled operations with a **closed, finite-resolution clock–detector–memory interaction**, and test how much of the unitary equivalence survives on its operational domain. Separately, derive the selected physics and actual experiential incidence from the point–sphere constitution if possible. A zero constraint is not automatically an objective whole energy; a putative experiential balance would not erase local suffering.
