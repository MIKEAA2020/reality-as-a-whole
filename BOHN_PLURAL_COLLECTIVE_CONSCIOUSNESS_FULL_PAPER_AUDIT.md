# Bohn's plural collective consciousness: full-paper audit and subject interface

## Source and direct result

The supplied [*Panpsychism, The Combination Problem, and Plural Collective Properties*](sources/uploads/bohn2018.pdf), by Einar Duenger Bohn, contains a cover page and 12 numbered article pages. We read the entire extracted text, including all footnotes and references, and visually inspected PDF pages 4, 6, 8, 10, 11 and 12. The supplied file is unchanged, SHA-256 `b43d1f76ba0e2fd9e0b24012ff0b132258af97867ca6c03fad706d1c99ca7533`. It gives the online publication date as 16 July 2018; the [journal citation](https://doi.org/10.1080/00048402.2018.1483410) is *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* **97** (2019), 383–394. References below use the **numbered pages in the supplied PDF**, which has pre-issue pagination. We have not compared it against a separately acquired final issue copy.

Bohn offers a genuine positive alternative to composing already conscious micro-subjects: a plurality of basic objects can **collectively and fundamentally instantiate** a consciousness property which is the same property instantiated by a composite conscious object. The plurality is, on his view, just those objects, not a further plural object. His proposal does not require each member to be a micro-subject and does not inherently posit one universal subject. It is not a physical algorithm for deciding which pluralities are conscious, and it expressly does not explain why consciousness properties exist at all.

This matters directly to the project: it supplies a serious prior formulation of an irreducibly plural experiential base, rather than another version of micro-subject bonding. Yet neither the plural predicate nor the proposed **trickling-down principle** independently chooses the E-L pair subjects, their `q` qualities, or a subject's identity across changing realizers. The [finite common-domain check](verification/bohn_collective_property_gate.py) shows, under clearly stated extra assumptions, that the same two local quality facts and one-way trickling principle permit both presence and absence of a designated *whole-plurality* quality fact. It does not show that such formal interpretations are metaphysically possible or establish the number of subjects.

## 1. Bohn's actual proposal

On pp. 1–2 Bohn defines basic objects and properties in terms of absence of a **full metaphysical explanation**, and initially assumes that nonfundamental facts are ultimately grounded in fundamental facts. His simplified traditional panpsychism attributes basic consciousness properties to individual basic objects; he explicitly says he is not defending panpsychism against physicalism or dualism as such. At the end he considers a non-well-founded alternative.

The familiar problem, as he presents it on pp. 2–3, is why some combinations of simpler conscious properties ground experience like ours and others do not. His pp. 3–4 alternative adds **plural properties** to the ontology: some basic objects may instantiate a consciousness property *collectively*, although no individual member has that same property alone. His example is apples surrounding an orange collectively, not each apple individually. The analogy motivates the grammar; apples are not evidence of collective consciousness. Footnote 10 explicitly notes a more complicated middle case involving proper subpluralities and sets it aside. We must not attribute a blanket ban on every conscious subplurality to his simplified contrast.

His formal proposal (p. 4) says, in substance: **if x has consciousness property M, and the being of x is grounded in the being of yy, then yy collectively have M**. Footnote 11 calls M multigrade: it applies to singular and plural subjects. He calls the position *pluralized panpsychism*. His thought is that a basic collective property instantiated by the basic objects grounding our body is **not a product of combining their simpler subjective properties**; the relevant property is the same basic property, rather than a new quality produced by fusion. He explicitly claims this avoids versions of the combination problem, including subject summing (p. 3 n.7). That is his ambitious philosophical claim, not an independently established solution to every remaining subject, boundary or quality issue.

Bohn denies that a plurality must be a single plural object or that it is ontologically posterior to its members (pp. 6–7). His discussion of God creating the basic things and properties (p. 7) is a familiar **illustrative creation picture**, not a premise that an external creator exists. The present framework does not adopt a creator or temporal creation process.

## 2. What he claims, and what he leaves open

- **Emergence:** Bohn defines the relevant strong emergence in terms of an unexplained property instantiated by a nonbasic object. On that definition he denies that a basic collective property of basic objects is emergent (pp. 6–7). This is a conditional claim about his definitions and grounding picture. It does not settle every rival use of “emergence,” or establish that plural consciousness is explained by the objects' singular physical properties.
- **Physical and causal character:** he suggests physical properties describe structural/relational aspects while intrinsic collective properties can be conscious; physical behavior is performed by conscious things (pp. 5–6). He does not supply a measured physical correlate or a unique physical-to-phenomenal map, and does not argue that consciousness as such supplies an extra physical cause. The proposal merits a noncircular account of how the same plural bearers are subjective and participate in physical behavior; declaring them intrinsic does not independently establish that account.
- **Quantum entanglement:** pp. 4 and 7 n.19 specifically avoid identifying conscious properties with entanglement. He offers entanglement as a *possible example* of a basic plural collective **physical** property, not as evidence that entanglement generates experience. It does not validate the E-L four-spin mismatch, fundamental strings or human subject boundaries.
- **Selection and bruteness:** he does not specify which pluralities are conscious, and considers the instantiation of basic consciousness properties by some pluralities to be brute rather than emerging from smaller conscious properties (pp. 4, 8–10). His comparison claims this is no worse than brute basic singular consciousness and better than a particular emergence view; that comparative verdict is contestable. It is not a derivation of the facts from something deeper.
- **Diachrony:** on p. 9 he treats the relevant plurality as potentially changing, comparing consciousness to a tornado whose realizing material changes. That analogy points to a persistence question, not a completed identity criterion or a demonstration of an external universal time. He explicitly defers a fuller treatment. A stable set selector alone likewise does not establish persistence in our model.
- **Empirical bearing:** on pp. 5–6 he suggests that if pluralities of multiple things had no empirical explanatory role beyond members and relations, this could favor a more traditional panpsychism; he calls this a kind of empirical falsifiability understood as **unnecessary postulation**. That is not a specified randomized test of phenomenal constitution. Conversely, successful individual-level physical prediction would not make the datum of experience disappear.
- **Life and consciousness:** p. 10 suggests life as a possible correlate but explicitly calls the thought speculative and nonessential to his account. Nonlinear behavior alone is not proof of a collectively instantiated basic consciousness property.

O’Conaill's later [*Panpsychism, Emergence, and Pluralities: Reply to Bohn*](https://doi.org/10.1080/00048402.2021.1925936), *Australasian Journal of Philosophy* 100 (2022), 419–424, has a directly relevant **abstract**: it argues the proposal resembles emergentism structurally and owes an account of how a plurality can be a subject. We read only the [public abstract and metadata](https://philpapers.org/rec/OCOPEA-2), not the article's full argument; the analysis below is our own and should not be presented as a detailed reconstruction of his objection.

## 3. A fair comparison with the project's proposals

Bohn intentionally treats some instantiation facts as basic. |

Bohn's use of collective properties is **not identical** to positing a collection of already individuated subject-fields or to experiential bonding between microsubjects. Nor does a collective property automatically become a separate agent or a singular subject simply because a notation can name the plurality. A proponent can take the conscious plurality itself as the subject, or relate it to a singular composite subject, but must make that interpretation explicit. The same *property type* holding of a composite and its grounding plurality is not by itself a proof of two numerically identical experience **tokens**, two separate subjects, or intersubjective access. Those are further claims. Bohn's language can be read as identifying the property, not as having already demonstrated any particular token-sharing account.

There is also a possible **grounding-direction mismatch** with the user's preferred whole, not an automatic contradiction. Bohn's basic objects' being has no full metaphysical explanation; if a theory instead says a distinct whole fully grounds the being of each of those objects, calling those same objects basic in Bohn's sense would conflict with that added grounding thesis. E-L's statement that a whole is self-sufficient or that parts are physically related does **not by itself** establish that full grounding claim or decide priority. A compatible adaptation could reject basic microscopic objects, alter the grounding story, or adopt plural collective experiential facts without Bohn's exact fundamentality assumptions; it must say which costs it accepts. The whole need not be a universal experiencer.

## 4. The one-way principle on the same four-spin sector

This is a formal application, not a claim about what Bohn says the four spins are. Fix `t=1/4`, the original one-update L selection, and its stipulated two pair fields. Make further assumptions solely for this comparison:

1. Regard the four physical nodes as possible **basic objects** in the comparison and postulate singular local composites `x_L,x_R` whose being is respectively grounded in the pluralities `P_L={0,1}` and `P_R={2,3}`. Neither grounding nor those composites follows from the spin dynamics or Bohn's paper.
2. Interpret the existing q=1 postulate for either pair as instantiating the *same type* `M` of designated unpleasant experiential property; q=0 does not instantiate **this M**. This does not say the q=0 subject has no experience or that two q=1 occurrences are one numerical token.
3. Apply Bohn's one-way principle to those two postulated groundings: if `x_L` or `x_R` has M, its respective plurality collectively has M. For the finite encoding also impose that no singleton node has M. Do not add a union-closure rule or an inverse rule automatically producing a separate singular composite from any plural M.

Take any of the physical states with `q_L=q_R=1`; the existing equilibrium distribution assigns that physical event probability `1/16`. Then `M(P_L)` and `M(P_R)` are required by the added assumptions. Compare two **illustrative interpretations**, each satisfying those implications and the singleton restriction:

| Property assignment | `M(P_L)` | `M(P_R)` | `M(P_L ∪ P_R)` |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Local-only plural extension | yes | yes | no |
| Whole-inclusive plural extension | yes | yes | yes |

The second interpretation says the whole plurality **collectively has M**; it does not assert that nothing there is conscious, and a theorist might identify that conscious plurality with a subject. Neither row supplies a separately stipulated singular whole-composite subject `x_W`, an ownership relation, or a validated assay. If one additionally stipulates an `x_W` with M whose being is grounded in the full plurality, Bohn's principle requires `M(P_L∪P_R)`; it does not follow from M on the two pairs alone. Conversely, the literal implication does not, without an added converse or a substantive plural-subject interpretation, force a distinct singular `x_W` from the last column.

The [program](verification/bohn_collective_property_gate.py) checks both assignments for all 16 spin states, preserving the pair q facts, one-way implications and singleton restriction, and transports their set-coded extensions through all 24 node relabelings: **32 assignments and 768 relabeling checks**. In the code, sets merely provide finite extensional bookkeeping; this does not reduce pluralities to set-objects in Bohn's ontology. These variants were constructed after reading the paper, not preregistered experiments. They do not show that the two descriptions are genuinely possible under the project's strong plenitude thesis, that these collective predicates have first-person character, or that the two L pairs are actually subjects. They show the **specific inference** from the stipulated pair facts and literal one-way principle to the full-plurality M fact is not forced without further premises.

This comparison is deliberately not a claim that Bohn endorses L or C, that his plural properties must be closed under unions, or that all pluralities have the property. Nor does equal physical probability decide an experiential reference-class measure. The issue is the scope of the additional experiential and grounding principles, not a physical disagreement about the kernel.

## 5. A useful warning about a singular fundamental whole

Read TDPc literally, including its singular/degenerate case. Suppose **as separate premises** that (i) an actual local subject `x` has M, (ii) the being of `x` is grounded in the being of one singular whole `W` alone, and (iii) TDPc applies to this grounding with `yy` the singleton plurality containing W. Then W's singleton plurality has M collectively. Bohn explicitly allows a singleton-degenerate case (p. 6). If one also adopts the further principle that a singular bearer of this M is a subject, the whole would be a subject of M.

This conditional does **not** show that E-L makes W the full singular ground of a subject's being, nor that TDPc is true, nor that W is the **only** subject. Even a whole that is a subject of some conscious property is not thereby shown to unify every local experience into one all-encompassing perspective. In a plural grounding, M belongs collectively to all the grounds; it need not belong to each one individually. The thought experiment exposes a genuine compatibility question to address before combining a whole-first ontology with Bohn's unrestricted-sounding principle, without importing a universal subject by definition.

## 6. Infinite descent and the explanatory demand

On pp. 10–11 Bohn grants that, if there are no absolutely basic objects, his basic-object definition fails, and proposes “gunky pluralized panpsychism”: the same consciousness property trickles down without end along the relevant grounding chain. He argues that non-well-founded descent need not require a priority cosmic subject; he also notes the analogous possibility of no top level. He does not prove that every possible grounding structure is coherent or that a literal infinite chain exists.

The displayed TDPc is written for singular `x` and plural `yy`. To implement repeated descent through *collective* grounds, an account must specify the grounding steps and how the principle applies again to a collectively instantiated property—for example by an explicitly justified plural-to-plural generalization or by intermediate singular bearers with applicable grounding relations. Collective instantiation alone does not distribute M to each member and therefore does not license applying the singular antecedent to every member. This is a clarification of an unelaborated formal obligation, **not** a claim that Bohn's gunky view is contradictory; he may intend a suitable generalization. A finitely checked toy chain would not settle the metaphysics of infinite ground.

Bohn explicitly does **not** aim to explain why consciousness exists at all (p. 8). Taking primitive collective experiences seriously can improve intelligibility relative to postulating intrinsically unknown microsubjects and an unexplained fusion. But if the project's governing C3 asks for **full structural and experiential intelligibility**, jointly with C1–C7, stopping at a brute distribution of basic qualities cannot be counted as having established that stronger target. That is a difference in explanatory ambition, not a proof that Bohn's more modest project fails on its own terms. Neither the user's strong actuality thesis nor finite consistency alone decides which complete account is genuinely possible.

## 7. What would advance the interface?

- Give an independently motivated, noncircular rule specifying **which** pluralities instantiate which first-personal properties, and whether plural instantiation itself is subjecthood or grounds a distinct subject. Explicitly distinguish property type, property instance, lived token and ownership.
- State whether and why those facts are basic, grounded, or part of a self-sufficient whole, including whether there are basic objects, how plural-to-plural descent works if not, and whether a singular whole is itself a consciousness bearer under the chosen grounding rules.
- Supply a principled account of relative boundaries and persistence when material realizers change, without deriving a universal subject or banning overlap by stipulation. Address the [bonding audit](PHENOMENAL_BONDING_SUBJECT_COMPOSITION_BRIDGE_AUDIT.md)'s distinction between within-field unity and intersubject composition.
- Specify and separately test physical–experiential predictions, including internal mismatch versus experience-respecting alternatives, with independently calibrated physical and first-person measurements. A collective label on `q` is no more an assay than the original label. The [physical measurement gate](FOUR_SPIN_PHYSICAL_MISMATCH_MEASUREMENT_GATE.md) is conditional and synthetic.

The paper makes the non-microsubject route more concrete and supplies a relevant alternative, but neither its primitive plural property nor the finite check establishes E-L's subject or quality bridge, physics recovery, a subject-selection measure, or ultimate self-existence. B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open. No substantive or external battery pass is claimed.
