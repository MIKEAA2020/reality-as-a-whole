# Constitutive candidates: what can actually distinguish them?

## Outcome

The proposed qualitative-constitution principle is still a schema. The next useful step is to distinguish **causal sufficiency**, **additional intrinsic constitution**, and **a merely psychophysical assignment**, then examine the separate composition rule each needs. None is selected by the current free-field mathematics or synthetic intervention results.

The most economical candidate to try to specify first is identity with **actual, adequately specified causal organization**, not identity with an abstract program or a behavioral interface. This is a research-ordering judgment, not evidence that causal organization is phenomenally sufficient. Adding an unspecified intrinsic property is not progress unless it independently constrains the phenomenal assignment.

## 1. Candidate R: realized causal sufficiency

### Substantive hypothesis

Whenever two actual processes are equivalent in the complete causally relevant organization, they are phenomenally equivalent, including whether episodes occur and their qualitative organization and incidence.

Formally, for an explicitly specified intervention domain D and realization relation,

\[
X\simeq_{D,\mathrm{causal}}Y\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
E(X)\simeq_{\mathrm{phen}}E(Y).
\]

The antecedent must concern actual realization of the relevant powers, not the observer's ability to assign an encoding to a history. It must specify temporal organization, intervention structure, and what is held fixed. The theory cannot use an unspecified “complete organization” to absorb every unfavorable result after the fact.

### What it buys

It rules out changes of phenomenal organization under genuine constitutive causal equivalence. It does not privilege biological material merely by naming it. It also does not grant experience to a lookup table or passive monitor merely because outputs match.

### What it does not yet buy

This invariance hypothesis alone says neither which organizations are conscious nor which qualities or boundaries they have. An enormous number of assignments remain invariant under causal equivalence. Source and monitor can fail to be equivalent without either being proved phenomenal. A constitutive map and a justified composition rule are still missing.

### What would count against it

A warranted phenomenal difference between actual realizations independently established as equivalent over the candidate's specified constitutive causal domain would count against that specified sufficiency claim. A merely behavioral match would not. Conversely, explaining a difference by a previously omitted causally relevant feature revises the original domain and must be documented, not treated as a successful advance prediction.

No such decisive comparison has been established by this repository.

## 2. Candidate I: causal organization plus a further intrinsic specification

### Substantive hypothesis

Causal organization alone does not suffice; some additional independently specified intrinsic constitution is required. Write the candidate basis as (R,ξ), with R the causal organization and ξ the proposed additional constitution. The candidate must specify how ξ fixes qualitative distinctions and episode incidence, or how its identity with those features is justified.

This is not the claim that a physical process cannot itself have qualitative character. If the qualitative character is identical with the adequately specified causal process, that belongs to candidate R rather than establishing an extra ξ.

### What it could buy

A concrete ξ might explain why causally coarse or abstract descriptions miss constitutive differences. A material-dependent property could qualify if its physical and qualitative relevance were independently justified. Such a candidate need not postulate a separate substance or a new force.

### The decisive burden

What specifies ξ other than “whatever makes this process conscious”? What constrains its assignments? How does it explain the qualitative distinctions and composition rather than restate them? If ξ has physical consequences, what are they? If not, what noncircular identity argument warrants it?

The earlier equal-stress/different-state constructions do **not** establish this candidate. They show that stress is incomplete state information, not that complete actual causal organization is phenomenally incomplete. Moving from one claim to the other would be an invalid inference.

An unconstrained ξ can reproduce every proposed phenomenal assignment. That does not refute every intrinsic-nature theory, but it means this unspecified version adds no selecting explanation.

## 3. Candidate B: an independently stipulated psychophysical map

### Substantive hypothesis

Retain a physical theory P and supply a law E=F(P) relating physical processes to phenomenal outcomes without identifying why F follows from their constitution.

A genuinely specified F can have empirical and explanatory value. It should not be dismissed just because it is not an identity theory. But calling F “identity” without an identity argument does not make it more constitutive.

### Burden under the whole-account objective

What explains this F rather than another? If F remains an independent fundamental law, a useful local consciousness theory may have been proposed, but the zero-residue constitutive objective remains unpaid. This is a distinction of explanatory scope, not an empirical refutation.

These are comparison families, not exhaustive mutually exclusive named schools. A developed theory may change category when it explains what originally appeared to be an extra primitive.

## 4. Composition is a separate test for every family

Neither causal sufficiency nor intrinsic character automatically yields a subject boundary. Each candidate must specify a rule Γ for episode incidence. The current candidates for Γ have different unpaid obligations:

| Proposed rule | What it specifies | What remains unjustified |
|---|---|---|
| Every maximal reciprocally connected core is one episode | An exact graph-support boundary | Why arbitrarily rare/weak bridges merge episodes, why these cores are phenomenal, and why the selected causal description is constitutive |
| One episode above an integration threshold | A graded statistic plus a decision rule | Why that statistic, scale, temporal window, threshold, and exclusion rule have constitutive significance |
| Constituents participating in actual joint events form episodes | A history-sensitive incidence proposal | What counts as the relevant joint event and temporal extent, and why its physical unity is phenomenal unity |
| Episode incidence is independently basic | A primitive phenomenal boundary | How it is related to physical organization and whether the ultimate explanation has advanced beyond stipulation |

The weak-coupling construction gives a precise burden for the first row: exact graph support jumps while finite-horizon transition predictions converge. It does not disprove a sharp phenomenal transition, because phenomenal continuity was not independently established. Requiring continuity merely to exclude the candidate would introduce another unsupported bridge.

Nor can lack of a theorem for one rule establish a rival. The admissible set may remain unresolved.

## 5. A concrete separating contrast—not a fabricated consciousness experiment

The existing source/monitor model supplies the cleanest small inverse problem. With ordinary calibration E(0,0)=0 and E(1,1)=1, four assignments remain:

| Candidate target | E(0,1) | E(1,0) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Source C | 0 | 1 |
| Monitor M | 1 | 0 |
| C AND M | 0 | 0 |
| C OR M | 1 | 1 |

The two off-diagonal target contrasts distinguish all four. These are already verified synthetic facts in [constitution versus monitoring](research/constitution_monitoring/REPORT.md), not new observations of experience.

The key point is **what counts as obtaining the target contrasts**. Forcing C and M apart is a physical intervention; it is not automatically a measurement of E. In this toy circuit the report R reads C. Assuming E=R during the dissociation immediately chooses the source candidate, but that assumption supplies the disputed target information.

A real study would need a justified target-evidence model whose reliability survives the intervention: convergent first-person discrimination, behavior, delayed reporting, or other measures may constrain it, but none is automatically an independent phenomenal meter. Their shared confounds must be modeled. Where reliable target evidence is unavailable, the correct result is an unresolved candidate set, not an inferred absence of experience.

This contrast can identify determination of a specified content within a supplied candidate class. Even success would not establish a theory of every quality or determine subject incidence. Content, monitoring, unity, and actuality remain different targets.

## 6. What a candidate must supply before another test suite is worthwhile

A concrete proposal should supply, in advance:

1. **Its constitutive basis:** exact causal variables, realization domain, and any additional ξ.
2. **Its phenomenal target:** presence, a specific qualitative contrast, or episode incidence—not an interchangeable use of all three.
3. **Its map or identity argument:** what links the basis to that target and what independently warrants the link.
4. **Its composition rule:** including temporal extent and behavior under added coupling or duplication.
5. **Its separating cases:** at least one contrast on which a serious rival gives a different outcome.
6. **Its evidence model:** what would warrant assigning the phenomenal target in those cases, including limits of report reliability.
7. **Its physical and explanatory debts:** what is recovered, what is assumed, and what would falsify or revise the proposal.

If these fields are blank, creating a numerical “constitution score” or assigning toy ground-truth consciousness labels would conceal the missing hypothesis rather than test it. The current repository contains no justified procedure for filling them automatically.

## 7. Revised research decision

Start by trying to specify candidate R with an explicit realization domain and a narrow, independently anchored phenomenal contrast. This is the minimal candidate because it does not add an unconstrained ξ. It is **not** thereby the most probable theory or an established sufficient condition.

Run the content-identification and composition problems separately. If the specified causal basis fails a warranted contrast, determine whether an omitted causal feature repairs it or an independently specified noncausal constitutive feature is genuinely needed. Do not infer either repair from failure alone.

The present evidence does not justify selecting a winner. The progress is a sharper research bottleneck: **a selective, noncircular warrant for the phenomenal identification and composition rule**, not a further calculation of free-field stress. No new consciousness data or mathematical theorem is claimed here, and the existing 1,012 executable research tests do not test the truth of any complete constitutive candidate.
