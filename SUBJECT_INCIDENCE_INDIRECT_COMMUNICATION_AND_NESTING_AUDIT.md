# Can indirect communication individuate nested subjects?

## Result

**Primary-source correction:** the [1971 tactile-writing follow-up](LEVY_NEBES_SPERRY_1971_TACTILE_WRITING_PRIMARY_AUDIT.md) recovered the original study's extracted text and distinguishes two tasks that the first version of section 3 below conflated. The archived Table II OCR is misaligned and its original image was not visually inspected; the follow-up relies on the authors' prose and states this limitation.

Elizabeth Schechter's 2015 paper supplies a genuinely motivated candidate for identifying distinct **thinkers**: if two independently qualified candidate thinkers interact psychologically only through effection and transduction—roughly, action and sensory registration—then they are distinct thinkers. Her argument makes sense of why carefully controlled split-brain behavior can be informative even when ordinary behavior looks unified. It is a serious alternative to simply counting response channels or brain regions.

Applied to this project's **private nested P**, however, it does not by itself distinguish a local subject from the global subject. Schechter's primary comparison is between two candidate systems, not between a local system and a physically encompassing one. P's whole support includes each local support, so a behavioral relay between left and right does not establish that local and global experiential processes interact only through that relay. Nor does a common physical element automatically establish a direct *mental-state* interaction. The relevant bridge from support and causal pathways to mental states—and from thinkers to first-person fields—remains to be defended.

The criterion is therefore a conditional, defeasible **source of evidence for a carefully specified pair**, not an established subject census, a refutation of P, or an empirically justified choice of C-layered. Its strong sufficiency premise has a cost: one must defend why two processes belonging to a single partially unified subject cannot meet the same pathway condition. A general claim that some mental states of one subject can interact directly does not entail that every relevant pair does so. Ordinary action–perception feedback and task-specific bottlenecks make the difference consequential.

## 2. Schechter's argument and its empirical ambition

At pp. 502–504, Schechter formulates three main premises:

1. **Candidate thinkers:** R and L are each eligible to be a thinker. For this argument R is the whole human animal minus the left hemisphere; L is the whole animal minus the right hemisphere. These are **overlapping whole-minus structures**, not just isolated hemispheres. Schechter largely assumes candidate eligibility and invokes comparison with hemispherectomy, a contested analogy (pp. 503, 513). That one isolated hemisphere can support a mind does not establish that the corresponding structure is a distinct mind when both are present.
2. **Individuation:** if R and L are candidate thinkers and their psychological states and activities interact only indirectly, then they are distinct thinkers. She defines the relevant indirectness through effection and transduction, not as absence of all causal relations. This is the conceptual premise defended in §§3–4.
3. **Empirical pathway hypothesis:** R's and L's mental states and activities interact only indirectly in the relevant case. She explicitly regards this as controversial and gives at most a *prima facie* defense, not a population-wide established result (p. 504). She suspects it may fit L.B. but not N.G. The candidate and pathway premises should be applied case by case.

Her aim is to reconstruct early neuropsychologists' reasoning and explain the duality intuition. A thinker is the entity standing in an individuating relation to its own mental phenomena; she appeals to characteristic causal relations among mental states and a broadly functionalist picture (pp. 513–516). She argues that states may behave like perception, thought and choice within R or L but not across the whole, rather than mere disjoint outputs proving two minds. She argues against reducing the evidence to an abnormal public display: coordinated behavior can result from shared body and environment and does not, by itself, decide the architecture (pp. 518–521).

She distinguishes **minds/thinkers** from **persons**, leaving room for two thinkers in one person (pp. 514 n. 9, 521–522); she does not claim her paper has already settled self-consciousness or moral and rational agency. The argument is not an inference that a single human animal is two people, or that each hemisphere is literally a separate person. Nor should its terms be rewritten as a direct clinical count of the project's three P fields.

## 3. L.B.'s tactile words: what the source actually says

The 1971 paper describes two commissurotomy patients, but L.B.'s **two tactile tasks must be distinguished**. In the separate-letter arrangement test he arranged six sets of two or three hidden plastic letters correctly; the authors report that he could not name any of the six afterward. In the preformed-word test, the plastic letters were already arranged into a word; on some trials he was asked to speak first and then write, and on others to write and then speak. The authors report no correct vocal identification before writing.

Schechter's reproduction (pp. 506–508) includes `PET`, first “don't know,” then written `PET`, then spoken `PET`; the original authors' prose independently describes that same sequence. Other entries include errors, and Schechter notes ellipses mean a response was **not requested**, not a failure. Schechter also describes the letters as arbitrarily ordered and, in one sentence, says the patient had viewed his written response; these descriptions conflict respectively with the primary preformed-word procedure and the screened hand. The hand was hidden; do not infer that he saw his own writing. The authors proposed kinesthetic feedback and report moving L.B.'s passive left hand through similar words and numbers, which he could generally verbalize. The primary Table II OCR is misaligned and the original image was not visually inspected; see the [primary-source audit](LEVY_NEBES_SPERRY_1971_TACTILE_WRITING_PRIMARY_AUDIT.md) rather than counting its malformed rows.

This is a meaningful process-level observation and a useful control for the proposed sensory channel. But the sequence—feel, write, later speak—is **not** a measured simultaneous local/global phenomenal conjunction. Passive movement's later naming supports the availability of a proprioceptive route; it does not prove that this was the only route on every original trial or that the prior written response was a distinct agent's report. Timings, control denominators, full trial records and independently verified individual anatomy are not established here. The authors themselves report that A.A.'s left-hand tactile input was not confined to the right hemisphere, so they used visual stimuli for him. Later cross-response results in other patients also underscore the need not to generalize a hand–hemisphere rule.

Schechter also reconstructs L.B.'s coordinated drawing and an anecdotal object-naming cross-cue, and J.W.'s compound-word drawings (pp. 506–510, 514–516). Those examples are not independently checked here and differ from each other; an anecdote is not a measured channel capacity. Her imaging discussion (pp. 510–512) contrasts an anesthetized child's reduced interhemispheric resting correlation with another patient's residual connectivity. Neither resting correlation nor its reduction is a direct measurement of subjecthood, directed information transfer, or awake same-window experience.

## 4. A disciplined candidate bridge—not a new discovery

To make Schechter's insight usable without pretending it is already an assay, define the following **proposed evidence contract**, inspired by her reconstruction but not identical to her thesis:

- `A`, `B`: prespecified candidate mental organizations for one person and a declared window, with independently justified evidence of actual first-person content in each. Eligibility must not be inferred merely from accurate output or identical content type. A capacity demonstrated in another patient or another time is not itself an occurrence in this window.
- `K`: a class of identified action–sensory relay paths, with specified timing and physical boundaries. The operational claim is that target-relevant interaction is dependent on K under a stated range of conditions; this requires positive path evidence, not simply failure of one alternate test. Physical causal influences outside the class, common causes, and bypasses need assessment.
- `I`: a stronger **psychological** premise that the relevant mental states of A and B interact only through such mediation, not merely that a chosen behavior does. Establishing `K` is not automatically establishing `I`; distinguishing physical signals from mental-state interactions requires an independently specified realization map.
- `S`: a subject-individuation principle that independently eligible A and B, with the relevant exclusive indirect psychological interaction, are numerically distinct subjects. This is stronger than the weak proposition that mental states within a subject *can sometimes* interact directly. Its scope must say whether partially unified subjects, contextual attention, short windows and indirect feedback within one subject are excluded, and why.
- `T`: evidence that the relevant subject-occurrences overlap in the target window and that the reporting/observation process relates to those occurrences, not merely to later memories or alternating states.

Under all these assumptions, distinct subject-occurrences for the pair are warranted **conditionally**; if A and B are specifically P's local-detail and coarse-global experiencers, this would support the project's `N=1`. The last identification is another nontrivial requirement. Removing `S` leaves a path finding, not a distinct-subject conclusion; removing separate evidence of experiences leaves cognitive organization, not two first-person witnesses. The contract does not imply that two subjects must be two independent agents or separate persons.

The necessity of the strong principle is contestable. A partially unified subject might have experienced aspects whose access is gated, with one aspect influencing another only after an action–feedback loop. Someone may read their own handwriting or look in a mirror to acquire information they cannot access earlier. Such examples do **not** prove that all of that subject's relevant mental states interact solely by those routes throughout the appropriate domain; a full account may posit other direct interactions. They do show why demonstrating one task-specific mediated path cannot alone establish the universal psychological premise, and why the one-subject competitor must be assessed rather than dismissed.

The converse also fails without further argument: a direct causal connection, or one common coordinating process, does not logically make two already independently established subjects identical. Ordinary people can exchange information through mediated channels, but defining every transducer crossing as a subject boundary would also multiply subjects within a person. Conversely, an independently justified neural or other direct interconnection between two distinct minds is not ruled out by a bare definition of first-personality. `S` would be a substantive restricted criterion, not a logical truth or a complete explanation of why physical organization is experiential.

## 5. Applying it to P and C-layered

In the [fixed four-spin completion](PRIVATE_NESTED_FIELDS_AND_EXPERIENTIAL_CONTENT_COMPARISON.md), P has two **private**, numerically distinct local subjects and a third, distinct global subject. Their local/global coarse experiences have related content types but are separate tokens; shared physical support does not imply shared token experience. The global subject has the coarse bilateral conjunction, while no designated P field has the detailed left–right conjunction. C-layered instead has one designated subject, a coarse global conjunction, and local detail aspects that are not conjoint. Both yield `D=0, G=1`, while P has `N=1` and C-layered `N=0`.

The following diagnoses are for that common, stipulated sector—not a claim that a patient is a four-spin system.

| Candidate pairing or evidence | Does it establish P's local/global `N`? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A demonstrated left-to-right action–sensory relay | **No.** | It concerns left versus right and a particular functional transfer, not an independently evidenced local-detail subject versus a global-conjunction subject. Subject eligibility, full route scope and the strong incidence bridge remain open. |
| Separate first-person evidence for local detail and global coarse conjunction, same window | **Not alone.** | C-layered allows distinct experiential aspects of one subject; P stipulates separate tokens and subjects. Content-type correspondence is neither token nor owner identity. |
| Local versus global supports `Pₗ ⊂ W` physically overlap | **No.** | A physical overlap does not itself establish one subject, direct interaction between two mental occurrences, or shared experiential tokens. Nor does it show distinct subjects. |
| Independently established `A` and `B` experiences, exclusive indirect **psychological** interaction, simultaneous occurrence, and defended `S`, with `A` mapped to local detail and `B` to global conjunction | **Conditionally yes:** `N=1`, challenging the closed C-layered package. | The finding would require all listed bridges and uncertainty bounds, including why the one-subject partial-unity rival cannot generate the pattern. It would not by itself identify P's third subject or its q law. |
| A genuine directly mediated mental interaction between mapped local and global processes | **No conclusion by this rule alone.** | It defeats that pair's *only-indirect* antecedent; a sufficient criterion with an unsatisfied antecedent says nothing about whether the owners are the same. It is not a refutation of P. |

There is a specific **nesting diagnostic**. If a physical comparison naïvely labels every element of `Pₗ` as A and every element of `W` as B, then each A element is already in B. A signal or operation in the shared support need not travel across any proposed left-to-right bodily relay to be physically present in both support descriptions. Thus classifying paths solely by whether they cross that relay can falsely make physical containment look like mental separation, or falsely count the common support as a demonstrated direct mental connection. It is necessary to specify **which distinct physical events realize which mental occurrences**, the causal pathway being assessed, and the criterion for direct psychological interaction. Numerical identity of physical constituents neither establishes nor excludes private phenomenal tokenhood. Schechter's R/L also physically overlap, but neither is simply the whole containing the other; her comparison does not automatically settle this different incidence problem.

There is a further asymmetry: Schechter's standard is a positive **sufficient** rule for a particular pair if its strong premises hold, not a complete enumeration rule. No independently validated instance here establishes either that global `B` is a distinct candidate thinker or that no additional subject exists. Failure to establish indirect-only interaction, absence of a local report, or direct interaction all leave `N` unresolved under this rule. A separately defensible population-coverage or exhaustive constitution principle would still be needed to warrant `N=0` and challenge P. `D=1` would challenge both P and C-layered for the stipulated episode regardless of whether the indirectness criterion applies; `G=1` alone fits both.

## 6. What would genuinely advance the comparison?

A patient-specific, ethically reviewed observational protocol could start by investigating the **operational** relay claim without assuming that it is a subject assay:

1. Prespecify independently generated inputs, timing, designated response windows and a target content, including same- and cross-field conditions; ask the participant nonleading questions about lived content and uncertainty as well as collecting performance. Match simultaneous and sequential arms where possible, and monitor fixation, visual, motor, tactile and acoustic leakage. Do not infer concurrent experience merely from concurrent inputs or from a later correct response.
2. Where safe and consented, compare normal and modified *sensory feedback* during otherwise comparable tasks. Test whether the predicted information-dependent behavior changes, and independently check motor ability, attention, response comprehension and cue detection. A feedback manipulation may itself alter experience or processing; it is not a clean isolation merely because a response changes. Unmonitored subcortical or other paths remain possible. No invasive intervention, no removal of a clinical connection and no clinical recommendation is proposed.
3. Report what the observed path data actually constrain, with trial-level timing and missingness, plausible shortcuts, patient-specific anatomy and uncertainty. If feedback-dependent behavior is reproducible, it supports a role for the route under those conditions; if feedback blockade fails, it challenges a **specified operational model** only when delivery, power, manipulation validity and report access are supported. Neither outcome alone refutes or verifies P or C-layered.
4. For a subject-level comparison, preregister **separate evidence** for a local-detail and a coarse-global phenomenal occurrence, their timing, and an independently defended rule connecting the particular psychological interactions to distinct owners. State a one-subject partial-unity model and a plural model with fixed physical and report predictions, including no response, and identify observations against each. If both can accommodate the same observations under credible bridges, return an identification set, not a forced count.
5. For P's exact three private fields or `N=0`, add independent coverage and token-individuation premises; this protocol does not yet supply them. `N=1` is existential and does not require a census, but still needs reliable identity and simultaneity evidence. Do not calibrate those by assuming which model is true.

**Decision:** the most valuable immediate follow-up is to develop and independently defend the local-to-global *psychological interaction* map, or obtain the original 1971 source and its protocol details. Neither should be presented as an already feasible clinical subject detector. The 1971 paper can test a process claim relevant to the analogy, but its sequential tactile task cannot by itself settle P's local/global subject incidence.

## 7. Remaining obligations

Schechter's candidate gives a precise reason to take some dissociations seriously, and prevents rejecting plural minds just because a shared body behaves coherently. Its application simultaneously exposes what must be argued: candidate mindhood, the quantification and significance of mental interaction, one-subject feedback alternatives, and an experiential subject-realization bridge. It offers no derivation of P's quality assignments, of C-layered's nontransitive unity, or of the four-spin selector from human anatomy. L is not IIT and no IIT value has been calculated.

C3, B4–B5/F2, B9/F3 and B10 remain open. The PAINgen 21 mean discrepancies still require actual conversion/export code; this source comparison does not resolve that separate issue. Nothing here claims a clinical subject census, a universal impossibility theorem, or a completed framework battery.
