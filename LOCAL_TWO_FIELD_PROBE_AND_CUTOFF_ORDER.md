# Local field probe: complete-order bound and an energy accounting gate

**Subsequent restricted advance:** [the complete two-field continuum analysis](TWO_FIELD_COMPLETE_INSTRUMENT_CONTINUUM.md) now gives an energy-constrained cutoff-to-continuum bound for both ordered instruments in this same 1+1 model. The bound is extremely conservative and does not complete the apparatus, 3+1 or gravity gates. The present report's statements that this convergence remained unproved describe its original scope.

## Result

The idealized distributed qubit of the [previous smooth-switching test](SMOOTH_SWITCHING_ZERO_MODE_LOCALITY_GATE.md) can be replaced, for the **system–probe interaction**, by a second relativistic scalar field. A smooth compact spacetime coupling between two free Klein–Gordon fields is an established model, not our invention: it is the explicit example in [Fewster and Verch, *Quantum fields and local measurements*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.06512).

For a specified 1+1-dimensional massive two-field model on a circle, this report establishes an **energy-constrained bound on the difference between the two complete operation orders at each Fourier cutoff**. The bound includes the full system and probe fields, a retained readout qubit, two recorded bits and arbitrary references. A source-tail estimate and a cutoff-independent classical energy estimate make the bound vanish faster than any power for the declared smooth smears. The continuum causality statement itself follows from the established local field theory, not from our numerical simulation.

A finite-mode Gaussian calculation independently checks the order bound, symplectic evolution and selected endpoint energies. The bounded result is an order comparison; it is **not yet a proof that the complete cutoff two-field instrument converges to the specified continuum instrument**. Nor does the model include quantum gravity, a physical control system or a fully relativistic record apparatus. The probe-field readout is still an idealized external qubit coupled to the outgoing probe field.

## 1. Declared model and record

Let `Φ` be the system field and `Ψ` the probe field on the full circle of circumference `2π`, **including both sine and cosine modes**. Their masses are `m_A=.5` and `m_B=.7`; free energies are normal ordered and denoted `H_A,H_B`. The common total input-energy condition is

```text
Tr[ρ (H_A+H_B)] ≤ E,
```

with arbitrary entanglement between the two fields and any untouched reference. For the instrument the readout qubit is independently prepared in `|0_z>`; it is not part of that arbitrary field–reference input.

Let `F_d` and `b_a` be the normalized, nonnegative compact `C∞` bumps in the [preceding report](SMOOTH_SWITCHING_ZERO_MODE_LOCALITY_GATE.md), with `d=.4`, `a=.08`. A system-source operation is generated by the free interaction-picture observable

```text
A_s = ∫ ds b_a(s) ∫ dx F_d(x) Π_A(s,x),       U_s = exp(−ig A_s).
```

The field–field probe interaction is

```text
H_int(t) = λ b_a(t−T) ∫ dx F_d(x−y) Φ(t,x) Ψ(t,x),
y=π/2,  T=.3,  g=λ=1 in the numerical illustration.
```

In the continuum its compactly supported interaction is the same bilinear type as the cited field–probe model. The prescribed coupling is a classical external control, not an autonomous detector. The minimum source–interaction spatial separation is `y−2d≈.770796`, while their maximum temporal separation is `T+2a=.46`. Thus their complete supports are spacelike separated.

After the interaction is off, an outgoing probe-field readout is made at time `.55` with a smooth time bump `b_a` and spatial bump `F_d(x−y)`. Its smeared probe observable `Q_r` couples to a fresh `|0_z>` qubit by `exp(−iη Q_r σ_y)`, with `η=1` in the illustration; the qubit is measured in the `σ_x` basis and its result copied into two bits with independent flip probability `.1`. The field and measured qubit are retained. This is a specified complete instrument. It is **not** a relativistic model of the readout qubit, bit memories, switching controller or reset process.

## 2. Continuum causal result

The underlying fields satisfy microcausality. Because the source support is spacelike from the entire interaction support, `A_s` commutes with the system field at every interaction point and also commutes with the independent probe field. Hence it commutes with the local interaction and its scattering operation. The outgoing readout acts on the probe field and qubit alone, so it also commutes with the source operation as an operation on the uncoupled system factor. The complete source-before-probe and source-after-probe instruments agree in this declared continuum model, including the retained fields and each subnormalized record outcome.

The second order is a formal algebraic comparison of operations whose prescribed interaction regions are spacelike; it is not an assertion that a later experimental run can reverse the coordinate time of those regions. The result is a specialized application of local field theory and the causal measurement framework, not a new general causality theorem. It does not imply that arbitrary global measurement prescriptions are causal, nor does it establish a physical 3+1 detector.

## 3. Complete finite-cutoff order bound

At cutoff N retain the zero mode and both real sine and cosine modes through N **for both fields**. Let `x` be the column of all field `q,p` coordinates, and let `J` be the block canonical symplectic matrix. The finite-mode time-ordered field–field interaction implements a real symplectic matrix `S_N`:

```text
V_N† x V_N = S_N x,       A_s,N = a_Nᵀ x,
D_N = V_N† A_s,N V_N − A_s,N = (S_Nᵀ−I)a_N · x.
```

Write the coefficients of `D_N` as `u_j q_j + v_j p_j`, over both fields, with their positive oscillator frequencies `ω_j`. Define

```text
C_N = Σ_j (u_j²/ω_j² + v_j²),
D0_N = Σ_j (u_j²/ω_j + ω_j v_j²).
```

The two pre-readout ordered unitaries differ by a Weyl displacement generated by `D_N`, up to an irrelevant scalar phase. Since the source commutes with the common probe-only readout, contraction under that readout and the standard creation/annihilation estimates imply

```text
|| J_N∘Ad(U_s,N) − Ad(U_s,N)∘J_N ||_{⋄,E}
 ≤ min{2, 2|g|[√(2E C_N)+√(D0_N/2)]}.       (1)
```

`J_N` here includes the interaction, complete outgoing probe readout, retained fields/qubit and records. The norm is the unhalved trace-distance supremum over inputs with the **total two-field** free energy at most E and arbitrary references. The bound allows the source to be applied before or after the readout without assuming the post-readout field energy remains below E: compare the two joint unitaries **before** their common probe-only readout.

### Why the bound vanishes

Let `P_N` be the spatial Fourier projector, and let `k_N(t,x)` be the commutator kernel between `A_s,N` and the truncated system field at a point in the interaction region. The untruncated kernel is zero throughout that region by spacelike separation. With `f_{n,c}` the cosine coefficients of the source bump and `ω_{A,n}=√(n²+m_A²)`,

```text
|k_N(t,x)| ≤ ε_N := (1/√π) Σ_{n>N} |f_{n,c} χ̂(ω_{A,n})|,
χ̂(ω)=∫b_a(s) cos(ωs) ds.
```

The source is even, so its sine coefficients vanish; this bound covers all `t,x` in the interaction support. The inequality is an infinite tail, **not** the result of fitting the finite numerical table. Since `F_d` is `C∞`, for every integer `r≥2`, integration by parts and `|χ̂|≤1` give the rigorous certificate

```text
ε_N ≤ ||F_d^(r)||₁ / [π(r−1) N^(r−1)].
```

The norms are finite for the declared bump; no numerical values for them are assumed.

Use the classical free-energy norm on each finite-mode phase space, `||(q,p)||²=Σ(ω²q²+p²)`. Spatial projection has norm one. The local bilinear interaction gives an interaction-picture generator with energy-norm bound `|λ b_a(t−T)| ||F_d||∞/m_*`, `m_*=min(m_A,m_B)`. Grönwall's inequality therefore gives a cutoff-independent bound on the symplectic evolution:

```text
||S_N(t)||_energy ≤ exp(K),
K = |λ| ||F_d||∞ / m_*.
```

Here `∫b_a=1`. Differentiating `a_NᵀS_N(t)` and using the kernel bound for the source–interaction commutator yields

```text
√C_N ≤ Q_N := |λ| exp(K) ||F_d||₂ ε_N/m_B,
D0_N ≤ Ω_N C_N,
Ω_N = max{√(N²+m_A²), √(N²+m_B²)}.
```

The factor `1/m_B` bounds the dual energy norm of the smeared probe field; the second inequality holds mode by mode. Combining these with (1),

```text
order distance ≤ min{2, 2|g| Q_N [√(2E)+√(Ω_N/2)]}.
```

For each fixed E and fixed smooth shapes, the right side tends to zero faster than any inverse power of N by choosing r arbitrarily large. The constants can be very conservative; this is not a useful certified physical cutoff in the example. It is also **not** a cutoff-to-continuum complete-instrument estimate: it compares the two cutoff orders with each other.

## 4. Endpoint energies and finite-mode controls

At finite N, the verifier integrates the full time-dependent linear symplectic equations for the interacting fields, with both cosine and sine modes. For the unperturbed product vacuum it computes the change in normal-ordered free energy separately for the system and probe. This is an exact Gaussian formula for the **finite model**; the numerical ODE and quadrature are approximate. The source and outgoing readout are separately priced, not blindly added to the interaction cost for an arbitrary input.

Illustrative values for `N=12` (`25` modes per field), `g=λ=η=1`, are:

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| vacuum energy gained by system during field–field interaction | 0.42169 |
| vacuum energy gained by probe during field–field interaction | 0.47148 |
| total vacuum field-energy change due to interaction | 0.89317 |
| source's **separate vacuum** field-energy increment | 12.73538 |
| outgoing qubit readout's nonselective probe-field increment | 0.82201 |
| two-noisy-bit vacuum order TV | 0.0000612 |
| full-order trace-distance upper bound, E=100 | 0.01281 |

The source value is vacuum-specific. The outgoing readout's increment is exactly `(η²/2) Σ_{j≤N} h_j² χ̂(ω_{B,j})²` for any finite-energy field input when the qubit is initially independent with `<σ_y>=0`; it does not cover the cost of operating or resetting that qubit. The interaction's energy change for an arbitrary input may contain cross terms and can be negative; the vacuum value is not a worst-case energy budget. External control, probe preparation, memory copying/noise/reset, local energy density, gravitational response and total work accounting remain open.

The sample bound at N=12 does **not** certify `.01281` for every higher cutoff. The actual finite-N values in the table are nonmonotone: the E=100 bound is approximately `.155` at N=4 but `.599` at N=6. Equation (1) is analytic; the displayed N-specific numbers depend on numerical integration and are not interval-certified. The general smooth-tail certificate above does not use them.

As a positive control, move the source to the interaction's spatial center while keeping its earlier switching window. Those supports are **not** spacelike separated. At N=12 the same vacuum record-order TV is approximately `.07929`, rather than the `.0000612` in the separated example. This checks that the finite model can actually transmit a source-dependent record; it is not an empirical detector validation.
