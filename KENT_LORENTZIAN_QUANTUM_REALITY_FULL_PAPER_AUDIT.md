# Kent's Lorentzian quantum reality: full-paper and beable audit

## Source and inspection

Adrian Kent, [*Lorentzian quantum reality: postulates and toy models*](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2014.0241), proposes a realist account of quantum theory on suitable relativistic backgrounds. The user-supplied [nine-page PDF](sources/uploads/kent2015.pdf), SHA-256 `1f83f8d754d72b2fe47dfd49a69b80bc06741c71e4d9eb27def5ab299929707c`, was read in full, including both toy models, discussion and references. It contains no numbered figures. The first page was checked visually; the rest was read from extracted text. The relevant equations and light-cone boundary were independently checked in a finite specialization below, not by a line-by-line reconstruction of a relativistic field theory. The cited earlier paper, [*A solution to the Lorentzian quantum reality problem*](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.90.012107), and later developments were not read for this audit; no claim is made that this paper is Kent's latest or final formulation.

## The proposal

A **beable** is a variable the theory takes to describe what is physically there, rather than merely a measurement outcome. Kent starts with a quantum state on an initial spacelike hypersurface and assumed unitary relativistic evolution. On a distant final spacelike hypersurface, he considers the Born-rule distribution of a hypothetical joint measurement of local mass–energy density. One final configuration is sampled from that distribution. This measurement is a mathematical prescription; no external observer or real final measurement is postulated.

For each spacetime point y, use the *same* sampled final configuration but condition only on the final data spacelike separated from y, outside its future light cone. A limiting sequence of spacelike hypersurfaces is used to specify the joint probabilities with the local stress-energy component. The conditioned expectation of the stress-energy tensor at y is the proposed local beable. The subset of final data changes with y, although the global final sample does not. An additional limiting prescription sends the final hypersurface to the asymptotic future; the paper assumes relevant coarse-grained distributions converge rather than establishing general convergence.

This is not a real collapse of the unitarily evolving wavefunction. It is a proposed single realized beable history specified by a global boundary condition. The light-cone rule gives the construction a Lorentzian geometric form rather than declaring one preferred simultaneity frame, but it does not by itself prove a fully rigorous interacting, covariant quantum field theory or settle quantum gravity. Calling the final condition part of a block description is an interpretation of the mathematical construction, not evidence that it is generated by a physical signal from the future or an agent who chooses outcomes.

## The two examples

Both examples are idealized 1+1-dimensional models. A massive system is initially in two separated localized states with weights |a|² and |b|². Photons move at light speed and bounce perfectly from the system; the model neglects system recoil, spread and self-dynamics. Kent explicitly notes that ignoring recoil violates momentum conservation in the toy interaction. This is not the same as a prediction of the exact underlying quantum theory.

**One photon.** If the final photon record corresponds to the first location, the local matter beables begin as two weighted mass clouds. After a first light-cone threshold the second cloud is zero while the first still has weight |a|²; only after the next threshold does the first cloud have full weight. Thus the sum of *these two matter-beable components on that coordinate slice* can be less than the system's full mass. This does not by itself establish nonconservation of the complete stress-energy beable, nor does it justify equating it with a physical collapse or a controlled energy loss. Kent also identifies a lightlike-boundary issue for the outgoing photon: strictly excluded lightlike endpoint data leave only fractional photon energy-momentum on the selected ray in the asymptotic construction. He tentatively regards it as a limitation of the sparse toy model.

**Two photons.** Opposite-direction records, including absence on alternative outgoing rays, allow selected mass and photon paths to be indicated more completely. In the chosen symmetric coordinates, the two matter clouds become one at t = t₁ − (x₂ − x₁), before the first photon–system encounter at t₁. That ordering is a feature of global conditional beables, not a demonstration of a controllable backwards-in-time intervention. Kent argues this model repairs the stated photon-path defect; it does not prove that arbitrary realistic environments or cosmologies do so.

Kent's discussion emphasizes that realistic 3+1-dimensional calculations, scattering with recoil, wavepacket spreading, and mathematically rigorous field-theoretic definitions remain to be supplied. His Bell discussion is qualitative, not a detailed worked Bell model. The Born weights of the final boundary do not automatically prove that every operational measurement record, in every physical setting, has been recovered; that requires an explicit record-recovery result. He states that the unmodified underlying quantum dynamics conserve energy, whereas interpreting the conditional beable as a gravitational source would require separate analysis.

## Independent finite geometry check

[The exact-arithmetic checker](verification/kent_final_record_toy_gate.py) implements a **discrete specialization**, not Kent's continuum QFT prescription: x₁ = 0, x₂ = 1, t₁ = 1, t₂ = 2, prior branch weights (1/3, 2/3), and deterministic alternative photon records at T = 8, 16 and 64. It conditions each matter location only on spacelike-separated final photon occupancy/absence records, with the lightlike boundary excluded. The [receipt](verification/kent_final_record_toy_gate.json) records 318 assertions, including passive Lorentz-boost checks at the chosen events.

For the selected first branch, the matter-beable weights are:

| Model | t = −1/2 | t = 0 | t = 1/2 | t = 1 | t = 3/2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| one photon | (1/3, 2/3) | (1/3, 2/3) | (1/3, 0) | (1/3, 0) | (1, 0) |
| two photons | (1/3, 2/3) | (1/3, 2/3) | (1, 0) | (1, 0) | (1, 0) |

These sample calculations agree with the paper's stated strict thresholds and distinguish *absence of a record at a spacelike-separated alternative* from data that are not eligible for conditioning. The invariance checks concern the spacelike relation for paired events, not covariance of a fully transformed quantum state, detector instrument or complete constructed beable field. Agreement for three finite T values and this finite time grid is **not** a proof of the asymptotic limit. The code does not model the photon-beable boundary issue, late matter records, recoil, noncommuting local stress-energy operators, quantum-field renormalization, gravitational backreaction, or subjective experience. It is a regression of the paper's explicit toy geometry rather than a new derivation of Kent's construction.

## Relevance and unresolved interfaces

1. **A serious alternative to a built-in measurement agent.** Kent provides a concrete example of a rule that purports to select a definite, covariantly described spacetime history without an observer causing a physical wavefunction collapse. It is prior art for any claim that no-observer quantum reality necessarily requires an external agent or preferred universal clock. Its initial and asymptotic surfaces are parts of the model's construction, not a clock dynamically imposed by a universal subject.
2. **Timeless fundamental whole versus emergent spacetime.** The model assumes a background spacetime, causal cones, quantum dynamics, and an initial state. It does not derive those structures from the project's timeless relational whole, explain why the required asymptotic data exist in all possible cosmologies, or solve the problem of time or gravity. The mathematical use of future boundary data can be embedded in a tenseless description, but doing so does not itself derive a self-sufficient whole.
3. **One selected history versus necessary plenitude.** Kent's stated rule samples one final configuration. The project instead seeks actuality of all genuine possibilities within the whole. A possible adaptation would include a family of allowed final conditions and their corresponding beable fields as relational constituents, rather than declaring one globally exclusive configuration. That is a *different ontological proposal*, not Kent's result. It would have to define compatibility and duplication of histories, their relation to quantum probability, a measure and self-location, and show why a situated subject sees definite records with Born statistics. Merely collecting all outcomes as actual supplies none of these results; nor does it require an actual simulation, external creator, or universal consciousness.
4. **Subject and experience bridge.** Local stress-energy expectations and environmental records do not identify a subject boundary or explain why any configuration is experienced as painful, pleasant or otherwise. Kent does not identify experience with a stress-energy value; our proposed intrinsic experiential ground remains an additional posit, and C3—**full structural and experiential intelligibility**—is not established. B4, B5, B6, B7, B9 and B10 remain open.
5. **Concrete checks required before use.** Define the final-data instrument and the local conditional quantities with a regulator; prove consistency across nested regions and a relevant limit; test record recovery and the Born rule, including spacelike separated outcomes; analyze local stress-energy conservation and the gravitational constraints for any proposed coupling; and derive or independently calibrate a subject/experience interface. A toy picture or a comparison against alternatives cannot substitute for those steps.

The net contribution is a precise existing **relational boundary-condition competitor** and an instructive finite check, not a solution of the framework's outstanding physics or experiential problems.
